## Story editing (reviewer-story-editing)

1. PASS WITH NOTE: A (recommended) or B represents the book honestly without spoiling it; C should be dropped because it shows the burnt Dutch camp the day after the capitulation, which gives away how Colachel ends.
2. N/A: removing the proof line from the copyright page touches no story, character, POV or timeline matter.
3. PASS: all nine blanks sit between a chapter's last page and the next chapter's first page, and no planned spread or page turn inside a chapter moves.
4. PASS WITH NOTE: automatic Virtual Panels may step through some pages in the wrong order and break a few beats the script built on the page; acceptable for launch, since the full page is always one tap away.
5. REVISE: the retail description says the book "ends at Colachel in 1741", but Colachel falls in chapters 14 and 15 of 28 and the comic ends at the Thrippadidanam in 1750. The opening line also reverses the book's first beat. Fixes below.

### Notes

**Item 1, cover.**
- A (ch 14 p11.4, charge into the Dutch square): honest. It shows the book's central battle at the moment of impact and gives away nothing more than the blurb's "Colachel in 1741". Nagoji (rust-red turban, red sash) is the clear lead. Dhanaji (white turban, indigo quilted tunic) rides level with him, whereas in the panel he is half a length ahead. That is fine on a cover, which should centre the narrator. One caution: A sells a battle book, and half the comic is court, household and frontier politics after Colachel. The revised description below covers that half, so cover and blurb together are honest.
- B (ch 11 p21.1, the ridge at sunset with Ibrahim and the Dutch ships): honest and spoiler free. It is the Dutch threat building, before any outcome. It is the quietest of the three as a store thumbnail, but on story grounds it is a fine second choice.
- C (described as ch 15 p1.3): do not use. The burnt stockade, the wrecked gun carriages and the charred camp behind Nagoji are the morning after the Dutch capitulation (V15 p15.1.1: "the day after the capitulation was signed"). That shows how the battle ended. The p15.1 scene is also the setup for the chapter's "lie", the march the King has no intention of letting the Dutch finish, so a reader who returns to the cover after chapter 15 will read it as that moment. The scripted p15.1.3 panel is "at the edge of the dunes" with hidden riders and a matchlock and no stockade, so C appears to combine p15.1.1 and p15.1.3. If C stays on the list at all, KINDLE-ITEMS should describe it accurately. Dhanaji is also cropped to an arm and a knee at the left edge, which reads as an error.

**Item 3, blanks.** Checked against the Kindle OPF spine (434 itemrefs, no side clashes) and against the V15 page counts in v15-panels.json. Each blank follows the final page of its chapter: ch 2 (6 pp), 4 (6), 6 (12), 10 (13), 11 (24), 12 (11), 17 (16), 22 (15), 25 (15). Each is followed directly by the next chapter's p01. Sides alternate unbroken inside every chapter, so the only scripted two-page spread named in the pack (ch 14 p3 left, p4 right) still pairs. The blanks add a turn between chapters and never take one away. No chapter's last page now faces the next chapter's opening, which protects the endings (for example ch 2 ends on the storm building in the hold, and the wreck comes on the turn).

**Item 4, phone reading.** Pages whose beats depend on the whole page layout are most at risk if automatic panel detection gets them wrong: the split panel at ch 11 p9.4 (Varma's and Padmini's eyelines meeting across the gutter), the cold-storm inset at ch 16 p21.2, and the inset on Ramayyan's smile at ch 21 p10.5. Worth checking these by hand in the Kindle Previewer before Publish. That is a check, not a change to the build.

**Item 5, description.** Checked against v15-panels.json (1,709 panels, 28 chapters, 422 pages; the "422 pages across 28 chapters" claim is correct).

- Factual error. Colachel is the midpoint, not the end: the charge is ch 14, the capitulation and march ch 15. Chapters 16 to 28 cover the years after the battle: Revathi and the house of Velinadu, the letters from the Deccan and the Maratha offer to come home (ch 19, 25), the knife at the shrine and the Kayamkulam frontier (ch 22, 23), the burning fields (ch 26), Kottarakkara (ch 27), and the Thrippadidanam in 1750 (ch 28). As written, the blurb misrepresents half the book, and a buyer who finishes Colachel at 50 percent will feel misled.
- The opening line reverses the first beat. In V15 the Portuguese take his name ("The first thing they took was my name", ch 1 p1.1), and he holds on to it ("Nagoji Sawant", ch 5 p7.1; "they tried to erase my name", ch 9 p7.4). "Until his name feels like a lie" says the opposite. The fix below uses the comic's own captions.
- Everything else checks out against V15: the Vasai capture (ch 7 p10.4), the Goa cellar and its questioning (ch 1), the ship and the storm (ch 2, 3), the washing ashore (ch 4), "young King" (V15's own words, ch 10 p12.1), the old noble houses (ch 10, 27, 28) and the horse training (ch 7).
- On spoilers, the revision names Colachel as a turning point but leaves out the outcome of the march, De Lannoy, the marriage, the deaths and the 1750 ending. Naming Colachel and its historical result is a matter of public record and fair for a blurb, so "defeated" may stay if the author prefers it to "met".

> Original: "They break him in a Portuguese cellar under Goa, question by question, until his name feels like a lie."
> Suggested: "In a Portuguese cellar under Goa, the first thing they take is his name. A shrug, and an entry in a book."

> Original: "Pulled into court as a useful stranger, Nagoji is set to train horsemen for a war no one can afford to lose. It ends at Colachel in 1741, on a beach where an Indian kingdom defeated a European power."
> Suggested: "Pulled into court as a useful stranger, Nagoji is set to train horsemen for a war no one can afford to lose. At Colachel in 1741, on a beach where an Indian kingdom met a European power, that war comes. What follows is harder: old houses with long knives, letters from the north calling him home, and a choice about whose man he is."

The rest of the description stands as written. The house rules are met: no em dashes and no banned phrases. KDP's "Series" advice in the pack (a separate "Blood and Thrones Graphic Novels" series) still fits with "It is Book One of Blood and Thrones" in the last paragraph, because that line names the story's series, not the KDP series field.

### Overall
The only blocking-grade problem in my lens is the description's claim that the book ends at Colachel, and the one-line fix above resolves it. On covers, choose A or B and drop C for the spoiler.
