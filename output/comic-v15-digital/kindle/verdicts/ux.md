## User experience (Kindle buyer and reader)

1. PASS WITH NOTE: A is the right cover. At store thumbnail size (about 100 x 160 px) and in e-ink greyscale, A is the only draft that still reads as a story: two riders, swords up, the charge. It also shows a real moment (ch 14, The Siege of Colachel, p11: "I did not wait for an order. I did not signal."). B goes dark and muddy below thumbnail size and in greyscale: the riders are small figures seen from behind and the ships turn into specks. C works as a portrait but looks static, and the figure cut off at the left edge reads as a crop mistake. In all three the title block is strong and the series line can't be read at thumbnail size, which is acceptable. Note: on A and C, the "A Graphic Novel" line sits on busy art and can barely be read at thumbnail size. That matters, because the line is what tells a browser this is a comic and not the prose novel. The KDP subtitle field covers it on the store page, so this is not a blocker. If there is time, a thin dark band or a stronger shadow behind that line on A would help. The cover page standing alone at 1600 x 2560 is correct.

2. PASS: A reader should not see "reading proof" in a bought book. The rest of the copyright page reads cleanly. Kindle opens at the bodymatter landmark (ch 1 p1, "The first thing they took was my name."), which is a strong first screen. Most readers will never see the title or copyright pages unless they page back.

3. PASS WITH NOTE: Two-page view on a tablet now pairs as in print, and the ch 14 p3 and p4 spread sits left and right as it should. Readers on a phone, an e-ink Kindle or a tablet in portrait see each blank as a full cream page. All nine come at a chapter end, right before a chapter opener, so they read as a chapter break and not a fault. On e-ink the cream shows as a light grey page, which looks a little like a page that didn't load. This is acceptable and common in comics. Confirm it in the Kindle Previewer on an e-ink profile. Do not add "intentionally blank" text: it adds noise.

4. PASS WITH NOTE: Fine for launch. Without Region Magnification, Kindle's automatic Virtual Panels step through the page in fixed sections, not panel by panel. On irregular layouts a phone reader may get a zoom that cuts through a panel or reads out of order, and will fall back to pinch-zoom. Check in the Previewer phone view: one dense dialogue page (for example ch 15 p1), one splash, and the ch 14 p3 and p4 spread. Nothing on the store page should promise guided panel view. The description doesn't, so keep it that way. Phone readers also download about 310 MB, which is slow on mobile data and heavy for an older e-ink device with little storage. Low-star reviews of large comics often mention this. Nothing to change now. Shrinking the images later would also cut the delivery fee if the author ever moves to 70%.

5. REVISE: The description misleads the buyer about what the book is. It says the story "ends at Colachel in 1741", but Colachel is chapters 13 to 15 of 28. The comic runs on to the Dutch submission, Ramayyan, the Kayamkulam frontier, De Lannoy's campaigns, and chapter 28's gathering in 1750 (ch 28, Servant of Padmanabha, p1: "The monsoon of 1750, three days after Kottarakkara fell."). A reader told the book ends at the battle meets 13 more chapters, and may feel the cover and blurb sold them a different book.
   > Original: "It ends at Colachel in 1741, on a beach where an Indian kingdom defeated a European power."
   > Suggested: "The war comes to Colachel in 1741, a beach where an Indian kingdom defeated a European power. Winning it is only the beginning."

   Other notes on the pack. The description has no em dashes, no banned words and nothing moving across a face. "Captured at the edge of victory" is not shown in the ch 1 text I read, so History should confirm it. On AI disclosure, the pack makes text disclosure depend on whether AI was used. The book's own copyright page already says "Comic adaptation, dialogue revision ... were developed with generative AI tools". The pack should say plainly: declare the text as AI-generated for the adapted captions and dialogue, so the KDP form matches what the reader sees in the book. On the series, the cover says "Blood and Thrones · Book One" and the OPF puts the book in the "Blood and Thrones" collection at position 1, but the pack advises against adding it to the novel's series. A buyer who owns the novel could think it is the same book. The subtitle "A Graphic Novel" and the line "Adapted from the historical novel" handle this on the store page, but the OPF collection and the pack should agree. Use one of them.

Overall: The build serves the reader well. A is the cover. The one thing to fix before upload is the "It ends at Colachel" line in the description. Everything else is a Previewer check (e-ink blanks, phone Virtual Panels) or making the paperwork consistent (AI text disclosure, series).
