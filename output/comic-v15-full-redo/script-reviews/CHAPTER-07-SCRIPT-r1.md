## PAGE 1

Five panels. Cold open on the sand, then the Madurai horse arrives. The novel's first line carries the page.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. Dawn. A narrow strip of Malabar beach pinned between a low laterite bluff crowned with coconut palms and the Arabian Sea. Low tide: a broad plain of packed wet sand shining like beaten tin. The sun has only just cleared the palms. Far down the beach, small, a column of lancers in high-wrapped turbans rides toward us on tall, heavy horses.

> CAPTION: The first time I tried to make Madurai horses run in Malabar sand, they laughed at me.

**1.2** Tight and low, at hoof level. A heavy horse's foreleg planted at the wet margin, the hoof sunk to the pastern, grey slurry welling around it, a thin sheet of foam sliding in.

> CAPTION: Not with their mouths. Horses do not waste breath on that.

**1.3** Tight on the same horse's eye, rolling, the white showing, turned toward us. A real horse's eye, no human expression, no grin.

> CAPTION (italic, the horses' voice as he hears it): You do not belong here, Deccan man.

**1.4** Medium. The troop has arrived. At its head, Raza Khan: lean, hawk nose, pointed moustache, a puckered scar on his ANATOMICAL LEFT cheek (our right as he faces us), green turban wrapped high. He salutes with his spear butt to his chest. Behind him the Madurai riders sit straight, lances gleaming, horses tossing their heads.

> CAPTION: His Hindustani carried the singsong of the Carnatic.

> RAZA KHAN: Raza Khan, at your service. The Nayak of Madurai sends his horse as promised.

> RAZA KHAN: We have fought for Madurai and for your king before.

**1.5** Reverse angle. Nagoji, mounted on a plain grey, first full view of the commander look (CONTINUITY, chapters 7 to 16): rust-red turban with his long curly hair gathered beneath it, cream tunic, broad red sash, cream trousers, boots, talwar at his hip. Thick curled moustache, CLEAN-SHAVEN CHIN, small gold ear stud. He is not looking at us. He is weighing them.

> CAPTION: Too confident.

## PAGE 3

Four panels. Nagoji holds his ground, Raza Khan saves face, and the charge goes. Give the last panel half the page.

**3.1** Nagoji, level, the sea bright behind him.

> NAGOJI: Your men ride on hard earth. Here the ground lies to you. It looks firm, but it drinks the hoof.

> CAPTION: The first pass was a disaster.

## PAGE 4

Four panels. The sand wins. Keep the horses safe: they flounder and recover, none falls or is hurt.

**4.1** Large, two thirds of the page. The turn at the water's edge. The heavy Madurai horses sink as they try to turn: hooves sucked into the slurry, legs flailing for footing, a lance tipping sideways, riders hauling on the reins to keep their seats. The proud line has become a knot.

> CAPTION: Bred for the dry country around Madurai.

**4.2** Same sand, a few lengths away. A Maravar pony pivots lightly on its hocks, barely marking the surface, its rider grinning back over his shoulder at the floundering southerners.

> *No text.*

**4.3** Nagoji riding his grey between the two lines, one arm out, calling to both.

> NAGOJI: See? The earth here judges weight. It punishes force.

**4.4** Ponnan bringing his pony alongside Nagoji's grey as the line wheels back toward firmer ground. He speaks quietly, eyes forward, not turning his head.

> PONNAN: This earth has judged hooves longer than you have ridden, Deccan man.

> PONNAN: We learned that under other kings.

## PAGE 5

Five panels. Two rival commanders, one order, and a long morning compressed into a single wide panel.

**5.1** Two-shot, riding side by side, Nagoji and Ponnan. In the background, small, Raza Khan cursing at his mount. Ponnan's eyes have slid toward him.

> NAGOJI: Then teach your Madurai friends. Today they followed me into the bog. Tomorrow they follow you around it.

> PONNAN: We will see whose tricks the Dutch remember.

**5.2** Raza Khan on firm ground, furious, kicking wet sand out of his stirrup.

> RAZA KHAN: It is a bog. No cavalry can fight here.

> NAGOJI (off): The Dutch will be here. If you cannot fight where they stand, why did you take the king's coin?

**5.3** Raza Khan glaring at Nagoji. But his eyes drop, for a moment, to his horse's sand-caked legs. The anger is already cooling into shame. Nagoji's balloons come from off panel.

> NAGOJI (off): Again. But this time, do not fight the ground.

> NAGOJI (off): Let the horse find the bottom before you ask for the turn. Ride with the sand, not against it.

**5.4** Wide, later. Heat shimmer. The lances stand stacked upright in the sand, no banners, no formation. The riders work in a mixed line along the water's edge, one heavy Madurai horse, then one light Maravar pony, then another, making slow turns in the slurry. Men soaked in sweat.

> CAPTION: We spent the morning in that slurry.

> CAPTION: The Malabar coast did not care about their lineage.

**5.5** Water pause, midday, the air rippling. Raza Khan drinking from a gourd, sweat-soaked, his green turban loosened a little. Nagoji beside him, squatting on his heels.

> RAZA KHAN: This is not war. This is labour.

> NAGOJI: It is the only war that matters today.

## PAGE 6

Five panels. The doctrine, drawn in the sand. Two insets for the map. The tide gets the last word.

**6.1** Nagoji talking, still on his heels. Behind him, out on the flat, a long wave slides in at a slant across the sand, fast and sideways.

> NAGOJI: The Dutch carry muskets that fire in volleys. Precise. Mechanical. If you charge them straight, they will break you.

> NAGOJI: You must come at them like the tide. Fast, from the angle they do not expect, and gone before they can reload.

**6.2** Raza Khan wiping his face with the tail of his turban cloth. In the foreground, Nagoji's hand closing on a piece of driftwood.

> RAZA KHAN: To ride like the tide, one must first not drown in the sand.

> NAGOJI (off): Exactly.

**6.3** Inset, straight down from overhead. The driftwood scoring one straight line in the wet sand.

> NAGOJI (off): The Dutch line.

**6.4** Inset, same overhead angle. Two curves sweep in from either flank, bend toward the line, and hook away before they touch it.

> NAGOJI (off): Us. We do not hit them. We bait them.

> NAGOJI (off): We ride close, close enough to make them level their muskets. Then we turn.

**6.5** Full width, at ground level. The drawing in the foreground. Nagoji and Raza Khan crouched over it. A thin sheet of foam has slid in and is softening the end of the Dutch line.

> NAGOJI: If we slip then, we die.

> NAGOJI: If we turn true, they fire at ghosts, and while they reload, we take them with the sabre.

## PAGE 7

Four panels. The gamble accepted, the change earned, and the ride up the slope.

**7.1** Raza Khan still crouched, studying the half-erased map.

> RAZA KHAN: It is a gamble. To ride into the teeth of the gun and turn away.

> NAGOJI: It is better than riding into the teeth and staying there.

**7.2** Raza Khan rising, looking out at the horses. A small balloon, almost to himself. Nagoji already walking back toward his grey.

> RAZA KHAN (small): If the horses hold.

> NAGOJI: Make them hold.

**7.3** Wide, half the page. Afternoon, the sun lower, the tide higher. The mixed line wheels together at the water's edge in one clean turn, a single arc of thrown spray behind it. The heavy horses give to the sand instead of fighting it. The riders sit quiet in the saddle. Nobody is hauling on a rein.

> CAPTION: Men began to trust that their mounts would not vanish under them at every turn.

> CAPTION: Horses began to trust that their riders would not ask for impossible strides.

**7.4** On the bluff. The king has risen from his stool and stands at the awning's edge, looking down at the riders regrouping below, tiny against the shining water. Ramayyan writes a last mark on a palm leaf. Nagoji arrives on foot, leading his grey, sweat drying on his skin, sand clinging to his legs.

> VARMA: You make them move like fish. Not like bulls.

## PAGE 10

Five panels. The one quiet scene in the chapter. Let the art carry it.

**10.1** Nagoji walking toward her down the line, past small rough-coated Maravar ponies with watchful eyes and taller, elegant Madurai horses shifting uneasily in the damp.

> CAPTION: No war horse is native to this coast. Every one comes by ship or over the passes, and costs more than a soldier's yearly wage.

> CAPTION: The Maravar ponies were not beautiful. But they knew the monsoon in their bones.

**10.2** Nagoji holding out his hand, palm up and low. The mare stretches her neck toward it, nostrils flared, taking his scent.

> NAGOJI: You do not belong to any of them either. Do you?

**10.3** She pushes her nose into his chest. His hand has come to rest on her neck. His eyes are closed.

> CAPTION: It was not Kanka's smell. It never would be.

> CAPTION: But it was honest.

**10.4** Close two-shot, his forehead near her face, the last light on her red-brown coat.

> NAGOJI: Kayal.

> CAPTION: The word came unbidden. The Malayalam name for the backwaters.

> NAGOJI: You will learn the sand with me. And when the Dutch come, we will show them what a backwater horse can do.

**10.5** She tosses her head, as if agreeing to terms she has not yet heard. Nagoji almost smiles.

> CAPTION: It was the first time since Vasai that I had named a horse.

> CAPTION: Kayal would carry me through Colachel and beyond.

## PAGE 11

Five panels. Ibrahim, the gods of the coast, and the chapter's last line.

**11.1** Evening shadow. Ibrahim Marakkar leaning against a palm trunk nearby, face half in shadow: shorter than the fishermen but broader, a scar running from his LEFT ear to his jaw, short coat over a vest with bulging pockets. Nagoji's hand still on Kayal's neck.

> IBRAHIM: You talk to horses. I have seen men talk to worse.

> NAGOJI: Horses do not argue. They simply refuse, or they follow. It is simpler than men.

**11.2** Ibrahim, arms folded, studying him.

> IBRAHIM: You are Hindu.

> NAGOJI: After a fashion. The Portuguese tried to convince me otherwise. It did not take.

**11.3** Ibrahim looking past Nagoji and Kayal toward the dark water, where the sea and the mouth of a backwater channel meet.

> IBRAHIM: I am Muslim. Yet my fathers lit lamps at Hindu shrines when our ships sailed.

> IBRAHIM: On this coast, the gods mix like the waters of the backwaters, salt and fresh. A man who insists on purity drowns faster than one who learns to float in both.

**11.4** Ibrahim pushing off the palm and walking away into the gathering dark, speaking over his shoulder.

> IBRAHIM: You are useful, Sawant. That is why you are still alive.

> IBRAHIM: The king does not keep men for their faith. He keeps them for what they can do.

**11.5** Full width, bottom. Night. Nagoji alone with Kayal at the edge of the wet sand, the sea a pale line behind them. At their feet, the day's hoofprints are filling with water.

> CAPTION: Storms did not ask permission.

> CAPTION (separate, weighted): Perhaps horses, taught well, did not either.

## Adaptation notes

- **Horse economics compressed to two captions (10.1).** The novel's paragraphs on pagoda prices, Arab horses failing in the damp, mixed-cavalry doctrine and Ramayyan's stable accounts are exposition. The comic keeps the one fact that matters for Kayal, that every war horse here is a costly import and the local ponies suit the monsoon. The mixed-cavalry idea is shown instead of told, in the mixed line of 5.4 and 7.3.
- **The "Watch him" beat cut.** Nagoji pointing a Madurai rider to a calm Maravar youth repeats what 5.4 already shows, so it gave its panel to the water pause.
- **Ibrahim's scene trimmed to one page.** Cut: his note that Nagoji bows in churches and touches mosque walls, Nagoji's "I pray to whatever is listening" reply, and the "And the king?" exchange. Kept: the Portuguese line (continuity with Goa), the backwaters image (which ties to Kayal's name), and "keeps them for what they can do". "You are useful too" became "You are useful", because the line it answered was cut.
- **Line trims.** "Ponnan to his riders" (2.1) is added so the reader can link Ponnam Pandya Deven to the PONNAN balloons. "The King's coin" is lower-cased to match the rest of the script. The two map exchanges and the fear/anticipation passage are otherwise verbatim.
- **Shown, not captioned.** Raza Khan's shame after the stumble (5.3), the king rising and Ramayyan's final marks (7.4), and the mare's head toss (10.5) are in the art only.
- **Deliberate echoes.** The horses' imagined "Deccan man" (1.3) is picked up by Ponnan (4.4). Kanka bleeds into 9.4 with the same device as Chapter 1, 6.5. The rolling eye of 1.3 is answered by Kayal's steady look in 9.5. The last caption echoes Chapter 1's closing line.
- **Continuity flags for the reviewer.** (1) Varma is drawn bare-headed with no peacock crest, as the novel has him on the beach. CONTINUITY.md lists the crest for court dress only. (2) Nagoji's daytime mount is a plain grey, which the novel does not specify, so that Kayal's first appearance cannot be confused with it. (3) Kayal is not yet in CONTINUITY.md. Recommend adding her as a bay mare with an ochre red-brown coat, sized between a Madurai charger and a Maravar pony. The novel gives her no white markings. (4) Kanka's death (a ball at Vasai) agrees with João's "we shot it" in Chapter 1.
