## PAGE 1

Five panels, one of them an inset. Months of sand and small wars squeezed into one page. The page is there to land Ramayyan's one line.

**1.1** Wide, full width. Wet sand at low tide, hard morning glare. Nagoji in his commander kit (rust-red turban, cream tunic, broad red sash, cream trousers, boots, talwar at his hip) stands in his stirrups, one arm flung out, mouth open in a shout. Ahead of him a ragged double line of riders: tall Madurai chargers beside small shaggy Maravar ponies, the line bowing where the ponies cannot keep pace. Beyond them, fishing boats drawn up in the shallows, fishermen watching from the gunwales, one doubled over laughing.

> CAPTION: Months of sun and salt.

> CAPTION: I became, in a small way, part of the landscape.

**1.2** Small inset, lower corner of 1.1. Evening in a thatched hut. A banana-leaf plate of red fish curry, a coconut-shell cup of toddy, and above them Nagoji's face, eyes streaming. A fisherman grins beside him.

> CAPTION: Spicy enough to make a Deccan man weep for milk.

**1.3** Wide. Inland now. A narrow earthen bund between flooded paddy fields, green and mirror-bright. The column comes along it in single file at full gallop, Nagoji at the head, talwar drawn. At the far end of the bund a knot of armed chavers turns toward the sound, an official in white on his knees behind them. Shoot from low at the waterline so the horses loom. No contact yet.

> CAPTION: But the king did not keep me only on the beach.

> CAPTION: Small wars that never reached European maps, but decided who paid tax and who held a temple key.

**1.4** Dusk. The column returning along a village road, fewer than before. Arms bound in cloth, a riderless horse led by its reins. The Nair riders behind Nagoji are watching his back, not the road. He does not turn around.

> CAPTION: We came back with a different kind of silence.

> CAPTION: The kind that forms when soldiers have watched you ride into danger instead of pointing at it from behind.

**1.5** Bottom strip. A palace verandah, the column passing below it. Ramayyan (thin, sharp features, receding hair, plain white cloth) sits at the verandah's edge with a palm-leaf bundle and stylus. He does not raise his eyes as the horses go by.

> RAMAYYAN: The Maharaja has seen you in blood, not only in sand.

## PAGE 2

Five panels. The summons. Ramayyan weighs Nagoji's birth before he will lodge him anywhere. Give the caste question room, because the whole chapter walks through that door.

**2.1** Wide establishing. A small open pavilion of dark oiled wood beside a temple wall, with the temple's copper-clad flagmast and tiered sloping roofs rising behind it. Nagoji steps up into the pavilion, a folded palm-leaf ola in his hand. Ramayyan sits cross-legged at a low desk, bundles of palm leaves stacked at his knee, stylus moving. He does not look up.

> CAPTION: Then the king's ola came. Not to the war hall.

> RAMAYYAN: The King is pleased with the sand. He thinks you might be useful indoors as well.

**2.2** Two-shot across the desk. Nagoji standing. Ramayyan still writing.

> NAGOJI: I prefer the sky.

> RAMAYYAN: Preferences are luxuries. There is an estate that can house you and your officers. Padmini Amma's lands.

> CAPTION: The woman who owned pepper hills.

**2.3** Close on Ramayyan as he finally looks up, the stylus paused above the leaf.

> CAPTION: His gaze was like a clerk's scale, weighing gold against brass.

> RAMAYYAN: She is Nair. Her house follows the old ways. You are Maratha? Which line?

**2.4** Nagoji, square to him, chin up. Shot slightly from below.

> NAGOJI: Sawant. Kshatriya. My father's house has held the sword for five generations.

> NAGOJI: We do not plough.

> CAPTION: In this land, as in mine, blood and birth could close a door faster than any bolt.

**2.5** Ramayyan already back at his leaf. Tight on the stylus scratching a line into the palm leaf, his face beyond it, finished with the matter.

> RAMAYYAN: Good. That will satisfy the temple priests who cook at her house.

> RAMAYYAN: Padmini Amma might tolerate a foreign captain, but she will not tolerate pollution.

## PAGE 3

Four panels. The house. Give the reveal the top half of the page, then end on the plant.

**3.1** Large, top half of the page. Seen from the approach road under the palms: Padmini Amma's house, sprawling low and wide, its tiled roofs sloping gently in several tiers, carved beams dark with oil and age, a long compound wall. Two great tuskers sway in the shade of that wall, grey as boulders. A long stable runs off to one side. Nagoji and Ibrahim are small figures on horseback in the foreground.

> CAPTION: The first time I saw Padmini Amma's house, I thought they had brought me to a temple by mistake.

> CAPTION: Not one of the great stone temples of the Deccan. Yet everything about it said power.

**3.2** Riding in under a gate arch woven from coconut fronds and mango leaves. Oil lamps hang in it, lit although the sun is high, thin smoke curling from the wicks. Ibrahim (shorter than the fishermen but broader, short coat over a vest with bulging pockets, a scar from his left ear to his jaw) leans toward Nagoji as they pass under.

> IBRAHIM: Do not look for the sardar. Here the house does not belong to the man who sleeps in the front room.

> IBRAHIM: It belongs to the name on the mother's line. You will see.

**3.3** Inside the outer yard. A thick-walled granary, its door open on mounds of paddy stacked high. Beyond it, men with spears stand not at the gate but in the cool shade of an inner verandah, easy and watchful.

> CAPTION: A house built to defend its wealth, not just display it.

**3.4** Inset, tight, in the shadow of the granary wall. A low wooden panel set into the wall, plain, its edges rubbed smooth and darker than the wood around them, as if hands have found it in the dark many times. No figure in frame.

> CAPTION: It looked like nothing.

> CAPTION: That was what made it feel important.

## PAGE 4

Five panels. The courtyard turns to look at him, and then she does.

**4.1** Wide. The long rectangular courtyard edged with pillared verandahs. On the left, women on woven mats sorting pepper berries, bangles at their wrists. On the right, by a well, boys with short spears and an older man in mid-correction. Every head has turned toward the gate, where Nagoji and Ibrahim are dismounting.

> CAPTION: A stranger. A Maratha. A man who had washed up from Portuguese wreckage, and now arrived on a horse instead of a stretcher.

**4.2** Nagoji's point of view down the length of the courtyard. At the far end, alone, a woman stands: in her fifties, thick black hair coiled at the nape, simple gold at her ears and throat, a cotton sari hitched above the ankles for walking, one hand resting on a long stick.

> IBRAHIM (small, under his breath): Padmini Amma.

> CAPTION: A stick that could have been a staff or a cane, depending on who held it.

**4.3** Mid-shot. Nagoji inclines his head in a measured bow, not a deep one. Ibrahim beside him, open-handed, presenting him.

> CAPTION: The bow I would have given a landholding Patil at home. Not a court bow. More than a nod to an equal.

> IBRAHIM: Amma. This is the rider I spoke of. The one the sea and the Portuguese fought over.

**4.4** Insert: where her eyes go. Nagoji's hand at his belt, the nails healed but ridged, the edge of the Portuguese brand showing at the left wrist below the sleeve, the belt leather worn pale.

> *No text.*

**4.5** Padmini, close, unhurried. Letterer's note: she speaks slow Malayalam, pitched so he can follow; letter it in plain English with no brackets.

> PADMINI: The one who survived. The sea spits out strange things.

> PADMINI: You understand me, Nagoji?

## PAGE 5

Five panels. He passes the first test by naming it. Then she starts teaching.

**5.1** Nagoji, level, answering in halting Malayalam. Compose this as a mirror of panel 3.3 in Chapter 1: the same tight framing, the same flat steadiness, but a free man's face this time.

> NAGOJI: Enough to know when I am being insulted, Amma.

> NAGOJI: And enough to know when I am being measured.

**5.2** Padmini. One corner of her mouth curves. Nothing else moves.

> PADMINI: Good. Men who cannot tell the difference do not live long here.

**5.3** A boy of about twelve trots up to take Nagoji's gelding by the reins, easy and unafraid. The horse snorts once, then goes with him. Padmini's stick gestures at the edge of frame.

> PADMINI (off): Come. Leave the horses to the boys.

**5.4** Walking along the shaded verandah, Padmini half a step ahead, her stick tapping the stone. Behind them in the courtyard, Ibrahim is already deep in talk with one of her stewards.

> PADMINI: The king sends you here to be near his capital and his horse. Clever man.

> NAGOJI: I am a soldier. I count men, hooves, distances.

**5.5** Padmini, not breaking stride, not looking at him.

> PADMINI: And who owns the land those hooves cross?

> PADMINI: Who feeds the men you count? Whose pepper pays for their powder?

## PAGE 6

Five panels. The house explains itself through its rooms.

**6.1** An open doorway into a cool room. Two young women bent over palm-leaf bundles, counting and tying; one glances up. Nagoji is smiling at them. In the foreground, Padmini's eyes slide sideways to him. She has noticed.

> CAPTION: I smiled at the women a bit longer than I should have.

**6.2** Another doorway: a dim, quiet room holding a wooden chest, a lamp niche, and a sleeping mat rolled against the wall. Padmini's stick points into it.

> PADMINI: My grandmother slept in that room. Her brother ran away with a dancer. Her sons went to fight for different chiefs.

**6.3** Padmini turning back to him, the room behind her.

> PADMINI: This house stayed with her sisters and their daughters.

> PADMINI: The men come and go. The name stays here.

**6.4** Nagoji, reasonable, sure of himself.

> NAGOJI: In the Deccan we inherit through the father's line. A man's field is his son's.

**6.5** Two beats in one panel. Low in frame, the tip of her stick strikes the floor. Above it, her face, turned full on him for the first time.

> PADMINI: If your son's wife hates you, does that field still feel like safety?

## PAGE 7

Five panels and an inset. The jackfruit tree. Women who do not vanish, and a question no Deccan woman would ask.

**7.1** Wide. A smaller inner courtyard shaded by a huge jackfruit tree, with a stone platform beneath it worn smooth by generations. Over a low wall behind, the heads and raised sticks of youths training in the kalari, a girl among them. Nagoji's head is turned toward the sound.

> PADMINI: Kalari. Our boys and some of our girls.

> PADMINI: When the king calls, they are ready. When the king forgets us, they are also ready.

> CAPTION: No threat in it. Only a fact, laid on the ground like a tool.

**7.2** Seated on the platform. A young woman sets down brass tumblers of water; another brings a plate of sliced fruit and fried snacks. Both meet Nagoji's eyes, unhurried, and neither leaves at once.

> CAPTION: In my village, women left food at the edge of the men's courtyard and vanished.

> CAPTION: Here they stayed to listen.

**7.3** Padmini, the young women gone now, speaking lower.

> PADMINI: The king has not yet decided whether you are a spear for his hand or a knife that might cut it.

> NAGOJI: I am still an experiment. He told me so himself.

**7.4** Padmini biting into a slice of jackfruit, the juice shining on her fingers, a cloth ready in her other hand.

> PADMINI: He is honest, then. Good. It makes it easier to decide how much to trust you.

**7.5** Nagoji. The question has found him somewhere unguarded.

> PADMINI (off): You have a family. Back in your hills.

> NAGOJI: Parents. A younger brother. Some fields that do not know whether I am alive.

**7.6** Small inset, bottom right. Padmini wiping her fingers on the cloth, eyes on him, casual.

> PADMINI: And a wife?

## PAGE 8

Five panels. He answers like a Deccan soldier. She answers like a landowner.

**8.1** Nagoji, caught off guard.

> NAGOJI: No.

> PADMINI: Why not?

> CAPTION: In the Deccan, such a question from a woman I had just met would have been unthinkable.

**8.2** Nagoji looking out past the tree, somewhere north.

> NAGOJI: I rode where the Peshwa's orders sent me.

> NAGOJI: I did not think it fair to ask a woman to wait for a man who might ride into Portuguese guns one morning and never ride back.

**8.3** Padmini, dry as dust.

> PADMINI: You assume she would wait.

> PADMINI: Here, if a man does not return, the house continues. Perhaps your hills should learn from that.

**8.4** Nagoji laughs, one short surprised laugh, his head tipped back.

> NAGOJI: You speak freely, Amma.

> PADMINI: I own this land. Why should I not?

**8.5** Padmini studying him. A decision made behind her eyes.

> PADMINI: You will hear a word here. *Sambandham.*

> PADMINI: An equal bond. Not ownership. Not conquest.

## PAGE 9

Five panels. The law of the house, cut to the bone. Keep her moving, gesturing and pointing, so this plays as argument, not lecture.

**9.1** Padmini's stick draws a line on the stone between them: her side, his side.

> PADMINI: A woman stays in her own house. Her *tharavadu.* The man comes to her.

> PADMINI: He does not own the house. He does not own the land. He does not own her.

**9.2** Over the low wall again: the kalari youths, sweat-shining, sticks crossed. Padmini's stick points toward them. Nagoji in the foreground.

> NAGOJI: And the children?

> PADMINI: Belong to her line. The father is honoured. Loved, often.

> PADMINI: But he is a guest in the house where his children grow.

**9.3** Nagoji, searching for a word in a language he only half has.

> NAGOJI: It sounds... complicated.

> PADMINI: It sounds practical. A man who goes to war may not return.

> PADMINI: In your system, his wife and children are ruined. In ours, they are exactly where they were before.

**9.4** Close on Padmini, smiling, showing teeth.

> PADMINI: We do not pretend men are reliable, Sawant. We plan for their failures.

**9.5** Two-shot. Nagoji pushing back. Padmini's smile is gone and her face is flat.

> NAGOJI: And if a woman wishes to leave? To follow her husband to his house?

> PADMINI: Then she is a fool.

## PAGE 10

Five panels. The terms of his billet, the warning, and a name.

**10.1** Padmini sitting back against the trunk of the jackfruit tree, taking him in.

> PADMINI: Your horses will graze in our fields. You will ride from this gate when the king calls.

> PADMINI: That makes you part of this house's story, whether you like it or not.

**10.2** Close. She has leaned forward, her voice gone flat and hard, and the camera comes in with her. Nagoji's face at the edge of frame.

> PADMINI: And tell your men to stay away from the girls in the house.

> PADMINI: If they try anything, we will carve them from navel to feet. Do not test us on this.

**10.3** Nagoji, not smiling now.

> NAGOJI: Houses have long memories.

> PADMINI: And longer tongues.

**10.4** Padmini easing back, almost amused again.

> PADMINI: You will meet one of those tongues soon. A princess of our Velinadu kin. Revathi Bayi.

> PADMINI: She has more opinions than I have pepper vines.

> CAPTION: I had heard the name on the road south. A royal woman who refused to act grateful when Europeans bowed.

**10.5** Two-shot under the tree.

> NAGOJI: She supports the king?

> PADMINI: She supports Travancore.

> PADMINI: Not always the same thing as supporting its current occupant.

## PAGE 11

Five panels. Many crowns, two days, and a man left alone with the sound of the house.

**11.1** Padmini in the foreground. Behind her, bleeding into the panel edge like a stain rather than a clean flashback frame (the Chapter 1 technique): Marthanda Varma on his low platform in the coastal hall, bare-chested in cream and gold, spear at his shoulder, maps at his feet.

> NAGOJI (off): He allows her to speak against him?

> PADMINI: He allows what he cannot easily stop. You outsiders see one crown.

> PADMINI: We see many, layered on the same land. Sometimes they sit well together. Sometimes they cut.

**11.2** Padmini rising from the platform, using the stick more as a symbol than as a support.

> NAGOJI: When do we go to Velinadu?

> PADMINI: In two days. You will come as my guest, not as the king's experiment.

> PADMINI: That will confuse people. Confusion is useful.

**11.3** Wide. Padmini walking away across the main courtyard. The women part for her without being told and close again behind her; men with account books step aside, then go back to their counting. At the edge of frame, a young girl carrying firewood has paused mid-step to look back at Nagoji, eyes bright.

> PADMINI (over her shoulder): Wash the salt from your skin. Then we will see whether you can speak to a princess without putting your Deccan boot in your mouth.

**11.4** Wide, high angle. Nagoji alone on the stone platform under the jackfruit tree, small in the frame, eyes closed. Listening.

> CAPTION: In Goa, power carried a crucifix and a gun.

> CAPTION: Here it sounded like pepper being sifted, like a woman's stick tapping stone.

> CAPTION: A house that remembered. A house whose women spoke.

**11.5** Bottom strip. Nagoji's face, close, eyes open again, a rueful half smile.

> CAPTION: Two days to learn how not to offend a princess.

> CAPTION: I had faced Portuguese interrogations and Arabian Sea storms. Somehow, this felt like the more delicate task.

## Adaptation notes

- **Beach and inland wars compressed to one page.** The novel's months on the sand and its three inland skirmishes (the pepper hill, the noble house, the chavers on the bund) become one drill panel, one curry inset and one charge on a bund. The page exists to set up Ramayyan's "blood, not only in sand" line. The charge stops before contact and shows the aftermath by absence (the riderless horse), with no gore.
- **Ramayyan trimmed.** Cut the "they are... particular" line, the stables line, and the pepper, salary, Muscat, Aden and *varahan* passage. Padmini's "Whose pepper pays for their powder?" (5.5) makes the economic point in her own voice, and the pollution line is a sharper page turn than the salary line.
- **Hands follow CONTINUITY.md, not the novel.** The novel still has bandaged fingers and linen-wrapped hands. The bible puts Nagoji in the commander look for chapters 7 to 16, with nails healed but ridged, so panel 4.4 draws ridged nails and the wrist brand instead. Flag this to the author if the linen should stay.
- **"matrilineal line" became "the mother's line"** in Ibrahim's balloon (3.2). "Matrilineal" is a modern anthropological word and would be anachronistic in 1740s speech.
- **Sambandham cut to its bones.** Cut the Sanskrit etymology (*sama* and *bandham*), the passage about fathers who ride for other lords, the "into the streets" follow-up to "Then she is a fool", the claim that the law, temples and kings uphold it, and the comparison with the Brahmins, Namboothiris and Syrian Christians. Kept her three strongest lines verbatim: "If your son's wife hates you...", "We do not pretend men are reliable...", and "Then she is a fool."
- **Small texture cuts.** Cut the old man tracing a cloth map (6.1 already carries the room-by-room walk), the cart, "Tomorrow we walk the fields" (to keep the last balloon short), and Pune from the closing comparison of powers. Goa stays because it calls back to Chapter 1.
- **Plants and rhymes.** The wooden panel by the granary (3.4) gets its own silent inset and stays unexplained, as it is in the novel. Padmini catching his smile in 6.1 sets up the warning in 10.2, and the girl with firewood in 11.3 keeps that thread alive. Panel 5.1 deliberately rhymes with Chapter 1, panel 3.3, because his answer echoes his line to Duarte.
- **Revathi is named but not shown.** Her face is saved for her own entrance in the next chapter. Varma appears only as a memory bleed (11.1).
- **Verbatim lines kept** include "His gaze was like a clerk's scale...", "We do not plough", "The sea spits out strange things", "Enough to know when I am being measured", "The men come and go. The name stays here", "a spear for his hand or a knife that might cut it", "I own this land. Why should I not?", "carve them from navel to feet", "Houses have long memories / And longer tongues", "She supports Travancore", "Confusion is useful", and the chapter's closing line.
- **Print.** Every frame placed in this chapter must reach 300 effective PPI or more at its printed size, as CONTINUITY.md requires. Upscale 2x with the project upscaler, or regenerate, before placing anything below that.
