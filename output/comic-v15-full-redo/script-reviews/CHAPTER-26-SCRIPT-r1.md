## Chapter 26: Fire in the Pepper Fields

## PAGE 1

Five panels. A smudge on the horizon becomes a war, and the war lands on Padmini's land.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. Midday in the dry season, seen from the fort wall: parched scrub, low hills, a dark green belt of pepper country and palms to the north. On the far horizon, a thin, uncertain line of grey smoke. Nagoji at the parapet in three-quarter back view, one hand on the warm stone. He is Ananthan Pillai now: Kerala topknot, NO turban, grey at the temples, thick curled moustache, clean-shaven chin, small gold ear stud; white European-cut coat, red waistcoat, brass buttons, silver chain of office. A Nair sentry beside him, unconcerned, leaning on his spear.

> CAPTION: At first it looked like nothing more than the usual dry season burn.

> CAPTION: A farmer clearing undergrowth. A careless coal from a cook fire.

**1.2** Narrow strip, same horizon, later. The line has thickened and broken, and now smoke rises in two separate places, far apart. Nagoji has straightened. The sentry has stopped leaning.

> *No text.*

**1.3** The war hall inside the fort. A long, low room with carved wooden pillars and a map table heaped with palm-leaf bundles. Padmini Amma (in her fifties, thick black hair coiled at the nape and threaded with grey, simple gold, cotton sari hitched for walking, long walking stick) mid-argument across the table with Ramayyan Dalawa (thin, sharp fine features, receding hair, plain white cloth, stylus in hand). Marthanda Varma (V13 face; cream and gold, peacock crest, gold necklaces, bare-chested, a spear leaning beside him) on a low platform behind them. Nagoji just entering from a side door. In the main doorway a runner bursts in, bare chest slick with sweat, a guard at his heels.

> CAPTION: Padmini Amma had come to the fort that morning to argue over grain rates and levy schedules.

> RUNNER: Raiders! From the west and the north.

**1.4** The runner down on one knee, panting, one hand on the floor. In the foreground, out of focus, the foot of Padmini's walking stick, stopped mid-tap an inch above the floor.

> RUNNER: Some with Dutch guns. Some with old house banners.

> RUNNER: They strike near Padmini Amma's upper fields.

**1.5** Full width, bottom. Close on Padmini, turning slowly toward the runner. The men around her are soft and blurred. She is very calm, and that is worse than shouting.

> PADMINI: They chose my fields. Of course they did.

> PADMINI: Old men fight their wars in land they have never walked; young ones set torches where women count vines.

## PAGE 2

Five panels. The council. The raid logistics become one caption and one imagined map; the page ends on the word nobody in the room wants.

**2.1** Ramayyan, fingers pinching the bridge of his nose, palm leaves spread before him, stylus laid down.

> RAMAYYAN: The timing is not an accident.

> RAMAYYAN: Someone has been counting our drills as carefully as we count their gossip.

> CAPTION: The northern column was Kollamkara's, bold with whispers of Dutch backing.

**2.2** Wide. The strip as Nagoji pictures it, an aerial view painted like a map coming alive: dense green walls of pepper vines climbing rough bark support posts on both sides of a narrow path; a canal running alongside with low boats; small shrines of stone and carved wood almost swallowed by foliage on the ridges. Two faint smoke trails converge on it, one from the coast, one from the northern hills.

> CAPTION: I saw the problem as soon as I pictured the map.

> VARMA (off): We cannot let them hold that strip. If they do, they have a knife at our throat.

**2.3** Back in the hall. Nagoji at the table, finger on the map where the path narrows. Ramayyan opposite him.

> NAGOJI: Our horse cannot move at speed through those vines. The path is too narrow. The canal banks too soft.

> RAMAYYAN: Foot.

**2.4** Nagoji shaking his head, eyes still on the map. Varma behind, leaning forward on his platform.

> NAGOJI: Foot will bleed in every bush. Every tree hides a musket. We will lose men in inches.

> VARMA: You have an alternative?

**2.5** Full width, bottom. Tight on Nagoji, lit from the side. Behind him, through a window slit, the two smudges of smoke on the horizon. He does not look like a man who has had a good idea.

> CAPTION: The answer came before I wanted it to.

> NAGOJI: Burn it.

## PAGE 3

Four panels. Padmini's objection. Give her the page, and end on her verdict.

**3.1** Wide. The whole hall gone still. Padmini's head has snapped toward Nagoji. Ramayyan's stylus has stopped above the leaf. Varma does not move. The runner, still kneeling, looks from face to face.

> CAPTION: The word hung in the air like smoke.

> PADMINI (small, soft lettering): Burn it?

**3.2** Nagoji leaning over the map table, tracing a thin zig zag along the canal with one finger. Quick, as if speed could make it smaller.

> NAGOJI: Not all. A strip. Narrow. Enough to deprive them of cover along the canal.

> NAGOJI: Then they must choose. Retreat into the open where our horse can run, or stay and be flanked in smaller patches.

**3.3** Two-shot across the table: Padmini standing with her stick, Nagoji straightening to face her.

> PADMINI: You speak of my vines as if they were scrub.

> NAGOJI: Today, to them, they are a shield. Tomorrow, if they live, they are coin.

> NAGOJI: I know what I am asking.

**3.4** Full width, bottom. Tight on Padmini's hand on the head of her stick, knuckles tightening. Her face above it, eyes fixed on him.

> PADMINI: You know.

> PADMINI: You do not feel.

## PAGE 4

Five panels. The cost is argued in the open, and consent is given as an accusation.

**4.1** Varma on his platform, looking between Nagoji and Padmini. His voice is quiet; the room leans in to hear it.

> VARMA: If we do nothing, they will move along that corridor until they reach places where our walls are thin and our men few.

> VARMA: They will write their own terms in our fields.

**4.2** Padmini turning to face the king.

> PADMINI: If we burn, you carve a wound through my land that will take years to heal.

> PADMINI: Pepper is not rice. It does not spring back in one season. You cut into my children and their children.

**4.3** Ramayyan, his stylus lying still on the leaf, looking up at her gently. Small inset in the lower corner: Padmini's eyes, closed.

> RAMAYYAN: Amma, if we let them keep that corridor, they carve different wounds.

> RAMAYYAN: Through forts. Through houses. Through your own name, when they brag of having used your land to break the king.

**4.4** Full width. Padmini, eyes open now, wet and furious, standing straight with both hands on her stick, facing all three men at once.

> PADMINI: Do it.

> PADMINI: But do not pretend you did it to me without my consent.

> PADMINI: Let every man who walks that blackened line in years to come know that I allowed it, and that I remember every step you take on it.

**4.5** Nagoji, a single nod. His throat is tight and his jaw is set.

> NAGOJI: I will ride there myself.

> NAGOJI: I will not ask another to light the first torch.

## PAGE 5

Five panels. The ride, the ridge, the torch. Hold the ignition back for the page turn.

**5.1** Wide. A column of riders on a dry red road, Nagoji at the head. Beside him Ponnan (broad-shouldered Maravar, curly hair, thick moustache, clean chin, red shoulder cloth, white mundu hitched, short jacket). Ahead, dark greasy columns of smoke; sparks drifting in the air around the horses.

> CAPTION: The ride to her upper fields was short and long at once.

> CAPTION: Short in distance. Long in what it stretched inside me.

**5.2** Large, most of a tier. From the ridge, looking down: pepper vines in dense ordered lines along both sides of the canal, green fruit clusters hanging like beads; the narrow path running between them like a vein. Raiders half hidden in the foliage, musket barrels glinting between leaves, small puffs of musket smoke from the shadows. Low, loaded boats on the canal. The riders reined in along the ridge in the foreground, in silhouette, beside a small shrine almost swallowed by leaves.

> CAPTION: The sight below made my stomach knot.

**5.3** Ponnan leaning in from his saddle at Nagoji's shoulder. Nagoji already swinging down.

> PONNAN: If we hit them from here, we expose ourselves. They have too many places to hide.

> NAGOJI: We do not hit them here.

> NAGOJI: We hit their shield.

**5.4** Nagoji on foot at the first row of vines, crisp leaf litter under his boots, heat shimmer rising from fires already licking at the far edges. A soldier hands him a torch, its head wrapped in oil-soaked cloth. Bleeding into the smoke above him like a stain rather than a clean flashback frame: Padmini's face and stick on one side; Revathi's face on the other, jasmine in her braid.

> CAPTION: You will always choose the king first, Revathi had said. The houses later.

**5.5** Full width, bottom. Tight: the torch held to the dry leaves and bark at the base of a support post. The flame touches. Nothing has caught yet. Nagoji's face behind the torch, lit orange, waiting.

> CAPTION: For a heartbeat, nothing happened.

*Page turn.*

## PAGE 6

Five panels. The burn and the fight. Staging and silhouette only; no bodies in close view, no wounds.

**6.1** Large, upper half of the page. The flame has caught. Fire crawls up the post and licks at the lower leaves; sap spits white steam from the stem. Nagoji steps back from it, torch still raised, soot already on his white coat.

> *No text.*

**6.2** High angle. A zig zag of separate fires along the line, with deliberate gaps between them. Nagoji moving along it, men spreading out on either side of him, setting their torches to post after post.

> CAPTION: A controlled burn, as much as such a thing can be in war.

> CAPTION: We left gaps, where new growth might find purchase in years to come.

**6.3** The fire jumping where no one meant it to: leaping a careful gap to catch a fallen branch, black smoke boiling up. Two Travancore men flinching back from the sudden heat.

> CAPTION: The fire responded with its own logic.

**6.4** Travancore musketeers (Nair men in white mundu with cross-belts and matchlocks; NOT blue coats, NOT red coats) kneeling in a line at the edge of the burn, firing into the smoke. In the gaps they created, raiders show as black silhouettes against the flame.

> CAPTION: This was a cruel way to fight.

**6.5** Full width, bottom. Raiders stumbling out of the smoke, coughing, arms over their faces, toward a waiting line of Travancore blades; beyond them, others throwing themselves into the canal as embers fall hissing on the water. In the foreground the wind has shifted, and Nagoji, face streaked black, hauls one of his own men back out of the smoke by his shoulder cloth. Behind them, figures lost in grey.

> CAPTION: I do not pretend there was honour in that moment.

> CAPTION: We pulled who we could back. We lost others in the smoke.

## PAGE 7

Five panels. Aftermath, and then Padmini walks into the hall having walked the line.

**7.1** Wide, late afternoon. The corridor is gone: a blackened stretch of ground through the green, charred posts fallen, earth scorched. On both sides, untouched pepper still stands, leaves fluttering in the hot wind. In the foreground ash, a dropped musket and a scorched house banner. Far off, a few survivors limping into neighbouring land.

> CAPTION: The fire had done its work.

> CAPTION: We did not pursue far.

**7.2** Night, the fort hall. Lamps lit. Soldiers sitting sooty and quiet along the walls. Nagoji, coat stained black at the cuffs, drinking from a brass cup and grimacing at the taste.

> CAPTION: That night the smell of smoke clung to everything. Even the water tasted of it.

**7.3** Padmini in the doorway, without ceremony: soot streaks on her sari, hair plastered to her temples with sweat, her stick in her hand. Every head in the hall turning toward her. Every conversation stopped.

> *No text.*

**7.4** Padmini standing in the centre of the room, looking straight at Nagoji. Her voice is flat.

> PADMINI: You did what you said you would. You burned a strip. Not all. Enough.

> PADMINI: I rode there. I walked the black line. It runs like a mark across my land.

**7.5** Nagoji, facing her. He does not look away.

> NAGOJI: When the raiders burned your lower fields years ago, you called it cowardice.

> NAGOJI: Today I did it with a torch in my own hand. I do not know which ash is heavier.

## PAGE 8

Four panels. Padmini's reckoning, and her exit. The king's promise is given and weighed.

**8.1** Padmini, eyes hard and unblinking.

> PADMINI: They burned to destroy. You burned to save.

> PADMINI: But ash tastes the same in my mouth, Ananthan. Do not expect me to distinguish the flavour.

**8.2** Closer on her. Her voice drops; this is harder for her than the anger.

> PADMINI: I do not say you were wrong. But do not expect me to thank you.

> PADMINI: There are wounds that are necessary and wounds that are kind. This was the first, not the second.

**8.3** Varma on his platform, face unreadable. Padmini half-turned toward him, leaning on her stick.

> VARMA: You have my word that the state will help replant, and support your house through the years it takes.

> PADMINI: Your word does not make plants grow faster.

> PADMINI: But perhaps it will keep some of my boys from selling their swords to men like Kollamkara when the hunger comes.

**8.4** Full width, bottom. Padmini at the doorway with her back to the hall, paused, not looking round. Nagoji small in the lamplight behind her.

> PADMINI: You have chosen your side firmly now, Pillai.

> PADMINI: When Dutch, Mysore or your own hills come in force, you will be in front of the king's fort. You will not be standing on my verandah.

> PADMINI: I will remember that.

## PAGE 9

Five panels. Revathi on the wall. Her anger is private, which makes it sharper.

**9.1** Wide. The fort wall at evening, a few days later. Far off, the blackened strip still visible as a dark line across the green. Nagoji alone at the parapet. Revathi Bayi (a little younger than Nagoji; deep indigo sari with gold, jasmine in her braid, grey threading her hair, tali at her throat, flat-coin necklace, gold armlets, sandalwood line at the hairline) coming along the wall walk toward him.

> CAPTION: Revathi's anger arrived a few evenings later, as I had known it would.

> REVATHI: You carved your own mark into this land.

**9.2** Nagoji turning to her. She has not stopped walking.

> NAGOJI: We carved it together. The king, Ramayyan, Padmini. I merely held the first torch.

> REVATHI: Men like you always say that. You hide behind councils when the fire is yours.

**9.3** Revathi leaning on the parapet beside him, staring north at the strip. Neither looks at the other.

> REVATHI: If a Dutchman had done what you did, you would have called him monster.

> REVATHI: Because you did it for a king you respect, you call it necessity.

**9.4** Small panel. Nagoji, turned toward her profile.

> NAGOJI: You think there was another choice?

**9.5** Full width, bottom. Revathi still facing the horizon after a long silence, the last light on her face.

> REVATHI: No.

> REVATHI: That is what angers me most. You chose well for the kingdom. Badly for the house. As I knew you would.

## PAGE 10

Five panels. Love named as danger, the scar at sunset, and Ramayyan keeping accounts.

**10.1** Revathi straightening from the parapet and facing him fully.

> REVATHI: This does not make you evil. It makes you dangerous.

> REVATHI: To love you, as I do, is to love a man whose first instinct is to save the larger pattern, not the smaller square.

**10.2** Two-shot. Her gaze flicks toward the blackened strip, then back to him.

> REVATHI: You once asked only for a corner of my house and a sliver of my heart. Today you carved a wound across my land instead.

> NAGOJI: You knew that before we stood before the goddess.

> REVATHI: Of course. I married you with my eyes open. I did not expect to like it when those days came.

**10.3** Revathi walking away along the wall walk, back straight, turning her head only halfway.

> REVATHI: But do not ask me to pretend this scar is not also your work.

**10.4** Full width. Sunset. The scorched posts on the distant strip stand as jagged black silhouettes against a red sky. Nagoji alone and small at the parapet.

> CAPTION: The fire in the pepper fields had done more than deny cover to raiders.

> CAPTION: It had burned away the illusion that love could soften the choices ahead.

**10.5** Full width, bottom. Months later, the war hall by day. Ramayyan at his palm leaves, not looking up, stylus moving. Nagoji standing across the table. Inset, upper left corner: Ibrahim Marakkar (shorter than the boatmen but broader, scar from his LEFT ear to the jaw, short coat over a vest with bulging pockets) on a canal bank at dusk beside bales of cloth, talking quietly with a man whose face we do not see.

> CAPTION: Months later, Ibrahim Marakkar's name kept floating to the surface of Ramayyan's rumours.

> NAGOJI: And if he betrayed us?

> RAMAYYAN: Then I would know. And he would know that I know.

> RAMAYYAN: That is usually enough.

## Adaptation notes

- **The runner's report and the council are merged into one room.** In the novel Padmini hears the news in the fort and Ramayyan's analysis comes "later, in the war hall." Here the runner bursts into the war hall itself, so Padmini's stopped stick, her first lines and the council happen in one continuous scene.
- **Raid logistics compressed.** The coastal column's hits on fishing villages and its retreat from cannon are cut; the two fronts are carried by the two smoke columns (1.2), the runner's lines and one caption naming Kollamkara (2.1). The meeting point is shown once, as the map Nagoji pictures (2.2), instead of described.
- **"Burn it." ends page 2** so the silence that follows opens page 3, and page 3 ends on the novel's hardest line, "You know. You do not feel."
- **The torch sequence is split across a page turn**: "For a heartbeat, nothing happened" closes page 5 and the flame catches, wordless, at the top of page 6.
- **The Padmini and Revathi memories** at the vine line are a stain in the smoke (5.4), matching the Kanka device from chapter 1, with Revathi's remembered words as the only caption.
- **Violence is staged, not shown.** The burned and drowned raiders are cut under the no-gore rule; the aftermath is a dropped musket, a scorched banner and distant survivors (7.1). "Blackened beyond recognition" and the bodies in the canal do not appear.
- **Captions cut as art duplication:** "Then the line thickened. Broke. Reappeared in two places at once" (1.2 is wordless), "Her stick stopped tapping" (1.4), the ridge description, "Then the flame caught" (6.1), "By late afternoon, the corridor no longer existed" (7.1), "She walked into the hall without ceremony" (7.3), and "As the sun set, its last light turned the scorched posts into jagged silhouettes" (10.4).
- **Dialogue trimmed:** Nagoji's "We stopped them. They will not use your vines as cover again," Padmini's "For years, men will ask..." and "My vines. The king's vines..." lines, and Revathi's Velinadu reminiscence about keeping the land free of foreign flags (its point survives in her Dutchman line) are cut for balloon space. Revathi's "We will work together when it suits the land..." is reduced to its last sentence.
- **The Ibrahim coda is compressed to one panel** (10.5). "Some men are most useful when both sides believe they are theirs" and "I keep them for him" are cut; the inset of Ibrahim with cloth bales carries the ambiguity, and Ramayyan's reply ends the chapter on a turn that hands straight into chapter 27, which opens in the same war hall.
- **Continuity:** Nagoji is Ananthan Pillai in the ch24 onward uniform (white European-cut coat, red waistcoat, brass buttons), Kerala topknot, no turban, silver chain of office, clean-shaven chin, grey at the temples. Revathi wears her tali and grey threading. Padmini is shown with grey in her hair and at full strength; she dies in chapter 27, so this is her last confrontation and it is given two pages. His horse is left unnamed, as in the novel.
- **Verbatim lines kept:** "Old men fight their wars in land they have never walked," "Someone has been counting our drills as carefully as we count their gossip," "Burn it," "Today, to them, they are a shield. Tomorrow, if they live, they are coin," "You know. You do not feel," "Pepper is not rice," Padmini's consent speech, "I will not ask another to light the first torch," "We hit their shield," "This was a cruel way to fight," "ash tastes the same in my mouth," "wounds that are necessary and wounds that are kind," the verandah line, "It makes you dangerous," "the larger pattern, not the smaller square," the corner and sliver callback to chapter 20, and "Then I would know. And he would know that I know."
- **Page count** is 10 against a target of 9, inside the 7 to 11 range. The extra page goes to the two private reckonings (Padmini on pages 7 and 8, Revathi on pages 9 and 10), which are the chapter's reason for existing; the raid exposition is paid down to a single caption and a map.
