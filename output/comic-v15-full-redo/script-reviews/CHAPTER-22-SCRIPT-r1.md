## PAGE 1

Five panels. The hill, the king, and the argument at the foot of the stair. End on Ramayyan handing the king's back to Nagoji.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. Dusk going to night. A rocky hill a little inland from the coast, half swallowed by mango trees; near its crown the low gopuram of an old Shiva shrine barely clears the canopy. At the hill's foot, a small party with no banners, no elephants, no palanquin: a handful of Nair guards with torches, a few horses. A narrow stone stair cuts up through the trees.

> CAPTION: The king liked to meet his gods without the court watching.

> CAPTION: The fishermen said this shrine was older than any king brave enough to pretend the coast belonged to him.

**1.2** At the foot of the stair. Marthanda Varma (V13 varma-v1 face) in plain temple dress: bare-chested, cream mundu with a thin gold border, a single gold chain, a fresh line of sandal paste across his forehead. No peacock crest, no spear. Ramayyan Dalawa at his stirrup: thin, sharp fine features, receding hair, plain white cloth, a palm-leaf bundle under his arm. He speaks up at the king, careful and insistent.

> RAMAYYAN: You remember the festival night, when you fell on the temple stones.

> RAMAYYAN: Not every knife announces itself.

**1.3** Varma dismounting, handing his reins to a waiting stable boy without looking at him. Behind him, still mounted, the heir: a boy of about ten with wide, watchful eyes, in a small white mundu. At the edge of the torchlight, Nagoji on foot. Ananthan Pillai look: Kerala topknot, no turban, cream mundu with a shoulder cloth, silver chain of office, thick curled moustache, clean-shaven chin. No talwar and no knife: he carries nothing into a god's courtyard.

> VARMA: If a king cannot sit with his god without a wall of men between them, he is already dead.

**1.4** Ramayyan, a step closer, voice lower, not backing down. Behind them the guards study the trees and pretend not to hear.

> RAMAYYAN: If you die, so do many of us who had no say in that decision.

> RAMAYYAN: And if the heir dies with you, this kingdom becomes a feast.

**1.5** Varma waves the guards toward the foot of the stair; they settle there with their torches. Ramayyan does not laugh. He bows, steps back, and tilts his head toward Nagoji in the background.

> VARMA: You will have your men. They will wait here.

> RAMAYYAN: Then take Ananthan. If you insist on trusting no one else with your back tonight, at least trust the man who has seen how Europeans strike.

## Adaptation notes

- **The shrine's lore** (older than the first palm-leaf records, the fishing villages' stories) is one caption in 1.1. "If I breathe too often under carved ceilings" moves from a remembered line into live speech on the climb (2.3), where it shows the king's private side before the fight.
- **The argument and compromise** fit on page 1. The palm-frond deadline ("storm the gods' courtyard and scold me") is cut; the guards settling at the stair's foot carries the compromise visually. Ramayyan's festival-night line is kept short because it calls back to chapter 21.
- **The courtyard and the prayer** share one wide panel (2.4). Nagoji's count of the courtyard is drawn as a four-inset grid (3.2) that deliberately echoes his counting in chapter 1.
- **The chaver's speech** is lettered as fragments in one broken balloon (4.1), because Nagoji only hears fragments.
- **The fight is kept almost beat for beat across pages 4 to 8**, because it is the chapter's drama and it earns the command. Cut from it: the knee to the belly, and "Blood slicked our hands."
- **No gore, per the continuity bible.** The assistant's death is a blocked blade and a silhouette (4.3, 4.4), then a guttering torch (9.2). The head-slams on the stone are a shadow on the wall (8.2). The killing cut happens in the gutter between Nagoji's face (8.4) and the dying man's view of the sky (8.5). "Warmth spilled over my hand," the blood from the king's lip and his spitting blood are removed. Injuries read as broken sandal paste, swelling, a hanging shoulder, and later bandages and stitches.
- **Injury continuity:** Nagoji's LEFT palm is cut (it is the hand that first seized the wrist) and his RIGHT shoulder is dislocated in the fall, so he takes the dagger left-handed. Varma's brow is split and his right shoulder hangs low. The fort room, the toast and the ride north all carry these.
- **Nagoji carries no weapon** at the shrine (1.3), which sets up the chaver finding nothing at his belt (7.5). This matches the continuity rule that the ivory-handled knife went to Dhanaji in chapter 20.
- **The chaver is not the V13 temple priest face.** That face is reserved for the real old priest.
- **Cut from the aftermath:** "You moved fast / Not fast enough", the red-line exchange ("Today, perhaps, he will underline it"), and Ramayyan's "Do not ride far until we have spoken." Ramayyan's suspicion of Nagoji is carried instead by "Ram worries about the north" and his measuring look in 11.4.
- **Cut from the fort room:** most of Ramayyan's list of threats (arrows from our own walls, poison in lamp oil), his "That buys you more than thanks," and Varma's "Padmini will scold you, Revathi will scold you." Revathi does not appear in this chapter's pages.
- **Cut from Padmini's visit:** "You managed a chaver. The frontier will be easier." The page keeps the swimming exchange, which sets up the river image in 13.5.
- **The last panel** draws the chapter's final line as a river crossing, so the caption carries only the feeling and the art carries the image.
- **Speaker labels** use NAGOJI throughout for the letterer, although other characters now call him Ananthan in dialogue.
- **Verbatim lines kept:** "Not every knife announces itself," "If a king cannot sit with his god without a wall of men between them, he is already dead," the feast line, "You will walk behind. You will watch the boy," the medicine caption, the carved-ceilings line, "the king is scolding his god," "Only his eyes were wrong. They were too still," "My body moved before thought caught up," "For the old houses. For those he broke," "For most men, that would have ended the fight. He was not most men," the dancers caption, "The metal felt warm from recent life," "Men like him are always for something old," "Bodies move toward what they trust," "Steps. You slipped on the steps" and "The gods are clumsy hosts," "That is why it infuriates me," "A chaver in reverse," "one foot in each river," "So noted," both toasts, "I hope your horse can swim," and the chapter's last line.
- **Page count** is 13, on target (range 10 to 16). Five pages go to the strike and the fight (4 to 8), paid for by fitting the approach onto pages 1 to 3 and the aftermath onto pages 9 and 10.
