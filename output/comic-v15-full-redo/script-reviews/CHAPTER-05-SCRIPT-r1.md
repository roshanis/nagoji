# Horse of the Servant, graphic novel V15

## Chapter 5: Road to Travancore

Source: `book1_horse_servant/book1_chapter05_road_to_travancore.md`, 2,007 words, adapted as 8 pages (top of the 6-8 range) and 39 panels.

## PAGE 1

Five panels. The healer's house, a day after the wreck. Ibrahim weighs the survivor.

**1.1** Wide. The healer's low house from Chapter 4: packed-earth floor swept smooth, hearth smoke curling up to a hole in the thatch, slatted daylight. Nagoji sits propped against the wall on a woven mat, a clay bowl of rice gruel beside him. He is bare-chested with a plain cotton cloth at his waist, hair loose, thick moustache, clean chin with light stubble at most; hands wrapped in clean linen strips; rope burns across his chest; the Portuguese brand on his upper arm (same arm and placement as Chapter 4). The grey-streaked healer works at the hearth, half turned away. In the doorway, backlit by the bright coast: Ibrahim, shorter than the fishermen but broader, a scar running from his LEFT ear to the corner of his jaw, plain coastal cotton, no coat yet.

> CAPTION: The man they called *kapitan* introduced himself as Ibrahim.

> CAPTION: He had decided what kind of man I was before he opened his mouth.

**1.2** Two-shot. Ibrahim crouched on his heels at the edge of the mat, forearms on his knees, at Nagoji's eye level.

> IBRAHIM: You were on the Portuguese ship. The storm did not like her.

> NAGOJI: The storm did not like any of us. I took the chance it gave.

**1.3** Over Nagoji's shoulder. Ibrahim's eyes travel over the brand, the bandaged hands, the rope burns. Draw the eyeline clearly: a horse trader looking over new stock.

> IBRAHIM: You are Maratha.

> NAGOJI: Yes.

> IBRAHIM: You fought them.

> NAGOJI: Yes.

**1.4** Tight on Ibrahim. His thumb traces the scar along his left jaw.

> CAPTION: A habit, I would learn. It meant he was filing information away.

> IBRAHIM: Our king fights them too. And their cousins, the Dutch.

**1.5** Ibrahim's thumb has stopped on the scar. His gaze slides off Nagoji toward the bright doorway and the sea beyond it. For one beat he is somewhere else. Nagoji in the soft foreground, watching him do it.

> CAPTION: A calculation. A road not taken.

> CAPTION: The fishermen had spoken the name Travancore. I wanted to hear it from this man's mouth.

> NAGOJI: Who is your king?

## PAGE 2

Five panels. The evasion, the healer's verdict, and the one word that hurts.

**2.1** Ibrahim, back in the room, his lips twitching toward a smile he does not allow.

> IBRAHIM: You will learn his names and his titles in good time.

> IBRAHIM: For now, think of him as the man whose land you lie on. And whose patience with Europeans is thin.

**2.2** Ibrahim tips his head toward the healer without looking at her. She snorts, hands on her hips, a trace of betel red at the corner of her mouth.

> IBRAHIM (Malayalam): Can he travel?

> HEALER (Malayalam): He should rest another week, but you will not wait. Men like you never do.

**2.3** Ibrahim rising to his feet, mild, already turning back to Nagoji.

> IBRAHIM (Malayalam): Men like me are paid to be impatient.

> IBRAHIM: Can you ride in a cart, or on a horse?

**2.4** Full width. Nagoji's face. Nothing moves. Bleeding into the panel edge like a stain, not a bordered flashback frame: the black mare's neck and mane from Chapter 1, a winter morning.

> CAPTION: At the word *horse* an ache went through me that had nothing to do with broken skin.

**2.5** Nagoji pushing himself up the wall, one bandaged hand flat against it. Ibrahim already in the doorway, a silhouette against the light.

> NAGOJI: I can sit. I can walk, if there is no better way.

> IBRAHIM: There is a better way, though you will not like it.

> IBRAHIM: Come.

*Page turn.*

## PAGE 3

Five panels. New cloth, the smell of the coast, and the men who carry news.

**3.1** Wide. The fishing village at late afternoon, back in its habits: women drawing water at a well, children chasing each other, men patching nets in the shade of leaning palms. One net-mender glances down the path toward the sea. Ibrahim leads Nagoji away from the shore, inland. Nagoji now wears the Chapter 5-6 costume: village cotton dhoti and a simple cream shirt, the sleeve down over the brand, hair tied back, bare-headed, barefoot, hands in clean linen.

> CAPTION: The cloth smelled faintly of coconut smoke. After months of prison rags, it felt strange on my skin.

**3.2** A narrow track winding between flooded paddy plots. The water reflects the darkening sky in strips. The two men walk single file, small in the frame. Nagoji's head is turned, catching a scent on the breeze.

> IBRAHIM: Pepper.

> IBRAHIM: It sits in the lungs. It pays for guns.

**3.3** Close on Nagoji, eyes half closed, breathing it in. Behind him, at the paddy's edge, dark pepper vines climb the trunk of a support tree.

> CAPTION: On the Deccan we had fought for the routes that carried this smell north.

> CAPTION: Now I was at the source.

> IBRAHIM (off): And for kings. That too.

**3.4** A small clearing. Two bullocks hitched to a cart whose bed is lined with sacks. One sack's mouth is open on dried, wrinkled black berries. Ibrahim rests a hand on the load.

> IBRAHIM: We send word ahead. Our people are the vines that run along this coast.

> IBRAHIM: We carry news as well as goods.

**3.5** Nagoji in the foreground, level, watching him. Ibrahim beyond, half turned to the bullocks.

> NAGOJI: What do you tell them?

## PAGE 4

Five panels. The price of a man, and the slow road at dusk.

**4.1** Ibrahim's shrug, a little too careful, with a sidelong glance at Nagoji.

> IBRAHIM: That I found a branded Maratha officer on our beach, with more teeth than the Viceroy's dungeons usually leave.

> IBRAHIM: That he speaks like a man who has led horse into gunfire and lived.

**4.2** Ibrahim up on the cart now, looking down at Nagoji, his broad hand held out.

> IBRAHIM: A man like that has value.

> IBRAHIM: The question is always to whom.

**4.3** Inset in the lower corner of 4.2, tight: Nagoji's linen-wrapped hand closing on Ibrahim's.

> IBRAHIM (off): Rest. The road is long, and the bulls do not hurry.

**4.4** Wide. Dusk, the sky shifting from bruised grey to deep blue. The cart is small on a broader road, the bullocks plodding. Fireflies spark over the wet fields. Far off, the roofline of a temple.

> IBRAHIM: Your people. What do they think of this coast?

> NAGOJI: They think of it when they want money.

**4.5** Two-shot on the sacks, faces in the last blue light, Nagoji's bandaged hands resting on his knees.

> NAGOJI: I serve, as all men serve. But I know the difference between a fort built to defend a people and a fort built to protect a treasury.

> IBRAHIM: Our fortresses are both. You will see.

## PAGE 5

Four panels. Into the hills, where even the road has owners.

**5.1** A small settlement, lamps lit in doorways. A knot of village boys runs alongside the cart, one of them pointing at Nagoji. Nagoji looks back at them.

> BOY (Malayalam): Who is he?

> IBRAHIM (Malayalam): A man who thought he could drown, then changed his mind.

**5.2** Wide. The road climbs. The coconut palms have thinned; taller, denser trees with dark leaves close overhead and the air looks heavy. The cart is small under the canopy. At the tree line, barely readable in the shadow, a single watcher with a spear.

> IBRAHIM: Beyond these hills, the land belongs to men who answer only to the king. They do not like uninvited guests.

> NAGOJI: And with you?

> IBRAHIM: With me you are still an uninvited guest. But one whose arrival has been announced.

**5.3** A wayside shrine: a worn stone image under a tree hung with cloth strips and bangles, a small oil lamp burning before it. Ibrahim, down from the cart, offers a coin; an old woman marks his forehead with ash. Her eyes have already gone past him to Nagoji on the cart.

> OLD WOMAN (Malayalam): That one carries storms in his bones.

**5.4** Nagoji with one eyebrow raised. Ibrahim climbing back up beside him, ash on his forehead, shrugging.

> IBRAHIM: I told her you carry horses there too.

> IBRAHIM: Storms and horses can both be useful, if you know how to ride them.

## PAGE 6

Five panels. The compound, the gate, the hall, and the one word that matters.

**6.1** Wide. Evening deepening. A settlement larger than the fishing village, not yet a town: a small bazaar at a crossroads, stalls of spices, cloth, oil and trinkets under oil lamps, deep wheel ruts in packed earth. At its centre, a compound wall of red stone blocks broken by a wooden gate. Two spearmen lounging there straighten as the cart comes in.

> GUARD (Malayalam): You come late, *kapitan*.

> IBRAHIM (Malayalam): The sea does not consult my schedule.

**6.2** The guard peering at Nagoji, his gaze on the bandaged hands and the line of the shoulders. Ibrahim jerks his chin toward Nagoji.

> IBRAHIM (Malayalam): This is the man I sent word about.

> GUARD (Malayalam): He looks like he should already be dead.

> IBRAHIM (Malayalam): He disagrees. Our king might too.

**6.3** Wide interior. A low hall, wooden pillars carved with curling designs, lamps hanging on chains, a packed-earth floor. Men sit cross-legged around a central mat. At the far end, on a slightly raised platform, a man with a stack of palm-leaf records and a stylus: simple dhoti, a shawl over one shoulder, no crown. He must NOT read as Ramayyan (thin and sharp-featured, Chapter 6): draw him older and heavier, with a greying moustache and a patient, round face. Every man in the room is angled toward him. Ibrahim leans to Nagoji's ear as they approach.

> IBRAHIM (whisper): This is not the king. He is busy elsewhere.

> IBRAHIM (whisper): This is one of his hands on this coast.

**6.4** The official looking up from his leaf, dry amusement in his face.

> OFFICIAL (Malayalam): You bring me shipwreck.

> IBRAHIM (Malayalam): The sea brought him. I only stopped the crabs from finishing.

**6.5** Tight on the official. His eyes on Nagoji, stylus poised above the leaf.

> OFFICIAL (Malayalam): Name.

*Page turn.*

## PAGE 7

Five panels. Name, rank, and the question that decides everything.

**7.1** Nagoji standing at the edge of the mat, upright despite everything, answering the platform.

> CAPTION: In Goa they had written me down as a number.

> NAGOJI: Nagoji Sawant.

**7.2** Insert, tight, a deliberate echo of the ledger insert in Chapter 1: the stylus cutting strokes into the palm leaf. Marks only, no legible script.

> OFFICIAL (off, Malayalam): Service?

> NAGOJI (off): *Huzurat* cavalry. Household horse under Chimaji Appa. Until the Portuguese netted me.

**7.3** The official's eyes flick to the bandaged hands, then to the sleeve over the brand.

> OFFICIAL (Malayalam): You raided them?

> NAGOJI: Yes.

> OFFICIAL (Malayalam): You know their forts?

> NAGOJI: Some.

**7.4** The official tapping the edge of his palm leaf with the stylus. Ibrahim at Nagoji's shoulder, echoing the words low in Konkani.

> OFFICIAL (Malayalam): Our king sharpens his sword for the Dutch now. And for the chiefs who hide behind European flags.

> OFFICIAL (Malayalam): He has horse from Madurai, musketeers of his own, ships that slip between the foreign hulls at night.

**7.5** Close on the official, patient, waiting.

> OFFICIAL (Malayalam): What does a Maratha rider know that he does not already have?

> CAPTION: This was the question that would decide whether I was given a place in this new game, or quietly dropped into some ditch.

*Page turn.*

## PAGE 8

Five panels. The answer, the smile, and the road pointing south.

**8.1** Nagoji turned toward Ibrahim to be translated, but his eyes are on the official's face.

> NAGOJI: Your king knows his own land. These hills, these backwaters, which chiefs will bend and which must be broken.

> NAGOJI: The Dutch and the Portuguese know their books and their drills.

**8.2** Tighter on Nagoji. Around the hall, the seated men have turned their heads toward him.

> NAGOJI: I know how men like them think when they stand on foreign soil and believe God and powder have made them superior.

**8.3** Close on Nagoji's eyes, flat and certain.

> NAGOJI: I know what a horse can do to a musket line when the man in the saddle has stopped being impressed by white skin.

**8.4** The official in the background on his platform, a very slight smile. Nagoji in the foreground, lowering his head.

> OFFICIAL (Malayalam): You speak well for someone who almost fed the fish.

> OFFICIAL (Malayalam): Tomorrow we take you to the king's war hall. He can decide whether your words are as sharp as you think.

> CAPTION: Not as a subject. Not yet.

**8.5** Full width, bottom. Night. Nagoji asleep on a mat under the eaves of the compound, lamplight gone, the red stone wall behind him. Beyond it, pepper vines climb their support trees in the dark. One bandaged hand lies loosely curled on the mat, as if around reins.

> CAPTION: The road that had begun in a Goan dungeon now pointed toward a king I had not yet met.

> CAPTION: Horses. Guns. Storms.

> CAPTION (separate, weighted): When sleep came, it carried no dreams of drowning. Only the steady beat of hooves on sand, and a distant roar like the sea.

## Adaptation notes

- **Page budget.** 8 pages, the top of the 6-8 range. The hall interview is the chapter's drama, so it gets three pages (6 to 8) and the travel (village, pepper path, cart, road, shrine) is compressed into pages 3 to 5.
- **Lettering convention.** Konkani between Ibrahim and Nagoji is lettered as plain English. Lines tagged (Malayalam) get a distinct treatment (for example angle brackets). Nagoji hears them through Ibrahim, whose translations are not repeated in balloons.
- **Cut for length.** The "Your masters. Not you." exchange, which is ambiguous out of context in balloons (Nagoji's "I serve, as all men serve" is kept). Ibrahim's "whose boats pull the pepper your people crave", "they will already know more about you than you think", "both eyes", and the "fewer coins and perhaps fewer fingers" warning. The village-life, bazaar and hall descriptions become staging only. The novel's "a king I had not yet met considered maps" line is merged into the closing road caption.
- **Staging added.** The watcher at the tree line (5.2) makes "men who answer only to the king" visible. The palm-leaf insert (7.2) and the "number" caption (7.1) echo the Chapter 1 ledger, so the name taken in Goa is given back here. The closing panel keeps the hooves in the caption and the reins in his sleeping hand, so the caption does not duplicate the art.
- **Plants kept as silent beats.** Ibrahim's flicker of calculation (1.5) and "The question is always to whom" (4.2).
- **Continuity.** Nagoji is bare-chested with the brand showing on pages 1-2, before he is dressed, and in the Chapter 5-6 village dhoti and cream shirt from page 3. Clean chin, thick moustache. Ibrahim's scar is on the LEFT side, and he has no coat until Chapter 6. The coastal official is unnamed and must be drawn distinctly from Ramayyan, who first appears in Chapter 6.
