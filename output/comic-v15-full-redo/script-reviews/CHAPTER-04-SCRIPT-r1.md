## Chapter 4: The Fishermen of the Pepper Coast

## PAGE 1

Four panels. The sea gives him back. Sound arrives before faces do.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. A storm-spent sea under a flat grey sky, long heavy swells breaking on a shallow curve of beach. Black rock outcrops break the sand. Small in the foam, a man is thrown face down onto the beach, one arm flung out. Floating timber in the surf behind him. No other people anywhere.

> CAPTION: When the sea finally spat me out, it did so without ceremony.

**1.2** Ground level, tight. Nagoji's bandaged hands, soaked and filthy, fingers dug into coarse wet grit. The retreating surf drags at his legs in the background. He is in the captivity rags, sodden: torn ochre sleeveless tunic, cream dhoti. Two loops of rope are still knotted around his chest, the frayed free end trailing into the foam. No irons. Raw chafe rings at wrists and ankles where iron once bit.

> CAPTION: Breathing was a battle. Every breath brought more salt, more pain.

**1.3** Close on his face, cheek pressed to wet sand, one eye half open, long curly black hair plastered across it. Thick curled moustache, clean-shaven chin with captivity stubble, small gold ear stud. A thin sheet of wave washes over his shoulder and back.

> CAPTION: My limbs felt like they belonged to someone else, a man made of lead and bruises.

**1.4** Full width, bottom strip. Same low angle along the sand, but the tide line has moved and the light has shifted: time has passed. At the far edge of the panel, three pairs of bare brown feet are approaching over the sand, leaving prints. We see no more of them than that.

> CAPTION: The sea was no longer alone.

> VOICE (off): ...alive...

> VOICE (off): Look at his hands...

> VOICE (off): See the brand on his arm.

*Page turn.*

## PAGE 2

Five panels. Not Portuguese. Water. Then the wreck, and what it does not show him.

**2.1** Nagoji's point of view, flat on his back, looking straight up. A lowering grey sky, the colour of stale rice water. Three faces close the frame around it like a ring, framed by beards and damp hair. These are Malabar fishermen: sun-and-salt brown skin, lungis hitched to the thigh, short sleeveless vests clinging wet to their chests. Establish all three now and hold them. **FISHERMAN**: forties, the leader, short grizzled beard, a cotton cloth over one shoulder, strong white teeth. **YOUNG FISHERMAN**: twenties, thin beard, quick eyes. **WARY FISHERMAN**: lean and older, leaning in least, already half turned away.

> CAPTION: They were not Portuguese.

**2.2** The Fisherman pushes Nagoji's hair back from his forehead with rough fingers. Nagoji flinches, one bandaged hand half rising to guard his face. Habit, not fear.

> FISHERMAN: Easy, *bhau*. You are on land now.

> CAPTION: *Bhau.* Brother, in Konkani. Near enough to my Marathi.

**2.3** Tight. A clay pot pressed to Nagoji's cracked lips, water spilling down his chin and moustache.

> NAGOJI: Water.

> CAPTION: The first swallow burned. The second soothed.

**2.4** Wide, from just behind Nagoji's shoulder as he props himself on one elbow and looks past the men. The shallow curve of beach, black rock, a line of coconut palms with fronds bent in the wind. On the sand to the right, the broken carcass of a ship's boat, planks scattered. Farther along the shore, half hidden in spray, the cracked-open hull of a Portuguese ship on an unseen reef. Timber bobbing in the surf.

> CAPTION: I knew the curve of a Portuguese hull.

**2.5** Narrow strip. Nagoji's eyes, searching the surf line from left to right. In his eyes' reflection or behind him: only foam and floating wood. Nothing else.

> CAPTION: No bodies. Not that I could see.

> CAPTION: Perhaps the sea had decided it had eaten enough men for one day.

## PAGE 3

Five panels. The examination, and the vote on whether he lives. Letterer: angle brackets <like this> mark Malayalam, which Nagoji cannot follow, translated for the reader. Plain balloons are Konkani, which he can.

**3.1** Two actions in one wide frame. The Young Fisherman lifts Nagoji's bandaged hand and turns it to show the heel of the palm, hard with rein and sword calluses. On the other side, the Fisherman tugs the torn ochre cloth down off Nagoji's shoulder.

> YOUNG FISHERMAN: He has soldier's calluses. Not a clerk. Not a merchant.

> FISHERMAN: Look at this.

**3.2** Insert, tight. The Portuguese brand on Nagoji's left upper arm, just below the shoulder: a crude cross burned into the skin, puckered and angry. No letters or numerals. Wet sand clings around it.

> *No text.*

**3.3** The Wary Fisherman stands apart, ankle-deep at the tide line, glancing up the coast toward the wreck and then back at the others. He wants to be anywhere else.

> CAPTION: They argued in Malayalam. I could not follow a word. I did not need to.

> WARY FISHERMAN: <Kill him. Why bring trouble to the village?>

> WARY FISHERMAN: <The Portuguese will send men for their ship. Better the sea finishes its work.>

**3.4** The Fisherman, still crouched at Nagoji's side, snorts and turns his head to the Wary Fisherman. The posture of a man haggling at a fish market.

> FISHERMAN: <The Portuguese and the Dutch ask questions anyway. We might as well gain something.>

> WARY FISHERMAN (off): <What? He is half dead.>

**3.5** The Fisherman turns back to Nagoji and leans in, close. The look of a man doing sums. Nagoji meets his eyes.

> FISHERMAN: <Half is still half.>

> FISHERMAN: You fought them, yes?

## PAGE 4

Five panels. A name from the south. The first time since Goa that anyone has offered him a future.

**4.1** Nagoji up on one elbow, water still running from his hair. His voice is a rasp but his eyes are steady.

> NAGOJI: Yes. On the Deccan roads. Near their forts.

**4.2** The Fisherman nods once, as if a calculation has come out right, and jerks his chin south down the coast. The line of palms runs away into haze.

> FISHERMAN: Our king to the south fights them too. And the other pale ones from the north, the Dutch.

> FISHERMAN: He sharpens his teeth for them.

**4.3** Close on Nagoji. Something wakes behind the exhaustion.

> CAPTION: King. Teeth. Dutch. The words caught like hooks.

> NAGOJI: Travancore.

**4.4** Two-shot. The Fisherman's brows go up. Nagoji, flat on the sand and half drowned, answers like a man sitting a horse.

> FISHERMAN: You know the name.

> NAGOJI: I know they have not yet learned to kneel when a foreign flag flies.

> NAGOJI: That is enough.

**4.5** Full width. The Fisherman grins, teeth white against weathered skin. The Young Fisherman takes Nagoji's shoulders, the Fisherman his legs; the Wary Fisherman is already walking ahead, scanning the shore. In the foreground, a small sand crab sidles out of the way.

> FISHERMAN: You see, he has a tongue as well as scars.

> FISHERMAN: Come, *bhau*. Before the crabs claim you.

## PAGE 5

Five panels. Inland, the village, and the woman who decides. End on his answer.

**5.1** Low angle from where Nagoji is being carried, head tipped back: coconut fronds against the grey sky, the shoulders of the men carrying him. Far behind and below, the wreck is small on the beach, untouched.

> CAPTION: The smell of earth instead of salt.

> CAPTION: After the dungeon and the storm, these small things felt like gifts.

**5.2** Wide. The village beyond the palms: narrow paths between wet paddy, low houses of mud and red laterite stone with palm-thatch roofs. Children stare from doorways. Women with water pots on their hips have stopped on the path. In the foreground, a small girl whispers to the child beside her, eyes on the dripping stranger.

> GIRL: <Portuguese.>

**5.3** The Young Fisherman, carrying the legs, turns his head sharply toward the girl. The girl shrinks back.

> YOUNG FISHERMAN: <No. Look at his eyes. Look at his mark.>

> YOUNG FISHERMAN: <This one is enemy of the Portuguese.>

**5.4** A low house at the edge of the village. A woman in her fifties steps out, wiping her hands on a plain cotton sari, grey streaks in her hair. A betel-stained smile flickers at the corner of her mouth and is gone. The fishermen talk at her; she does not interrupt. Her eyes go to the brand, the bandaged hands, the raw rope burns across his chest.

> WOMAN: <Bring him in.>

> WOMAN: <If he dies, burn him quietly. Otherwise, send word to the *kapitan*.>

**5.5** Interior, cool and dim. Packed earth floor swept smooth; smoke from a small hearth curling up toward a hole in the thatch. Nagoji on a woven mat, the salt-stiff tunic peeled off and set aside, the cream dhoti left on him. The woman washes him with a cloth and a clay basin, brisk rather than gentle. She has unwound the bandage from one hand. Frame it so his fingers are turned away from us or lost in her shadow: we read the damage only in her face, her mouth pulled tight. The Fisherman squats at the mat's edge.

> FISHERMAN: She asks what they did to you.

> NAGOJI: Asked questions.

## PAGE 6

Five panels. Word goes out, and something comes back. End on the weighing.

**6.1** The woman clicks her tongue and wrings the cloth hard into the basin, speaking fast and sharp at no one in particular. The Fisherman relays it with half a smile.

> FISHERMAN: She says keep your stories for the *kapitan*.

> FISHERMAN: She does not need to hear them to know what kind of men they are.

**6.2** A boy in the doorway, backlit by grey daylight, panting, one hand on the frame, talking fast. The fishermen exchange a look across the room.

> BOY: ... *kapitan* ... *kapitan* ...

Letterer: this one balloon is NOT translated. Letter it as a rush of unreadable speech (broken wavy strokes, no real script), with only the word *kapitan* in clear type, twice. It is all Nagoji catches.

> CAPTION: So. I was not the first piece of foreign wreckage to wash up in their nets.

**6.3** The Fisherman crouched close to the mat, settling the cloth on his shoulder, voice low. Nagoji looking up at him.

> FISHERMAN: Rest. When the *kapitan* comes, speak carefully.

> FISHERMAN: He works for men who have long arms.

**6.4** Narrow strip. Nagoji's eyes closed, hearth light on his face. Behind him, out of focus, the fishermen's heads are turning toward the door, straightening, and a broad shadow falls across the swept floor.

> CAPTION: Storms on land were different from storms at sea. They crept in on quiet feet, through conversations and calculations.

**6.5** Large, the bottom half of the page. The *kapitan* has stepped into the house, grey daylight behind him, the fishermen drawing back with respect. Shorter than the fishermen but broader. A white turban, a short grey-flecked beard, a pale vest over a white mundu. A scar runs from his LEFT ear to the corner of his jaw; turn his left side to the hearth so it reads. He says nothing. His eyes are on Nagoji, going over brand, hands, shoulders, the way a horse trader looks at new stock. Nagoji, on the mat, has opened his eyes and is looking straight back.

> CAPTION: His eyes moved over me the way a horse trader's eyes move over new stock.

> CAPTION: I had seen that look before, in market squares where men were sold.

> CAPTION: In that silence, I was being weighed.

## Adaptation notes

- **Page budget.** Six pages, on target. The vote on the beach (page 3) and the Travancore exchange (page 4) get a full page each because they are the chapter's drama: a stranger's life decided by a haggle, and the first time anyone offers him a future. The walk inland and the village are compressed into the top of page 5.
- **Language convention.** The novel reports the Malayalam argument even though Nagoji "could not follow" it. The comic does the same with angle-bracket balloons, and keeps his POV honest with the page 3 caption ("I did not need to") and with the boy's untranslated balloon on page 6, where he catches only *kapitan*.
- **Cut: the first "sea gods had eaten enough" line.** The novel uses the idea twice; the comic keeps the later, sharper version over the wreck, where "No bodies" also quietly carries Keshavrao, lost in chapter 3.
- **Cut: "A man who has seen Portuguese dungeons and still breathes may be useful."** The motive is carried by "Half is still half" and "We might as well gain something."
- **Trimmed: the Wary Fisherman's objection** loses its Dutch clause; the Dutch arrive one balloon later from the Fisherman.
- **Cut: the carry inland in detail** (sand grit at the heels, the crow, the furtive pace, no one stripping the wreck). Kept only the smell of earth and "these small things felt like gifts"; the untouched wreck survives as art in 5.1.
- **Cut: "The kapitan. A local headman, or something more?"** and "I had escaped ... by chance and stubbornness." The question is raised by the art and answered by the final panel; the stubbornness is already on the page in 4.4.
- **Cut: the kapitan's entrance line about "salt and foreign places".** Kept the horse-trader and market-square lines, which tie the chapter's end to his horseman's eye and to the slave ship.
- **No nudity, no gore.** The woman strips only the tunic; the dhoti stays. His ruined fingers are never shown directly; her face carries them (5.5).
- **Continuity flags for CONTINUITY.md.** (1) Irons: the novel leaves Nagoji shackled when he jumps in chapter 3 and never says how the irons came off; the bible drops them for chapter 4, so this script shows no irons, only chafe rings. (2) The brand's shape and side are not fixed anywhere; this script proposes a crude cross, no letters, on the LEFT upper arm, to match the left-side placement the bible uses from chapter 7. Please confirm or correct before generation. (3) The *kapitan* (Ibrahim Marakkar, not named until chapter 5) is drawn in his chapter 6 look minus the coat, keeping V14's white turban and grey beard, with the LEFT-side scar.
