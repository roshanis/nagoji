## PAGE 1

Five panels. The night temple, the gaps in the crowd, a name spoken whole. End on the king coming in with nothing but a sword.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. The mandapa of Padmanabhaswamy at night. Oil lamps in brass holders burn along every wall and pillar, their flames doubled in the polished stone floor until the whole hall seems to float on fire. Carved granite pillars recede into shadow. Beyond an open outer doorway, monsoon rain falls in a silver sheet. The nobles of Travancore stand packed in ranks, bare-chested in temple fashion, fine mundus and shoulder cloths, gold at their ears and throats catching the lamplight.

> CAPTION: The monsoon of 1750, three days after Kottarakkara fell. The king called every noble in the kingdom to Padmanabhaswamy.

> CAPTION: Not for war council. Not for treaty.

**1.2** The front ranks from behind and a little above. Nobles in order of importance, composed faces in profile, jewels glinting. In the front rank, one clear gap on the stone where no one stands, a single lamp's reflection lying in it.

> CAPTION: Near the front was the place where Padmini would once have stood, stick planted, eyes sharp.

> CAPTION: Fever had taken her months before. The gap she left was as visible as any banner.

**1.3** Nagoji at the back of the hall among the lesser officers. He is Ananthan Pillai in temple dress: Kerala topknot with grey at the temples, cream mundu, shoulder cloth over a bare chest, silver chain of office; strong cheekbones, thick curled moustache, clean-shaven chin, small gold ear stud. No coat inside the temple. Two nobles in the rank ahead glance back at him sidelong.

> CAPTION: Revathi had not come.

> CAPTION: Her absence was noted, weighed, filed away.

**1.4** A temple attendant lowers a great conch from his lips. A herald steps into the lamplight before the assembly, chin raised, voice trained to cut through drums and crowd.

> HERALD: Listen and witness.

> HERALD: His Highness Sree Anizham Thirunal Marthanda Varma, Maharaja of Travancore.

> HERALD: Victor of Colachel. Breaker of the Eight Houses.

**1.5** Full width, bottom. At the far doorway, Marthanda Varma enters. V13 varma-v1 face, aged: low knot, plain white mundu, bare torso with the sacred thread across his chest, no crown, no jewels, no headwear. He carries a sword flat across both palms, its hilt wrapped in silk, its blade catching the flames. Every head in the hall has turned.

> CAPTION: I had heard him called Varma, called Maharaja, called tiger.

> CAPTION: Hearing the whole weight of him spoken aloud inside a temple made the air feel thinner.

## Adaptation notes

- **Opening exposition cut.** "Broken things could be mended if the glue was strong enough" and the reasoning behind the ceremony are cut; two captions in 1.1 carry the date and "Not for war council. Not for treaty." The ceremony itself shows the purpose.
- **Temple dress.** Kerala temple custom has men enter bare-chested in mundu and shoulder cloth, so in the temple (pages 1 to 5) Nagoji wears the bible's ch17-27 Ananthan Pillai dress rather than the ch24+ uniform; the uniform returns at the fort on page 6. Varma follows the bible's ch28 dedication look, with the sacred thread the novel gives him.
- **The Marakkar captains** Nagoji stands among are not drawn; they carry no line, and in the bare-chested temple crowd they would not read clearly.
- **"The titles landed like drumbeats"** and the gooseflesh are cut; the rest of that reaction stays verbatim on 1.5 over the entrance.
- **The vault.** The coin list is trimmed to one inset (2.3) with two captions.
- **The sweep.** "Not a token gesture" and the step-by-step route are carried by the large circuit panel (5.1) rather than captions. "No one spoke. No one moved" is cut as art duplication.
- **Revathi's scene** is kept at two full pages. Cut: "She did not speak to me at first" (shown by 6.1 and 6.2), "I did not answer" (shown by the silent panel 6.3), and "We will stand in the same halls. We will sign the same treaties", which the next line covers. "This woman I might have loved in another life" is cut: they have been married since Chapter 20 and her tali is in the frame, so on the page the line would read as a contradiction. Her exit in 7.5 carries it.
- **The treaty** is one panel (8.1). "Reciprocate friendship", the arms sales and "They had sold their allies to save their trade" are cut for space.
- **The rampart** keeps Varma's speeches nearly whole. Only the envoy list is trimmed. The young Savitri laughing is added as a stain image (9.3) to give "I remember her laugh" a face.
- **The chapel** cuts the "What words?" exchange and "the words are the same in any language", the chapel's Syrian Christian visitors, "three men who had come to this coast by different storms", and Ramayyan's "This kingdom uses what it finds". Kept: the Ramayyan mortar joke (it answers "checking the mortar" in 4.1), and De Lannoy's burial wish (he is in fact buried at Udayagiri).
- **Duarte's raised hand** gets its own wordless panel (11.1) so the blessing pays off his hesitation and nod in Chapter 1.
- **The epilogue.** "A hundred small kingdoms that were now one" and the army catalogue are folded into one large panel (12.4) with two captions. The officer's question, reported in the novel, is made a balloon. "Fool or a necessary storm", the children in the Velinadu house, and "I stood guard over the thing we had built from the ashes of what they had lost" are cut so the last page can breathe. "I lifted my face to the rain" is the art of 13.4, not a caption.
- **The dungeon callback (13.1)** quotes Nagoji's own Chapter 1 line, "He seems to favour whoever has the better powder", the line the Chapter 1 script kept verbatim to pay off here.
- **Verbatim lines kept:** "Not for war council. Not for treaty"; Varma's vow; both of the chief's whispers; "He had built walls of stone and armies of men..."; "And god's servant still held the sword"; all of Revathi's major lines, including "Closed is not the same as open" and "To remember"; "more than pepper and prayers"; "I remember her laugh"; "For this"; Duarte's blessing; "It is a strange household. But it holds"; "I miss what it cost"; and the book's last three lines.
- **Page count** is 13 against a target of 12, inside the 10 to 14 range. The extra page goes to the finale (pages 12 and 13), because this is the last chapter of the book. The exposition before and around the ceremony is compressed to pay for it.
- **Print.** Panels 1.1, 5.1, 9.5, 12.4 and 13.4 are drawn close to full page width or half the page. Each must reach at least 300 effective PPI at its printed size (6x9 in trim plus bleed), or be upscaled 2x before placement, per CONTINUITY.md.
