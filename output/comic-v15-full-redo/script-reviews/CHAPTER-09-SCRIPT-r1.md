## PAGE 1

Five panels. Compress the ride. Let the land do the talking, then give us the name the chapter turns on.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. Pre-dawn, the sky the grey of old iron. A small party of riders leaving through the gate of Padmini Amma's estate. Padmini at the head, side saddle on a compact horse. Nagoji a little behind and to her left. Ibrahim Marakkar (short coat over a vest with bulging pockets, scar from his left ear to his jaw) and two of Padmini's male relatives following.

> CAPTION: We left Padmini Amma's estate before sunrise, while the air still clung to the night's cool.

**1.2** Wide landscape, morning mist lifting. The road winds inland through low green hills. Lotus leaves on a pond. An ancient tree with a small shrine at its foot, cloth bundles and oil lamps on its trunk. Women with baskets on their hips heading for the pepper gardens. In the foreground, children racing along a narrow bund between flooded channels. The riders small in the middle distance.

> CAPTION: Their feet were sure on ridges that would have sent me tumbling into the channels.

**1.3** Medium two-shot, riding. Padmini in muted earth-toned silk hitched for riding, simple gold at throat and wrists, thick black hair coiled at the nape, a sword with a worn leather-wrapped hilt at her right side, her long walking stick strapped along the saddle. She does not turn her head to speak. Nagoji in his commander's clothes: rust-red turban, cream tunic, broad red sash, cream trousers, boots, talwar. Thick curled moustache, clean-shaven chin.

> PADMINI: You have heard of Velinadu. What did your hills tell you?

> NAGOJI: Stories. Of company men who thought themselves safe until women closed the gates on them.

> NAGOJI: Of queens who could sign treaties without asking anyone's permission.

**1.4** Padmini in profile, lifting her chin toward the road ahead. The faintest smile.

> PADMINI: Stories travel badly. But the bones of that one are not wrong.

> PADMINI: Remember that when you meet Revathi. She carries those bones in her tongue.

**1.5** Wide, full width. Velinadu Kovilakam ahead. No towering walls, no bastions: a low ring of red laterite and earth circling a cluster of tiled roofs and broad trees. The gate is wide enough for carts and elephants. Nair guards with spears watch the riders come.

> CAPTION: My Deccan mind had expected towering walls and bastions.

> CAPTION: Its guards cared more about who entered than what.

## PAGE 2

Five panels. The gate, the warning, the wait. End on footsteps.

**2.1** At the gate. A Nair guard bows his head to Padmini, still mounted, but his eyes have already slid to Nagoji.

> GUARD: Amma. You come early.

> GUARD: And this?

> PADMINI: A rider from the north whom the sea sent back to us. The Maharaja plays with him. I thought Velinadu should have its share.

**2.2** Wide, inside the ring. The ground rises toward a central hall with a tiled roof, banners hanging heavy and still. Women along the verandahs in plain cotton and bright silk, heads uncovered, hair oiled and coiled or braided. Fewer men: one with a writing tablet, one with a sword, both glancing at a doorway where no one has yet appeared. Padmini and Nagoji dismounting in the foreground.

> PADMINI: You stand in a place where land, not men, is the first thing named.

> PADMINI: Do not address her as if she were a sardar's daughter waiting for a husband to give her meaning.

**2.3** Close two-shot. Nagoji, deadpan. Padmini's sudden flash of white teeth.

> NAGOJI: If I forget, I expect you to kick me.

> PADMINI: That can be arranged.

**2.4** A side verandah. Nagoji seated cross-legged on a woven mat, his boots set neatly by the step, a brass tumbler of coconut water and a banana leaf parcel before him. Padmini beside him, entirely at ease. Beyond the pillars, deep in the complex, drums and bells drawn as sound only.

> PADMINI: They will make you wait. That is part of the weighing.

> CAPTION: My stomach had begun to learn this land. Rice instead of bhakri. Coconut instead of groundnut. Fish where once there had been mutton.

**2.5** Low angle along the verandah floor. A woman's bare feet, a gold anklet, the hem of a deep indigo sari shot with gold, stepping onto the stone. In the background, out of focus, Nagoji's back straightening, the tumbler halfway to his mouth.

> CAPTION: Waiting is familiar to any soldier. This was the other kind.

> CAPTION: The waiting men endure when summoned before someone who can change their fate with a word.

## PAGE 3

Three panels. The reveal. Give her the page.

**3.1** Large, two thirds of the page. Revathi Bayi on the verandah, full figure, morning light through the pillars behind her. Deep indigo sari shot with gold, draped in the local style over one shoulder. Gold armlets. A necklace of flat gold coins. Her braid looped and threaded with jasmine. A thin line of sandalwood paste along her hairline. Kohl. No tali. Her dark eyes level and fixed on Nagoji, who has risen to his feet at the frame's edge.

> CAPTION: She was perhaps a little younger than I. The way she held herself made her years hard to guess.

> REVATHI: Padmini Amma. You come with interesting company.

**3.2** Padmini and Revathi, each giving the other the same almost-bow, the smallest fraction of a head.

> PADMINI: Revathi Bayi. You asked to see what the southern winds had blown in.

> PADMINI: I brought him before the salt left his skin.

**3.3** Full width. Revathi turning her gaze fully on Nagoji. Over her shoulder, his face, steady, taking it.

> REVATHI: You are the Maratha rider. The one the Portuguese could not quite break and the sea could not quite keep.

> NAGOJI: So they tell me.

## PAGE 4

Five panels. The first fencing. She tests his tongue, then his loyalty.

**4.1** Revathi, head tilted a degree.

> REVATHI: You speak our tongue?

> NAGOJI: I learn it. Slowly. Like a horse finding its footing on new ground.

**4.2** Tight on her mouth. A twitch, not quite a smile.

> REVATHI: Good. Men who refuse to learn languages normally insist theirs is the only one that matters.

> REVATHI: Those men bore me.

**4.3** Wide. She sits on the mat opposite him with liquid ease, legs folded under her. A servant, appearing as if summoned by thought, sets a brass plate of food by her knee. She does not look at it.

> REVATHI: Sit. If you crane your neck the whole time, you will despise me before we have even argued.

**4.4** Across the mats, the two of them level now. In the background, Padmini rising quietly and withdrawing toward the inner rooms.

> REVATHI: You ride for Marthanda Varma now.

> NAGOJI: I ride where I am told. At present, that is for your king.

**4.5** Close on Revathi, tasting the words.

> REVATHI: My king.

> REVATHI: He takes heads when he must and lands when he can. He calls this kingdom his.

> REVATHI: That does not mean the earth beneath his feet has forgotten who owned it before.

## PAGE 5

Five panels. The brand, the sand, the reason. The first thing they agree on.

**5.1** Revathi leaning back slightly, studying him.

> NAGOJI: You prefer the old ways?

> REVATHI: I prefer ways that remember their promises.

> REVATHI: And you. What letters do you carry on your skin, Sawant?

**5.2** Insert, tight. Nagoji pushing up his left sleeve: the Portuguese brand on his forearm, and beside it a long white scar. His nails healed but ridged.

> NAGOJI: A brand. Some scars. Joints that ache in the rain. Nothing a scribe would waste ink on.

> REVATHI (off): You underestimate scribes. They love scars.

**5.3** Revathi holding a piece of plantain between finger and thumb, not eating it. Bleeding into the panel edge behind her like a stain, not a clean flashback frame: riders wheeling on wet sand at low tide.

> REVATHI: They say you trained the Madurai horse on wet sand.

> REVATHI: You think you can teach our men to kill Dutch as easily as you killed Portuguese on your hills?

> NAGOJI (off): There was nothing easy about it. The ground does not care whose blood it drinks.

**5.4** Full width. Nagoji's face, hard. Bleeding in behind him, echoing chapter 1, panel 1.2: a clerk's hand and quill over a ledger in a Goa cellar, the writing illegible.

> REVATHI (off): Yet you keep pouring. Why?

> NAGOJI: Because in Goa, they tried to erase my name and replace it with a number in their books.

> NAGOJI: Give them an inch of riverbank, and they will swallow the whole valley before you have finished signing their treaty.

**5.5** Revathi's eyes narrowing slightly. She eats the plantain at last, slowly, as if chewing helps her think.

> REVATHI: On that, we agree.

## PAGE 6

Five panels. She shows her real stake. Then the first shadow.

**6.1** Low angle. Revathi's finger pointing down at the earth between the two mats, large in frame, the two of them beyond it.

> REVATHI: This land belongs to my line.

> REVATHI: Not to my husband, if I take one. Not to my sons, if I have them. To my sisters. To their daughters. To the name we carry.

**6.2** Revathi turned toward the south, past the pillars, where the land curves away beyond the trees. We do not see the sea.

> CAPTION: Beyond the curve of the land, out of sight, Dutch ships rode at anchor.

> REVATHI: The company men thought if they signed the right paper, they owned the pepper, the trees, the very wind.

> REVATHI: Now they send different men, different papers.

**6.3** Back to Nagoji, cool and even.

> REVATHI: Your king is not a fool. He builds a state that answers to the crown, not to the old lines.

> REVATHI: That is good for facing Europeans. It is less good for those of us whose names are older than his.

**6.4** Close on Revathi. A shadow crosses her face, there and gone. For once she is not looking at him.

> REVATHI: My mother's cousin sits in Kottarakkara. Savitri. Of the Elayadathu line.

> REVATHI: She writes to me sometimes. About the old ways. About what is owed.

**6.5** Wide and still. The two of them on the mats with a space between them, the plate untouched, the banners motionless. Hold the silence a beat too long.

> CAPTION: I did not know the name. I did not know the house.

> CAPTION: The weight of what she was not saying settled between us like monsoon humidity.

## PAGE 7

Five panels. The question that lands like a dagger.

**7.1** Revathi, recovered, flat.

> REVATHI: Kottarakkara has not yet bent.

> REVATHI: Your king circles that house the way a dog circles a wounded bird.

**7.2** Nagoji leaning in a little. Revathi does not move.

> NAGOJI: You fear he will use men like me to cut your houses down.

> REVATHI: I do not fear. Fear is for people who have no knives.

**7.3** Revathi, measured, one hand resting on her knee.

> REVATHI: I see him giving land and commands to men whose ties to this coast are thin.

> REVATHI: Easier to shift such men when the time comes. Harder for them to say no.

**7.4** Tight on her face. She is smiling now, and the smile has an edge.

> REVATHI: Will you say no, Nagoji Sawant, when he points you at a house that will not bow as deeply as he likes?

> REVATHI: If he tells you my pepper fields shelter Dutch spies, will you burn them for him?

**7.5** Full width. Nagoji's face, unmoving. Behind him, bleeding into the panel: Padmini's women sorting pepper, the boys in the kalari pit, and furthest back, his family's fields near Nashik under a column of smoke, seen from a long way off.

> CAPTION: It was only a question. It landed like a thrown dagger.

*Page turn.*

## PAGE 8

Five panels. His answer, and her refusal to let him hide in it.

**8.1** Nagoji, slow, choosing each word.

> NAGOJI: I ride for the orders I am given. But I choose which king I allow to give them.

> NAGOJI: That choice is not made lightly.

**8.2** Revathi, immediate.

> REVATHI: You have already made it.

> REVATHI: You could have run north when the sea spat you out. Instead you stayed.

> REVATHI: Do not pretend you stand at a crossroads when your feet are already on the path.

**8.3** Nagoji, jaw tight, looking away from her for the first time.

> CAPTION: Her bluntness stung because it was true.

> NAGOJI: I stayed because he fights Europeans.

**8.4** Revathi looking toward the inner hall. Its doorway is dark. Low women's voices beyond it, drawn as sound only.

> REVATHI: So you chose him.

> REVATHI: Understand that you also chose to stand on the other side of some doors when they close.

**8.5** Wide. Revathi back on him. Balloons in the upper third; keep the two faces clear below.

> REVATHI: They groom you. Padmini warms your feet at her hearth so your loyalty tastes local.

> REVATHI: You will be offered many things. Land. Honour. Perhaps even a wife from some house that can make you feel rooted here.

*Page turn.*

## PAGE 9

Five panels. Three words, then she leaves him with an instruction.

**9.1** Tight. Revathi's bare shoulder lifting in a small shrug. Her eyes never leave him.

> REVATHI: Not from mine.

**9.2** Nagoji. Nothing moves in his face.

> CAPTION: The words were light. The tone was not.

> REVATHI (off): Men like you rarely have to ask. I state my position early.

**9.3** She rises, smoothing her sari. He is still seated, looking up at her.

> REVATHI: Go back to the Maharaja and tell him Velinadu smiled at you.

> REVATHI: Tell him also that we are still watching.

**9.4** At the threshold of the inner hall, half in its shade, she pauses and looks back over her shoulder.

> REVATHI: And Nagoji. When your horses trample that wet sand, they trample land that belongs to more than one king.

> REVATHI: Every hoofprint has a name beneath it. Learn those names.

**9.5** Narrow strip. The empty doorway. Shade. A single jasmine blossom on the stone sill.

## PAGE 10

Five panels. Padmini's verdict, his reckoning, and a summons.

**10.1** Padmini stepping back onto the verandah from the inner rooms, letting out a slow breath, walking stick in hand. Nagoji still on the mat.

> PADMINI: You did not disgrace yourself.

> NAGOJI: I am not sure she agrees.

**10.2** Padmini, looking toward the doorway where Revathi went.

> PADMINI: She does not have to. Her place in this story is not to approve.

> PADMINI: It is to remind men like you and the Maharaja that the land remembers longer than crowns do.

**10.3** Wide. The drums have started again deeper in the complex. Nagoji following Padmini across the courtyard toward the hall. Within the frame, a tight detail: his bare feet on the red earth, his boots left behind on the verandah step.

> CAPTION: In Goa, I had fought men who believed God and gunpowder gave them the right to rule.

> CAPTION: In Velinadu, I had met a woman who believed lineage and land gave her the right to refuse.

**10.4** Nagoji small in a big frame, a figure between the gate behind him and the hall ahead.

> CAPTION: Between those two certainties, my own place felt precarious.

> CAPTION: Balance is not found on flat, solid ground. It is found on the shifting deck, between one wave and the next, when you decide whether to fall or to ride.

**10.5** Night. Oil lamps. The outer steps of the hall. Through a lit doorway, Padmini talking quietly with house elders. A servant leaning in toward Nagoji.

> CAPTION: That night.

> SERVANT: The Velinadu lady asks if your feet remember her verandah.

*Page turn.*

## PAGE 11

Five panels. The night verandah. Quiet and close. Nothing is touched.

**11.1** Wide. The side verandah by lamplight, where he first saw her. Revathi standing, waiting, the coins at her throat dimmer now. Nagoji arriving at the edge of the light.

> REVATHI: You heard many words today. Tell me one thing you learned.

> NAGOJI: That treaties are nets. And that Velinadu does not let anyone else hold the rope.

> REVATHI: Not bad. And what did you learn about yourself?

**11.2** Nagoji, after a hesitation, looking out at the dark courtyard rather than at her.

> NAGOJI: That I am still a man the sea has not decided to keep.

> NAGOJI: And that I stand on land that can throw me off if I forget whose it is.

**11.3** Revathi's gaze dropping to his bared left forearm, the long white scar in the lamplight.

> REVATHI: That one? Sea or fort?

> NAGOJI: Dungeon. A Portuguese man with time and tools.

**11.4** Tight, lamplit. His forearm, the scar. Her hand lifted, stopping a finger's breadth from the mark. The gap between fingertip and skin is the subject of the panel.

> REVATHI: Travancore has its own tools.

> REVATHI: Do not give us reason to use them.

**11.5** Full width, bottom. Nagoji walking into the lit hall, where incense curls and old women sit in a circle mid-argument. Behind him on the dark verandah, Revathi, her hand dropped, already half turned away.

> REVATHI (off): Go. Padmini will think I am trying to steal her new toy.

> CAPTION: The question of where I would stand, when the king's wishes scraped against the houses of this coast, remained unanswered.

## Adaptation notes

- **Page count.** 11 pages against a target of 10. The chapter is 3,062 words and almost all of it is one conversation, so the pages go to Revathi (pages 3 to 9) and to the night verandah (page 11), which plants the relationship that pays off in chapter 20. The ride and arrival are compressed to two pages.
- **Ride compressed.** The ponds, shrines, fields, bullocks and children are folded into one landscape panel (1.2). Padmini's muted silk and sword are kept; the bible's walking stick is added, strapped to the saddle and then in hand on page 10.
- **Cut at the gate.** Padmini's "tongues still loose from sleep" line, to keep panel 2.1 light.
- **Cut from the conversation.** Ramayyan's "grove of palm leaves"; the exchange "You know what this place is? / Land of queens" (the queens idea is carried by Nagoji's line in 1.3); the second half of "I stayed because he fights Europeans" about Deccan chiefs squabbling over forts; the lines on Ramayyan marking his uses and the Maharaja testing his judgment; and Revathi's invitation to watch the ceremony. Her long speeches on the king, the land and the grooming are trimmed to their strongest sentences, which stay verbatim.
- **Cut from the close.** "Yet there was a strange comfort in that" and "Revathi Bayi had made her position clear", since the art of 9.1 to 9.5 already makes her position clear.
- **Kept verbatim** for later payoff: "Not from mine" (chapter 20, when she takes the tali), the Savitri and Kottarakkara shadow (chapter 27), "Every hoofprint has a name beneath it", "the land remembers longer than crowns do", and "Travancore has its own tools".
- **Callbacks.** 5.4 echoes the chapter 1 ledger (1.2), with the writing kept illegible because the art may carry no text. 5.3 bleeds in the wet-sand drill.
- **Continuity.** Nagoji is in the chapter 7 to 16 commander sheet, with a clean-shaven chin and thick moustache and no facial scar. He leaves his boots at the verandah step on page 2 and is barefoot from then on, which the novel's "bare feet" requires. The brand and the long white scar are both on his left forearm. Revathi wears no tali, because she is a widow until chapter 20. Padmini is in the bible's look, with her sari hitched for riding.
- **Staging.** Nothing is touched on page 11; the near-touch in 11.4 carries the scene. The burning Nashik fields in 7.5 are shown as distant smoke only.
- **Print.** Every panel for this chapter must reach at least 300 effective PPI at printed size, per the continuity bible. Upscale 2x or regenerate anything below that before placement.
