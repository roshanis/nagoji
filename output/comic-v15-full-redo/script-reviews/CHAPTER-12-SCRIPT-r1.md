# Horse of the Servant, graphic novel V15

## Chapter 12: The Shadow from Arcot

Source: `book1_horse_servant/book2_chapter12_the_shadow_from_arcot.md`, 2,021 words; 8 pages, 40 panels.

---

## PAGE 1

Five panels. The shadow is introduced as a force before it is a man. We never see his face.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. The Carnatic plain in the dry season, baked brown, with the wall of the Western Ghats rising behind it. A long column of cavalry winds toward a notch in the hills, its dust hanging in the air behind it. Bullock-drawn cannon at the tail. Hard white light. Distance only, no faces. The riders wear dust-coloured quilted coats and dark turbans; no green turbans, so they never read as Raza Khan's Madurai lancers of later chapters.

> CAPTION: Before the Dutch became the enemy we watched from our beaches, there was another shadow that fell across Travancore each dry season.

**1.2** Insert, tight. A rider's ringed hand holding a folded letter, tied with silk cord and closed by a heavy wax seal. The letter stays folded: no writing is visible, only the seal.

> CAPTION: It came with demands written in Persian and backed by Mughal seals that meant nothing in Delhi but everything to the men who carried them.

**1.3** Low angle. A commander on a fine horse at the top of a ridge, backlit by the sun so his face is lost in shadow. A high turban with a jewelled aigrette, a rich saddle cloth, a banner bearer a length behind him. He is looking south, away from us.

> CAPTION: Chanda Sahib.

> CAPTION: I had heard the name in Pune, spoken with the wary respect one gives to a competent rival.

**1.4** Over his shoulder, what he looks at: the green south opening out below the hills. Pepper vines climbing their host trees on terraced slopes, a river, and far off a port town with ships at anchor where the sea is a line of light.

> CAPTION: He had married into the Nawab of Arcot's family and climbed until he held Madurai in his fist.

> CAPTION: From there he looked south and saw what every ambitious man saw.

**1.5** Narrow strip, full width. Marthanda Varma in his hall, standing straight, spear grounded at his side, looking directly out of the panel. Court dress: cream and gold, peacock crest, gold necklaces, bare-chested. Unbowed.

> CAPTION: Pepper, ports, and a king who had not yet learned to bow.

## PAGE 2

Five panels. The temple steps at sunset. Ramayyan tells the history; Nagoji recognises it from the other side.

**2.1** Wide. Evening. The steps of a small laterite temple among coconut palms, the sun sinking behind the trunks. Ramayyan and Nagoji sit side by side on the steps, small in the frame. Ramayyan: thin, sharp fine features, receding hair, plain white cloth, a palm-leaf bundle and stylus beside him. Nagoji in the commander sheet: rust-red turban, cream tunic, broad red sash, cream trousers, boots, talwar laid across his knees; clean-shaven chin, thick moustache, gold ear stud.

> RAMAYYAN: He came first in thirty-six. Before you washed up on our shore.

> RAMAYYAN: We were weak, and Chanda Sahib knew it.

**2.2** Tight. Ramayyan's long fingers turning a small stone over and over.

> RAMAYYAN: His cavalry came through Shenkottai pass like water through a cracked dam.

**2.3** Memory image, bleeding in at the panel edges rather than framed as a clean flashback. A village of thatched houses burning at dusk, seen from a distance. Horsemen are silhouettes against the flames. A family hurries away along a bund with bundles on their heads. No bodies, no blood.

> RAMAYYAN (off): They burned villages. They took what they wanted.

> RAMAYYAN (off): They sat on the edges of our kingdom and waited for the Maharaja to come with gold.

**2.4** Back on the steps, two-shot. Nagoji turned toward him. Ramayyan looking straight ahead, unashamed.

> NAGOJI: And he came?

> RAMAYYAN: He had no choice. We paid. Tribute, they called it. Protection money is what it was.

> RAMAYYAN: Fifty thousand rupees the first time.

**2.5** Full width. Nagoji in profile against the last of the sunset. Behind him, bleeding in like a stain: a dry Deccan field, a line of Maratha horsemen under a saffron swallow-tailed pennant at its edge, and a farmer standing in the stubble with his hoe, watching them. Nagoji himself is not in the memory.

> CAPTION: I thought of the Maratha sardars I had known, riding into Mughal country each dry season to collect chauth.

> CAPTION: We called it our right. The Mughals called it extortion. The farmers caught in between called it survival.

## PAGE 3

Five panels. Tribute buys time. Walls buy something else. The page turns on Ramayyan's smile.

**3.1** Medium two-shot, the light going blue. Nagoji asking; Ramayyan still turning the stone.

> NAGOJI: How many times?

> RAMAYYAN: Every year since. Sometimes Chanda Sahib himself. Sometimes just the threat of him.

> RAMAYYAN: The Dutch watched all of it.

**3.2** Wide, almost silhouette. Ramayyan's arm following through as the stone arcs away into the gathering dark between the palms.

> RAMAYYAN: Every rupee we paid him was a rupee we could not spend on powder or walls.

**3.3** Ramayyan pointing toward the southeastern hills. The last light is a thin gold line along the ridge.

> RAMAYYAN: But we did build walls. At Aramboli. At the passes.

> RAMAYYAN: The Maharaja understood that tribute buys time, not safety.

**3.4** Wide, bleeding in from the ridgeline: the Aramboli lines across the neck of a pass. A deep ditch, a raw earthen rampart, sharpened stakes, musket loopholes, a timber gate. A few Nair sentries, tiny against the works. Dusk. Leave generous sky for the balloons.

> NAGOJI (off): I have seen them. From a distance.

> RAMAYYAN (off): You will see them closer.

**3.5** Narrow strip. Ramayyan's face in the last light, a thin smile.

> RAMAYYAN: Walls cost less than tribute, in the long run. And they do not ride home when the rains come.

## PAGE 4

Five panels. Nagoji learns the enemy at first hand, then keeps his own counsel.

**4.1** Wide action. Open scrub near Nagercoil, palmyra palms, red earth. Nagoji riding hard with Dhanaji at his shoulder (Deccan rider of Nagoji's generation, talwar drawn). Ahead of them, the enemy horsemen from 1.1 wheel away across the open ground toward the hills, faster, already out of reach. Dust. No one falls.

> CAPTION: Near Nagercoil I rode against his cavalry, with Dhanaji at my shoulder.

> CAPTION: We spoke of it afterward with the frustrated respect of men who had faced a worthy enemy.

**4.2** Afterward, late afternoon. Their horses blowing, heads down. Dhanaji sits on a fallen palmyra trunk wiping sweat from his eyes; Nagoji stands holding both bridles. Dhanaji gestures toward the distant hills.

> DHANAJI: Their horse were better than ours. Faster on open ground. They knew the passes, knew the water holes.

> DHANAJI: We did not fight them in the open. We let the land fight for us.

**4.3** High, wide view from the hills. A small walled town at the mouth of a pass, where the green slopes break into the flat brown plains beyond. A road runs through it and out the other side.

> CAPTION: He had demanded Shenkottai, where the passes met the plains. A dagger pointed at Travancore's belly.

> CAPTION: It changed hands more times than Ramayyan could count. Each time, we paid in gold and blood.

**4.4** Night. Nagoji alone on a rampart walk, looking northeast, his face lit by a single lamp. Bleeding in above him across the stars: a column of Maratha horse under saffron pennants, moving south.

> CAPTION: What I did not tell them was what I knew from my own people.

> CAPTION: Raghuji Bhonsle's men were moving south. And Chanda Sahib, for all his cavalry and his Persian letters, was overextended.

**4.5** Narrow strip, full width. Tight on Nagoji's eyes, fixed on the dark horizon.

> CAPTION: Men might think me a spy. Or worse, a lever to bargain with powers I no longer served.

> CAPTION: So I kept my silence and watched the horizon for news.

## PAGE 5

Five panels. The news arrives. Let the silence of the hall do the work.

**5.1** Wide. Dawn at the coastal camp: palm-thatched shelters, horse lines, the tiled roof of the war hall beyond. A lone rider comes in off the northeast road at a stumbling canter, his horse lathered white at chest and flank, trembling. Guards run to catch the bridle.

> CAPTION: Spring, 1741. The Dutch were tightening their grip on the coast.

**5.2** Insert, tight. The messenger's shaking hand holding out a single palm-leaf ola, bound with cord and sealed with a blob of wax. No writing visible.

> CAPTION: He had ridden through the night, through country that was still contested.

**5.3** Wide. The war hall: timber pillars, brass lamps on stands, a low table stacked with palm-leaf bundles. Marthanda Varma seated on a low dais, chiefs on either side. Ramayyan standing near a lamp, breaking the wax. Nagoji standing apart at the back, beside a pillar.

> *No text.*

**5.4** Tight. Ramayyan's hands on the leaf. His knuckles whiten; his fingers tighten on its edges. His face, at the top of the frame, has not changed at all.

> *No text.*

**5.5** Medium. Ramayyan lowering the leaf and looking up at the king. The whole hall still.

> RAMAYYAN: Chanda Sahib has fallen.

*Page turn.*

## PAGE 6

Five panels. The shadow is lifted by Nagoji's own people, and every face in the room remembers it.

**6.1** Varma leaning forward from his seat. Ramayyan stepping up to hand him the leaf.

> VARMA: Fallen? Dead?

> RAMAYYAN: Captured. The Marathas took Tiruchirappalli. Raghuji Bhonsle's men have him in chains.

**6.2** Wide, from the front of the hall looking back. Every chief's head turned over his shoulder toward Nagoji, who stands at the back by his pillar and does not move.

> CAPTION: They knew where I came from. They knew what the orange flag meant.

**6.3** Varma reading the leaf slowly, lips moving slightly. His expression gives nothing away.

> VARMA: The Peshwa's war has done what our tribute could not.

> VARMA: Chanda Sahib will not ride south this season. Perhaps not ever.

**6.4** A local chief in the foreground (an older Nair chieftain in white mundu and shoulder cloth, heavy gold earrings) muttering sideways. Varma in the background, not troubling to look at him.

> CHIEF: The Marathas will want payment for their service. They always do.

> VARMA: Let them want. They are far away. The Dutch are close.

> VARMA: I will worry about debts when the creditors can reach my door.

**6.5** Varma turning his head and looking straight down the hall at Nagoji. The chiefs between them lean out of the line of his gaze.

> VARMA: You knew this was coming, Sawant?

## PAGE 7

Five panels. Nagoji answers carefully, and is useful. The king ends the talk and turns the chapter toward the sea.

**7.1** Close on Nagoji, stepping forward from the pillar. Measured.

> CAPTION: I chose my words carefully.

> NAGOJI: I knew the Peshwa had interests in the Carnatic. I did not know when or how the blow would fall.

> NAGOJI: I have been here, Maharaja, not in Pune. My knowledge is old.

**7.2** Varma studying him for a long moment, the leaf lowered in his hand.

> VARMA: Old knowledge is still knowledge.

> VARMA: What do the Marathas do with a captured Nawab?

**7.3** Nagoji, sure of himself now, speaking to the king directly. Chiefs listening on either side.

> NAGOJI: They will hold him. He is worth more alive than dead.

> NAGOJI: The Marathas are patient. They will extract every pagoda they can before they let him go.

**7.4** Three-shot: Varma, Ramayyan beside the dais, Nagoji at the edge of frame.

> VARMA: And while they extract?

> NAGOJI: While they extract, he cannot ride south.

> RAMAYYAN: Then we have a window. The Marathas have given us a gift, whether they meant to or not.

**7.5** Wide, full width. Varma rising, spear in hand, the chiefs getting to their feet around him. He has already turned toward the open side of the hall and the sea beyond it.

> VARMA: Enough talk of Arcot. The Dutch are the enemy in front of us.

> VARMA: Let the Marathas deal with the shadow at our back. We have our own war to win.

## PAGE 8

Five panels. Night. The irony belongs to Nagoji alone. End facing the sand.

**8.1** Wide. Night in the camp, cookfires and low talk. Nagoji walks alone between the groups. One old soldier nods to him as he passes; beside that man another watches him go with narrowed eyes.

> CAPTION: Some looked at me with new respect, as if my people's victory was somehow mine.

> CAPTION: Others wondered what other secrets I kept behind my Maratha face.

**8.2** Nagoji on a low rise above the coast, seen from behind. The sea is black; the Dutch ships cannot be seen. Wind moves the tail of his turban.

> CAPTION: I had fled the Peshwa's justice. I had washed up on this foreign shore with nothing but the scars on my back and the skills in my hands.

**8.3** Vision, bleeding across the sky above the sea. Somewhere in the Carnatic, a captive of rank seated in a tent, his face in shadow, fine clothes soiled, iron at his wrists. Maratha officers count coin on a cloth before him. A saffron pennant hangs at the tent door.

> CAPTION: Now the very power I had escaped had done more to secure Travancore's eastern frontier than all my drills and all my riding.

> CAPTION: The world turns strangely.

**8.4** Close on Nagoji's face in the sea wind, campfire smoke drifting across it. Not triumphant. Certain.

> CAPTION: Tomorrow, or the day after, the Dutch would land at Colachel, thinking they faced a king still bleeding from two fronts.

> CAPTION: They were wrong.

**8.5** Full width, bottom. Nagoji has turned from the sea and is walking back toward the fires of the camp. Below the rise, the long pale beach runs away into the dark, empty, waiting.

> CAPTION: The shadow from Arcot had lifted. Now there was only the sea, and the enemy upon it, and the sand where we would meet them.

> CAPTION (separate, small): There was still work to do.

## Adaptation notes

- **Page count.** 8 pages, the top of the 6 to 8 range. Most of the chapter is backstory told in conversation, so four pages (1 to 4) carry roughly 1,100 words of history. The war hall, the chapter's only live scene, gets three pages (5 to 7), and the night reflection, which is its payoff, gets a full page (8).
- **Chanda Sahib never shows his face.** There is no continuity sheet for him, and a man in shadow reads better as "the shadow". He appears only as the backlit rider (1.3) and the shadowed captive (8.3).
- **Cut from the temple steps:** "Before the king had finished breaking the Pillamar" (the balloon was too long, and the Pillamar are covered in earlier chapters); the English Company loans (already in chapter 11); "Then they came back the next year and asked for more" (folded into "Every year since"); the Van Imhoff and earthworks speech; and "Ditches deep enough to break a horse's leg", because 3.4 draws the ditch.
- **Dhanaji.** The novel puts him at Nagoji's shoulder near Nagercoil and then has Nagoji ask him "How did you survive?", which cannot be asked of a man who fought in the same skirmish. That question is cut. Dhanaji keeps his own lines, and "We did not fight them in the open. We let the land fight for us" now answers his own complaint. "Rivers flood...", "Elephants cannot climb where goats can" and "And we paid" are cut: the land is drawn in 4.3, and the tribute beat is Ramayyan's.
- **Shenkottai.** Ramayyan's second dialogue (the pepper routes, "gave it up, then took it back") is compressed into two narration captions in 4.3, which keep "Each time, we paid in gold and blood."
- **Maratha politics.** "The Nizam was distracted" is cut. Raghuji Bhonsle is kept, because his name pays off in 6.1.
- **War hall.** Cut: "His family will pay... or the Nizam will pay"; "His generals will scatter. His army will dissolve"; and the exchange "The Dutch do not know it yet" / "They will know soon enough. Their spies are everywhere". The king's closing speech, "Old knowledge is still knowledge" and "creditors can reach my door" stay verbatim.
- **Night.** "One kingdom's loss becomes another kingdom's gain" and the thought about the Marathas planting flags are cut. The salt and smoke of the sea wind are drawn in 8.4 rather than captioned. "I turned back toward the camp" is drawn in 8.5, so only "There was still work to do" is lettered.
- **Visual continuity.** Nagoji wears the commander sheet (CONTINUITY, chapters 7 to 16) throughout. Chanda Sahib's riders wear no green turbans, so they are not confused with Raza Khan's lancers. The Maratha flag is a saffron swallow-tailed pennant, drawn only in memory and vision panels (2.5, 4.4, 8.3). The ola and the Persian letter are drawn sealed, because the art may contain no text.
- **Print.** The full-width landscape panels (1.1, 3.4, 4.3, 7.5, 8.5) are the most likely to fall short at trim width. Each must reach 300 PPI or more at its placed size; upscale 2x before placement, or regenerate, if it does not.
