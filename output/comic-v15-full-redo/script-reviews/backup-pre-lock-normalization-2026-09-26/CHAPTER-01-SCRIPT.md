# Horse of the Servant, graphic novel v8

## Chapter 1: Dungeons of Goa

**Pilot script. 8 pages, replacing the 3 pages in v7 (story pages 1 to 3).**

Source: `book1_horse_servant/book1_chapter01_dungeons_of_goa.md`, 1,952 words.

### Rules this script follows

1. **A caption may not describe what the panel shows.** Captions carry only what art cannot: his name, his rank, his interiority, elapsed time. If a caption can be deleted without losing information, it is deleted.
2. **Every page ends on a question**, not an announcement. No "NEXT:" labels.
3. **Verbatim where the novel is strong.** Marked lines are lifted from the manuscript unchanged.
4. **Duarte's hesitation is planted three times** on pages 6, 7 and 8, because it pays off in chapters 24 and 25.
5. No em dashes.

## PAGE 1

Five panels. Establish the cellar, the man, and the theft of his name.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. A curved cellar wall running with damp. Fifteen men chained in a row along it, ankles linked by a single iron bar, backs to the stone. Nagoji nearest us, three-quarter back view, hands wrapped in filthy cloth. A slit window high up gives the only light, and it is failing.

> CAPTION: The first thing they took was my name.

> CAPTION: Not with ceremony or proclamation. A shrug, and an entry in a book.

**1.2** Insert panel, tight. A clerk's hand, a quill, a ledger page. Visible in the ledger: a number, then the words *prisioneiro marata*. The ink is still wet.

> CAPTION: I was Nagoji Sawant. Twenty-five. Huzurat cavalry, household horse to Chimaji Appa, picked out by sardars who had watched me ride.

**1.3** Nagoji's face, close, lit from the high window. Eyes open. Not despairing. Watching.

> CAPTION: Here I was a number.

**1.4** His point of view, low angle along the floor: the gaoler's boots on the bottom stair. A ring of keys at a belt. A bucket.

> CAPTION: Down here the hours were kept by boots on stone, and by the bucket that carried away what we left behind.

**1.5** Narrow panel, bottom strip. Water falling from the ceiling into a black pool. Above, faintly, the shape of church bells rendered as sound, not object.

> CAPTION: Above us, the bells rang for evening prayer.

> CAPTION: Steady as monsoon rain on a Deccan roof.

## PAGE 2

Four panels. Duarte arrives. The chapter's second man is established by smell and detail, not exposition.

**2.1** Two figures descending the stair into the cellar. A thin priest in a black cassock, holding the hem clear of the slime, and behind him a broad gaoler whose belly strains his leather belt. Backlit. We do not see faces yet.

> CAPTION: Then Father Duarte came.

**2.2** Duarte in the foreground now, moving along the line of prisoners. Grey eyes, a scholar's stoop, ink on his fingers. Draw the calluses on his right hand clearly. He is looking at the ruined men and he is not enjoying it.

> CAPTION: A holy man built for argument, not battle.

> CAPTION: The calluses on his right hand said he had once held something heavier than a quill.

**2.3** João passing Nagoji. He taps Nagoji's shoulder with his boot, almost companionably. He is humming. Small musical notes at his lips.

> JOÃO: Este. This one is stubborn.

**2.4** Nagoji's face, flat and unreadable, looking at nothing.

> CAPTION: He smelled of garlic sausage and chapel incense.

> CAPTION: He was humming the hymn the bells had played.

> CAPTION (lower, separate box): Stubborn is better than broken. In any language.

---

## PAGE 3

Five panels. The exchange that establishes Nagoji is not cowed. Ends on the order.

**3.1** Duarte crouched to Nagoji's level. His eyes go to the bandaged hands. He winces. His fingers twitch inside his sleeves. Draw the wince. It matters.

> DUARTE: How long since the last questioning?

> JOÃO (off): Two days. He still insists he is only a horseman.

**3.2** Nagoji looking back at him, level.

> DUARTE: You understand our language, senhor marata?

**3.3** Tight on Nagoji's mouth, cracked and rust-dark.

> NAGOJI: Enough to know when I am being lied to, Father.

> NAGOJI: But you can speak your questions. I have heard most of them already.

**3.4** João laughing, delighted, hands on his belt.

> JOÃO: You see. He understands.

**3.5** Duarte standing, turning away. His decision is already made and he does not look at Nagoji while making it.

> DUARTE: Bring him.

---

## PAGE 4

Four panels. The walk. Withhold the room until the turn.

**4.1** Guards hauling Nagoji upright. His joints lock after weeks folded under him. His face shows nothing.

> CAPTION: My joints had forgotten the shape of standing.

**4.2** Being walked down a low vaulted corridor, Duarte ahead, João behind. Torchlight. Twenty paces of stone.

> CAPTION: A Maratha horseman does not whimper in front of an enemy. We bleed, we fall, we curse the gods and get back up.

**4.3** Tight on Nagoji's bare feet on wet stone, one step, the chain dragging.

> CAPTION: On the black soil of the Deccan that had been simple enough.

**4.4** The doorway ahead of him, dark, a red glow coming from inside it. We still do not see the room. His face in profile, lit by that glow.

> CAPTION: Here it took more effort.

*Page turn.*

---

## PAGE 5

Three panels. The room. Give it space. This is the reveal the turn was built for.

**5.1** Large, two thirds of the page. The interrogation chamber. Vaulted stone thick enough to swallow sound. Ropes hanging from an iron ring in the ceiling. A brazier glowing on the far side, heat shimmer rising. And a table, laid out neatly.

> CAPTION: The smell changed as we walked. Rotting straw and waste gave way to oil, sweat, and something sharp that stung the nose.

**5.2** Insert, tight on the table. Pliers. Hammers. Wooden wedges. Laid out with care, evenly spaced. Not one of them is a tool for working metal.

> CAPTION: Wine and old fear.

**5.3** Narrow strip. Nagoji being seated on a stool, wrists bound to its legs. João checking the knot. Duarte standing back, hands tucked into his sleeves, fingers worrying at the cloth.

> CAPTION: This was where the Portuguese forged information.

---

## PAGE 6

Five panels. The interrogation proper. Nagoji wins the exchange. Then Kanka.

**6.1** Duarte, formal now, reciting what they already have. He uses the name deliberately.

> DUARTE: Nagoji Sawant. The Viceroy's spies insist you were huzurat cavalry under Chimaji Appa himself.

> DUARTE: What we do not know is who ordered the attacks, how many men you had, and which forts you meant to strike next.

**6.2** Nagoji, bound to the stool, entirely unimpressed.

> NAGOJI: You know more than I do, then.

> NAGOJI: You have maps and books. You draw lines on paper and call them borders.

**6.3** Tight, his eyes.

> NAGOJI: My horse does not read.

**6.4** João in the background, not even looking up, picking a pair of iron tongs off the table. Casual. Conversational. This is the cruelty: it costs him nothing.

> JOÃO: Your horse is dead. We shot it when we brought you in.

> JOÃO: A pity. Fine animal.

**6.5** Full width, bottom. Nagoji's face. Nothing moves. Nothing at all. But behind him, bleeding into the panel edge like a stain rather than a clean flashback frame: a black mare's neck, a winter morning, a man's cheek against her mane.

> *No text. Hold the silence.*

---

## PAGE 7

Five panels. The question that the whole novel turns on. Then the nod.

**7.1** Nagoji, recovered, flat.

> NAGOJI: Then he died better than I will.

**7.2** Duarte studying him. Something has shifted. He steps closer and he lowers his voice, and the guards are no longer really in the room.

> DUARTE: Tell me this at least, Nagoji. Not for the Viceroy. For me.

> DUARTE: Do you believe you fight for God, for some higher good, or is it simply for plunder and the honour of your people?

**7.3** Small panel. Nagoji's eyes, unfocused. He is not in the room.

> CAPTION: For an instant the dungeon was gone, and I was on a ridge above the Godavari, watching the dust of a marching column.

**7.4** Back on his face, present again, answering.

> NAGOJI: I fight because I was born into a world where men who do not fight are trampled.

> NAGOJI: As for God, Father, I leave Him to the Brahmins and to you.

> NAGOJI: He seems to favour whoever has the better powder.

**7.5** Duarte's mouth. The smallest twitch. A shadow of something that might almost have been a smile, and behind it something worse, because he knows what he is about to do.

> JOÃO (off): You see. He gives you nothing. Let me loosen his tongue.

*Page turn.*

---

## PAGE 8

Six panels. The cost, the mercy, and the hook. Do not linger on gore; the novel's own instinct is that the mind floats free, so shoot it that way.

**8.1** Duarte. His jaw works, as if he is chewing words he cannot swallow. Hold on him for a full panel. This is the beat the reader must remember in chapter 24.

> *No text.*

**8.2** He inclines his head. A small, weary nod.

> *No text.*

**8.3** Wide. Shot from high in the vault, looking down. Small figures. The stool, the men around it, the brazier. Distance instead of detail.

> CAPTION: The first wedge went under what remained of my left thumbnail.

> CAPTION: After that, pain lost its degrees.

**8.4** Tight and strange: the ceiling. Wet stone, a rope, the iron ring. This is what he is looking at.

> CAPTION: Once you have felt your joints forced against the way they were meant to bend, the mind floats above the body and watches priests and gaolers ply their trade.

**8.5** João's hand reaching for the brazier. Duarte's hand shooting out, closing hard on his wrist. Both hands in frame. Nagoji, blurred, in the background.

> DUARTE: Not yet.

> DUARTE: The Viceroy needs a hand that can still hold a quill.

**8.6** Bottom strip, full width. Duarte's face, turned away from the stool, looking at nothing.

> DUARTE: Enough.

> JOÃO (off): He still holds back names.

> DUARTE: A dead prisoner cannot testify.

## PAGE 9 (optional ninth page)

If the budget allows nine rather than eight, this page carries the chapter out properly instead of compressing the ending onto page 8. Recommended.

Four panels.

**9.1** Back in the cellar. Nagoji chained in his place. Eyes closed. His hands are raw meat.

> CAPTION: I closed my eyes. Not in prayer. In calculation.

**9.2** A grid of four small insets inside the panel, what he is counting: guards at the door, a key ring, boots on the stair, the bucket.

> CAPTION: How many guards. How often they changed. Which men wore keys and which carried only cudgels. How many steps from the stair to the courtyard.

**9.3** A whisper passing along the chained line, man to man, drawn as leaning heads in the dark.

> PRISONER: They are gathering us for transport. South. By ship. For labour.

**9.4** Full width. Nagoji's eyes opening in the dark.

> CAPTION: Ships meant movement. Movement meant chances.

> CAPTION: They had taken my name. They had tried to break my body.

> CAPTION: They had forgotten the simplest lesson of the Deccan monsoon.

> CAPTION (separate, weighted): Storms do not ask permission.

## What changed, and why

| v7 (3 pages) | v8 (8 or 9 pages) |
| --- | --- |
| Torture is one caption, "the hours stretched and blurred" | Given a page, shot at distance and through the ceiling rather than in gore |
| "YOUR HORSE IS DEAD" as a floating balloon | Delivered by João while he picks up tongs, then a silent full-width reaction panel |
| Duarte's question asked, answer cut | Answer restored verbatim, including the powder line that pays off in chapter 28 |
| "THE PRIEST GAVE A SMALL, WEARY NOD" as caption | Two wordless panels, 8.1 and 8.2, so the reader sees the hesitation and remembers it |
| João is a name | Garlic sausage, chapel incense, humming the bells' hymn, cruelty that costs him nothing |
| "ENOUGH" unmotivated | Set up by the wrist grab at the brazier, so the mercy has a shape |
| Ends on a "NEXT:" label | Ends on "Storms do not ask permission", which is also the chapter's last line in the novel |

Captions cut from v7 as art duplication: "MY THROAT CLOSED", "I FORCED THE MEMORY DOWN", "THE PRIEST GAVE A SMALL, WEARY NOD", "THE HOURS STRETCHED AND BLURRED".

## Page budget implication

Chapter 1 is 1,952 words and needs 8 to 9 pages. The novel is 94,000 words. Scaling this density across all 28 chapters gives roughly 290 to 330 story pages, against the 152 in v7.

That is the real number. It is close to double, and it is what the source material actually needs. A 300 page graphic novel is a normal length for an adapted historical epic; 152 is a condensed edition.
