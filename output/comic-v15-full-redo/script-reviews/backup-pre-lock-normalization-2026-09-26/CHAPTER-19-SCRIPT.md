## Adaptation notes

- **Page count.** 12 pages against a target of 10, the top of the 8 to 12 range. The review restored lines the first draft had cut: the king's reason and reward, the guilt over Keshavrao, Padmini's "current", Revathi's barb and the scars line. It also split the overloaded panels. The extra pages go to the king's warning (pages 5 to 7), the night of the decision (pages 8 and 9) and the letter (pages 10 and 11). Page 6 ends on "prey" so that the threat opens page 7 as the turn. Page 12 has six panels because the naming, the Revathi hope and the closing lines each need their own frame.
- **Ramji and Bhalerao.** The novel sends the gold "to the house of Ramji Sawant" and addresses the letter "To Bhalerao Sawant". It never says who Ramji is, so the script does not either. The first draft's caption naming Ramji as the father was invented, and it is cut. The script names the younger brother Bhalerao when his handwriting first appears (1.5). He appears only as ink-stained hands (9.3), so he needs no face this chapter.
- **Cut for continuity.** "Held a dying chaver's wrist while a king drove steel through his throat" (the hands passage) refers to the temple fight in Chapter 22 (`book4_chapter22_command_of_the_marches.md`, lines 111 to 139), which has not happened yet. It is also violent, so it is cut. Flag this to the author as a forward reference in the novel itself. The Travancore half of that sentence, "They had trained Travancore cavalry in wet sand", is kept (9.2).
- **Cut, because it is exposition.** The opening paragraph on how news travels and the "sand, horse, fort, Dutch" summary. The brother's local news (a neighbour's death, a cousin's marriage, the new well) and his line about quarrels with Delhi and Mysore.
