A small troop of Nair household guards in white, swords and round shields, stays at the gate with their eyes on the road and the perimeter. Road dust still hangs in the last light. ## Adaptation notes

- **Page count.** 13 pages against a target of 11 (range 9 to 13). The first draft ran 12. Review restored a set of verbatim novel lines (the walls and foundation ending, the knife as a second mark, "I understood them now", the servants line, the Rani's mother's-line speech, the ministers question, "on a borrowed horse"). Rather than crowd those into existing panels, the night alone with the name and the knife now has its own page (10), and the Revathi sequence runs across three full pages (11 to 13), ending on the lamp going out. The other extra space still goes to Padmini's claim on the brand (the page turn into the kiss on 8.2).
- **Arrival compressed.** The guards, palanquins and household reaction fold into two panels. Nagoji's "No, Highness" to the ministers and grain-wagon question is cut; a glance at Padmini does its work.
- **Nalukettu description** becomes one establishing panel (3.4) with art notes. The comparison to Padmanabhapuram and the Mint Palace is cut: it is exposition that cannot be staged in the scene.
- **Flashback.** The King's story is drawn (4.4 to 5.2, with a small inset at 5.4) instead of being told over talking heads. The buttermilk is held back until Padmini says it (5.4), so the joke is not given away in 5.2. "She fed me when I was starving" is cut to keep 5.5 readable.
- **The caste scroll** is drawn as a rolled palm-leaf ola writ. "You are a Nair now, Sawant. Or you will be, once the water touches you" is cut; "water and oath" in the formal request carries it. The narrator's explanation of *tharavadu* inclusion is reduced to one caption about debts, feuds and ancestors (7.3).
- **Cellar visit, a novel inconsistency.** The King tells Valiyamma to show the boy the cellar, but Padmini stays with Nagoji and the King returns with the boy. The script has the King take the boy himself ("Good. Come."), which keeps Padmini with Nagoji for the naming. The King's line about a woman with a sharp tongue is said with his eyes on Padmini.
- **Cut for space:** "Keep it close. Let every man who sees it understand whose work you do"; "The name settled on me like a second skin"; "a *Thampuran* and trusted general of the king"; the "same soul, but the man has changed" exchange (the "man who washed up on this coast" exchange says the same thing more concretely); the "one night had already become two" paragraph, reduced to "We both knew it was a lie"; "In the cellar where kings had hidden... I was neither fugitive nor guest"; "She will not return until evening" (see Time of day); "One night," I agreed; "I know."; and "Some burdens are not chosen. Some names are not earned." The third sentence of that closing paragraph, on debts, is kept.
- **Intimacy** is implied by merging shadows on the cellar wall with both fully clothed (13.1) and an afterward framed at the shoulders under a cloth (13.2, 13.3), matching ch16. The unlacing of the blouse is not shown. The chapter closes on the lamp going out.
- **Costume change (staging choice, flag for the author).** Nagoji bares his head to receive the oil (8.4), so the man the King calls Ananthan Pillai ("Now you look like you belong", 9.4) is no longer in a Maratha turban. He stays bare-headed through the naming and the knife, and alone that night (10.1) gathers his hair into the knot for the first time, the folded turban at his feet. From page 11, three days later, he is in the full Ananthan Pillai look per CONTINUITY.md, with the conch knife at his waist.
- **Time of day.** Dusk into night for pages 1 to 10 (the lattice light on 9.2 is moonlight). Day in the rain for pages 11 and 12. "She will not return until evening" is cut so that "Tonight, let me hold it" and "One night" can stand verbatim: the scene runs on into the night, and Revathi leaves under moonlight (13.4).
- **No text in the art.** The brand is drawn as ridges without legible letters, and the ola writ, palm leaf and knife carry no readable script.
- **Speaker label.** The balloons after the naming keep the label NAGOJI for lettering consistency. In-world he is now addressed as Ananthan Pillai.
