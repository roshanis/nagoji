## Adaptation notes

- **Two-front exposition compressed to page 1.** Anchuthengu, the Madurai claim, borrowed muskets, English favours and Ramayyan's leaves full of debts are cut; the tired, stretched army is carried by the turn north in 1.1 and Varma's face in 1.5. "The first days were confusion" and the galloping messengers are folded into the same page. The allied houses keep their verb ("were quick to stand under that foreign umbrella"), so the reader knows whose side they are on.
- **The flashback (1.2 to 1.4)** runs in time order: the sealing (1739), the November shelling (1740), the months of occupation (1741). A shared sepia wash and ragged frames mark it as memory.
- **The Elayadathu deal** (1.2) names the house, Kottarakkara, the grievance and the cheap pepper, as the novel does. This pays off the Kottarakkara plant from chapters 9, 11 and 13 and sets up Ramayyan's "price of pepper" (4.5). The princess herself is not named in the novel here. Her face is held back for chapter 27, so only her hand is drawn.
- **Cavalry as gaolers** compressed to one panel (2.4). The Madurai and Maratha riders on the wider tracks, and cutting the inland water, are implied by the forager with water skins rather than stated.
- **"The Dutch were trapped in a box where the walls were fire and the floor was water"** is cut: Antony's closing line on page 3 lands the same idea in a character's voice. Ibrahim's line ("At sea, Ibrahim's divers and boatmen bled their supplies and nerves") is kept on 4.2, which holds the thread from chapter 13 to Ibrahim's arrival in chapter 15.
- **Tiruvattar** is an inset in 4.1 rather than a scene, to plant the temple for the return visit in chapter 16.
- **The tall figure pacing the perimeter** (4.3) is kept unnamed, as in the novel. The art gives only height, pale hair and a blue coat, and every other Dutch officer is drawn short and dark, so De Lannoy's walk-out on page 9 can land as recognition without the script asserting it.
- **"Then the world turned white"** becomes the wordless large panel 6.3. As a caption it would only describe the art.
- **"I was already riding when I saw it"** is dropped. In a caption it would contradict the seam-spotting panel that comes before the charge; the order on the page is see, then ride.
- **The charge is kept at full length** (pages 7 and 8) because it is the chapter's reason for existing and its title. Dhanaji's bloody sword and "the wet crunch of bodies falling" are removed under the no-gore rule; impact is staged through horses, dust and a buckling block of coats. The novel places only Maravar and Madurai horse in the grove but then has Deccan riders pour through the gap; the script puts all three, and Dhanaji, in the grove from 7.4.
- **The sergeant's shout** is wordless; the novel does not give his words.
- **Ramayyan's history** (Kollam, the Desinganadu campaigns, the supply and grain routes) is cut to the verbatim "A general who cannot count is a butcher. Ramayyan could count, and he could kill." The reader has already watched him lead the centre in 7.3.
- **Varma is present at the parley** (9.3, background) so that his "they cannot teach us how to build what they just lost" (10.3) reads as the king taking up the prisoner's words.
- **Verbatim lines kept:** Dhanaji's and Ramayyan's "They are stubborn," Antony's anchor and ocean lines, Ibrahim's divers, Ramayyan on merchants and the price of pepper, "I do not believe in luck," "A square that turns is no longer a square," De Lannoy's "I would rather build than rot," Varma's "Dead men cannot sign treaties" (which deliberately echoes De Lannoy), "I knew the silence to come would be more dangerous than the noise," and the chapter's last line.
