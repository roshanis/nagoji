## PAGE 1

Five panels. Open on the pyre, not the map. An old house loses its mistress before the last old house loses its throne. End on an image.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. The inner courtyard of Padmini Amma's estate at first light after the rains. The great jackfruit tree over its worn stone platform. Beneath the branches, a pyre of stacked mango wood. Mourners in white stand back at a distance. Leaning against the trunk, alone: Padmini's long walking stick.

> CAPTION: The fever that followed the last rains took Padmini Amma in three days.

> CAPTION: Quick and unsentimental, as she would have chosen.

**1.2** Four men bearing a white-shrouded body on a bamboo bier around a ring of old stones set into the courtyard floor. Nagoji at the front right shoulder: Kerala topknot, white mundu, shoulder cloth, no coat, no chain of office. A Hindu funeral priest (V13 temple priest face) walks ahead of the bier, lips moving.

> CAPTION: As her adopted son, some duties fell to me that no woman, however senior, could perform in the eyes of temple and custom.

**1.3** Tight. The priest holds out a burning brand. Nagoji's hand, old ridged nails, closing around it. Fire lights the underside of his moustache and his set jaw.

> CAPTION: The priests looked to me to answer where a son might have stood.

**1.4** Medium. Nandini, a small girl, both arms wrapped around a brass water pot so large it rests against her stomach. Beside her Revathi Bayi (tali, grey threading at the temples, in plain mourning white for the rites), one hand on the child's shoulder. Behind them the house elders, shoulder cloths knotted tight.

> CAPTION: Her heir was her sister's girl, Nandini.

> CAPTION: The elders asked Revathi and me to stand with the house until the child could carry the name herself.

**1.5** Full width, bottom. The pyre alight. Smoke climbs through the jackfruit leaves into a pale sky. Nagoji small beside the flames, the brand lowered. In the foreground, soft focus, the walking stick against the trunk.

> CAPTION: She had scolded kings in the shade of that tree.

## PAGE 2

Five panels. The caretaker's duty in one inset, then the war hall and the king's arithmetic. End on the fist.

**2.1** Inset strip, top. Some months later. The verandah of Padmini's house, a low writing desk. Nandini presses an inked thumb onto a document, tongue between her teeth. Beside her mark Nagoji (mundu, shoulder cloth, silver chain of office) signs with a reed pen. A tax collector waits on the step with his palm-leaf bundle.

> CAPTION: I did not inherit that estate. No man truly does here.

> CAPTION: My work was to keep the roof sound and the vines alive until she grew into her own temper.

**2.2** Wide. The war hall. A large painted map of the coast across a table, crowded with marks in four inks: red to the north, black to the northeast, brown to the east, small blue ships all along the sea. Travancore itself a narrow shape at the southern tip. Ramayyan Dalawa (thin, receding hair, plain white cloth) sits at the table's end among palm-leaf bundles, stylus in hand. Marthanda Varma (V13 face, cream and gold) stands over the map. Nagoji across from him in uniform: white European-cut coat, red waistcoat, brass buttons, topknot, silver chain.

> CAPTION: Mysore. The Nizam. Arcot.

> CAPTION: And all around the coast, the Europeans, with the patient hunger of men who thought in generations.

**2.3** Tight. Varma's finger tracing the border of his own kingdom, very small against the inks pressing on it.

> VARMA: We are small. If even two of them agreed to split us, we would last a season.

> VARMA: Perhaps two.

**2.4** Varma looking up across the table at Nagoji.

> VARMA: Do you know why I have spent twenty years taking every small kingdom on this coast?

> NAGOJI: To deny them footholds.

**2.5** Full width, bottom. Varma closes his hand into a fist and sets it on the centre of the map, knuckles over the crowded inks. His face level. Not greed. Calculation.

> VARMA: Partly. A hundred small kingdoms are a hundred small throats to cut.

> VARMA: Divided, we are meat. United, we are at least a problem.

## PAGE 3

Five panels. A name falls into the room. Compress the siege politics to one letter and the king's two bad choices. End on Ramayyan.

**3.1** Ramayyan setting his stylus down across the palm leaf, deliberately. Varma and Nagoji turn toward him.

> RAMAYYAN: Kottarakkara.

> CAPTION: The name fell into the room like a stone into water.

**3.2** Varma over the map, his finger now resting on a hill town ringed in Ramayyan's ink.

> VARMA: The last of the old houses that still believes it can stand alone.

> CAPTION: I had known this day was coming.

**3.3** Insert, tight. A letter laid on the map, its wax seal broken clean in two. Varma's hand holds it flat.

> CAPTION: The raja fled to Kochi. His kinswoman stayed, to claim the chair he left behind.

> CAPTION: Savitri. A name that meant nothing to me when I first washed up on this coast, and everything now.

**3.4** Varma turned half away from the table, the letter lowered. Nagoji watching him. Small inset in the lower corner: Velinadu at night, Revathi by a lamp, a folded letter unopened in her lap, looking at nothing.

> VARMA: If I give her the throne, every house I have absorbed will demand the same.

> NAGOJI: And if you refuse?

> VARMA: Then the Dutch will use her. And Velinadu...

> CAPTION (on the inset): Velinadu was Revathi. And Revathi was blood kin to Savitri.

**3.5** Full width, bottom. Ramayyan, stylus back in his fingers, eyes on the leaf and not on the king.

> VARMA (off): Did you know it would be her?

> RAMAYYAN: I knew it would be someone like her. Sharp. Legitimate. Connected.

> RAMAYYAN: The worst kind of enemy: one who is not wrong.

## PAGE 4

Five panels. Siege without walls, then the old palace. Savitri takes the measure of the man sent to measure her. End on her charge.

**4.1** Wide, full page width. Dusk outside Kottarakkara. A road blocked with felled palms. A canal dammed with packed earth. Merchant carts turned back by Travancore sentries while supply carts roll the other way toward the siege lines. Nagoji and Eustachius De Lannoy (tall, pale hair, white Travancore coat) watch from a bund. On the hill beyond, the tiled roofs of an old palace.

> LANNOY: This is not war. This is siege without walls.

> NAGOJI: Walls can be rebuilt. What we are breaking cannot.

**4.2** Interior. A pillared hall of dark carved wood, its great doors braced shut with timber beams. Retainers with spears along the walls. At the far end, Savitri of Kottarakkara in white mourning clothes: older, lines of care around her eyes and mouth, straight-backed. Nagoji has come in alone, sword sheathed.

> SAVITRI: The Maratha rider. Marthanda Varma's foreign sword.

**4.3** Two-shot, a few paces apart.

> NAGOJI: I am not here to fight you.

> SAVITRI: No. You are here to measure me.

> SAVITRI: To see if I am the kind of threat that can be bought, or frightened, or simply waited out.

**4.4** Nagoji level. Savitri smiling, and there is no warmth in it.

> NAGOJI: And which are you?

> SAVITRI: I am the kind that remembers.

**4.5** Full width, bottom. Savitri close, her face hard in lamplight. Behind her, bleeding into the panel edge like a stain rather than a clean flashback frame: a shut door, a narrow barred window, a man's open hand flat against the wood.

> SAVITRI: My kinsman died in a room he could not leave, asking for air, asking for home.

> SAVITRI: Your king calls that statecraft. I call it murder dressed in manners.

## PAGE 5

Five panels. The argument over the old law. Give Savitri the floor. End on her best line.

**5.1** Nagoji holding his ground, hands at his sides.

> NAGOJI: He invited our enemies into his court.

> SAVITRI: He defended his house. As his mothers did before him. As I will.

**5.2** She steps in. Tight two-shot, her eyes on his.

> SAVITRI: Do you know why your king fears the matrilineal law?

> SAVITRI: Not because it is old. Because it gives women like me standing.

**5.3** Savitri turned toward the doorway of the house shrine, where a tall brass lamp burns with a steady flame. She does not look at it. She knows it is there.

> SAVITRI: Your king builds a state where power flows downward from one throne.

> SAVITRI: We come from a world where it flows sideways. Through sisters, through daughters, through the women who remember.

**5.4** Nagoji, quiet, the lamp's glow on the brass buttons of his coat.

> NAGOJI: That world is ending.

> SAVITRI (off): Perhaps. Or perhaps it will outlast his walls.

**5.5** Full width, bottom. Savitri facing him fully, the lamp behind her head.

> SAVITRI: Empires fall. Women remain.

## PAGE 6

Five panels. Her terms, then the field. The battle is only what Nagoji sees: her, and the arithmetic. End on the arithmetic.

**6.1** Savitri at a window, her back to Nagoji, looking out over the ring of siege fires on the plain.

> SAVITRI: Tell him I will not disappear quietly. Tell him the Dutch have already made offers.

> NAGOJI: You would sell your coast to the very powers you claim to oppose?

**6.2** Her profile against the fires.

> SAVITRI: I would survive.

> SAVITRI: We tend our gardens long after their forts have fallen.

**6.3** Wide, full page width. A field of trampled paddy under drifting smoke, a small roadside shrine half hidden in the palms. Across it Savitri sits a horse, her white mourning clothes grey with dust and smoke, a sword at her side. Behind her a mixed force: men with Dutch muskets, men with local spears, a faded old-house banner. Travancore lines in the foreground, Nagoji's shoulder at the panel edge. No blood.

> CAPTION: When the palace could no longer hold, she went to the Dutch. They armed her cause.

> CAPTION: This was house land, temple land, ground where every tree held a memory.

**6.4** Tight, across the field. Savitri's eyes finding his through the dust.

> CAPTION: She did not look like a villain. She looked like a woman who had weighed her choices and found them all bitter.

**6.5** Full width, bottom. Evening. The field emptied. Spears in the mud, the old-house banner trodden down, an abandoned musket. Far off on the shore, small figures of Dutch factors hurrying into boats.

> CAPTION: We won. Not because we were right, but because we had more.

> CAPTION: More guns. More discipline. More of the terrible arithmetic that decides such things.

## PAGE 8

Five panels. Sentence, curse, answer, departure. End on the chapter's last line.

**8.1** Varma turned away from her, then back, the king again.

> VARMA: I will not kill you. You will go to Kochi, to the Dutch who promised you so much and delivered so little.

> VARMA: If you set foot in Travancore again, I will treat you as I would any invader.

**8.2** Tight on Savitri. Her jaw tightens.

> SAVITRI: Exile.

> VARMA (off): Survival. More than your kinsman received.

**8.3** Savitri leaning forward against her bound hands, fierce.

> SAVITRI: You are building something. I see it.

> SAVITRI: But every house you broke, every woman you sent into exile, we become stories. And stories outlast walls.

> SAVITRI: They will remember me.

**8.4** Varma unmoved, Savitri already being turned toward the flap by a guard's hand on her arm.

> VARMA: Let them. I build for the living, not the remembered.

> SAVITRI: That is why you will never understand us.

**8.5** Full width, bottom. Evening. Guards lead Savitri down a path toward the coast, where a Dutch ship waits at anchor. Smoke from Kottarakkara's burned fields drifts across the sky and mixes with the sea haze. Nagoji in the foreground, back to us, watching. Compose it to echo 1.5: the same rising smoke, a different fire.

> CAPTION: The last of the old houses had fallen.

> CAPTION (separate, weighted): Now came the work of making sure they could never rise again.

## Adaptation notes

- **Padmini's death moved to the opening.** The novel tells it in retrospect after the map scene ("By then Padmini Amma was gone"). Opening on the pyre is chronologically true and bookends the chapter in smoke: her pyre on page 1, Kottarakkara's fields on page 8. Her death gets a full page because it is a principal's last appearance.
- **Funeral rites shown, not captioned.** "It was my hand that carried the fire" and "It was my shoulder that helped bear her body around the inner stones" become panels 1.2 and 1.3; the captions carry only custom and meaning. "She had scolded kings in the shade of that tree" replaces the novel's longer jackfruit sentence, which would otherwise describe the panel.
- **Revathi wears plain mourning white at the funeral** in place of her usual indigo, a situational variant of the continuity look. Everything else about her (tali, grey threading) follows CONTINUITY.md.
- **The caretaker passage** (tax men, Marakkars haggling, temple stewards and lamp oil) is compressed to one inset, 2.1, keeping its two strongest sentences.
- **The map exposition** is one wide panel. The descriptions of each power (Mysore's hill army, the Nizam swallowing kingdoms "for breakfast", Arcot selling its loyalty) are cut to their names; only "the patient hunger of men who thought in generations" is kept. Nagoji's longer answer is trimmed to its first sentence.
- **Ramayyan and Nagoji on the raja** ("They will eat him", "pride is a powerful drug", "Including that one") are cut. Savitri's double correspondence is carried by the Revathi inset in 3.4, and the novel's twice-told flight of the raja is merged into one caption in 3.3.
- **The precedent speech** (Kollamkara remnants, Kayamkulam kin, Thekkumkur nephews) is reduced to one line. "He did not finish. He did not need to." is carried by Varma's broken-off balloon rather than a caption.
- **Lannoy's "siege without walls"** is moved from before the letter to the approach to the palace (4.1), so it is spoken inside the siege it describes. The blocked roads and diverted canals are shown in the art instead of captioned.
- **Savitri's interview is kept nearly whole** across pages 4 to 6, because it is the chapter's argument. Light trims only: "It is not because it is old. It is because..." becomes "Not because it is old. Because...", and her threat about Mysore rewarding the houses that resisted is cut.
- **The battle is three panels** (6.3 to 6.5): her in the field, the meeting of eyes, the aftermath. "The fighting was ugly" is honoured by staging and aftermath only, with no gore. The Dutch factors' flight is shown, not told.
- **The tent scene gets two full pages** as the chapter's climax. The novel's "For a long moment, neither spoke" becomes the wordless panel 7.3, and Varma's memory of her laugh gets a bleed image in 7.4 using the chapter 1 technique.
- **Cut for space:** Savitri's "Perhaps it will outlast the Dutch. Perhaps your name will be spoken with respect a hundred years from now", "When your forts are ruins and your laws are dust", and Nagoji's "I watched until she was gone from sight, this woman who had been willing to burn everything for the right to remain." The final panel's staging of Nagoji watching carries that last beat.
- **Verbatim lines kept:** "We are small", "Perhaps two", Varma's twenty-years question, "To deny them footholds", "Divided, we are meat. United, we are at least a problem", "The name fell into the room like a stone into water", "The last of the old houses that still believes it can stand alone", "I had known this day was coming", "Sharp. Legitimate. Connected. The worst kind of enemy: one who is not wrong", Lannoy's siege line and Nagoji's reply, all of Savitri's interview except the trims noted above, "She did not look like a villain", "the terrible arithmetic", the whole tent exchange from "You could have run" through "That is why you will never understand us", and the chapter's last two lines.
- **Page count** is 8, on target. Pages were spent on Savitri (four and a half pages) and saved by folding the map scene, caretaker duties and siege politics into pages 2 and 3.
