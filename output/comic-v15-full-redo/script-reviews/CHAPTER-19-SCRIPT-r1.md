## PAGE 1

Five panels. The north reaches the pepper coast in a cloth bundle. The page ends on his brother's handwriting.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. Padmini Amma's estate at midday in the rains. Beyond the red-tiled roofs and coconut palms, a bank of heavy cloud is building in the north. In the inner courtyard, Nagoji sits on the worn stone platform under the big jackfruit tree. He dresses as Ananthan Pillai now: Kerala topknot, no turban, white mundu and shoulder cloth, the ivory-handled knife with the conch mark at his waist, the silver chain of office on his chest. Clean-shaven chin, thick moustache, gold ear stud. Beyond the low wall, boys spar in the kalari pit.

> CAPTION: News from the Deccan arrived the way monsoon storms do.

> CAPTION: Not as a single, clear front, but as a series of clouds that thickened until you realised the sky had changed.

**1.2** Padmini, walking stick in one hand, tossing a small cloth-wrapped bundle into his lap with the other. In her fifties, thick black hair coiled at the nape, simple gold, cotton sari hitched for walking. Her face is softer than usual.

> PADMINI: From your hills, my son.

**1.3** Padmini glancing back over her shoulder at the kitchen steps. A thin, sun-blackened Deccan village boy squats there with a coarse black wool blanket over one shoulder and cracked, dusty bare feet, shovelling rice from a leaf as fast as his hand can move.

> PADMINI: The boy who brought it walked like a man used to stones, not sand.

> PADMINI: He eats too fast. That is how I know he comes from a place where grain is a worry.

**1.4** Close on Nagoji's hands unwrapping the cloth. Red-brown dust sifts out of the folds onto his white mundu. The fingernails have grown back ridged and discoloured. The fingers are not quite steady.

> CAPTION: For a long time after Goa and the storm, I had trained myself not to look north.

> CAPTION: Then a letter arrived with my village's dust still clinging to it.

**1.5** Insert. A folded sheet of paper, not palm leaf, opening in his hands. Lines of dense handwriting drawn as ink marks only, nothing readable.

> CAPTION: My name, in a hand I recognised even before I read the words.

> CAPTION: My younger brother Bhalerao's, grown more angular since I had last seen it.

## PAGE 4

Four panels. Padmini reads him before he speaks. Then the summons.

**4.1** Wide. The shade has moved across the courtyard. Nagoji tucks the folded letter into the waist of his mundu. The estate goes on around him: women calling across the yard, boys in the kalari, a servant with a brass pot. Padmini stands a respectful distance off, leaning on her stick.

> CAPTION: Deccan names wrapped themselves around Travancore sounds until it felt as if two different songs were playing at once.

> PADMINI: Bad news?

> NAGOJI: Mixed. Which is the only kind that travels far.

**4.2** Two-shot. Padmini has come close and looks down at him, stick planted.

> PADMINI: Your people live.

> NAGOJI: Yes. For now.

> PADMINI: And the men who rule your hills?

> NAGOJI: They grow sharper. And more divided.

**4.3** Padmini alone, nodding once.

> PADMINI: Sharp and divided. Dangerous combination.

> CAPTION: Her words should have steadied me.

**4.4** Dusk at the estate gate. A court runner in a plain white mundu, sweat on his chest, pulls up breathing hard. Nagoji is rising from the stone platform behind him.

> CAPTION: Instead, I heard Ibrahim's voice. The wind blows in circles, and names travel.

> RUNNER: The Maharaja wants you. Bring the letter.

## PAGE 5

Five panels. A small room, not the war hall. The king is easy with him until he is not.

**5.1** Wide. A small lamp-lit room off the war hall. Shelves of palm-leaf bundles, a few cloth-wrapped European volumes that were once Portuguese property, rolled maps. Marthanda Varma (V13 face, cream and gold, gold necklaces, bare-chested) sits on a low platform holding the letter. Nagoji sits on a mat below him. No guards in frame.

**5.2** Varma, a faint smile, already fading.

> VARMA: The Maratha confederacy is a storm with many centres. What interests me is this.

**5.3** Insert. The king's forefinger tapping one line near the foot of the page. Ink marks only.

> VARMA: Do you think they will try?

**5.4** Nagoji, eyes lowered, working it through.

> NAGOJI: If they think you are weak, yes.

> NAGOJI: If they think you are useful, they will keep you as you are. They have many enemies closer to home.

**5.5** Varma leaning forward. The lamp throws his shadow across the mat and over Nagoji.

> VARMA: And you?

> VARMA: If they sent a letter with your brother, ordering you back under their banner, what would you do?

## PAGE 6

Five panels. His answer, and the king's warning.

**6.1** Nagoji from a low angle, the lamp between them. Bleeding in at the panel edges like stains: his father bent over a plough; his mother's turmeric-stained hands; his brother by lamplight with his tongue between his teeth, writing; a Deccan fort wall with guns in its embrasures.

> CAPTION: The words landed like weights on the mat between us.

**6.2** Nagoji meets the king's eyes. Measured.

> NAGOJI: I would read the letter. I would weigh it.

> NAGOJI: I would remember Goa. I would remember the storm. I would remember Colachel. Then I would choose.

**6.3** Two-shot across the lamp.

> VARMA: You do not say which way you would choose.

> NAGOJI: Because I am not arrogant enough to think I know what the world will look like when that letter arrives.

> NAGOJI: If it arrives.

**6.4** Varma sets the letter down beside him on the platform, studying Nagoji.

> VARMA: Honest. I prefer honest answers to pious lies.

> VARMA: Understand this, Ananthan. You are useful to me because you are Maratha *and* because you are Padmini's son.

**6.5** Tight on the king's face, lit from below, his gaze sharpened.

> VARMA: You can make them fear and respect this kingdom, or you can make them see it as prey.

> VARMA: If you ever choose the second, we will stand on opposite sides of a very narrow river.

## PAGE 7

Five panels. Dismissed, but watched. Two women tell him the same thing in different voices.

**7.1** Wide. Nagoji walking away down a dark palace corridor. The lamp in the doorway behind him throws his shadow far ahead along the floor. The king, small in the lit doorway, lifts a hand in a dismissive flick.

> VARMA: For now, you stay. The north will have to wait its turn.

> CAPTION: The shadows of the Deccan had lengthened into Travancore's corridors.

**7.2** Day, some days later. A fort yard where men load bullock carts with supplies for a northern fort. Two officers in the foreground look after Nagoji as he passes, weighing him. Padmini walks beside him, watching the carts.

> CAPTION: Not mistrust exactly. More calculation.

> PADMINI: You cannot stand with one foot in each river forever.

**7.3** Two-shot, Padmini and Nagoji, the carts rolling out behind them.

> NAGOJI: What if I cling to a rock between them?

> PADMINI: Then you drown slower. But you still drown.

**7.4** Evening. The verandah of the Velinadu house, carved wooden pillars, lamps just lit. Revathi (indigo sari with gold, jasmine in her braid, flat-coin necklace, gold armlets, sandalwood line at the hairline, no tali) stands facing him, arms folded.

> REVATHI: Your hills call you. Your king here calls you. Neither will mourn very long if they must cut you loose.

> NAGOJI: You have a cruel tongue.

> REVATHI: No. I have an impatient one.

**7.5** Revathi leaning back against a carved pillar, a playful glint in her eyes, glancing down at the silver chain on his chest.

> REVATHI: Besides, do not let that silver chain weigh you down with too much hope.

> REVATHI: I am a Princess of Velinadu. We do not marry commoners, Nagoji. Even ones who think they have found a root.

> CAPTION: It was said in jest, but the wall was there, invisible and ancient.

## Adaptation notes

- **Page count.** 11 pages against a target of 10, inside the 8 to 12 range. The extra page goes to the renaming: page 10 ends on Padmini's "Then who stands before me?" and page 11 gives the answer a page of its own. The king's loyalty test (pages 5 and 6) and the letter to his brother (page 9) get full pages because that is where the drama is. The letter's arrival fits into one page and the letter itself into two.
- **Ramji and Bhalerao.** The novel sends the gold "to the house of Ramji Sawant" and addresses the letter "To Bhalerao Sawant". The script names the younger brother Bhalerao when his handwriting first appears (1.5). His ink-stained hands appear in 6.1 and 8.4. Then 9.3 states it outright: the gold is for his father, Ramji, and the words are for his younger brother, Bhalerao, the one who wrote to him. The family house carries the father's name, and the letter goes to the brother who reads and writes.
- **Cut for continuity.** "Held a dying chaver's wrist while a king drove steel through his throat" (the hands passage) refers to the temple fight in Chapter 22 (`book4_chapter22_command_of_the_marches.md`, around lines 111 to 139), which has not happened yet. It is also violent, so it is cut. Flag this to the author as a forward reference in the novel itself.
- **Cut, because it is exposition.** The opening paragraph on how news travels (letters, caravan guards, men who walked too far) and the "sand, horse, fort, Dutch" summary. The brother's local news (a neighbour's death, a cousin's marriage, the new well) and his line about quarrels with Delhi and Mysore. The runner is not named as Ramayyan's man. "When I left the room, the air outside felt thicker." The asset and risk paragraph, which 7.2 now carries as a look plus "Not mistrust exactly. More calculation." "Do not tell me otherwise." "The Travancore soldier had finally arrived," because "two coasts in his chest" makes the same point more strongly.
- **Cut, for space.** The meaning of the name (Ananthan, the endless one, the serpent who holds up worlds; Pillai as the mark of Padmini's son). It is a good line and could come back in Chapter 20 or 21 if a panel has room. Revathi's "You are flattered to be wanted by both." The fuller description of Revathi's letter (levies, Velinadu rights, the king's new walls) is folded into one caption.
- **Trimmed, meaning kept.** The king's "If they called you back, if they sent a letter with your brother, ordering you to return to serve under their banner again" becomes two balloons (5.5). "The name felt strange on my tongue, new and stiff like unconquered leather" becomes "New and stiff like unconquered leather", because the balloon in 11.3 has just spoken the name. The line about the currents is split across 11.1 and 11.2. Everything else in quotation is verbatim, including "Sharp and divided. Dangerous combination", "a very narrow river", "Then you drown slower", the whole letter to Bhalerao, "It was a lie. And it was the truest thing I had ever written" and the release "the way you release a horse too old to ride".
- **Lettering conventions.** Letter text is lettered in paper-textured boxes tagged `CAPTION (letter, ...)`, kept distinct from Nagoji's narration boxes. Bhalerao's letter is in one hand (pages 2 and 3) and Nagoji's in another (page 9). The Deccan scenes in Bhalerao's letter are drawn in a warm, dry palette with a torn-paper edge. Memories of the storm and the family bleed in as stains, the technique from Chapter 1, panel 6.5. No text appears in the art: the letters and the hundi show ink marks only.
- **Callbacks.** "The sea had kept him" and the snapped rope pay off Chapter 3, panels 9 and 10. The horse in 11.2 continues the horse motif from Chapters 1 and 3. Ibrahim's "the wind blows in circles" in 4.4 needs Chapter 18 to keep that line. Revathi's letter in 11.5 sets up Chapter 20, "Guest in Velinadu".
- **Continuity.** Nagoji wears the Ananthan Pillai look for chapters 17 to 20: Kerala topknot, no turban, white mundu and shoulder cloth, the ivory-handled knife with the conch mark, and the silver chain of office. Clean-shaven chin, thick moustache, ridged and discoloured nails. Revathi is still a widow, so she wears no tali until Chapter 20. Keshavrao's drowning (3.3, 8.3) shows no blood. The parents, Bhalerao and the Gujarati sowcar are new faces that CONTINUITY.md does not yet define. Suggest adding Bhalerao (younger than Nagoji, with a family likeness and ink-stained fingers), Ramji and the mother (Deccan farmers, aged and bent), and the sowcar (a one-chapter figure in a red pagdi and white angarkha) before these pages are generated. The Deccan boy's black wool blanket and the Pune sardars' pagdis and angarkhas are period Maratha dress.
