# Horse of the Servant, graphic novel V15

## Chapter 3: The Choice in the Storm

Source: `book1_horse_servant/book1_chapter03_choice_in_the_storm.md`, 2,845 words, adapted as 10 pages (target 9, range 7 to 11) and 47 panels.

---

## PAGE 1

Five panels. The storm reaches them through the hull before anyone names it. The page ends with the musket in the air.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. The hold in near darkness. Plank shelves run along the hull, split into pens by upright boards, with four or five men in each. Every man wears a neck collar, wrist manacles and ankle irons, and the collars are chained to iron rings bolted into the beams overhead. Tilt the whole image hard. Bodies slide against one another and chains jerk taut. Nagoji is in the foreground pen, awake, one bandaged hand flat on the planks as if he were reading them. Clean-shaven chin, thick moustache, long loose hair, torn ochre tunic.

> CAPTION: The first warning was not the shout of a sailor or the crack of thunder.

> CAPTION: It was the change in the way the ship moved.

**1.2** Close two-shot, Nagoji and Keshavrao side by side in the pen, their faces a hand's width apart. Keshavrao is young, with narrow shoulders and black hair hacked short, and his eyes are turned up to the deck. Nagoji listens with his eyes half closed.

> KESHAVRAO: Storm.

> CAPTION: Ships speak when they are in trouble.

**1.3** Low angle up at the underside of the deck. Grey light cuts through the seams and water drips from them. Boot shadows cross the gaps at a run. Jagged, overlapping balloons come from above and off panel.

> SAILOR (off, above): Reef the sails! Haul, haul!

> SAILOR (off, above): Tie that down! Move, you son of a dog!

**1.4** The hatch is flung open onto a square of storm light, and a sheet of rain pours through before a canvas awning is dragged across it. A Portuguese guard braces himself at the top of the ladder, one hand on the frame and a flintlock musket in the other. He wears a soaked shirt and a buff leather jerkin, not a red coat and not a blue one.

> GUARD: Quiet down there. No shouting. No prayers.

> GUARD: Try for the deck and we put a ball in your gut and throw you over.

**1.5** Same angle, a heartbeat later. The ship lurches. The guard's foot slips off the frame, his arm flies wide and he drops out of the square of light. The musket has left his hand and turns end over end as it falls into the hold.

> CAPTION: It is easy to make threats on solid ground.

## PAGE 2

Four panels. The musket. Plant it hard, because it pays off on page 6.

**2.1** Large. The musket in the foreground, spinning to a stop against a beam near the foot of the ladder, a few yards from Nagoji's pen. Behind it every chained head in the hold has turned to look at it. A lull in the roll leaves the chains hanging slack. Nobody moves.

> CAPTION: Muskets are like horses. They draw every eye in a space.

> CAPTION: Even men who have never fired one recognise the shape of power.

**2.2** The hatch above. A second guard's head and shoulders are silhouetted there as he shouts back toward the deck. He never looks down.

> GUARD 2: Leave it! Get back to work.

> GUARD 2: The pigs below are not our problem if the mast goes.

**2.3** Close on Nagoji and Keshavrao. Keshavrao's eyes are on the musket and Nagoji's are on the hatch. Seawater is starting to thread across the boards past their feet.

> KESHAVRAO: Sahib, if the ship breaks...

> NAGOJI: If the ship breaks, no musket will save them.

**2.4** Narrow strip at the bottom, at floor level. The musket lies against the beam with a thin current curling around its stock, and the ladder rises beyond it into grey light.

> CAPTION: Too far to reach. Too close to ignore.

## PAGE 3

Five panels. The first real wave, and the plan. Nagoji takes command without any rank.

**3.1** Wide, shot from a distance rather than in detail. The wave hits broadside. The hold is thrown sideways, men are almost weightless for a heartbeat, the chains snap taut and cold water surges across the floor to the knee.

> CAPTION: The storm did not descend at once. It built in layers.

**3.2** Nagoji shouts down the line with both forearms hooked over the timber above his head, taking the strain off his collar. Two or three men nearest him copy the grip.

> NAGOJI (in Marathi): Hold to the beams! Hook your arms over the wood.

> NAGOJI (in Marathi): Do not let the chain drag you.

**3.3** The ship rolls and Keshavrao loses his grip. Nagoji's bandaged hand clamps onto his forearm, and the iron between their wrists swings.

> *No text.*

**3.4** Tight two-shot with their faces close and water streaming down them. Nagoji keeps his voice low, under the noise.

> NAGOJI: Storms break ships. When wood breaks, bolts pull out. Chains go loose.

> NAGOJI: The men above will think only of their own skins. There will be one moment when we can move.

**3.5** Keshavrao, eyes wide in the gloom. A flicker of lightning through the seams of the hatch lights half his face.

> KESHAVRAO: Move where?

> NAGOJI (off): Towards air. Towards anything that floats.

> KESHAVRAO: And if there is no such thing?

## PAGE 4

Five panels. The answer, and then the storm starts taking the ship apart above them. The page ends on the ring.

**4.1** Nagoji does not soften it. The hull groans, and the plank at the end of the pen bows behind him.

> NAGOJI: Then we die here in the dark.

> NAGOJI: You have seen the dungeon. That is what awaits us at the end of this journey. Tell me which death you prefer.

**4.2** Keshavrao swallows, then gives the ghost of a smile.

> KESHAVRAO: With you, I prefer the one that involves a chance.

**4.3** Wide. Lightning through the gaps around the hatch cover freezes the hold in one harsh flash, like a portrait: men clinging to beams and to each other, mouths open, the whites of their eyes showing.

> CAPTION: The storm grew teeth.

**4.4** Looking up at the deck beams as they shudder. A long split runs through a timber overhead and shakes down grit and water. Panicked balloons come from above.

> SAILOR (off, above): The mainmast! Cut the rope, it will take us over!

> SAILOR (off, above): She is taking water! Pump, for the love of God!

**4.5** Water pours in, surging waist-deep, and the chains drag heavy through it. A prisoner behind them gasps. Keshavrao shouts into Nagoji's ear with his chin jerked upward.

> PRISONER: We will drown down here.

> KESHAVRAO: Look. The ring.

## PAGE 5

Five panels. The ring gives way, and they have slack iron for the first time since Goa.

**5.1** Close on the iron ring bolted into the beam overhead, with the chain running down from it to their collars. Dark cracks spread through the wood around the bolts and splinters lift at the edges.

> NAGOJI (off): When it goes, we move.

**5.2** The ship drops away beneath them. In a spray of splinters the ring tears half out of the beam and now hangs from a single bolt. The chain falls and sags, jerking every collar in the section.

> CAPTION: A sound like a tooth being pulled from rotten gum.

**5.3** The prisoners cry out in wild, sudden hope. Nagoji rounds on them with his teeth bared.

> NAGOJI: Quiet! You want them to hear?

**5.4** Nagoji and Keshavrao haul backward together on the slack chain, collars biting into their necks, heels braced against the plank of the pen, timing the pull to the roll.

> NAGOJI: On three. Pull as if you are trying to tear your own head off.

> NAGOJI: One, two...

**5.5** The last bolt rips out of the rotten wood. The length of chain that linked their collars drops into the water between them. They stagger, suddenly unmoored, wrists still manacled and ankles still chained, and both look up into open space above their heads.

> *No text. The splash is the line.*

## PAGE 6

Five panels. The others, the musket and the ladder. The page ends on the silhouette.

**6.1** Wide. Nagoji and Keshavrao work along the beam as far as their ankle chains allow, yanking at rings and kicking at the wood. One section's chain drops free. In the next, a ring sunk in sound timber will not give, and its men stare after them.

> CAPTION: Liberty was a generous word. We were still in a dark box, chained at wrist and ankle.

> CAPTION: Yet slack iron at the throat did something to the spine that torture had not.

**6.2** At the foot of the swaying ladder, knee-deep, with the musket rocking in the current at their feet. Nagoji looks up at the hatch.

> NAGOJI: The hatch. We need air. We need to see.

> KESHAVRAO: If we take it, they will shoot us.

> NAGOJI: If we stay, we may drown before they can load.

**6.3** Insert, tight. Nagoji's bandaged hand closes on the musket stock under the water. The frizzen hangs open and the priming pan is empty.

> CAPTION: It was not loaded. Sensible men do not leave a primed weapon lying about.

> CAPTION: Still, weight is weight.

**6.4** Low angle up the ladder. Nagoji climbs with the musket reversed in one hand, and the ankle chain cuts every step short. Keshavrao waits at the foot of the ladder below him.

> NAGOJI: Stay behind me.

> CAPTION: The ladder felt narrower than any siege stair I had ever mounted.

**6.5** Nagoji's point of view, looking straight up. A guard stands in the square of the hatch, backlit by a white flash of lightning, only a silhouette, bringing his musket barrel down to aim.

> GUARD: Back! Back, or I fire!

## PAGE 7

Four panels. Staging and aftermath. We see the ladder cleared, not the guard's death.

**7.1** Side-on and from a distance, both men small against the lightning in the hatch. The ship lurches, and the guard's hand leaves the frame as he loses his balance. Nagoji drives the musket butt upward into his wrist, and the guard's weapon spins away into the dark. Do not show the injury close up.

> CAPTION: Years of riding into gaps in enemy lines had trained my body to move before my mind could think.

> CAPTION: Bone cracked.

**7.2** The guard falls past Nagoji, a dark shape between the rungs with his arms reaching for nothing. Nagoji presses himself flat to the ladder and does not look.

> *No text.*

**7.3** Looking down into the hold. At the foot of the ladder the current rolls a still, face-down shape against the planks like loose cargo, and the prisoners press back from it. No blood.

> CAPTION: The ladder was clear.

**7.4** Nagoji is at the top of the ladder, half out of the hatch, with rain hammering his back. He twists round to shout down into the dark.

> NAGOJI: Now! Move! All of you who can climb, move!

## PAGE 8

Four panels. The deck. Give the storm room, then stop time.

**8.1** Large, half the page. Nagoji bursts out of the hatch just aft of the mainmast into the storm. Rain falls in sheets and the deck is heeled over. The mainmast has snapped and leans at an angle, its torn canvas cracking like flags of surrender. Sailors fight the weather everywhere: some cut away rigging with knives in their teeth, others heave at the pump handles, their faces white in the lightning. Nagoji grabs a rope to keep from sliding toward the black water. Not one sailor is looking at him.

> CAPTION: The storm greeted me like an enemy who had been waiting.

> CAPTION: To them I was just another piece of loose cargo tossed up from below.

**8.2** Behind him, Keshavrao's head and shoulders come up out of the hatch. His eyes are huge and one thin, manacled hand grips the frame. The wind tears his voice away.

> KESHAVRAO: Sahib!

**8.3** Wide, full width, looking to starboard on Nagoji's right. A wave rears higher than the ship, a wall of water turned silver by lightning. Three small insets run along the bottom edge of the panel, showing what he sees in the flash: Keshavrao's ankles, still chained in a pair; a coil of rope lying half loose by the starboard rail at Nagoji's feet; a shattered spar floating just off the ship's side.

> CAPTION: Leap now, reach the spar. Keshavrao follows, and the chains drag him under.

> CAPTION: Wait to free him, and we are both smashed against the rail.

**8.4** Narrow strip. The crest of the wave curls over the rail and fills the top of the panel.

> CAPTION: The wave fell.

## PAGE 9

Five panels. The rope. The promise breaks in his hands.

**9.1** After the wave. The deck is awash in grey and white, and Nagoji is on his knees with his fingers clawed into the coil of rope. Nobody on deck is standing.

> CAPTION: For a moment I was nowhere. Only a body in a cold, roaring universe.

**9.2** Keshavrao still clings to the hatch, half in and half out, drenched, his hair slicked to his skull and a rain-washed graze at his hairline (no blood drawn). His right hand twitches at his side, reaching for a sword that is not there.

> KESHAVRAO: Sahib! Do not leave me!

**9.3** Nagoji throws one end of the rope across the tilting deck toward him. His face is tight with effort behind the throw, and the rope arcs across the whole panel.

> CAPTION: He had said, in the dark below, that he would follow wherever I jumped.

> NAGOJI: Tie it! Around your chest! Quick!

**9.4** Wide. The bow plunges into the next wave and the world turns to grey and white. In the foreground Nagoji's hands hold the rope as it snaps taut.

> CAPTION: For an instant I thought he had managed it.

**9.5** Close on Nagoji's bandaged palms as the rope tears through them. The cloth is scorched and stripped away, and the rope's end whips out of the panel toward the rail.

> CAPTION: Then the line took the wrong kind of weight.

*Page turn.*

## PAGE 10

Five panels. The choice is made, and the chapter ends underwater.

**10.1** Wide and silent. The empty hatch, the broken ladder, water streaming into the hold, and no one there. Nagoji stands small on the deck, looking at it, his torn palms open at his sides.

> CAPTION: Keshavrao was gone.

> CAPTION: The burns on my palms were the only proof he had been there at all.

**10.2** Tight on Nagoji's face, rain running over it, his eyes still on the hatch. The next wave is already rearing behind him and he has not seen it. At the panel edge, a memory seeps in like a stain rather than sitting in a clean flashback frame: Deccan riders at a night campfire, one raising a cup.

> CAPTION: In the Deccan, my troop had a pact. If a man fell, the others rode on.

> CAPTION: We would mourn later, in camp, with liquor and song.

**10.3** Nagoji winds what is left of the coil twice around his chest and knots it with numb, bandaged fingers, leaving the free end to trail. He staggers toward the rail.

> CAPTION: This was my field now.

**10.4** At the rail. Below and close now, the spar rides the foam, splintered at one end, with barnacles clinging to its underside. Nagoji in profile, taking one breath.

> NAGOJI: Forgive me.

> CAPTION: To the boy I promised not to leave. To the horse who died better than I would.

> CAPTION: To whatever gods listen to men who jump into storms.

**10.5** Wide, full width, the last panel of the chapter, seen from the waterline. The ship heels away, black against a lightning sky, and the rail is empty. White foam marks where he went in, and the rope's free end trails across the surface toward the spar.

> CAPTION: The sea closed over my head like the hand of an angry god.

## Adaptation notes

- **Page count.** 10 pages against a target of 9, which is inside the 7 to 11 range. The extra page goes to the deck and to Keshavrao's loss (pages 8 to 10), where the chapter's drama is. The stretch in the hold before the ring breaks, which is mostly exposition, fits into four pages.
- **Cut, to avoid gore and repetition.** The prisoner whose arm bends the wrong way under another man's weight. It repeats the violence of the wave and would break the no-gore rule.
- **Cut, for space.** The second round of Portuguese orders, reduced to two overhead panels. "The spell broke." Keshavrao's Adam's apple. The long explanation of slipping the chain from the soft wood, simplified to a ring left hanging on its last bolt. "Again" and the four separate pulls, reduced to one pull and its result. The "Others" / "Quickly" exchange, folded into the montage in 6.1. "The storm offered no time for noble speeches now." "Sometimes the gods look kindly on men who refuse to freeze." The thought of Keshavrao's "with you, sahib" at the rail, since the reader has just seen the rope.
- **Cut, because the art shows it.** "Then I hurled myself over the side": 10.5 shows the aftermath of the leap, so the last caption is the chapter's final line on its own. The guard's "wet, final sound" is not lettered either, because 7.2 and 7.3 carry it through the fall and the still shape afterward.
- **Softened for the standing art rules.** The novel's blood on Keshavrao's forehead becomes a rain-washed graze with no blood drawn. The guard's broken wrist is staged side-on at a distance and lettered only as "Bone cracked."
- **Language.** As in the novel, the guards' Konkani and the sailors' Portuguese are lettered in English. Nagoji's orders to the hold are tagged as Marathi, because that is the moment he speaks for all the prisoners.
- **Callbacks to keep in the neighbouring scripts.** "The horse who died better than I would" (10.4) pays off Chapter 1, panel 7.1 ("Then he died better than I will"). "Muskets are like horses" keeps the horse motif going. Keshavrao's "Do not leave me", 9.3's "follow wherever I jumped" and 10.4's "the boy I promised not to leave" all depend on Chapter 2 keeping two lines: Keshavrao's "I will follow wherever you jump, sahib" and Nagoji's "If there is a chance, I will not leave you". Keshavrao's hand twitching for a sword is also planted in Chapter 2.
- **Continuity.** Nagoji wears the captivity sheet (clean-shaven chin, thick moustache, gold stud, torn ochre tunic, cream dhoti, bandaged hands, barefoot, hair loose). He also wears the ship irons from Chapter 2 (neck collar, wrist manacles, ankle chain), which CONTINUITY.md does not list for chapters 1 to 3 and should add. He ends the chapter with rope burns on his palms and a rope tied round his chest, which sets up the chest rope burns listed for Chapter 4. Portuguese guards and sailors wear shirts and buff leather, never red or blue coats.
