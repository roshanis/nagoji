## PAGE 1

Five panels. The road south and the arrival, compressed to one page so the hall gets the room. Nagoji (attach `concepts/nagoji-v2.png`): village cotton dhoti, simple cream shirt, bare-headed, long curly hair tied back, clean-shaven chin, thick curled moustache, gold ear stud, hands still bandaged, no weapon.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. A road clinging to the edge of the land. On the left, palm groves and pepper gardens climb gentle slopes; on the right, low cliffs drop to a strip of sand and a flat grey ocean. Two small riders heading south in a salt haze.

> CAPTION: They did not take me to a palace.

> CAPTION: The bulls were gone. This time I rode, though not as I had once ridden.

**1.2** Medium, the two riders side by side. Nagoji on a small, rough-coated coastal horse with a suspicious eye, smaller than any Deccan mare; his bandaged hands are light on the reins and his spine is straight. Ibrahim on a sturdy pony: shorter than the fishermen but broader, scar from his LEFT ear to the jaw, a short coat over his vest, the pockets bulging. He pats one. He has caught Nagoji looking.

> IBRAHIM: Gifts. Information costs, *bhau*. So do favours.

> IBRAHIM: When we reach the hall, remember that.

**1.3** The largest panel on the page. The road dips to a narrow inlet, dark and still. Boats drawn up along its banks, broad-bellied cargo craft and slender river canoes, cooking smoke drifting. On the rise beyond, an outer wall of rough red stone blocks topped with a musketeers' parapet; inside it a long open-fronted hall on wooden pillars carved with vines and tigers. Behind, on higher ground, a rampart runs inland along the shape of the land. Not large. Purpose in every line.

> IBRAHIM (small, soft): This is only one claw. The kingdom itself is the beast.

**1.4** The gate. Travancore guards in white cotton with dark sashes, matchlocks and spears, alert but not nervous. One raises a hand to Ibrahim in greeting. Nagoji in the foreground, turning his head sharply at the words.

> GUARD: The king is in council. You are expected.

> CAPTION: In most kingdoms I had known, a stranger waited days for an audience. Here the king himself waited at the end of the road.

**1.5** Narrow strip, bottom. Nagoji dismounted, his bandaged hand on the coastal horse's neck. The horse has tossed its head away from him, toward the groom taking its reins. Nagoji is still patting a neck that is already leaving.

> CAPTION: This horse did not know me.

> CAPTION: Kanka would have nudged my shoulder.

> CAPTION: In the Deccan, kings received men in high halls thick with incense, with carpets to muffle footsteps and paintings to remind visitors who held power.

> CAPTION: Here, when the hall fell quiet, you could still hear the sea.

He is looking at the map, not at us.

The king's eyes are on the map; Ramayyan's are on us, and on everything else. Two seated men in the foreground lean their heads together.

**2.4** Ibrahim close at Nagoji's ear, lips barely moving, hand resting on a clinking pocket.

## PAGE 3

Five panels. The first questions. Varma speaks Malayalam, which Nagoji cannot follow, so the reader hears the king only through his diwan. **Lettering convention for the whole chapter:** a line written `> VARMA: [in Malayalam]` is set as a small balloon holding only that italic tag. Ramayyan's Konkani follows a beat later in a normal balloon. Varma's only lettered words in the chapter are "Ram."

**3.1** Nagoji bowing: not as deeply as a subject would, but enough to set the man on the platform apart from a fellow soldier. Ibrahim beside him, smooth, one open hand toward Nagoji.

> IBRAHIM: Your Majesty. This is the Maratha rider I wrote of.

> IBRAHIM: He comes from Portuguese chains and storm water, and he carries knowledge of both.

**3.2** Tight horizontal strip across Varma's eyes as they leave the map and settle on Nagoji. Dark. Steady. Nothing soft, nothing careless.

> CAPTION: The kind of eyes that measure before they strike.

> CAPTION: I had seen them in generals' tents, and in men who held small forts against impossible odds.

**3.3** Two-shot from behind Nagoji's shoulder. Varma speaks, looking only at Nagoji. Ramayyan translates a heartbeat later without taking his eyes off Nagoji either.

> VARMA: [in Malayalam]

> RAMAYYAN: The Maharaja asks if you were on the ship that broke near our fishermen's village.

> NAGOJI: Yes. I was.

**3.4** Wide and flat, the rhythm panel. King, diwan and prisoner in one line across the frame. Stagger the balloons left to right so the questions come fast.

> RAMAYYAN: He asks if you rode for the Peshwa.

> NAGOJI: I did.

> RAMAYYAN: You raided Portuguese outposts on the western roads that feed their ports.

> NAGOJI: I did.

**3.5** Varma's hand in the foreground, two fingers flicking toward Nagoji's bandaged fingers. Nagoji's wrapped hands at the far edge of frame, still at his sides.

> RAMAYYAN (off): They questioned you.

> NAGOJI: They tried.

## PAGE 4

Five panels. He is set to talk, and the diwan's stylus tells us what Travancore actually wants. Ends on a question.

**4.1** Varma, close. Something like amusement crosses his face and is already gone. His eyes have moved on before any translation could have reached him.

> CAPTION: If he understood my answers without his minister's help, he gave no sign beyond the speed of his eyes.

**4.2** Ramayyan pointing with the stylus to a mat laid a few paces from the platform. Nagoji lowering himself onto it, stiff, favouring healing wounds.

> RAMAYYAN: Sit. Speak. Tell him how they fight now on your coast.

> RAMAYYAN: How their forts stand, how their captains think, how quickly they bring ships when a local chief resists.

**4.3** Nagoji seated, talking, one bandaged hand shaping a low wall in the air. Bleeding in behind him like a stain rather than a clean flashback border (the Chapter 1, panel 6.5 device): a Portuguese bastion at Chaul, thick and low, a cannonball skipping off its slope in a spray of stone dust.

> CAPTION: For the next while, time lost its edges.

> NAGOJI: At Chaul and Daman they build bastions thick and low, so cannonballs skip off instead of biting deep.

**4.4** The stain shifts and softens, because this is hearsay, not memory: a padre in a black cassock walking ahead of a column of Portuguese soldiers with a cross held high, and behind him an officer eyeing the cannon on a small raja's wall. In the foreground edge, Varma's fingers make one small tap on the spear shaft.

> NAGOJI: Their padres walk ahead of the soldiers talking of one God, while the men behind them count how many guns a king can spare.

> NAGOJI: Rajas on this coast signed their bargains to keep neighbours away. They invited a hungry wolf into their courtyards.

**4.5** Full width, bottom. Varma leaning forward over the unrolled map, one finger on a point where a river mouth meets the sea. Inset, top left corner of the panel: Ramayyan's stylus, still until now, scratching fast on a palm leaf.

> NAGOJI (off, in the inset): And their infantry do not know what to do with sudden horse on the flank.

> VARMA: [in Malayalam]

> RAMAYYAN: Here. This river mouth. How many days from their nearest stronghold at full sail?

## PAGE 5

Five panels. The pilots. The king shows he is ahead of his interpreter, and the diwan shows he is ahead of his king.

**5.1** Nagoji, eyes closed. Behind him, the stain: the coastline seen from a galloping horse's back, sails far out on the water, a Portuguese warehouse burning on a quay at night.

> CAPTION: I thought of wind, and of how long their replacement ships had taken after we burned a warehouse.

**5.2** Nagoji opening his eyes, answering the king directly.

> NAGOJI: If the wind favours them, three days. Four if they are unlucky.

> NAGOJI: Longer if someone whispers wrong directions into the ear of a pilot.

**5.3** Varma's head coming up from the map, eyes narrowing, BEFORE Ramayyan has opened his mouth. Ramayyan's lips still closed, stylus paused mid-stroke. The king snaps a few syllables.

> VARMA (sharp): [in Malayalam]

> RAMAYYAN: You mistrust pilots?

> CAPTION: I had the prickling sense that the Maharaja already understood more than he chose to show.

**5.4** Nagoji, level, speaking to the king and not to the diwan.

> NAGOJI: I mistrust everyone who touches a coin.

> NAGOJI: But men who steer ships can be made to see profit in many directions.

**5.5** Two-shot, platform. The corner of Varma's mouth twitches; he does not turn his head. Beside him Ramayyan is already writing, and does not look up either.

> VARMA: Ram.

> RAMAYYAN (soft): Already marked, Maharaja.

## PAGE 6

Four panels. The sting, and then Ibrahim is put on the scale. Ends on a question.

**6.1** Wide. The hall gone still, as if waiting for something. Varma speaks at length, even-toned, the spear upright beside him. The seated rows motionless. Through the open side, the sea.

> VARMA: [in Malayalam]

> CAPTION: I caught none of the words. Only the weight.

**6.2** Ramayyan translating. His voice is soft; the edge is in the words, not his face.

> RAMAYYAN: He says you speak as if you have counted men and ships for years.

> RAMAYYAN: Yet your own masters failed to keep you from their dungeons.

**6.3** Nagoji. Jaw set, bandaged hands flat on his knees. He does not drop his eyes.

> CAPTION: It was not meant as an insult. It was simple fact. Still, it stung.

> NAGOJI: Any war has its losses. I misjudged a patrol near the coast. I paid the price.

> NAGOJI: That does not make my other calculations less true.

**6.4** Varma, unreadable, speaks briefly. Ramayyan turns his head toward Ibrahim, who stands at the edge of the clear space with his hands folded over his pockets.

> RAMAYYAN: He says, perhaps.

> RAMAYYAN: And he asks Ibrahim if he trusts you.

Five panels. The real question, and the view that answers it. Ends on the horizon.

**7.1** Ibrahim, easy, spreading his hands. The scar along his left jaw catches the light from the open side of the hall.

> IBRAHIM: As far as one can trust a man who owes you his life and hates the same enemies.

> IBRAHIM: Beyond that, trust is for priests.

**7.2** Small panel. Varma chuckles once, head tipped back a fraction. Ramayyan leans in and murmurs something, and the king's mouth twitches.

## PAGE 8

Five panels. The answer the chapter exists for. Give the speech room and break it across the memory.

**8.1** Nagoji turning back from the sea to face the platform. Slow. He is not pleading; he is laying out terms.

> NAGOJI: You have horse from Madurai. You have Nair warriors who know this land as they know their own pulse.

> NAGOJI: What you do not yet have is someone who has ridden into a European line and felt it break.

**8.2** Large. The stain again, the largest yet: Kanka, the black mare of Chapter 1, on wet sand near Chaul, Nagoji low over her neck. A line of Portuguese musketeers (wide-brimmed hats, buff coats; not British red, not Dutch blue) flinching and falling out of order as the horses come in at an angle they never drilled for. No blood. Present-day Nagoji small at the bottom edge, seated on his mat, speaking.

> NAGOJI: Someone who has watched their neat formations falter not from fear of God, but because a hoof struck at the right moment in the right place.

**8.3** Narrow, cold. The stain turns: only Keshavrao's eyes, huge in storm light, rain across them, at the hatch of the breaking ship.

> CAPTION: Keshavrao.

> CAPTION: And the weight I now carried.

**8.4** Nagoji, close, leaning in, voice lower and harder.

> NAGOJI: I can help you turn speed into a weapon against men who think their guns make them untouchable.

> NAGOJI: I can show your riders where to strike, when to pull away, how to make men who drilled on flat European fields die confused on this coast.

**8.5** Full width, bottom. The hall silent. A bird on the parapet outside; waves on the sand below. Varma taps the butt of his spear against the mat, once, twice (two small motion ticks), eyes never leaving Nagoji.

> VARMA: Ram.

## PAGE 9

Five panels. The verdict, delivered by a minister whose king is smiling at him.

**9.1** Ramayyan inclines his head, then turns to Nagoji and speaks to him directly, in Konkani, before any word from the king.

> RAMAYYAN: The Madurai horsemen know the straight charge. They do not know the feint and vanish the way Deccan riders do.

> RAMAYYAN: You could help shape that.

**9.2** Varma smiling thinly at his minister; then his gaze returns to Nagoji and he speaks briefly. Ramayyan, amused.

> VARMA: [in Malayalam]

> RAMAYYAN: He says I always see the two faces of the coin. And he says, very well, Nagoji Sawant.

**9.3** Tight on Ramayyan, the amusement gone, delivering it straight.

> RAMAYYAN: For now, you are not a guest. You are not yet a servant either.

> RAMAYYAN: You are an experiment.

**9.4** Varma glancing past Nagoji toward the open side of the hall. Show what he is looking at: below the fort, a narrow strip of wet sand pinned between a low bluff and the sea. This is the ground of Chapter 7; plant it clearly.

> VARMA: [in Malayalam]

> RAMAYYAN: Outside this fort a strip of shore runs along the sea, too wet for most cavalry, too narrow for big batteries. It is where the Dutch will expect us to be weakest.

**9.5** Full width, bottom. Nagoji, small on his mat before the platform, the king and diwan above him.

> RAMAYYAN: Take the Madurai horse and a hundred Maravar riders, and show him something there he has not already imagined.

> RAMAYYAN: If you are wrong, you will find Travancore worse than the Portuguese.

## PAGE 10

Five panels. The charge, the exit and the vow. End on the chapter's last line.

**10.1** Nagoji rising from the mat, ignoring the pull of healing wounds, standing straight before the platform.

> NAGOJI: As he commands.

**10.2** Varma, voice lower, looking only at Nagoji. Ramayyan translating quietly.

> VARMA: [in Malayalam]

> RAMAYYAN: He says I am to give you what you need. And you are not to waste men.

> RAMAYYAN: He has spent too many lives to sit on that mat. He does not throw them away lightly, not even when the gods demand it.

**10.3** Ramayyan on his feet, gathering his palm leaves under one arm, turning to lead Nagoji out of the hall.

> RAMAYYAN: Come. Let us see what a storm can do when given horse instead of chains.

**10.4** Outside, at the top of the stone steps. Wind off the sea in Nagoji's hair, the surf loud below. Ramayyan already going down ahead of him.

> CAPTION: Storms had tried to kill me. Now I would learn whether I could become one.

**10.5** Full width, bottom. Nagoji from behind on the steps. His bandaged hand has gone to his hip, to the empty place where a sword should hang. Ramayyan and the shore lie south, to his left; Nagoji's head is turned the other way, north up the coast.

> CAPTION (italic): *I will serve this king. I will harden his riders and break his enemies.*

> CAPTION (italic): *When that debt is paid, I will turn north again, find Chimaji, and burn the Portuguese out of Goa, stone by bloody stone.*

> CAPTION (separate, weighted): For now, Travancore was the forge. But the blade was meant for another war.

## Adaptation notes

- **Ten pages against a target of nine.** The chapter is one audience scene built from short exchanges, so the pages go to the hall (pages 2 to 10). To pay for that, the road and arrival are compressed from what would naturally be two pages to one (page 1).
- **Road exposition cut:** the "wind had tasted blood" line, the landscape catalogue, and "He did not know my hand, and I did not yet know his language" (its meaning is carried by the dismount beat at 1.5).
- **Varma's Malayalam stays untranslated** through the `[in Malayalam]` convention (page 3 note). This keeps Nagoji's POV and lets the art carry the novel's hint that the king understands more than he shows (5.3, where he reacts before the translation). His only lettered word is "Ram," which makes the two "Ram" beats (5.5 and 8.5, the second answered by Ramayyan's own summary at 9.1) land as the king and diwan's private shorthand.
- **The whisper about Ramayyan** is a caption rather than a balloon, since the seated men would not be whispering in a language Nagoji understands.
- **Testimony compressed** into three short lines over stain images (4.3 to 4.5). Cut: the palm-leaf treaties "tighter than ropes" and the banias and Brahmin clerks as the source of the hearsay; the softer, less detailed stain at 4.4 marks it as hearsay instead.
- **Cut from the speech:** "You have men who can build ships and forts" and "teach your riders not to fear the sound of European volleys", for length. The strongest sentences stay verbatim.
- **Cut from the verdict:** Ramayyan's sentence on the Maravar riders lacking discipline against guns, and the narration "his king had imagined a great deal". Kept: the shore assignment and "where the Dutch will expect us to be weakest", because it plants Chapter 7 and Colachel.
- **Cut:** "For the first time since Goa, my blood beat in my veins with something other than pain and stubbornness", and "Not in threat, but in promise". The north-facing turn of his head at 10.5 carries the second.
- **Kept verbatim:** "They did not take me to a palace"; "This is only one claw"; "trust is for priests"; "Already marked, Maharaja"; "You are an experiment"; "worse than the Portuguese"; "horse instead of chains"; "whether I could become one"; the vow including "stone by bloody stone" (spoken only; nothing violent is drawn); and the closing forge and blade line.
- **Continuity reconciliations:** Varma combines the novel (no crown, knot to the left, Vaishnavite forehead lines, one cloth over the shoulder, spear, sword in reach) with CONTINUITY.md (V13 face, peacock crest, gold necklaces). Nagoji is in his ch5 to 6 village look with bandaged hands and no sword. Ibrahim's coat with bulging pockets is introduced here, as CONTINUITY.md requires.
- **Flag for the author:** the novel gives Kanka two sexes. Chapter 6 says "his hooves" (and Chapter 1 "his easy stride"), while Chapter 7 and the prequel call Kanka a black mare. This script avoids pronouns in all lettering and draws Kanka as the black mare, matching the approved Chapter 1 script.
