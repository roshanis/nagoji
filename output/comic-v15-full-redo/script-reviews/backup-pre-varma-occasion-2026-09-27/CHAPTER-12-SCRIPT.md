## PAGE 1

Five panels. The shadow is introduced as a force before it is a man. We never see his face.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. The Carnatic plain in the dry season, baked brown, with the wall of the Western Ghats rising behind it. A long column of cavalry winds toward a notch in the hills, its dust hanging in the air behind it. Bullock-drawn cannon at the tail. Hard white light. Distance only, no faces. The riders wear dust-coloured quilted coats and dark indigo or black turbans; no green anywhere on the riders, no red sashes.

> CAPTION: Before the Dutch became the enemy we watched from our beaches, there was another shadow that fell across Travancore each dry season.

**1.2** Insert, tight. A rider's ringed hand holding a rolled Persian letter inside a brocade silk kharita pouch, its drawstring closed with a lac seal. No writing visible anywhere, only the brocade and the seal.

> CAPTION: It came with demands written in Persian and backed by Mughal seals that meant nothing in Delhi but everything to the men who carried them.

**1.3** Low angle. A commander on a fine horse at the top of a ridge, backlit by the sun so his face is lost in shadow: a heavy, bearded man in a white Mughal jama and a high turban of gold-shot cream silk with a jewelled aigrette, on a rich saddle cloth. He wears no red turban and no red sash. A banner bearer rides a length behind him with a plain dark banner, no device or lettering. The commander is looking south, away from us.

> CAPTION: Chanda Sahib.

> CAPTION: I had heard the name in Pune, spoken with the wary respect one gives to a competent rival.

**1.4** Over the commander's shoulder, his cream turban and shoulder a dark silhouette in the near foreground. What he looks at: the green south opening out below the hills. Dense forested slopes and homestead gardens, pepper vines climbing jackfruit and areca trees, paddy on the valley floor, a river, and far off a port town with ships at anchor where the sea is a line of light. No terraces, no plantation rows.

> CAPTION: He had married into the Nawab of Arcot's family and climbed until he held Madurai in his fist.

**1.5** Narrow strip, full width. Marthanda Varma in his hall, standing straight, spear grounded at his side, looking directly out of the panel. Court dress: cream and gold, peacock crest, gold necklaces, bare-chested. Unbowed.

> CAPTION: He saw what every ambitious man saw: pepper, ports, and a king who had not yet learned to bow.

## PAGE 2

Five panels. The temple steps at sunset. Ramayyan tells the history; Nagoji recognises it from the other side.

**2.1** Wide. Evening. The steps of a small Kerala-style shrine among coconut palms: laterite base, sloping tiled roof, no gopuram. The sun is sinking behind the trunks. Ramayyan and Nagoji sit side by side on the steps, small in the frame. Ramayyan: thin, sharp fine features, receding hair, plain white cloth, a palm-leaf bundle and stylus beside him. Nagoji in the commander sheet: rust-red turban, cream tunic, broad red sash, cream trousers, talwar laid across his knees; clean-shaven chin, thick moustache, gold ear stud. His boots are off and set side by side on the ground below the bottom step; his feet are bare.

> RAMAYYAN: He came first in thirty-six. Before you washed up on our shore.

> RAMAYYAN: We were weak, and Chanda Sahib knew it.

**2.2** Tight. Ramayyan's long fingers turning a small stone over and over.

> RAMAYYAN: His cavalry came through Shenkottai pass like water through a cracked dam.

**2.3** Memory image, bleeding in at the panel edges rather than framed as a clean flashback. A village of thatched houses burning at dusk, seen from a distance. Horsemen are silhouettes against the flames. A family hurries away along a bund with bundles on their heads. No bodies, no blood.

> RAMAYYAN (off): They burned villages. They took what they wanted.

> RAMAYYAN (off): They sat on the edges of our kingdom and waited for the Maharaja to come with gold.

**2.4** Back on the steps, two-shot. Nagoji turned toward him. Ramayyan looking straight ahead, unashamed.

> NAGOJI: And he came?

> RAMAYYAN: He had no choice. We paid. Tribute, they called it. Protection money is what it was.

> RAMAYYAN: Fifty thousand rupees the first time.

**2.5** Full width. Nagoji in profile against the last of the sunset. Behind him, bleeding in like a stain: a dry Deccan field, a line of Maratha riders under a saffron swallow-tailed pennant at its edge, in white angarkhas and white pagdis with long bamboo lances, no red sashes, and a farmer standing in the stubble with his hoe, watching them. Nagoji himself is not in the memory.

> CAPTION: I thought of the Maratha sardars I had known, riding into Mughal country each dry season to collect chauth.

> CAPTION: We called it our right. The Mughals called it extortion. The farmers caught in between called it survival.

## PAGE 3

Five panels. Tribute buys time. Walls buy something else. The page turns on Ramayyan's smile.

**3.1** Medium two-shot, the light going blue. Nagoji asking; Ramayyan still turning the stone.

> NAGOJI: How many times?

> RAMAYYAN: Every year since. Sometimes Chanda Sahib himself. Sometimes his generals. Sometimes just the threat of them.

**3.2** Wide, almost silhouette: the two men on the shrine steps against the last light. Ramayyan's arm follows through as the stone arcs away into the gathering dark between the palms; Nagoji sits still beside him.

> RAMAYYAN: The Dutch watched all of it. They saw a king who had to buy his peace from one direction while they circled from another.

**3.3** Ramayyan pointing toward the southeastern hills, Nagoji following the line of his arm. The last light is a thin gold line along the ridge.

> RAMAYYAN: Every rupee we paid to Chanda Sahib was a rupee we could not spend on powder or walls.

> RAMAYYAN: But we did build walls. At Aramboli. At the passes.

**3.4** Wide, bleeding in from the ridgeline: the Aramboli lines across the neck of a pass. A deep ditch, an earthen rampart topped by a timber palisade with firing loopholes, sharpened stakes, a timber gate. A few Nair sentries, tiny against the works: bare-headed with a front topknot, white mundu, matchlocks. Dusk. Leave generous sky for the balloons.

> RAMAYYAN (off): The Maharaja understood that tribute buys time, not safety.

> NAGOJI (off): I have seen them. From a distance.

> RAMAYYAN (off): You will see them closer.

**3.5** Narrow strip. Ramayyan's face in the last light, a thin smile.

> RAMAYYAN: Walls cost less than tribute, in the long run. And they do not ride home when the rains come.

## PAGE 4

Five panels. Nagoji learns the enemy at first hand, learns the land that beats them, then keeps his own counsel.

**4.1** Wide action. Near Nagercoil, where red-earth scrub and palmyra palms break into stream-cut, rocky ground at the foot of the hills. Nagoji, mounted, holds the mouth of a ravine with Dhanaji at his shoulder and a line of Nair musketmen kneeling among the rocks, matchlocks levelled; the Nairs are bare-headed with a front topknot, white mundu hitched for fighting. Dhanaji: stockier and broader than Nagoji, short trimmed beard, white Maratha pagdi turban, indigo quilted tunic, no red sash, talwar drawn. Only Nagoji wears the rust-red turban and red sash. Out on the open scrub beyond, Chanda Sahib's riders in dust-coloured quilted coats and dark indigo or black turbans, no green, no red sashes, wheel away from the ravine mouth, faster, refusing the broken ground. Dust. No one falls.

> CAPTION: Near Nagercoil.

> CAPTION: We spoke of it afterward with the frustrated respect of men who had faced a worthy enemy.

**4.2** Afterward, late afternoon, in the same broken ground at the ravine mouth. Their horses blowing, heads down. Dhanaji (short trimmed beard, white pagdi, indigo quilted tunic, no red sash) sits on a rock wiping sweat from his eyes; Nagoji stands holding both bridles. Dhanaji gestures at the rocks and gullies around them and the hills beyond.

> CAPTION: Dhanaji admitted it.

> DHANAJI: Their horse were better than ours. Faster on open ground. They knew the passes, knew the water holes.

> DHANAJI: We did not fight them in the open. We let the land fight for us.

**4.3** High, wide view from the hills. A small walled town at the mouth of a pass, where the green slopes break into the flat brown plains beyond. A road runs through it and out the other side. No figures near the viewer. Leave open sky for the balloons, whose tails run off the panel edge.

> CAPTION: He had demanded Shenkottai. A dagger pointed at Travancore's belly.

> NAGOJI (off): And the Maharaja refused to give it up?

> RAMAYYAN (off): He gave it up, then took it back, then gave it up again.

> RAMAYYAN (off): Each time, men died. Each time, we paid in gold and blood.

**4.4** Night. Nagoji alone on the laterite parapet walk of a Travancore fort, looking northeast, his face lit from below by a clay oil lamp, an open wick dish, set on the parapet. Bleeding in above him across the stars: a column of Maratha horse under saffron swallow-tailed pennants, moving south, the riders in white angarkhas and white pagdis with long bamboo lances, no red sashes.

> CAPTION: What I did not tell them was what I knew from my own people.

> CAPTION: Raghuji Bhonsle's men were moving south. And Chanda Sahib, for all his cavalry and his Persian letters, was overextended.

**4.5** Full width. High and behind, from inside the fort. In the yard below the rampart, a lit doorway where Ramayyan (plain white cloth) and Dhanaji (white pagdi, indigo quilted tunic) stand talking, small, unaware. Above them on the rampart, Nagoji, seen from behind, pinches out the wick of the clay lamp. The parapet goes dark, and he stays facing the black horizon.

> CAPTION: I did not know how the war would end. Men might think I was a spy, or worse, a lever they could use to bargain with powers I no longer served.

> CAPTION: So I kept my silence and watched the horizon for news.

He wears a plain cotton head cloth wrapped low and a dusty cream tunic and dhoti, no quilted coat. Nair guards in white mundu, bare-headed with a front topknot, carrying swords and round shields, run to catch the bridle.

## PAGE 6

Five panels. The shadow is lifted by Nagoji's own people, and every face in the room remembers it.

**6.1** Varma leaning forward from his seat. Ramayyan stepping up to hand him the leaf.

> VARMA: Fallen? Dead?

> RAMAYYAN: Captured. The Marathas took Tiruchirappalli. Raghuji Bhonsle's men have him in chains.

**6.2** Wide, from the front of the hall looking back. Every chief's head turned over his shoulder toward the back of the hall. The chiefs are Nair chieftains, bare-headed with a front topknot, white mundu and shoulder cloth, gold earrings, no turbans. Nagoji stands at the back by his pillar, the only turbaned man in the hall, and does not move.

> CAPTION: They knew where I came from. They knew what the orange flag meant.

**6.3** Varma reading the leaf slowly, lips moving slightly. His expression gives nothing away.

> VARMA: The Peshwa's war has done what our tribute could not.

> VARMA: Chanda Sahib will not ride south this season. Perhaps not ever.

**6.4** A local chief in the foreground (an older Nair chieftain, bare-headed with a front topknot, white mundu and shoulder cloth, heavy gold earrings) muttering sideways. Varma in the background, not troubling to look at him.

> CHIEF: The Marathas will want payment for their service. They always do.

> VARMA: Let them want. They are far away. The Dutch are close.

> VARMA: I will worry about debts when the creditors can reach my door.

**6.5** Over Varma's shoulder in the foreground, looking straight down the hall. Nagoji small at the far end beside his pillar. The Nair chieftains between them (bare-headed, topknots, white mundu, no turbans) lean out of the line of the king's gaze.

> VARMA: You knew this was coming, Sawant?

## PAGE 7

Five panels. Nagoji answers carefully, and is useful. Every exchange has a piece of business. The king ends the talk and turns the chapter toward the sea.

**7.1** Close on Nagoji, stepping forward from the pillar. Measured.

> NAGOJI: I knew Raghuji Bhonsle was ambitious and that Chanda Sahib had made enemies.

> NAGOJI: But I did not know when or how the blow would fall.

> NAGOJI: I have been here, Maharaja, not in Pune. My knowledge is old.

**7.2** Varma on the dais lowering the ola from the lamplight beside his seat, studying Nagoji over its edge for a long moment.

> VARMA: Old knowledge is still knowledge.

> VARMA: What do the Marathas do with a captured Nawab?

**7.3** Nagoji at the low table, sure of himself now, setting one plain gold coin on a small stack from the brass tray as he speaks toward Varma, who is off-panel. Nair chieftains listening on either side, bare-headed, topknots, no turbans.

> NAGOJI: They will hold him. He is worth more alive than dead.

> NAGOJI: The Marathas are patient. They will extract every pagoda they can before they let him go.

**7.4** Three-shot, with the open side of the hall and the sea showing beyond them. Varma on the dais hands the ola back; Ramayyan, beside the dais, takes it and tucks it into the fold of his white cloth; Nagoji at the edge of the frame.

> VARMA: And while they extract?

> NAGOJI: While they extract, he cannot ride south.

> RAMAYYAN: Then we have a window. The Marathas have given us a gift, whether they meant to or not.

**7.5** Wide, full width. Varma rising, spear in hand, the Nair chieftains (bare-headed, topknots, white mundu, no turbans) getting to their feet around him. He has already turned toward the open side of the hall and the sea beyond it.

> VARMA: Enough talk of Arcot. The Dutch are the enemy in front of us.

> VARMA: Let the Marathas deal with the shadow at our back. We have our own war to win.

## PAGE 8

Five panels. Night. The irony belongs to Nagoji alone. End facing the sand.

**8.1** Wide. Night in the camp, cookfires and low talk: Nair soldiers (bare-headed, topknots, white mundu) and a few Deccan riders in white pagdis sit at the fires. Nagoji walks alone between the groups. One old Nair soldier nods to him as he passes; beside that man another Nair watches him go with narrowed eyes.

> CAPTION: As if my people's victory was somehow mine.

> CAPTION: Others wondered what other secrets I kept behind my Maratha face.

> CAPTION: Now that shadow had been lifted by men I no longer served, men who would not even know my name if I returned to Pune.

**8.2** Nagoji on a low rise above the coast, seen from behind. The sea is black; the Dutch ships cannot be seen. Wind moves the tail of his turban.

> CAPTION: I had fled the Peshwa's justice. I had washed up on this foreign shore with nothing but the scars on my back and the skills in my hands.

**8.3** Vision only, bleeding across the night sky above the sea; the figure on the rise is not in this panel. Somewhere in the Carnatic, a captive of rank seated in a tent: a heavy, bearded man in a soiled white Mughal jama and a high cream turban with its jewel gone, his face in shadow, iron at his wrists. Maratha officers in white angarkhas and white or saffron pagdis, no red sashes, count coin on a cloth before him. A saffron swallow-tailed pennant hangs at the tent door.

> CAPTION: And now, without knowing it, the very power I had escaped had done more to secure Travancore's eastern frontier than all my drills and all my riding.

> CAPTION: The world turns strangely. One man's capture becomes another man's freedom.

**8.4** Close on Nagoji's face in the sea wind, campfire smoke drifting across it. Not triumphant. Certain.

> CAPTION: Tomorrow, or the day after, the Dutch would land at Colachel, thinking they faced a king still bleeding from two fronts.

> CAPTION: They were wrong.

**8.5** Full width, bottom. Nagoji has turned from the sea and is walking back toward the fires of the camp. Below the rise, the long pale beach runs away into the dark, empty, waiting.

> CAPTION: The shadow from Arcot had lifted. Now there was only the sea, and the enemy upon it, and the sand where we would meet them.

## Adaptation notes

- **Page count.** 8 pages, the top of the 6 to 8 range. Most of the chapter is backstory told in conversation, so four pages (1 to 4) carry roughly 1,100 words of history. The war hall, the chapter's only live scene, gets three pages (5 to 7), and the night reflection, which is its payoff, gets a full page (8).
- **Chanda Sahib never shows his face.** There is no continuity sheet for him, and a man in shadow reads better as "the shadow". He appears only as the backlit rider (1.3, and his silhouette in 1.4) and the shadowed captive (8.3). Both are dressed alike (heavy build, beard, white Mughal jama, high cream turban) so the captive reads as the rider fallen; the missing jewel in 8.3 marks the fall.
- **Cut from the temple steps:** "Before the king had finished breaking the Pillamar" (the balloon was too long, and the Pillamar are covered in earlier chapters); the English Company loans (already in chapter 11); "Then they came back the next year and asked for more" (folded into "Every year since"); the Van Imhoff and earthworks speech; and "Ditches deep enough to break a horse's leg", because 3.4 draws the ditch.
- **Dhanaji.** The novel puts him at Nagoji's shoulder near Nagercoil and then has Nagoji ask him "How did you survive?", which cannot be asked of a man who fought in the same skirmish. That question is cut. Dhanaji keeps his own lines, and "We did not fight them in the open. We let the land fight for us" now answers his own complaint, over the broken ground where they held (4.1, 4.2). "Rivers flood...", "Elephants cannot climb where goats can" and "And we paid" are cut: the land is drawn in 4.1 to 4.3, and the tribute beat is Ramayyan's.
- **Shenkottai.** Dramatised as the novel has it: Nagoji's question and Ramayyan's answer, as off-panel balloons over the town (4.3). The pepper-routes speech and "more times than I can count" are cut.
- **Maratha politics.** "The Nizam was distracted" is cut. Raghuji Bhonsle is kept, because his name pays off in 6.1 and in Nagoji's admission in 7.1.
- **War hall.** Cut: "I knew the Peshwa had interests in the Carnatic" (Varma has already said "The Peshwa's war" in 6.3); "I chose my words carefully" (the measured performance carries it); "Tell me what happens now"; "His family will pay... or the Nizam will pay"; "His generals will scatter. His army will dissolve"; and the exchange "The Dutch do not know it yet" / "They will know soon enough. Their spies are everywhere". The king's closing speech, "Old knowledge is still knowledge" and "creditors can reach my door" stay verbatim. The coin tray planted in 5.3 lets 7.3 show what a pagoda is.
- **Night.** "One kingdom's loss becomes another kingdom's gain" and the thought about the Marathas planting flags are cut. The salt and smoke of the sea wind are drawn in 8.4 rather than captioned. The chapter ends on the sand line; the novel's last sentences ("I turned back toward the camp. There was still work to do.") are carried by the figure walking back to the fires in 8.5. If the author wants the last line lettered, set it alone in a silent narrow strip after 8.5.
- **Visual continuity.** Nagoji wears the commander sheet (CONTINUITY, chapters 7 to 16) throughout, and is the only man in a turban in the war hall; the Nair chieftains, guards and soldiers are bare-headed with a front topknot. Chanda Sahib's riders wear dust-coloured quilted coats and dark indigo or black turbans, never green, so they are not confused with Raza Khan's Madurai lancers, who have served Travancore since chapter 7. The Maratha flag is a saffron swallow-tailed pennant, drawn only in memory and vision panels (2.5, 4.4, 8.3), and Maratha riders wear white angarkhas and white pagdis, never Nagoji's red. The ola and the Persian letter (in a kharita pouch) are drawn sealed, because the art may contain no text. The rampart lamp is a clay oil lamp, never a glass lantern.
