## PAGE 1

Five panels. Rain, a small room, a leaf under a stone. End on the name he no longer uses.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. The fort at Udayagiri under a grey afternoon, rain sweeping in from the west and blurring the line of the hills behind it. A lone rider comes in through the gate, cloak of rain around him. Nagoji on a bay horse, horse and rider dark with wet. Ananthan Pillai look (CONTINUITY, ch24 onward): Kerala topknot, NO turban, white European-cut coat, red waistcoat, brass buttons, silver chain of office, talwar at the hip; clean-shaven chin, thick curled moustache, gold ear stud, grey at the temples.

> CAPTION: The ola came on a day that smelled of ink and rain.

> CAPTION: By the time I reached the fort, my clothes were damp and my temper short.

**1.2** Interior. Ramayyan's small room off the war hall: whitewashed laterite, a low table, a low stool, an oil lamp burning in a wall niche, a window onto a narrow courtyard where a single neem tree stands in the rain. Ramayyan (thin, sharp fine features, receding hair, plain white cloth) seated, palm-leaf bundles at his knee. Nagoji in the doorway, shaking rain from his coat sleeves, water running off his topknot.

> RAMAYYAN: You ride in all weathers now.

> NAGOJI: The marches do not stay still when the sun hides.

**1.3** Medium, over Ramayyan's shoulder. He nods Nagoji toward the stool. On the table between them, a single palm leaf lies weighted with a small river stone. Ramayyan's hands rest on his knees, well away from it.

> RAMAYYAN: Sit. We have a matter to discuss that is not for the hall.

**1.4** Two-shot across the table, Nagoji now seated, coat dripping onto the floor. Ramayyan's fingers lift the stone.

> RAMAYYAN: You have been busy. Putting out small fires along the northern border.

> NAGOJI: You told me that was my work.

> RAMAYYAN: It is. So is this.

**1.5** Full width, bottom. Nagoji's face, close, lit by the niche lamp, reading. The leaf is in the foreground seen edge-on from behind, so no writing faces the reader. His expression has gone still. Letterer's note: all text from leaves and letters in this chapter is set as CAPTION (letter) or CAPTION (leaf), an italic box with a pale palm-leaf tint, distinct from Nagoji's narration. The art never shows legible script.

> CAPTION: It was not his hand. The curves and strokes were Deccan.

> CAPTION (letter): Nagoji Sawant, who rode once under our banners and now rides for a southern king.

> SARDAR: You see further than I do, Nagoji. One day that will get you killed or much land.

> CAPTION: Here he was, offering the latter and implying the former.

## PAGE 3

Five panels. Ramayyan explains nothing he does not have to. Keep the camera close and the room small. End on the wall.

**3.1** Nagoji setting the leaf back on the table. Ramayyan regarding him levelly.

> NAGOJI: And you did not burn it. Interesting.

> RAMAYYAN: It is not my place to burn your past. Only to weigh what it asks of your present.

**3.2** Close on Nagoji, wary.

> NAGOJI: You think this is a trick.

> RAMAYYAN (off): Everything is a trick. That does not mean it is not also sincere.

**3.3** Ramayyan leaning back, hands resting lightly on his knees. The neem tree in the window behind him, bowed with rain.

> RAMAYYAN: They ask for information. Not yet for betrayal.

> RAMAYYAN: That comes later, if the first request goes unchallenged.

**3.4** Two-shot, level. Neither man looks away.

> RAMAYYAN: I could have had the letter intercepted and never told you. Instead, I show you. Ask yourself why.

> NAGOJI: You want to move me from the "risk" column in your books to the "asset" one.

**3.5** Full width, bottom. Ramayyan, and above him the underside of the roof tiles, rain running off their lips in a bead curtain past the window. He does not pretend otherwise; his face says so.

> RAMAYYAN: The Maharaja trusts you more than most of his own nobles. That trust also makes you a potential crack.

> RAMAYYAN: I prefer to test a wall before the storm does.

## PAGE 4

Five panels. The temptation, then the cost. Two visions, warm and burning, then back into the small room for the first bargain. End on Ramayyan's correction.

**4.1** Wide, warm Deccan light, soft-edged like memory. A village courtyard on the black soil: Nagoji's father, grey and bent, straightening his back; his brother's wife at the hearth ladling ghee; two nephews in new cloth. Inset, lower corner: Nagoji riding up to the gate of an old Deccan hill fort as a man of standing, a scribe at the gate already writing.

> CAPTION (letter): Estates confirmed. Family freed from certain levies. Advice heard.

**4.2** Wide, cold and grey, the same soft edges turned hard. A Travancore star fort seen from inland with its bastions' teeth turned the wrong way, and Maratha horse pouring down from the northern ghats toward it under saffron pennants. Offshore, small against the sea, European ships with men watching from the rails.

> CAPTION: I could also see the other images the leaf did not paint.

**4.3** Padmini's estate at night: the pepper vines and the jackfruit tree lit by fire, Maratha riders silhouetted against the flames. No bodies. Inset: Revathi (indigo sari with gold, tali, grey threading her hair, jasmine in her braid) at a window in Velinadu, watching smoke on the horizon.

> CAPTION: Padmini's estate burned not by Kollamkara's men but by my own countrymen.

> CAPTION: Velinadu, folded by yet another power into a larger map.

**4.4** Back in the room. Nagoji, the Deccan leaf flat under his palm.

> NAGOJI: They ask for what I know.

> NAGOJI: If I tell them nothing, I insult men I once rode beside. If I tell them everything, I open this kingdom's ribs.

**4.5** Full width, bottom. Ramayyan, precise, a faint dryness at the mouth.

> RAMAYYAN: If you tell them only what helps them avoid company mistakes without helping them cut us, you may keep two houses from collapsing on each other.

> NAGOJI: Half truths.

> RAMAYYAN: Selected truths.

## PAGE 5

Five panels. The test is named and the stylus is handed over. End on him beginning.

**5.1** Ramayyan leaning forward into the lamplight, eyes sharpened.

> RAMAYYAN: This is the test, Ananthan. Not whether you will ride north tomorrow. I do not believe you will.

**5.2** Close on Nagoji, the lamp flame small in his eyes.

> RAMAYYAN (off): The test is whether, when confronted with a choice between making Travancore easier prey and making your old masters slightly less blind...

> RAMAYYAN (off): ...you know where to place your weight.

**5.3** Insert, tight. Ramayyan's thin fingers sliding a blank palm leaf and an iron stylus across the table toward the Deccan leaf.

> RAMAYYAN: Write. I will not guide your hand.

> RAMAYYAN: I will read what you write. Then I will decide what to send.

**5.4** Tight on Nagoji's hand closing on the stylus, gripping it the way a swordsman grips a hilt. The ridged nails and the old brand at the wrist show where the white cuff has slid back.

> CAPTION: The stylus felt heavier than any sword.

**5.5** Full width, bottom. Nagoji bent over the blank leaf, and flowing out of the lamplight around him, one bleeding into the next like smoke: his mother tying a black thread charm on a young man's wrist; Padmini, her hair still black, handing a brass cup of water under the jackfruit tree; Keshavrao's hand slipping from a rope in a storm-black sea; De Lannoy's profile against a half-built bastion; a boy high on a palm tower, shading his eyes toward far sails.

> CAPTION: I began to write.

> CAPTION: Not quickly, not slowly.

## PAGE 6

Five panels. What he wrote and what he left out, drawn as light and shadow. Then the verdict, and his line in the sand.

**6.1** Wide. Nagoji writing, stylus scoring the leaf, Ramayyan motionless across the table. Inset, upper corner, sun-hard: Colachel beach, a Dutch square hemmed on a narrow strip of sand, boots sinking in wet ground.

> CAPTION: I wrote of Colachel, but not of every detail.

> CAPTION: I wrote of European arrogance, how it made them underestimate Indian infantry.

**6.2** A new star bastion on a Travancore wall, a battery of guns on it, masons patching a section of wall below. The panel is drawn half in lamp-shadow, as if a hand had passed across it.

> CAPTION: I did not mention the new bastions' angles, the range of certain batteries, the weaknesses we had patched and those we had only marked for later.

**6.3** Close on Nagoji writing, jaw set, the lamp guttering.

> CAPTION: I wrote that the confederacy would need to treat coastal houses as partners, not possessions.

> CAPTION: I did not write that such understanding came with a cost my old masters had never seemed willing to pay.

**6.4** Ramayyan reading the finished leaf in silence, eyes stopped on one line. Nagoji across from him flexing his aching writing hand.

> RAMAYYAN: You have given them enough to avoid dying stupidly if they ever fight Europeans on beaches.

> RAMAYYAN: You have not given them a clear path through our forts.

**6.5** Full width, bottom. Nagoji, level, forearms on the table.

> NAGOJI: If they do come, I would rather they hit Dutch and other company men effectively than stumble into our guns like idiots.

> NAGOJI: But I will not pave their road over Padmini's fields or Velinadu's shrines.

## PAGE 7

Five panels. The good letter, and why a good letter is not enough. End on the warning, with the leaves already at the flame.

**7.1** Ramayyan nodding, gathering the written leaf toward himself.

> RAMAYYAN: The messenger waits at the border. He is patient.

> NAGOJI: And if he is not?

> RAMAYYAN: Then his horse will sink in sand one day and I will not weep.

**7.2** Tight insert. Ramayyan's hand reaching for the leaf. Nagoji's hand coming down over his, pinning it.

> NAGOJI: No.

**7.3** Ramayyan looking up, eyebrows raised. Nagoji's hand still covering his.

> NAGOJI: It is a good letter. It allows me to keep a foot in the north. To answer a friend. To be a Maratha who helps Travancore.

> RAMAYYAN: Yes.

**7.4** Close on Nagoji. Nothing theatrical. Quiet certainty.

> NAGOJI: I am not a Maratha who helps Travancore.

> NAGOJI: Not anymore.

**7.5** Full width, bottom. Nagoji standing at the wall niche with both leaves in his hand, the answer and the Deccan letter, their edges a finger's width from the lamp flame. Ramayyan still seated behind him, turned in his chair to watch.

> RAMAYYAN (soft): Ananthan. Once you do this, there is no echo. You become silent to them.

## Adaptation notes

- **Page count** is 12 against a target of 11, inside the 9 to 13 range. The extra page goes to the burning (page 8, a near-silent climax) and to the Duarte reveal, which is a second full turn in the chapter and needs pages 10 and 11 to land. Exposition is compressed to pay for it.
- **Turban changed.** The novel has Nagoji shake rain from his turban. CONTINUITY.md overrides on appearance (topknot, no turban from ch17; uniform from ch24), so he shakes rain from his coat instead.
- **Setting.** The novel names only "the fort" and the war hall. Udayagiri in 1.1 is a staging choice (De Lannoy's fort, where the chapel of Chapter 28 stands); change it if an earlier script fixed Ramayyan's office elsewhere. The horse is left unnamed because the novel does not name Nagoji's mount this late.
- **The opening ride** on the marches is cut to one caption ("my clothes were damp and my temper short").
- **The letter** is cut to its essentials: the greeting, Chanda Sahib, the offer, the threat, and the ask. "You have seen their methods now," the Colachel jibe and "Think on this" are dropped. The narrator's gloss on the Bhonsle of Nagpur is carried by the image of Chanda Sahib being led away in 2.2.
- **Cut from the reasoning scene:** "How long have you had this?" and its answer, and "They offer you land, status, a place beside men whose blood you share." "You brought me here knowing this" is folded into Ramayyan's "Ask yourself why."
- **The temptation visions** (4.1 to 4.3) are drawn rather than captioned. The father, the ghee and the nephews are art only; the caption repeats the letter's three promises, which is the point. "Travancore forts with their star teeth turned inward" becomes the image in 4.2.
- **The memory montage** (5.5) keeps five of the six images. The brother's letter about Pune mispronouncing Marthanda Varma's name is cut because it cannot be drawn without text.
- **The writing** is compressed from five paragraphs to three captioned panels, alternating what he wrote and what he withheld, with the withheld things (bastions, batteries, patched walls) drawn in shadow. The internal rifts among Travancore's chiefs are cut for space.
- **"We watched them burn until the ash fell onto the stone floor"** is not lettered; 8.2 and 8.3 show it.
- **Ramayyan's "You understand what you have done... You have closed a door on one future"** is trimmed to its last three sentences.
- **"Since you have proven yourself today, you may as well know the shape of other pieces on the board"** is cut to "There is one more thing."
- **The Duarte section** loses "He absolved them of their doubts and pointed them south," the dungeon recollection paragraph (replaced by the Chapter 1 callback shot in 10.3), "I did not know whether to laugh or weep," "And you let me threaten him / I let you have your moment," "Does he know I know now?", the promise of many future meetings, "Your reach is longer than I imagined," and "That does not erase what he did. But it may change what it means." What stays is the fact, the history, whether any of it was real, and where the two men now stand.
- **The chaplain** is kept (12.1 and 12.2) because it sets up the chapel scene with De Lannoy and Duarte in Chapter 28. Goa's "ghosts in the corridors" is cut.
- **Revathi's letter** drops its first sentence ("You have done what I expected and feared"). "Ramayyan's test had not been whether I loved my hills more than this coast" is cut; the final image of Nagoji on the wall carries it.
- **Echoes built in:** 9.2 mirrors Chapter 1 panel 1.2 (a hand making an entry that decides who he is), and 10.3 repeats Chapter 1 panel 8.3. "The entry had been made" in 12.5 closes that loop.
- **Verbatim lines kept:** "The ola came on a day that smelled of ink and rain," the sardar's "killed or much land," "offering the latter and implying the former," "Everything is a trick," "I prefer to test a wall before the storm does," "Half truths / Selected truths," "The stylus felt heavier than any sword," "I will not pave their road over Padmini's fields or Velinadu's shrines," "I am not a Maratha who helps Travancore. Not anymore," "Once you do this, there is no echo," "My sword is here. My words stay here," the record entry, "You keep it in your bones," "He has been in my pay for eleven years," "On the same side of the wall. Though you came to it by different doors," Padmini's shelf exchange, and the chapter's last line.
- **Print.** Per the author, every drawn panel must reach 300 DPI or more at its printed size; upscale 2x or regenerate any frame that falls short (CONTINUITY.md, print rule).
