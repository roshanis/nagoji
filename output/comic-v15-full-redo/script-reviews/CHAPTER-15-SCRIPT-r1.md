## PAGE 1

Five panels. The Dutch march out under their own drums. Everyone on the beach except the Dutch knows how this ends.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. Colachel beach at mid-morning, the day after the capitulation was signed. The charred stockade and the burnt-out Dutch camp behind, ash grey on the sand, the smoke gone. Far out, Dutch ships riding small and distant. In the foreground a Dutch column forming ranks to march out: torn, soot-stained blue coats, muskets shouldered, drums slung, officers with swords at their hips. Beaten men standing as straight as they can.

> CAPTION: Colachel. The thirteenth of August, 1741.

> CAPTION: The end came with a lie, or perhaps just a misunderstanding of what honour meant on this coast.

**1.2** Medium, inside the column. Eustachius De Lannoy: tall, pale hair, his blue coat as torn and soot-stained as the rest, a fresh bandage showing. His eyes are not on the road ahead but on the silent Nair lines along it.

> CAPTION: For two days De Lannoy had carried terms between our lines and their camp. Terms, counter-terms, and the slow grinding of pride against reality.

> CAPTION: Yesterday, they signed.

**1.3** Nagoji on horseback at the edge of the dunes, Dhanaji mounted at his shoulder. Nagoji in the commander look: rust-red turban, cream tunic, broad red sash, cream trousers, boots, talwar; thick curled moustache, clean-shaven chin, small gold ear stud. Behind them, half hidden in a hollow of the dunes, a glimpse of waiting riders and the muzzle of a matchlock.

> DHANAJI: They look relieved.

> DHANAJI: They think they are going for a long walk and then a boat home.

**1.4** Close on Nagoji, eyes on the column.

> NAGOJI: The King does not let tigers walk away because they promise not to bite.

**1.5** Wide, low angle up the rise where the main battery stood. Marthanda Varma waiting there, spear upright, in cream and gold with the peacock crest. Below him Nair infantry line both sides of the Dutch path, silent and impassive. At Varma's side a Nair holds a white conch shell at his hip, not yet raised.

## PAGE 3

Five panels. Ramayyan's answer, De Lannoy's counsel, and the moment the column could still choose to fight.

**3.1** Ramayyan on his horse, unmoved, hands easy on the reins.

> RAMAYYAN: Terms change when kings have time to think.

> RAMAYYAN: You burned our temples. You took our people as slaves. Did you think we would simply wave goodbye?

**3.2** De Lannoy stepping to Donnadi's side, speaking low, close to his ear. Lettering: dotted whisper balloon; translated Dutch is shown in angle brackets.

> DE LANNOY (in Dutch): <Joseph. Look around you. This is not a battle we can win.>

> DE LANNOY (in Dutch): <The alternative is worse than surrender.>

**3.3** Ramayyan, calm, not raising his voice.

> RAMAYYAN: You have marched out.

> RAMAYYAN: Now you will drop the arms.

**3.4** Close, a row of Dutch hands tightening on musket stocks. In the middle of them, Donnadi's knuckles white on his sword hilt.

> CAPTION: For a moment, I thought they would fight.

> CAPTION: Twenty to one, starving and shocked. But they were soldiers.

**3.5** Nagoji in the saddle, a brass spyglass to his eye.

> CAPTION: I had seen that expression before. On Keshavrao's face in the storm. On my own in the Goan dungeon.

## PAGE 4

Four panels. Donnadi's decision, slowly, in the order the novel gives it.

**4.1** Circular spyglass view, a round vignette in a black surround. Donnadi's face filling it, looking up at the sky, lips moving without sound.

> CAPTION: The moment when a man realizes that every road leads to the same wall.

**4.2** Beside Donnadi, a drummer boy of no more than fourteen, rigid, his hands shaking on his drumsticks. Donnadi's hand resting on the boy's shoulder. The boy's face on the edge of crumpling, and steadying.

> CAPTION: Something passed between them. Permission, perhaps, or apology.

**4.3** Tight. Donnadi's hands holding his unbuckled sword belt. His thumb tracing the VOC monogram on the brass of the hilt, the metal catching the sun.

> DONNADI (a short, sharp oath): Verdomme.

> CAPTION: That monogram had been his identity since he was a boy in the Low Countries, dreaming of foreign shores.

**4.4** Full width, sand-level. The sword and belt lying in the sand, grains still settling around them. Donnadi's boots above; the column behind him, waiting.

> CAPTION: A soft thud, not a clatter. A quiet death for a quiet surrender.

> DONNADI (off, cracked balloon): Down.

## PAGE 5

Four panels. The arms go down, and the prisoners go inland, not south.

**5.1** Large, about half the page. The whole column letting its muskets fall onto the beach at once. Some men weeping, others rigid, staring at nothing. The drummer boy's drum set down on the sand. In the foreground a sergeant on his knees, forehead pressed to his fallen musket like a man saying goodbye to an old friend.

> CAPTION: It sounded like a heavy rain.

**5.2** Varma on the rise, spear grounded. He does not raise his voice.

> VARMA: Take them.

**5.3** The disarmed prisoners marched inland along a red earth road between palms, Nair guards on both flanks. The sea is behind them now.

> CAPTION: Twenty-four European officers. Hundreds of men.

> CAPTION: We did not march them to Kanyakumari.

**5.4** Wide, bottom. The granite walls of Udayagiri Fort rising against green hills. In the prisoner column below, De Lannoy walks with his head up, his eyes travelling along the walls and bastions, the only prisoner not looking at the ground.

> CAPTION: As if he were already measuring them for improvement.

## PAGE 6

Five panels. Ibrahim Marakkar comes in with the surf. A friend who is not quite one.

**6.1** Wide. Morning on the beach. A small boat sliding in through the breakers, a second behind it loaded with crates and bundles in oiled cloth. Ibrahim Marakkar stepping barefoot onto the sand: shorter than his boatmen but broader, a scar from his LEFT ear to his jaw, short coat over a vest with bulging pockets. He looks over the wrecked Dutch camp the way a merchant looks over goods at auction.

> CAPTION: The next morning, before the prisoners were even counted, Ibrahim Marakkar came.

**6.2** Nagoji walking down off the dune to meet him.

> NAGOJI: You move quickly.

> IBRAHIM: The sea moves quickly. I merely follow its currents.

**6.3** Ibrahim's open hand sweeping across the burned stockade and the scattered rope, metal and canvas.

> IBRAHIM: There will be salvage. Things that have no flag now.

> NAGOJI: Those belong to the king.

> IBRAHIM: Some do.

**6.4** His crew unloading the second boat: sacks of rice, bundles of dried fish, jars of coconut oil. Nagoji looking at the sacks, not at Ibrahim.

> IBRAHIM: For the prisoners. Marthanda Varma will remember who fed his new recruits.

> IBRAHIM: A man who is remembered kindly by both sides is a man who survives.

> NAGOJI: Is that all you care about? Surviving?

**6.5** Wide, bottom strip. Ibrahim wading back toward his boats, turning his head to answer, and for once the merchant's mask has slipped. Nagoji on the sand in the foreground, his back to us.

> IBRAHIM: I care about this coast. The sea belongs to no one.

> IBRAHIM: It only lends itself to those clever enough to use it.

> CAPTION: Which was more dangerous: an enemy you could see, or a friend you could never quite trust?

## PAGE 7

Five panels. No rest. Blue coats out of Kanyakumari, and a lesson in where not to chase them.

**7.1** The edge of the dunes. Varma with his eyes on the sea, Nagoji at his side. Behind them a runner, still panting, hands a palm-leaf message to Ramayyan.

> CAPTION: The victory did not give us the luxury of rest. Fresh blue coats had slipped in under the headlands at Kanyakumari.

> VARMA: I will not let the company write a second ending to this battle while my men are drinking water.

**7.2** Varma pointing south, down the coast to where the land narrows and the sea presses close. Behind Nagoji, Dhanaji is already swinging into his saddle.

> VARMA: Ride. Find what has come out of Kanyakumari and break its teeth. But do not chase it into stone.

> DHANAJI: If you are going to ride into trouble, I will be close enough to drag you out.

**7.3** Wide, pure speed. The gallop south along the beach track, coconut palms blurred into a green wall. Nagoji leading, jaw clenched, one hand pressed for a moment to his bruised ribs. Dhanaji beside him; Maravar scouts and Madurai lancers strung out behind.

> CAPTION: Pain was a tax you paid for staying alive.

**7.4** The clash where the siege trenches and palm-log barricades bend around a shallow stream. A handful of Dutch with fixed bayonets caught on wet sand as the horsemen hit them, their own volley smoke blowing back into their faces. Nagoji wheeling left at the last moment; Dhanaji driving in from the right with the Maravar scouts. Staging and motion only, no wounds shown.

> CAPTION: They tried to lock into the comfort of drill.

> CAPTION: But drill is a luxury when hooves are already inside your breath.

**7.5** Wide, bottom. The Dutch falling back south into the haze, an officer limping between two of his men. In the foreground a runner at Nagoji's stirrup with a palm-leaf message, and Nagoji holding his sword across his chest: the signal to halt.

> RUNNER: Order from the Dalawa. Do not pursue. Not into their guns.

> NAGOJI: Let them run. Let them carry the story back.

> NAGOJI: Today they learned that even the road home has teeth.

**11.1** Nagoji, level, from the door.

## Adaptation notes

- **Page count.** 14 pages against a target of 13, which is inside the 10 to 16 range. The extra page goes to the two scenes that carry the chapter: the surrender (pages 1 to 5) and De Lannoy's turning, in the audience and the coat scene (pages 8 to 14). The exposition is compressed to make room for them.
- **Negotiation.** The two days of De Lannoy carrying terms are compressed into the two captions in 1.2.
- **Donnadi's decision.** In the novel he looks at the sky, his boots, the drummer boy, his men, the Nair ring, the sea and De Lannoy. The script keeps the sky and the boy. The simile about the ships as "spectators" is cut, but the ships stay visible in 1.1. His oath "Verdomme" gives a word to the novel's "He swore". The drummer's drum on the sand (5.1) is a small visual addition.
- **Ibrahim.** Cut: his grandfather and the history of wreck salvage, the "safe distance" exchange, "Generous / Practical", "three hundred years", and the salvage inventory promised to Ramayyan. Kept: his arrival from the sea, the currents line, "no flag", feeding the prisoners as "new recruits", "remembered kindly by both sides", "the sea belongs to no one", and Nagoji's closing doubt, turned into the page's question.
- **Kanyakumari sortie.** Compressed to one page. Cut: the tactical detail, the point-blank miss, taking a man with the flat of the blade for questioning, the casualty count, "My horse wanted to follow. So did my blood.", and Nagoji's reasoning about fighting under their stone. Kept: the order ("break its teeth", "do not chase it into stone"), Dhanaji's promise, the pain-as-tax line, the drill line, the runner's order and "even the road home has teeth".
- **Tharakan.** The passage about the Syrian Christian houses and the kalari is cut down to one name caption and "loud as any cannon". His new silk and gold chains follow CONTINUITY.md for ch15.
- **Audience.** Cut: "I have men. Donnadi. The others." with Varma's reply, "You rise as high as your skill takes you", and the "Tomorrow? / The war isn't over" exchange. Everything else is kept in the novel's order, mostly word for word.
- **Coat scene.** Cut: "the first thing I bought with my own wages", the two fumbles and the Flemish curse (the tuck holding in 13.4 stands in for them), and the "ghost of himself" image, which is now art direction in 13.4. "He did not smile" is also in the art direction (14.2) rather than a caption. Nagoji's Portuguese brand is visible in 12.4, so he also carries the mark of a flag he left. This is a visual echo only and is not captioned.
- **POV.** In the novel Nagoji hears De Lannoy's quiet Dutch in 3.2 although he is watching from a distance. The script keeps the line as a whisper balloon, because the book establishes that Nagoji knows the language (12.1).
- **Continuity flags.** Donnadi and the drummer boy are not in CONTINUITY.md. 2.3 gives Donnadi a working look, which should be added to the bible if he returns in ch16. Nagoji is in the commander sheet, with no grey at the temples yet. De Lannoy wears the stained blue coat until 13.2 and white mundu and tunic after that, as the bible sets for ch15.
