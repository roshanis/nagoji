# Horse of the Servant, graphic novel V15

## Chapter 2: The Slave Ship South

## PAGE 1

Five panels. The waking. João, the chain, and a friend who will not look back.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. The cellar from Chapter 1 in the grey before dawn, the slit window barely paler than the stone. Chained men stirring along the curved wall. At the top of the stair the door stands open and torchlight spills down it: João, broad and ruddy in his leather jerkin, key ring at his wide belt, three Portuguese guards behind him. Nagoji in the foreground, three-quarter back view, head already lifted. Nagoji per the captivity sheet: torn ochre sleeveless tunic, cream dhoti, bandaged hands and wrists, ankle irons, barefoot, long curly hair loose, thick moustache, clean chin with light stubble at most, small gold ear stud.

> CAPTION: They woke us under cover of bells.

> CAPTION: I counted the strokes.

> JOÃO: Up! Those marked go to the docks.

**1.2** João working down the line, a leather strip painted with marks in one hand (symbols only, nothing legible). He taps one man with it, skips two, cuffs a sleeper awake with the back of his hand. Small musical notes at his lips.

> CAPTION: The same hymn the bells had played.

**1.3** João standing over Nagoji without a glance at the tally scratches on the wall behind him. A guard crouches at Nagoji's feet, unlocking the long bar that runs through his ankle irons. The cuffs stay on; only the bar comes away.

> CAPTION: He knew where I sat. He had spent too many hours standing over me.

> JOÃO: On your feet, marata. The Viceroy has found a use for you.

**1.4** Nagoji rising in one movement, back straight, jaw set. His body wants to fold and he will not let it. João already turning away, uninterested.

> CAPTION: My legs swung free, terrifyingly light.

> CAPTION: I forced myself not to limp.

**1.5** Full width, bottom. Six men on one length of chain at the foot of the stair: wrists manacled, iron collars clamped at the neck. Two places ahead of Nagoji, a young man with narrow shoulders and black hair hacked short, his right hand twitching at his empty hip where a sword hilt used to hang. A guard with a club walks between them. The young man's head has jerked, very slightly. He does not turn.

> CAPTION: Keshavrao. One of my riders.

> NAGOJI (whisper): Rao.

## PAGE 2

Five panels. Up into the light, and the priest at the gate. This is Duarte's second hesitation after the nod in Chapter 1; it pays off in chapters 24 and 25, so give it the page.

**2.1** The chain of men climbing a turning stone stair, seen from below. The stone is black at the bottom of the frame and bleaches toward white at the top, where daylight waits.

> CAPTION: The climb was like being born through a tunnel of stone.

> CAPTION: Oil. Bread. Sea.

**2.2** Wide. A courtyard of whitewashed walls under a glaring sky. The prisoners flinch and squint, raising chained hands against the light. Portuguese musketeers line the walls: broad hats tilted, buff leather coats, matchlock muskets, matchcords coiled at their belts. They must not read as British redcoats or Dutch blue-coats. Far across the yard, near the gate, one thin black figure in a broad travel hat.

> *No text. Let the glare hurt.*

**2.3** Over Nagoji's shoulder, the chain shuffling past the gate. Duarte close now: broad hat shading grey, tired eyes, a leather satchel over one shoulder, plain black cassock with a small cross, no white collar tab, scholar's stoop. His gaze has travelled the length of the chain and found Nagoji. Their eyes meet.

> CAPTION: Father Duarte. I expected triumph, or at least satisfaction.

> CAPTION: The stubborn Maratha, broken at last.

**2.4** Tight on Duarte. His jaw has tightened. His ink-stained right hand rises, slow and deliberate, and makes a small sign of the cross toward the chain. Not a flinch. A gesture he means.

> CAPTION: A blessing, perhaps. Or an apology.

**2.5** Full width, bottom. Background: Duarte's back as he passes through a side door into the fortress, the door half shut behind him. Foreground: Nagoji's face in profile, still watching the door, the chain pulling him on.

> CAPTION: Men who hesitate are men who can be used.

> CAPTION: Someday, I thought, that hesitation might matter.

## PAGE 3

Four panels. The town and the harbour, compressed to one page. Make 3.2 the largest panel on the page.

**3.1** A narrow street, the houses leaning inward, balconies almost touching overhead. Goan Catholics in clean cotton stepping aside, hands over their mouths and noses. In a doorway a Hindu merchant stands with a blank face, his eyes on the chain.

> CAPTION: The Hindu merchants measured us, and weighed the value of the bodies in chains.

**3.2** Large, about half the page. The harbour. Masts clustered against the sky like a forest of stripped trees; gulls; ropes and water. One ship looms over the rest, hull black and high, her stern built up like a little fortress of carved wood and painted saints. On a wharf platform beneath her, a man in a broad-brimmed hat checks a list against the lines of chained prisoners. Nagoji's chain small in the foreground. Keep open sky at the top for lettering.

> PRISONER (behind): To Angola. Africa.

> OLD PRISONER: Not this one. The Jesuits need bodies for their lands to the south.

> OLD PRISONER: They will work us until we forget our own names.

**3.3** The gangplank, slick with sea and tar. João at the top, hauling on the chain. Tight on Nagoji's bare feet on the planks as they flex, a strip of dark water glinting through the gap below.

> JOÃO: Faster. The tide waits for no man. Not even maratas.

> CAPTION: An old, absurd thought.

> CAPTION (separate, weighted): Horses would hate this.

**3.4** Full width, bottom. The deck. Sailors hauling lines around the prisoners, some dark-skinned men from other parts of the empire, some pale Europeans burned red by the sun. Canvas cracking overhead. A guard driving the chain toward an open hatch, a black square in the planks. Nagoji at its lip, looking back once toward the shore: the white town, the fortress, the hills beyond.

> CAPTION: The world of land, with its forts and fields and familiar dust, already felt far away.

## PAGE 4

Five panels. The hold. Keshavrao speaks at last.

**4.1** Wide. The hold. Wooden shelves along both sides, divided into little pens by upright planks, four or five chained men packed into each. Iron rings bolted into the beams overhead take the chains. The guards' lantern retreating up the ladder, the dark closing in behind it.

> *No text. Let the reader take in the pens.*

**4.2** Insert, tight. The plank just above Nagoji's head, scratched with lines of worn, abstract marks (not legible letters of any real script). His bandaged fingertips resting on them.

> CAPTION: Prayers, in a script I could not read.

> CAPTION: Another prisoner, another ship, another journey that ended somewhere in chains.

**4.3** Close two-shot in the dark, faces a hand's width apart. Keshavrao, in the same pen by accident or indifference, has turned his head at last.

> KESHAVRAO: Sahib.

> CAPTION: In camp I had insisted he use my name. Now there was comfort in the old habit.

**4.4** Nagoji answering. Keshavrao's small breathless laugh turning into a cough, his narrow shoulders hunching against the collar.

> NAGOJI: Rao. You are still alive.

> KESHAVRAO: For now.

**4.5** Full width, bottom. Their point of view, straight up through the hatch far above: a square of blue sky cut by strips of rigging. A gull crosses it, a white streak against the blue.

> CAPTION: And the ship began to move.

## PAGE 5

Five panels. The long dark. What Keshavrao fears, what he used to be, and the men above who put a price on them.

**5.1** The pen in dim light through a hatch grating. Days have passed: a wooden trough of rice and dried fish, men coughing along the shelves, a sick man sagging against a stronger one. Nagoji and Keshavrao sit shoulder to shoulder.

> CAPTION: Time on a ship is different from time on land.

> KESHAVRAO: Do you know where they send us?

> NAGOJI: South. That much is certain.

**5.2** Keshavrao, low and bitter, eyes on the planks.

> KESHAVRAO: They need men to cut cane. Fields of sugar as tall as a man.

> KESHAVRAO: We will sweat for their salvation.

**5.3** Present Keshavrao, silent, head down. Set into the corner as an inset with a soft, stained edge like the mare in Chapter 1: a warm-toned memory of Keshavrao bareheaded on horseback near Chaul, mouth open in song, musket smoke behind him.

> CAPTION: Near Chaul he rode bareheaded, singing over the muskets, daring the Portuguese to shoot the song out of his mouth.

> CAPTION: The dungeon had scraped that away.

**5.4** Two-shot. Nagoji steady; Keshavrao half smiling for the first time, tired.

> NAGOJI: We are not there yet. As long as we move, there is a chance.

> KESHAVRAO: The sahib still counts chances.

> NAGOJI: It is what I know.

**5.5** Full width, bottom. Up-angle through the open hatch: Portuguese sailors and an officer silhouetted against a bright sky, one leaning on the coaming, laughing. Light pours down onto the upturned, sick faces in the hold. Leave the sky area clear for lettering.

> SAILOR (above): This run is cursed. The last ship took lightning in the mast.

> OFFICER (above): God sends opportunity. These bodies are silver if they live.

> OFFICER (above): Pray for their health, not their souls.

## PAGE 6

Five panels. The promise that Chapter 3 will break. Hold the quiet, then open onto the sea.

**6.1** The hatch shut again; near dark. Keshavrao shivering, knees drawn up, chain running from his collar to the ring overhead. The hull tilts a little further than it did.

> KESHAVRAO: If there is a storm, will the ship break?

> NAGOJI: All ships break. The question is when and how.

**6.2** Keshavrao's face, very quiet, not looking at Nagoji. At his side his right hand twitches, reaching for the hilt that is not there.

> KESHAVRAO: If the chains break too, I will follow wherever you jump, sahib.

**6.3** Nagoji's face, close. Nothing moves in it, but something heavy has arrived.

> CAPTION: The words settled over me heavier than iron.

> CAPTION: I had never meant to collect followers.

**6.4** Tight on hands. Nagoji's bandaged hand reaching across the narrow gap to rest on Keshavrao's forearm. Keshavrao's thin fingers closing briefly over it. Manacles on both wrists.

> NAGOJI: If there is a chance, I will not leave you.

> CAPTION: Neither of us mentioned that promises made in chains are the easiest to break.

**6.5** Full width, bottom. Exterior at dusk. The black ship small on a heaving Arabian Sea, heeling hard, sails straining, a wall of storm cloud building on the horizon with lightning far off inside it.

> CAPTION: The Arabian Sea was beginning to remind us who owned this ship.

## Adaptation notes

- **Page count.** 6 pages, 29 panels, on target. The town, the harbour and the boarding are compressed to one page (3) so the pages go to Duarte's blessing (2) and to Keshavrao (4 to 6), because his promise is the thing Chapter 3 breaks.
- **Planted for later.** Duarte's sign of the cross gets its own panel (2.4) as the second hesitation after the Chapter 1 nod, for chapters 24 and 25. Keshavrao's twitching right hand appears at 1.5 and 6.2 because Chapter 3 uses it at his last appearance. "They will work us until we forget our own names" (3.2) is kept because it echoes the Chapter 1 opening.
- **Cut as art duplication or exposition.** Duarte "did not speak, did not step forward" (shown in 2.5). The Portuguese streets, tar, fish and sailor smells (reduced to "Oil. Bread. Sea."). The hymn chanted in a coastal tongue in the hold. The stale water and festering wounds (moved into the background of 5.1). The first sailor's "Wind is with us... round the cape" line, so 5.5 fits with room for the art. The unspecified cruzados per head, folded into "silver."
- **Shortened.** "Men who feel guilt carry weight that slows them down" is cut; its first half and "Someday, I thought, that hesitation might matter" carry the beat. The Nashik reflection on leading men is reduced to "I had never meant to collect followers." The cane speech drops its middle clauses about the sun and the priest's promise.
- **Reconciled with CONTINUITY.md.** The novel unlocks "the shackles at my ankles." The captivity sheet keeps ankle irons through Chapter 3, so 1.3 unlocks the row bar and leaves the cuffs on. Duarte travels without a collar tab. João stays a gaoler in a leather jerkin. The musketeers are staged so they do not read as redcoats or VOC blue.
- **Staging choice.** The novel's last beat is heard from the hold. 6.5 cuts outside to show the storm building, which leads into Chapter 3's opening.
- **Art constraints.** No legible text in the art: João's list and the prayers in the hold are abstract marks. No gore; the sickness in the hold is shown through posture and coughing.
