## Chapter 14: The Siege of Colachel

Source: `book1_horse_servant/book3_chapter14_charge_at_colachel.md`, 2,645 words, adapted as 10 pages.

## PAGE 1

Five panels. A tired army and a stretched king. Compress the two-front exposition into one page and end on Varma's face.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. A weary Travancore column in monsoon-wet paddy, turning north. Bullocks and men haul cannon through ankle-deep mud along a narrow village bund. Mid-column, Nagoji on Kayal, his bay mare with a coat the ochre red-brown of earth after rain. Commander dress: rust-red turban, cream tunic, broad red sash, cream trousers, boots, talwar. Mud to the mare's knees. Grey sky.

> CAPTION: In 1741, by the time we closed our ring around Colachel, we were already tired from another war.

> CAPTION: The same guns had faced Chanda Sahib's lines in the south.

**1.2** The Colachel shore months earlier. Burned fishermen's huts, blackened palm thatch, upturned boats. Far out on the grey water, three Dutch ships at anchor, low smoke drifting from their gun ports.

> CAPTION: They had tested this coast the November before, and left a warning written in salt and ash.

**1.3** Insert, tight. A contract on a table. A Dutch hand in a blue cuff holds the paper flat. A woman's hand with gold bangles presses a seal into red wax. We do not see her face.

> CAPTION: Two years before Colachel, the company had decided to stop being a guest at our table.

> CAPTION: They reached for the oldest weapon on this coast, a royal house divided.

**1.4** A village street near Kottar. Dutch VOC soldiers in blue coats sit at ease on a verandah, muskets stacked, one man eating from a banana leaf. Villagers watch from doorways, silent.

> CAPTION: Desinganadu, Kochi, Purakkad, Thekkumkur. Each nursing its own wounds.

> CAPTION: For a few months they sat here as if Padmanabhapuram were already within reach.

**1.5** Full width, bottom. Marthanda Varma (V13 varma-v1 face, cream and gold) on horseback at the head of his staff, mud on his legs, reining north. Tired eyes, set jaw. Rain beginning.

> CAPTION: The Dutch had not come when Travancore was fresh and waiting.

> CAPTION: They were striking a man who had only just pushed one invader back, and had not yet found his footing again.

## PAGE 2

Five panels. The war becomes a weight. Establish the noose, the cavalry's new job, and the second front, then leave Nagoji alone at the black water.

**2.1** Wide, high angle over the siege. Our trench lines curve around the Dutch beachhead like a tightening noose, revetted with palm trunks and woven mats. Batteries puff white smoke. Inside, a shrinking blue and grey mass of men pressed against the sea. Heat haze and salt spray.

> CAPTION: The war became a weight, not a collision.

> CAPTION: There was no grand charge, no thunder of hooves as I had imagined in restless sleep. Only the slow, grinding work of earth and patience.

**2.2** At a trench parapet. Dhanaji (Deccan rider, Nagoji's age, talwar at his hip) wipes sweat from his eyes. Nagoji beside him. Beyond, a fresh plume of smoke rises from the Dutch camp.

> DHANAJI: They are stubborn.

> DHANAJI: Most men would have swum for the ships by now.

**2.3** Nagoji, eyes on the enemy camp, calm.

> NAGOJI: They are waiting for help that is not coming.

> NAGOJI: And they are afraid to admit the great Company has been trapped by a king they thought was a petty chieftain.

**2.4** Inland scrub and low palms. Ponnan (broad-shouldered Maravar, curly hair, thick moustache, clean chin, red shoulder cloth, white mundu hitched, short jacket) leads light Maravar horse weaving through the brush. A Dutch forager, water skins slung over his shoulder, has been ridden down; he drops the skins and raises his hands before a lowered lance.

> CAPTION: My riders did not charge squares. We became the gaolers of the perimeter.

> CAPTION: Ponnan's Maravar closed gaps my Deccan eyes would have missed.

**2.5** Full width, bottom. Night. Nagoji alone at the water's edge, far from the batteries, a small figure against a black, heaving sea. Monsoon cloud over the moon. Nothing visible on the water.

> CAPTION: But this war had two fronts: the land where we stood, and the sea where the fishermen ruled.

> CAPTION: No sane man sailed these waters in the dark during the rains.

*Page turn.*

## PAGE 3

Five panels. The Mukkuvar night. Antony gets the page; let him be dry and unhurried.

**3.1** Wide. The black surf, and on it, shadows. Low, narrow boats blackened with soot slip through the waves. Mukkuvar men, bodies oiled and glistening, paddle almost without a splash.

> CAPTION: But it was alive.

**3.2** A group of them dragging a craft up the sand, grinning, teeth white in the gloom. Nagoji walking toward them.

> NAGOJI: Good fishing?

**3.3** Antony, a scarred Mukkuvar headman, oiled and bare-chested, spits a stream of betel juice into the sand.

> ANTONY: Very good, Ayya. We found a supply boat coming in from a ship further out.

> ANTONY: It has a hole in its hull now. The rice is for the fish.

**3.4** Two-shot. Nagoji asking; Antony shrugs and gestures toward the dark water.

> NAGOJI: And the crew?

> ANTONY: Some swam. The sea is hungry tonight.

> ANTONY: They think big ships make them masters. But big ships must anchor. And anchors act like tethers for goats.

**3.5** Full width, bottom, at the waterline far offshore. The towering black hull of a Dutch ship. Its anchor cable runs down into the sea, and on it, half submerged, a dark swimmer with a blade in his teeth and both hands on the rope.

> ANTONY (off): We swim out, we cut, we drill.

> ANTONY (off): They are fighting the ocean, and the ocean is on our side.

## PAGE 4

Five panels. The siege tightens. Plant Tiruvattar, show the Dutch holding, and end on Ramayyan's verdict on merchants.

**4.1** Wide, dusk. Nair sappers push our trench line closer to the stockade, earth flying from their spades. Inset in the upper corner: Varma barefoot in a white cloth on the stone steps of the Adi Kesava temple at Tiruvattar, ghee lamps behind him, river water over black stone.

> CAPTION: In late May, when the air began to taste of rain, Varma rode north to Tiruvattar and begged Adi Kesava Swami for success.

> CAPTION: He came back without ceremony and ordered the trenches pushed closer.

**4.2** The Dutch camp seen over our parapet. Mud ramparts sagging under their own guns, one cannon tilted into slumped earth. Sick men lying under torn canvas. Empty barrels stacked by a wall.

> CAPTION: Hackert, the commander they had trusted, was gone. Lieutenant Rijtel held this shrinking patch of sand with sick men and empty barrels.

**4.3** Distant, through heat shimmer. A tall figure paces the Dutch perimeter, pushing men into a gap, pointing. Too far to see a face, only the height and pale hair catching the light. A thin, ragged volley puffs from the wall.

> CAPTION: Yet they held.

**4.4** Evening, under a camp awning. Ramayyan (thin, sharp fine features, receding hair, plain white cloth) stands over a spread of palm-leaf casualty reports. Varma sits on a camp stool sharpening a small knife, eyes on the distant Dutch fires. Nagoji stands at the edge of the lamplight.

> RAMAYYAN: They are stubborn. Good officers, wasted on a merchant's war.

> VARMA: They think they serve their nation.

**4.5** Full width, bottom. Ramayyan, close, lit from below by the lamp. Behind him, Varma's hand and the knife have gone still on the whetstone.

> RAMAYYAN: They serve a company.

> RAMAYYAN: Merchants who will check the cost of their rescue against the price of pepper, and decide they are too expensive.

## PAGE 5

Five panels. The 10th of August. Slow heat, a memory, an order, a gun. Build the stillness so the next page can break it.

**5.1** Wide. Sun blazing on wet sand, the air shimmering. At our main battery nearest the beach, gunners slump in the heat, one asleep against a wheel. The Dutch stockade beyond, quiet.

> CAPTION: The 10th of August. The end, when it came, arrived on a wind from the sea.

> CAPTION: The siege stank of unburied waste and rotting fish and burnt powder.

**5.2** Insert, memory. A palm leaf on a floor, a hand with a stick of charcoal (Karl August's: pale, sickly yellow cast, rough cotton cuff). The coastline, a small square, one corner shaded.

> CAPTION: I thought of Karl August's charcoal marks on Ramayyan's leaf. The little shaded square, where powder and rice lay too close together.

**5.3** Nagoji on Kayal, reining up beside a Nair captain at the battery.

> NAGOJI: Wake them up.

> NAGOJI: If the Dutch think we are sleeping, they might try a sortie.

**5.4** The gun crew at work: one sponges the bore, one rams the charge home, a third squints along the barrel against the glare, hand on the elevation wedge. The barrel points at the heart of the Dutch fort.

> CAPTION: They laid it by habit toward the heart of the fort, where Ramayyan had quietly told them the enemy kept its teeth.

**5.5** Full width, bottom. The gun fires. A puff of white smoke, the barrel kicking back. Nagoji watching, still.

> CAPTION: I do not believe in luck. I believe in preparation meeting opportunity.

> CAPTION: But sometimes, war throws dice.

*Page turn.*

## PAGE 6

Five panels, one of them half the page. The explosion. Let the art carry it; the white-out panel takes no text at all.

**6.1** Wide and bright. The ball, a dark speck against a hard blue sky, arcing over the Dutch outer wall and a row of tents where the sick lie.

> CAPTION: It dropped with the precision of a hawk.

**6.2** Insert, tight. The heart of the Dutch camp: a small stone church used as a storehouse, barrels stacked in its doorway beside sacks of rice. A single Dutch soldier looks up.

> CAPTION: For a heartbeat, nothing happened.

**6.3** Large, half the page. White-out. A pillar of flame and boiling black smoke stands over the Dutch camp, debris spinning in its heart. In the foreground our forward trench, men flung flat by the blast. Kayal rearing, Nagoji hauling on the reins, his turban end whipping back.

> *No text. Hold the silence.*

**6.4** Dhanaji at Nagoji's stirrup, shouting up at him, face streaked with dust. Letter his balloon with a thin, wavering border: Nagoji hears him through ringing ears.

> DHANAJI: The magazine! They hit the powder room!

> CAPTION: The church, Karl August had said. Stone walls keep damp out better than canvas.

**6.5** Full width, bottom. Fire sweeping through the stockade on the wind, secondary explosions blooming along its length. Tiny distant figures running between burning huts. Keep them small and far.

> CAPTION: I thought the explosion would break them.

> CAPTION: I was wrong.

*Page turn.*

## PAGE 7

Five panels. The sortie. The Dutch come out fighting, the centre holds, and Nagoji sees the seam.

**7.1** Wide. Through smoke and flame, blue coats and grey coats step out of the burning gaps in their wall. Not scattering: forming ranks, bayonets fixed, muskets levelled.

> DHANAJI (off): They're coming out!

**7.2** The first Dutch volley. A long bank of musket smoke along their line; our forward trench, men ducking and falling back from the parapet. Staging and distance only.

> CAPTION: They fired and reloaded like men who had drilled until the motions lived in their bones.

**7.3** Our centre. Ramayyan in a mud-splattered white dhoti, a curved Nair sword raised, among Nair infantry who brace shields, swords and spears into a wall against the oncoming bayonets.

> RAMAYYAN: Hold!

> RAMAYYAN: Hold the centre!

**7.4** The left flank, a grove of palms. Nagoji on Kayal among Maravar and Madurai horse, Ponnan near him, all hidden in shade. Nagoji leaning forward in the saddle, staring through the smoke.

> CAPTION: They were not running to the sea. They were cutting their way out.

**7.5** Full width, bottom. Nagoji's view: the Dutch square from the side. Its front ranks pressing the Nair line, its rear watching the burning fort, and its flank facing the scrubland thinned to a single broken file as officers pull men forward.

> CAPTION: From the palms I saw what Ramayyan might have missed in the smoke.

> CAPTION: The square had a seam.

## PAGE 8

Five panels. The charge. Speed, then impact, then the square coming apart. No blood; the violence is in the staging.

**8.1** Nagoji drives Kayal out of the palms at a gallop, talwar drawn. Behind him the Maravar and Madurai riders burst from the grove in a single wave.

> CAPTION: I did not wait for an order. I did not signal.

> CAPTION: My riders followed like water through a broken dam.

**8.2** Rider's-eye view at speed, the horse's ears in the foreground. Dutch faces turning, eyes widening. A sergeant with his mouth open, arm out, trying to wheel his men. Bayonets catching sunlight at angles that will never meet a horse.

> CAPTION: The last hundred yards stretched like hours.

**8.3** Wide. Impact. Our horse slams into the side of the square: horses rearing, steel flashing, men going down in dust and smoke. The ordered block of blue coats buckles.

> CAPTION: A square that turns is no longer a square. It is a crowd.

**8.4** Inside the break. Dhanaji beside Nagoji, then ahead of him, sword raised. Deccan riders pour through the gap. Beyond them, Dutch soldiers fighting in knots, back to back, while the Nair infantry press in from the front.

> CAPTION: Too close for their muskets. Too fast for their bayonets.

**8.5** Full width, bottom strip. Low and quiet. A Dutch musket drops into the trampled sand. Beyond it, out of focus, hands going up.

> CAPTION: And then, as suddenly as it had begun, it was over.

## PAGE 9

Five panels. Sunset, surrender, and a stranger who asks to be useful.

**9.1** Wide. Sunset in lurid purples and reds through the smoke. The stockade burned down to glowing embers. The Dutch survivors huddled on the beach, some kneeling, some standing hollow-eyed with hands raised, their blue coats stained with ash.

> CAPTION: They had not surrendered meekly.

> CAPTION: They had come out fighting, and it had taken our charge to end them.

**9.2** Through the haze, a tall, pale-haired man in a torn, soot-stained blue coat walks out from the Dutch lines toward ours, hands open at his sides. Behind him, Rijtel and the other surviving officers watch, hollow-eyed.

> CAPTION: He called to us in Portuguese.

> DE LANNOY: I am Eustachius De Lannoy. Captain of the Dutch East India Company.

> DE LANNOY: I speak for the garrison.

**9.3** Ramayyan steps forward to meet him, sword still in hand, dhoti splattered with mud and soot.

> RAMAYYAN: You speak for men who burned our temples and took our people as slaves.

> RAMAYYAN: Why should we listen?

**9.4** De Lannoy, close. He does not flinch.

> DE LANNOY: Because dead men cannot teach you how to build what we just lost.

> DE LANNOY: And I would rather build than rot.

**9.5** Full width, bottom. Nagoji on Kayal at the edge of the group, watching De Lannoy with narrowed eyes, the smoke of the ruined fort behind them both.

> CAPTION: It was the first time I heard those words from his own mouth. Later, I would learn what they meant.

> CAPTION: In that moment, they sounded like a man who had already decided which side of the wall he wanted to stand on.

## PAGE 10

Five stacked panels and one inset. The Diwan's sword, the king's mercy, and the turn from noise to words.

**10.1** Wide, twilight. Nagoji rides up to Ramayyan. The Diwan stands in his mud-and-soot-splattered white dhoti, wiping the curved Nair sword with a piece of silk, the motion as careful as calligraphy.

> CAPTION: He had led the infantry centre himself. A general who cannot count is a butcher. Ramayyan could count, and he could kill.

> NAGOJI: They are done.

**10.2** Insert, tight. The blade sliding home into its sheath.

> RAMAYYAN: Yes. They are done.

**10.3** Ramayyan turns to look at the king. Varma on horseback, looking past them at the miserable huddle on the sand and, far out, the Dutch ships turning away in the deepening twilight. A Nair captain at his stirrup.

> CAPTAIN: Shall we finish them?

**10.4** Varma, close. Still, unhurried, the embers red on one side of his face.

> VARMA: No.

> VARMA: Dead men cannot sign treaties. And they cannot teach us how to build what they just lost.

**10.5** Varma turning his horse away from the beach, speaking over his shoulder. Nagoji and Ramayyan watch him go.

> VARMA: Let them simmer tonight. Let them taste the ash.

> VARMA: Tomorrow, we will see what remains.

**10.6** Full width, bottom. The smoking ruin of the fort under a last red sky. A line of prisoners being led away along the beach. Nagoji on Kayal in silhouette, small against it.

> CAPTION: The explosion had wounded them. The cavalry had finished it.

> CAPTION: Now came the words.

> CAPTION (separate, weighted): And words, in my experience, could be sharper than any sword.

## Adaptation notes

- **Two-front exposition compressed to page 1.** Anchuthengu, the Madurai claim, borrowed muskets, English favours and Ramayyan's leaves full of debts are cut; the fact of the tired, stretched army is carried by the turn north in 1.1 and Varma's face in 1.5. "The first days were confusion" and the galloping messengers are folded into the same page.
- **The November shelling** is one panel (1.2) with one caption.
- **The Elayadathu deal** is shown only as a sealing hand (1.3). The princess is neither named nor shown, because the novel does not name her here and her face belongs to later chapters.
- **Cavalry as gaolers** compressed to one panel (2.4). The Madurai and Maratha riders on the wider tracks, and cutting the inland water, are implied by the forager with water skins rather than stated.
- **"The Dutch were trapped in a box where the walls were fire and the floor was water"** is cut: Antony's closing line on page 3 lands the same idea in a character's voice.
- **Tiruvattar** is an inset in 4.1 rather than a scene, to plant the temple for the return visit in chapter 16.
- **The tall figure pacing the perimeter** (4.3) is kept unnamed, as in the novel. The art gives only height and pale hair, so De Lannoy's walk-out on page 9 can land as recognition without the script asserting it.
- **"Then the world turned white"** becomes the wordless half-page panel 6.3. As a caption it would only describe the art.
- **"I was already riding when I saw it"** is dropped. In a caption it would contradict the seam-spotting panel that comes before the charge; the order on the page is see, then ride.
- **The charge is kept at full length** (pages 7 and 8) because it is the chapter's reason for existing and its title. Dhanaji's bloody sword and "the wet crunch of bodies falling" are removed under the no-gore rule; impact is staged through horses, dust and a buckling block of coats.
- **The sergeant's shout** is wordless; the novel does not give his words.
- **Ramayyan's history** (Kollam, the Desinganadu campaigns, the supply and grain routes) is reduced to one caption ending on the verbatim "A general who cannot count is a butcher. Ramayyan could count, and he could kill."
- **Verbatim lines kept:** Dhanaji's and Ramayyan's "They are stubborn," Antony's anchor and ocean lines, Ramayyan on merchants and the price of pepper, "I do not believe in luck," "A square that turns is no longer a square," De Lannoy's "I would rather build than rot," Varma's "Dead men cannot sign treaties" (which deliberately echoes De Lannoy), and the chapter's last line.
- **Page count** is 10 against a target of 9, inside the 7 to 11 range. The extra page goes to the explosion, sortie and charge sequence, paid for by compressing the opening exposition to a single page.
