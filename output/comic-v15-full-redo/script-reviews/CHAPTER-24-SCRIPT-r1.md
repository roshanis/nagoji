Five panels. The foreigner's tooth, the combined drill, the banner. Open on two men who have become a partnership, and end on the resentment it breeds.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. A new star-fort bastion of laterite and packed earth jutting from the fort wall like an angled tooth, seen from below and to one side. Dry-season light, red dust hanging in the air. On its point, two small figures under a banner staff.

> CAPTION: By the first dry season after Kollamkara's valley, the fort had a name for the new bastion.

> CAPTION: The foreigner's tooth. Half in jest, half in grudging respect.

**1.2** From the bastion, looking down on the drill ground. Maravar horsemen in pairs wheeling between marked posts along the base of the wall. Behind them a rank of Nair musketeers firing a controlled volley over the horses' backs at wooden targets in the scrub. A small field gun recoiling on well-braced wheels, white smoke rolling.

> CAPTION: A drill that would have made my younger self, and his old company captains, splutter.

**1.3** On the bastion. De Lannoy: tall, pale hair, a worn white Travancore coat patched at the elbows, shading his eyes with one hand. Nagoji beside him, arms folded. > NAGOJI: You sound almost pleased.

**1.4** Low angle up the staff. The king's emblem flies at the top. Beneath it, a smaller pennant: a plain diagonal line crossed by a curved one. Both snapping in the wind.

> CAPTION: A line for the wall. A curve for the horse. He had drawn it himself.

> LANNOY (off): If you insist on naming bastions after me, I insist on at least choosing a decent symbol.

**1.5** Full width, bottom. The two men from behind, looking out over the drill, the point of the tooth aimed outward at the scrub and the hills.

> NAGOJI: Better a tooth than a sore. At least this one bites outward.

Five panels. The coin, the threat, and the son's verdict.

## Adaptation notes

- **Page count.** 29 pages and 145 panels against a target of 28 (range 22 to 34). The extra page goes to the chapter's three dramatic centres: the coat (pages 5 to 13), Father Duarte (pages 14 to 19) and the heir (pages 20 to 25). The opening exposition and the Thoma and Avraham material pay for it.
- **Opening exposition compressed to four pages.** The chiefs who delay their levies, the sharper questions about levy structures and the officers' tightened mouths become one caption over a gap-toothed drill line (2.3). "We fight so that whoever sits on that platform cannot easily undo what we have built" is cut down to "a selfish defence", which keeps the argument's shape. Ibrahim's new flags and Padmini's "more than one kind of jaw" share one panel (4.1).
- **"I have served you in the clothes I brought from the Deccan"** becomes "in my own clothes" (7.3). CONTINUITY.md dresses him as Ananthan Pillai in mundu and topknot from chapter 17, so the novel's wording would contradict the art.
- **The Goa officer** is not in the chapter 1 to 3 scripts. He appears only in stains, with his face cropped, so he stays a coat and a pair of cuffs, which is what the chapter needs. His guards stay buff-coated to match chapter 2, so no Portuguese soldier reads as a redcoat. "Blood on the floor" is kept as speech, but the floor is never shown (11.2). The rope in 11.3 is a wrist rope on its iron ring, not a noose. Nagoji's cuff gesture in 13.3 mirrors 11.1 on purpose.
- **Captions that would describe the art were cut.** "My throat tightened" is the silent panel 7.1. "The others filed out. I stayed" is 7.5. The novel's one-word paragraph "Fear" is carried by Duarte's face in 15.4, with the caption stopping at "something I had not expected." The sequence of Duarte's expressions is drawn as three slices (15.3), not listed.
- **Chapter 1 and 2 plants paid off.** Duarte's nod (15.1, echoing 1:8.2), the wrist grab at the brazier (18.2, echoing 1:8.5) and the slow sign of the cross (18.3, echoing 2:2.4). Lannoy's "Dead men cannot testify" (9.4) is kept word for word because it echoes Duarte's "A dead prisoner cannot testify" in chapter 1.
- **Cut from the Duarte scene:** "You were right about one thing... the men who fight become something they did not expect," because "The man you tortured is gone" carries the same turn in fewer words; and "Sign whatever treaties Ramayyan puts in front of you," trimmed to "tell them what you saw here." Keshavrao's line is narrowed to his own forgiveness, with the other dead implied.
- **The heir.** The novel's "assassins' blood pooled on temple stone" and "the King's blood on white silk" become a bloodless stain of a blade and a frozen boy (20.3). Ramayyan's exchange is cut down to two lines and the page turn "The Senior Rani has concerns" (21.5). "Then teach him your accounts" is cut. The Senior Rani keeps the coin, the line and the threat, but loses "Four hundred years of women" and "a woman who had learned to carry weight"; the art carries her walk. Also cut: the Prince's "dangerous woman" exchange, the palanquin and Pillamar horse lore, and the third-month exchange on De Lannoy calling Nagoji too hard ("I have my own wars").
- **Thoma and Avraham** (three sections, about 1,500 words) are compressed to two pages. Neither recurs in chapters 25 to 28. Cut: the Dutch foundry lesson, the Malabari and Paradesi distinction, Ladino, trade routes to Basra and Amsterdam, and "Survive first. Remember always." Thoma's "That is not surrender. That is wisdom" is kept because it answers the coat question from outside the Lannoy partnership.
- **Continuity.** Nagoji is in topknot, mundu and silver chain until 13.2, and in the white coat, red waistcoat and brass buttons from then on. His chin is clean-shaven and his moustache thick throughout. Lannoy is in the white Travancore coat from page 1, because the king says "He wears the coat already." The Prince is about fourteen. Duarte is older and greyer, with no collar tab. Kayal follows the chapter 14 script.
- **Verbatim lines kept:** "Better a tooth than a sore," "Preventable deaths exhaust me," "Complicated," "Not a rabble.
