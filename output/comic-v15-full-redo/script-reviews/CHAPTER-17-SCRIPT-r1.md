**6.4** The King leaning forward, elbows on knees. The boy beside him leans forward too.

> VARMA: You are a stranger here. A man with no clan, no *tharavadu* to claim him.

> VARMA: In Travancore, a man without a house is like smoke. He drifts with the wind.

> VARMA: I cannot have my best cavalry commander drift.

**6.5** Ramayyan holding out a small scroll tied with saffron thread (no legible writing anywhere on it). The King does not take it. He looks at Nagoji over it, dry.

> VARMA: The priests say a man's caste is written in bone.

> VARMA: But I have found that even the gods' writing can be edited, if the ink is gold.

## Adaptation notes

- **Page count.** 12 pages against a target of 11. The extra page goes to the two scenes carrying the chapter: Padmini's claim on the brand (a page turn into the kiss on 8.2) and the Revathi sequence (three pages, so the romance plays on the page, not in a caption).
- **Arrival compressed.** The guards, palanquins and household reaction fold into two panels. Nagoji's "No, Highness" to the grain-wagon question is cut; a glance at Padmini does its work.
- **Nalukettu description** becomes one establishing panel (3.4) with art notes. The comparison to Padmanabhapuram and the Mint Palace is cut: it is exposition that cannot be staged in the scene.
- **Flashback.** The King's story is drawn (4.4 to 5.2) instead of being told over talking heads. "She fed me when I was starving" is cut to keep 5.5 readable.
- **The caste scroll.** "You are a Nair now, Sawant. Or you will be, once the water touches you" is cut; "water and oath" in the formal request carries it. The narrator's explanation of *tharavadu* inclusion is reduced to one caption about debts, feuds and ancestors (7.3).
- **Cellar visit, a novel inconsistency.** The King tells Valiyamma to show the boy the cellar, but Padmini stays with Nagoji and the King returns with the boy. The script has the King take the boy himself ("Come."), which keeps Padmini with Nagoji for the naming.
- **Cut for space:** the Rani's line on the mother's line ("You have no mother here. But you have Valiyamma"); "Keep it close. Let every man who sees it understand whose work you do"; "The name settled on me like a second skin"; "a *Thampuran* and trusted general of the king"; the "same soul, but the man has changed" exchange (the "man who washed up on this coast" exchange says the same thing more concretely); the "one night had already become two" paragraph, reduced to "We both knew it was a lie"; and "Some burdens are not chosen. Some names are not earned."
- **Intimacy** is implied by merging shadows on the cellar wall (12.3) and an afterward with both covered to the shoulders (12.4). The unlacing of the blouse is not shown. The chapter closes on the lamp going out.
- **Costume change (staging choice, flag for the author).** Nagoji keeps the commander look through the ceremony. On 10.1, alone that night, he has taken the turban off. From 10.3, three days later, he is in the Ananthan Pillai look per CONTINUITY.md, with the conch knife at his waist.
- **No text in the art.** The brand is drawn as ridges without legible letters, and the scroll and palm leaf carry no readable script.
- **Speaker label.** The balloons after the naming keep the label NAGOJI for lettering consistency. In-world he is now addressed as Ananthan Pillai.
