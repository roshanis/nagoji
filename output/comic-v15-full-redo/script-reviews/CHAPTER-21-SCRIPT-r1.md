## PAGE 1

Five panels. The squall, the wrong door, and the room. Hold the reveal for the half-page panel.

**1.1** Wide, full page width. A fort courtyard of laterite walls and tiled roofs under a bruised sky. A grey squall line is charging in from the sea over the far wall. Men scramble to drag oiled cloth over powder barrels. In the mud, pegs and cord mark out the footing of a new storehouse; a plan board lies abandoned, spotted with rain. Among the scattering group, Nagoji as Ananthan Pillai: Kerala topknot, no turban, cream mundu with shoulder cloth, silver chain of office, thick curled moustache, clean-shaven chin, first grey at the temples, the Goa brand on his left wrist. No knife at his waist.

> CAPTION: I had always known that Ramayyan wrote things down.

> CAPTION: What I had not seen, until that afternoon, was the scale of his book.

**1.2** Nagoji ducking through a low doorway in an inner wall, shoulder cloth pulled up over his topknot, rain sheeting off the eaves behind him. Darkness ahead.

> CAPTION: I found it by accident.

**1.3** Large, half the page. The room, seen from the doorway. Wooden shelves from floor to ceiling, packed with palm-leaf bundles, each tied with string and hung with a small clay tag bearing abstract impressed marks (not legible script). The rows recede into shadow. Grey light falls from a single high, narrow window. A small coconut-oil lamp burns on a low table in the centre. At the table, cross-legged, Ramayyan Dalawa: thin, sharp fine features, receding hair, plain white cloth, ink stains on his fingers, stylus in hand. He looks up. He is not startled.

> CAPTION: There was no dust. Someone came here often enough to keep it away.

> RAMAYYAN: You found my garden.

**1.4** Nagoji just inside the door, rainwater running from his hair and cloth, a small pool forming on the swept floor.

> NAGOJI: Garden?

**1.5** Ramayyan, still seated, lifts one ink-stained hand toward the shelves, the way a man shows a guest his trees.

> RAMAYYAN: These are the plants I tend.

> RAMAYYAN: Some bear fruit. Some only thorns.

## PAGE 2

Five panels. Nagoji finds his own name and learns what he is worth on paper.

**2.1** Nagoji moving between the shelves, turned sideways so he does not brush the bundles. Close in the foreground, a row of clay tags swinging slightly on their strings.

> NAGOJI: What are they?

> CAPTION: I had already guessed.

**2.2** Ramayyan untying a bundle with the ease of long habit, the string coming loose in one pull. He does not look at his hands.

> RAMAYYAN: Records. Of land. Of grain. Of taxes. Of temple offerings. Of letters.

> RAMAYYAN: Of men.

**2.3** Insert, tight. The top leaf of the bundle, lamp-lit, with ruled blank bands. Ramayyan's ink-stained finger stops on one entry. Draw the leaf with no marks; the LEAF lines below are lettered later directly onto the leaf in a small incised-script face, never in caption boxes. This convention holds for every LEAF line in the chapter.

> LEAF: Sawant, Nagoji.

> LEAF: Origin: Deccan, near Nashik.

> LEAF: First appearance: Goan dungeon, retrieved by Travancore allies.

> LEAF: Family: father (farmer), mother, younger brother.

**2.4** The same leaf, the finger sliding down to the last lines.

> LEAF: Loyalties: to Maratha ideal, to personal honour, increasingly to Travancore king.

> LEAF: Risks: potential Maratha recall, emotional ties northward, relationship with Revathi Bayi (alliance tied, marriage).

**2.5** Two-shot. Nagoji mid-cough, fist at his mouth. Ramayyan calm, the leaf still open in his lap.

> NAGOJI: You write this of me?

> RAMAYYAN: I would be negligent if I did not. You are a piece on the board.

> RAMAYYAN: Pieces can be lost in many ways. I keep track.

## PAGE 3

Five panels. The other names. The friends get faces; the threat gets only ink. End on the leaf he will not discuss.

**3.1** Ramayyan turning leaves in quick succession, fanned in his hands. Bleeding in at the panel's edge like a stain, not a clean frame: Padmini Amma on her verandah, thick black hair coiled at the nape, cotton sari, long walking stick planted beside her.

> LEAF: Padmini Amma. Landholdings. Kin ties. Temper.

> LEAF: Heir: Nandini, a niece.

> CAPTION: Years later he would add, in the same neat hand, the date of Padmini's death beside it.

**3.2** The next leaf. At the edge, ghosted the same way: Revathi Bayi in an indigo sari with gold, jasmine in her braid, a tali at her throat, the first grey threading her hair.

> LEAF: Revathi Bayi. Velinadu rights. Alliances. Known grudges.

> LEAF: Eight Houses of the Marches. Broken formally, roots remain.

**3.3** Insert. A leaf caught mid-turn, the light raking across it. No ghosted face this time. Only the leaf and Ramayyan's hand already moving to hide it.

> LEAF: Elayadathu Swaroopam (Kottarakkara).

> LEAF: Senior lady: Savitri. Disposition: hostile.

> LEAF: Dutch contact: confirmed. Letters intercepted: three.

**3.4** Extreme close. Ramayyan's fingertip has paused on one line and stays there a beat too long. Two short ink strokes sit under the lowest blank band.

> LEAF: Velinadu connection: blood tie to Revathi Bayi.

> LEAF (letterer: set this line on the two underline strokes): Risk assessment: HIGH. Timeline: before next monsoon.

**3.5** Nagoji's face, eyes still on the leaf as Ramayyan lays it face down on the table.

> CAPTION: He did not explain. I did not ask.

> CAPTION: Some ledgers are not meant to be read aloud.

## PAGE 4

Five panels, one inset. Everyone is watched, even the king, even the watcher. End on Ramayyan's own leaf snapping shut.

**4.1** Ramayyan tapping another leaf with one finger, deflecting. Ghosted at the panel's edge: Eustachius De Lannoy, tall, pale hair, white cotton coat, holding a folded letter to a candle flame.

> LEAF: Lannoy. Family in Europe. Letters sent and burned. Possible breaking points.

> RAMAYYAN: He worries about his soul. That is useful to know.

**4.2** Nagoji, caught between amusement and discomfort, half a smile that does not reach his eyes.

> NAGOJI: And you keep such lists on everyone? Even the king?

> RAMAYYAN (off): Especially the king. He understands that forgetting is a luxury small kingdoms cannot afford.

**4.3** Two-shot. Ramayyan standing now, one hand resting on a shelf whose tags carry the names of distant villages. Nagoji facing him across the aisle.

> NAGOJI: You watch us all.

> RAMAYYAN: Yes.

**4.4** Ramayyan, a little sad around the mouth.

> NAGOJI (off): Is that not... exhausting?

> RAMAYYAN: It is what I know how to do. You count men and hooves. I count tendencies.

> RAMAYYAN: Between us, perhaps we keep this place alive.

**4.5** Wide. Ramayyan draws a single bundle from a low shelf at his own knee and opens it a crack toward Nagoji. **Inset, lower right:** the top leaf, a hair's width from snapping shut.

> NAGOJI: You write of my loyalties. Do you write of your own?

> RAMAYYAN: Of course. Only not where anyone else can read.

> LEAF (in the inset): Ramayyan. Loyalties: to the idea of a Travancore that outlives this generation. To the king as long as he remembers that.

## PAGE 5

Five panels. The argument. Nagoji pushes back with a memory, and Ramayyan answers with his whole reason for the room.

**5.1** Wide. The two men among the shelves in silence. Rain murmuring at the high window, the lamp flame steady. Nagoji not looking at him.

> NAGOJI: Does it not feel like spying? Keeping such close watch on men who trust you?

**5.2** Ramayyan, composed, hands folded.

> RAMAYYAN: Spying is what others call watching when they disapprove of the watchers.

> RAMAYYAN: I call it preparation. Goodwill is a poor shield. Knowledge is better.

**5.3** Nagoji turning to face him, hard.

> NAGOJI: Knowledge can twist. It can make you cut men down before they have done anything, because you fear they might.

> RAMAYYAN (off): You speak like someone who has seen that happen.

**5.4** Full width, bleeding in like a stain. Pune. A sardar in silk and pearls behind a carved wooden screen, one ringed hand raised in judgement. Beside him a Brahmin astrologer with a star chart unrolled (do not use the V13 temple priest face). In the foreground a loyal cavalry captain on his knees, head bowed, his sword still sheathed at his hip. Cut before any stroke: only an executioner's shadow falls across him. Nagoji is not in frame; this is his point of view.

> NAGOJI (off, from the archive): In Pune, I watched a sardar order a loyal captain executed because a Brahmin said the stars looked wrong.

> NAGOJI (off): Fear and jealousy filled the gaps. The supposed traitor never even drew his sword.

**5.5** Back in the room. Ramayyan nodding slowly, eyes on the shelves rather than on Nagoji.

> RAMAYYAN: Fear is my enemy too. That is why I write.

> RAMAYYAN: It gives fear less room to invent.

## PAGE 6

Five panels. Ramayyan names Nagoji's real fear, then shows him his true size. End on the weariness under the composure.

**6.1** Ramayyan leaning forward over the low table, the lamp flame between them. His gaze has sharpened.

> RAMAYYAN: You fear that one day you will open a letter from your brother, and I will be reading over your shoulder.

> NAGOJI: Should I not?

**6.2** Ramayyan retying the bundle that holds Nagoji's name, knotting the string without looking.

> RAMAYYAN: If I decide to keep you from riding north, it will not be because of a letter.

> RAMAYYAN: It will be because the balance of this kingdom requires it.

**6.3** Wide, high angle. Ramayyan sliding the bundle back into its slot on a wall of hundreds of identical bundles. Nagoji small beside the table below. The shelves dwarf them both.

> RAMAYYAN: You are one line in a very long text.

> CAPTION: There was no malice in his tone. That made it harder, not easier.

**6.4** Ramayyan turning back to him. Nagoji with his arms folded.

> RAMAYYAN: You are disturbed. That is good. Men who are comfortable being watched are either fools or tyrants.

> NAGOJI: And you? Which are you?

**6.5** Tight. Ramayyan's smile, a sad twist. For the first time the weariness shows in the lines around his eyes, lit by the high window.

> RAMAYYAN: I am tired. And determined. That is all.

## PAGE 7

Five panels. The real reason for the tour. The rain eases as the talk turns hard, then the festival night surfaces.

**7.1** The rain has thinned to drizzle; the light through the high window is soft and pale. Nagoji by the door, as if ready to leave, looking back.

> NAGOJI: Why show me this? You could have sent me away.

> RAMAYYAN: Because you walk with half your weight in the north and half here.

**7.2** Two-shot, close and level. Nagoji squared up to him. Ramayyan unmoved.

> NAGOJI: I married into Velinadu. I sent my brother the final gold. I am here.

> RAMAYYAN: Marriages end. Gold runs out.

**7.3** Tight on Ramayyan.

> RAMAYYAN: Roots are harder to kill than branches.

> RAMAYYAN: You are not just a man who trains horses. You are a potential message. A symbol. A warning.

**7.4** Ramayyan with his back to Nagoji, one palm flat against the bundles on a shelf, as if feeling for a pulse in them.

> RAMAYYAN: I have already seen three serious attempts to cut his life short.

> RAMAYYAN: I would rather fill shelves than graves.

**7.5** Full width, bottom, bleeding in from the edge. Dusk on the inner stones at Padmanabhapuram. Marthanda Varma (V13 varma-v1 face), forehead bare of any crown, bare-chested in a plain mundu, sweeping the stones with a broom. Women set rows of oil-lamp flames along the walls; priests chant in the far background, small, faces not featured. A crowd presses close in a restless ring, every head and shoulder bent in reverence, except one man near the king who stands upright.

> CAPTION: I remembered that festival night.

> CAPTION: I saw him not by his face, but by the way his shoulders did not bend in reverence with everyone else's.

Five panels. The knife, the fall, the man who got away, and the ledger that answers Goa.

**8.1** Tight and fast. The upright man's knife flashing toward the king's side. Nagoji's left hand, the brand on the wrist, clamps his wrist; only Nagoji's forearm is in frame. Behind them the king's bare foot slides on spilled lamp oil, his body tipping toward the flagstones, the broom flying from his hands.

> *No text.*

**8.2** High and distant, aftermath. Broken clay pots, scattered flowers, spilled oil catching the lamplight, a dropped knife on the stones. Two still forms lie covered with temple cloth. Guards kneel around the king, who sits up with a hand to his head. At the crowd's edge a gap is closing as a third man's back vanishes into the press.

> CAPTION: We never learned whether the third man carried Dutch coin, Kochi promises, or only the old houses' grudges in his sleeve.

**8.3** Night, the present. Padmini Amma's courtyard, the ground dried. Nagoji lying on his back on the stone platform under the jackfruit tree, eyes open. A drop of rainwater hangs from a leaf above him.

> CAPTION: That night I thought of Ramayyan's books.

> CAPTION: Of my line in them. Of Revathi's. Of Padmini's. Of Lannoy's. Of the king's own.

**8.4** Split panel, two matched halves. Left: an exact recomposition of chapter 1 panel 1.2, a Portuguese clerk's hand in a dark cuff, a quill, a bound paper ledger, wet ink, cold grey light. Right: the same angle and framing, Ramayyan's ink-stained fingers, a stylus, a palm leaf, warm lamp light. Both pages carry blank bands for lettering.

> LEDGER (lettered on the left page): prisioneiro marata

> LEAF (lettered on the right leaf): Sawant, Nagoji.

> CAPTION: In Goa, I had been a prisoner number. Here, I was a sentence in a longer story.

> CAPTION: It was a small difference. It was also vast.

**8.5** Full width, bottom. Pull back high over the courtyard: the jackfruit tree, Nagoji small on the stone, the estate dark. Overhead the sky has cleared to stars, but on the far horizon, beyond the palms toward the sea, a new bank of cloud glows with silent lightning.

> CAPTION: We all kept our own accounts, in our own ways.

> CAPTION: Mine were carved in memories of hooves on sand and the weight of men's trust.

> CAPTION: His were written in palm leaves and tags.

> CAPTION (separate, weighted): Both, I suspected, would matter when the next storm came.

## Adaptation notes

- **Page count** is 8 against a target of 7, inside the 6 to 8 range. The extra page goes to the chapter's two memories (Pune on page 5, the festival night across pages 7 and 8) and the Goa ledger echo in 8.4. It is paid for by cutting most of Ramayyan's explanatory speeches, since the chapter is otherwise one long conversation in one room.
- **The storehouse meeting** is reduced to the establishing panel 1.1. "History sat in rows" and "He did not look surprised to see me" are dropped as captions that would only describe the art.
- **Nagoji's leaf** keeps name, origin, first appearance, family, loyalties and risks. "Skills" and "(mitigated by Velinadu bond)" are cut for space; "emotional ties northward" stays because Ramayyan's "half your weight in the north" on page 7 answers it.
- **Padmini's heir and death date** are kept as a flash-forward caption in 3.1 to plant Nandini and chapter 27.
- **The Kottarakkara leaf** is kept across two inserts (3.3, 3.4) as the chapter 27 plant, with "before next monsoon" underlined twice as in the novel. "Chief: confined. Health: declining. Succession: disputed" and "Claims: legitimate under matrilineal law" are cut. Savitri's face is withheld until chapter 27; the device of ghosted faces for friends (Padmini, Revathi, De Lannoy) and bare ink for the threat makes the difference readable without a caption.
- **"Roots remain"** on the Eight Houses leaf (3.2) is kept deliberately so Ramayyan's "Roots are harder to kill than branches" (7.3) lands as an echo.
- **Cut as exposition:** the land and village-grain speech ("another kind of army"), "He would be angry if I did not," the "You will burn that" exchange and the avalanche speech, the "gossip dies on paper" examples (Padmini's flooded fields, Revathi refusing three Dutch offers), "What then? / I am not omniscient, Ananthan," the "lines on leaves / I eat at Padmini's hearth" exchange, the "old men with styluses / Old?" joke, and the historians-and-spider paragraph. The argument now runs spying, preparation, twisting, Pune, fear, which is the spine of the scene.
- **The three attempts** (arrow, poisoned sanctum oil, festival knife) are reduced to the one Nagoji witnessed, which is shown rather than listed. The king's fall on the oil is kept because chapter 22 refers back to "the festival night when you fell on the temple stones," and the broom plants the chapter 28 dedication.
- **Festival violence** is staged as a wrist grab and an aftermath at distance: two covered forms, a dropped knife, no wounds. "We killed two of the men there" is carried by the art, so the caption spends its words on the man who escaped. Nagoji appears only as a branded forearm so the flashback does not commit to a costume date.
- **The Pune execution** is cut before the stroke and shot from Nagoji's point of view, so no pre-capture look for him has to be invented. The astrologer is kept off the V13 temple priest face, which is reserved for confirmed ritual priests.
- **Reordered:** Ramayyan retying and shelving Nagoji's bundle is moved up to 6.2 and 6.3 so "You are one line in a very long text" is staged against the wall of shelves.
- **The exit from the room** ("the shelves watched me," "wet stone and ink") is cut; the chapter jumps from the festival aftermath to night under the jackfruit tree.
- **Goa echo:** 8.4 recomposes chapter 1 panel 1.2 beside Ramayyan's leaf, and letters "prisioneiro marata" against "Sawant, Nagoji." The name taken in the chapter's first caption of the book is written back here, which is the visual form of the novel's "prisoner number / sentence in a longer story."
- **Verbatim lines kept:** "You found my garden," "Some bear fruit. Some only thorns," "Of men," "Pieces can be lost in many ways. I keep track," "Some ledgers are not meant to be read aloud," "He worries about his soul. That is useful to know," "forgetting is a luxury small kingdoms cannot afford," "You count men and hooves. I count tendencies," Ramayyan's own loyalty leaf, "Spying is what others call watching...," "It gives fear less room to invent," "You are one line in a very long text," "either fools or tyrants," "I am tired. And determined. That is all," "Marriages end. Gold runs out. Roots are harder to kill than branches," "I would rather fill shelves than graves," and the chapter's last line.
- **Continuity:** Nagoji is in the Ananthan Pillai look (topknot, mundu, shoulder cloth, silver chain of office, left-wrist brand) with no ivory knife, which he gave to Dhanaji in chapter 20. Revathi wears the tali and first grey from chapter 20. De Lannoy is in his white cotton coat.
- **Print:** per the continuity bible, every placed panel must reach at least 300 effective PPI at its printed size. The largest placements here (1.3 at half page; 5.4, 7.5 and 8.5 at full width) are the most likely to need the 2x upscale before placement.
