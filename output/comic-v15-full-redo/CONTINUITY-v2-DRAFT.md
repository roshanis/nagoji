## Nagoji Sawant / Ananthan Pillai

Constant identity: strong cheekbones, straight nose, thick curled moustache, CLEAN-SHAVEN
CHIN (light stubble at most, captivity only), long curly black hair, small gold ear stud,
lean cavalryman build. No forehead marks, no sacred thread, no royal jewellery. No facial scar.
In memory panels set before his capture (ch6, panel 8.2; also relevant to the figure-less ch10
opening), he wears plain Maratha cavalry dress matching the commander sheet: rust-red turban,
cream tunic, red sash, talwar, hands unbandaged.

## Other principals

- **Marthanda Varma**: V13 varma-v1 face. Court: cream and gold, peacock crest, gold necklaces, often a spear, frequently bare-chested; scripts differ on what the peacock crest is: a feather pinned into a bare side-knot (konda) above his left ear, with NO turban and no crown (ch6, ch7 beach and field, the ch11 informal war hall, ch18), or a cream-and-gold turban carrying a jewelled peacock crest, matching the varma-v1 concept sheet itself (ch14 campaign dress, ch19 formal court audience). See Conflicts for the author; this line is left as is. Formal court, additional and non-conflicting: a plain shoulder cloth and a string of rudraksha beads among the gold, still no crown (ch11). War hall, informal (ch11): bare-chested, hair pulled back under a narrow white cloth band, not a turban, spear laid aside. Beach and field (ch7): V13 face, NO turban and NO crest, hair in a bun to his left, Vaishnavite forehead lines, bare-chested, one cream-and-gold cloth over the shoulder, gold necklaces. Temple visit at Tiruvattar, including the ride back (ch16): plain white cloth, a narrow shoulder dressing, no crest, bare-headed; court dress elsewhere in that chapter. Ch22 hill shrine: plain temple dress, bare-headed with his side knot above his left ear, no crest, no spear, bare chest, cream mundu with a thin gold border, one gold chain; ch22 fort room: the same, with a cream cloth over his injured right shoulder and a stitched brow. Ch23: a knife wound at the back of his LEFT shoulder, distinct from the ch22 right-shoulder strain; the bandaged left shoulder carries into the opening of ch24. Ch21 festival memory, younger than the ch28 dedication: dark hair bound back, forehead bare of any crown, bare-chested in a plain mundu. Ch28 dedication: same face aged, low knot, plain mundu, bare torso, no jewels, no headwear, the sacred thread across his chest.
- **Ramayyan Dalawa**: thin, sharp fine features, receding hair, plain white cloth, palm-leaf bundles and stylus. Ch14 battle: mud-splattered white dhoti, curved Nair sword.
- **Padmini Amma**: in her fifties at first; thick black hair coiled at the nape, going grey later; simple gold; cotton sari hitched for walking, the old Kerala drape with no stitched blouse; long walking stick. Dies ch27.
- **Revathi Bayi**: a little younger than Nagoji; deep blue or indigo sari with gold, deep green in ch11; jasmine in a braid; flat-coin necklace; gold armlets; sandalwood line at the hairline. Widow until ch20, then wears a tali. Grey threading from ch20. Ch27 funeral: plain white, no jasmine, no gold except the tali.
- **Father Duarte**: thin Portuguese Jesuit, scholar's stoop, grey tired eyes, ink-stained fingers, callused right hand, plain black cassock with a small cross. NO white Roman collar tab (a 19th-century anachronism). Ch1-2; returns older and greyer in ch24-25 and white-haired in ch28.
- **Joao**: broad, heavy, ruddy Portuguese gaoler; brown leather jerkin over a cream shirt; wide belt; iron key ring. NOT a priest, no cassock, no collar. Ch1-2 only.
- **Keshavrao**: young, narrow shoulders, black hair hacked short, prisoner rags; the ch2 lock: about nineteen, a faint moustache, a grey-brown tunic, no ear stud, so he cannot be mistaken for Nagoji. Ch2-3.
- **Ibrahim Marakkar** ("kapitan"): shorter than the fishermen but broader, scar from the LEFT ear to the jaw, a coastal Muslim broker; white turban and a short grey-flecked beard from ch4. Pale vest over a white mundu, no coat, through ch5, with ash on his forehead from ch5 panel 5.3 to the end of that chapter. Short coat over a vest with bulging pockets from ch6.
- **Eustachius De Lannoy**: tall, pale hair. A distant figure in a Dutch blue coat and black tricorne in ch11; torn and soot-stained in ch14-15, with a linen bandage on his left hand and wrist from ch15; changes there into a white ankle-length mundu, a plain white cotton tunic-coat and sandals, kept through ch16, ch18 and ch21; white Travancore coat in ch24; grey by ch28.
- **Raza Khan**: lean, hawk nose, pointed moustache, scar on his ANATOMICAL LEFT cheek, green high-wrapped turban. Madurai lancer from ch7.
- **Ponnan (Ponnam Pandya Deven)**: broad-shouldered Maravar, curly hair, thick moustache, clean chin, red shoulder cloth, white mundu hitched, short jacket, bare knees; rides a smaller Maravar horse, a head lower than Nagoji's mount.

His raiders (ch26): dark indigo or earth-brown cloth, head cloths, bare-chested, no cross-belts, never blue coats; their banner is plain faded green with no writing.

## Horses

- **Kanka**: Nagoji's black mare from before his capture, with a temper like monsoon lightning; seen only in memory (ch1, ch6 panel 8.2, referenced in ch7). Killed by a musket ball at Vasai, before the story opens.
- **Kayal**: Nagoji's bay mare, an ochre red-brown coat with a black mane, tail and lower legs, no white markings, sized between a Madurai charger and a Maravar pony. Chosen at the ch7 picket line; carries him through Colachel in ch14 and into ch13, ch15, ch16, ch20, ch22, ch23, ch24 and ch26.
- **Nagoji's grey gelding**: his plain grey daytime mount in ch7-8, distinct from Kayal.
- **Megha**: a grey horse with a black mane, named for the monsoon clouds; ridden in ch16 only, where his death frames the chapter.

## Standing rules for generated art

- No text of any kind in the art: blank balloon and caption reserves only, lettering composited later.
- No nooses. Interrogation ropes hang from an iron ring for binding wrists, never tied as hanging nooses.
- Portuguese guards must not read as British redcoats or as Dutch blue-coats. The Dutch VOC soldiers are the blue coats.
- No blood or gore. Violence is shown through staging, distance and aftermath.
- Period: 1738-1753 Malabar coast, Travancore and Portuguese Goa. No anachronisms.
- Print: every placed image must reach at least 300 effective PPI at its printed size. Below that, upscale 2x with `~/AI/upscalers/upscale2x.py` (Real-ESRGAN x2plus, BSD-3-Clause) before placement, or regenerate.
- Flags and banners: the Travancore standard is a silver conch on red, never lettered, and its regular troops wear white mundu with crossed leather belts under it. The Maratha flag seen in memory and vision panels only is a saffron swallow-tailed pennant, and background Maratha riders in those panels wear white or cream angarkhas and pagdis, never Nagoji's rust-red turban and sash. Raiders' and rivals' banners are plain, unlettered cloth. Indian troops, like the Portuguese and the Dutch, must not read as British redcoats.
- Oil lamps are clay or brass with open wicks; no glass chimneys and no glass lanterns.
