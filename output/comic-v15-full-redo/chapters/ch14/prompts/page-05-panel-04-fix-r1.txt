Draw ONE smooth-bore iron muzzle-loading cannon on a wooden truck carriage. Its closed breech ends in a single round cascabel knob with nothing attached to or inserted behind it. Its muzzle is run up to an embrasure in the woven palm-mat parapet and points straight at the heart of the Dutch stockade across the sand. All loading happens at the MUZZLE end. The sponger stands beside the muzzle, his wet sheepskin sponge on a long staff just drawn out of the bore and lifted clear. The rammer man stands beside him and drives the charge home into the muzzle with a plain wooden rammer staff. The third gunner crouches behind the breech with his face visible in three-quarter view, one eye shut, squinting along the top of the barrel toward the Dutch fort against the glare, one hand on the wooden wedge (quoin) under the breech. Keep the bare-chested gunners in white mundu and white head cloths matching panel 5.1, the same palm-mat battery, the blazing sun and the clear blue sky across the top for the caption. The Dutch stockade is quiet, with no smoke rising from it. Any Dutch flag is a plain red-white-blue horizontal tricolour with no monogram.

SETTING (this panel): Travancore earthwork trenches and gun batteries curve in a closing ring round the Dutch beachhead on the sandy Colachel shore, parapets revetted with palm trunks and woven palm-leaf mats, iron guns on wooden carriages, coconut palms and sea scrub behind. Across open sand stands the Dutch stockade, sagging mud ramparts topped by a timber palisade with canvas tents and rough huts inside, and the grey sea at its back. Light: hard coastal daylight, heat haze and salt spray.
