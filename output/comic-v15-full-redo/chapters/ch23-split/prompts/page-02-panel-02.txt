Large, half the page. Dusk in a village between Padmini's lands and Kollamkara's hill. A modest temple festival. The shrine has a Kerala-style gatehouse with a low, sloping tiled roof and carved wooden figures on its gables, not a tall stone tower. Bright cloths tied between trees, oil lamps flickering in niches along the outer wall, drummers and nadaswaram players. Men and women circle the shrine with small clay lamps cupped in their hands, faces lit from below like the carved figures on the gables. A procession winds out of the sanctum: girls carrying a palanquin with a simple wooden image of the goddess decked in fresh flowers, older women behind with brass plates of rice and turmeric. Padmini, dismounted, has slipped into their line as if she had always been there. Men bow their heads as she passes.
