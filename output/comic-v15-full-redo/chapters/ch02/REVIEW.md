## What was drawn and reviewed

All 29 prepared frame jobs were generated using built-in imagegen with character references. The chapter moves from the prisoners' departure through Goa and Duarte's blessing to embarkation, confinement in the hold, Nagoji's memory of freedom and the ship's violent motion. The approved Chapter 1 r6 art and page renders were the visual benchmark. The approved character sheets and Chapter 2 continuity rows governed identity and costume.

Final candidates were inspected at native resolution before final selection. Checks included Nagoji's curled moustache, clean-shaven chin and small gold stud; captivity rags, rigid iron collar and wrist restraints; covered left arm; Duarte's gaunt face, grey swept hair and plain standing collar without a white Roman tab; Joao and Keshavrao against their sheets; hands, period clothing and guards; absence of text in the generated art, nooses, blood and gore; and enough clean space for native lettering. The text in the delivered pages was placed by the compositor, not generated in the paintings. Historical plausibility was checked visually against the supplied material; this was not a new historical research audit.

All six final 300 DPI pages were then inspected by the root agent. An independent reviewer also inspected all six. The final checks found no unresolved clipping, missing copy, unreadable lettering, beard drift, white collar tab, exposed brand, noose or gore. The final page 5 memory has the visible sheathed sword; the final page 6 remains inside the hold and includes the separately loose chain.

## Remaining deviations

- Some strips are centred inside full-width layout rows and do not extend to both outer edges. Contain fitting also creates extra visible white space around some paired panels beyond the nominal 2 point row gap. This preserves complete art and lettering but is a composition adaptation from literal full-width staging.
- Collar-chain routing varies between a shared chain or beam-ring arrangement and separately trailing slack chains in different hold views. The rigid collars and individual wrist restraints remain, but the connecting chain is not an identical continuous rig in every panel.
- The memory image in 5.2 occupies a substantial right-hand inset rather than a small corner. In 6.5, the reassuring contact rests around the bandaged wrist and forearm rather than reproducing the exact finger-over-hand pose.

These differences remain visible and are not hidden by the technical pass. No additional PDF revision was made after the successful r1 rendered-page review.
