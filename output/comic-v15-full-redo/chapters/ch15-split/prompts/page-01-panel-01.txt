V15 graphic novel panel page-01-panel-01 for chapter 15. Drawn in the book's inked graphic-novel style: clean ink line and painted colour, never photorealistic. Place: Colachel beach and the burnt Dutch camp.

Wide, full page width. Colachel beach at mid-morning, the day after the capitulation was signed. The charred stockade and the burnt-out Dutch camp, ash grey on the sand, the smoke gone. A Dutch column forms ranks inside the burnt gap of its own stockade: torn, soot-stained blue coats, tricorn hats, muskets shouldered, drums slung, officers with swords at their hips. Beaten men standing as straight as they can. Along the camp perimeter, the Nair pickets who have guarded it for two days (bare-chested, forward-tied topknots, short white mundus, spears) step back to open the way. Far out, Dutch ships riding small and distant.

FRAME SHAPE: a wide horizontal strip, about 3 to 1 (width to height), filling the whole width; keep the key action inside the middle half of the height.

PEOPLE IN THIS PANEL: no principal characters.

ART RULES:
- 1740s dress, arms, objects.
- No blood or gore; stage aftermath.
- Clay or brass oil lamps; open wicks.
- Non-Maratha raiders' and rivals' banners are plain cloth.
- Dutch East India Company soldiers wear blue coats. No soldier, Indian or European, wears a British red coat.

Leave clear, low-detail space (sky, wall, floor or shadow) for them, preferably in the upper part of the frame. Keep faces, hands and important action outside that space. Draw NO balloons, caption boxes, panel borders or a border line around the picture, and no written characters, numerals, pseudo-writing, logos or watermark anywhere, including on banners, flags, cloth, walls and documents. Any balloon, caption or letterer wording in the description means space only, never painted lettering.

SETTING (this panel): Colachel beach on the Travancore coast: a long pale sand shore under low dunes and coconut palms, the charred timber stockade and burnt-out Dutch camp lying ash grey on the sand, a low sandy rise where the Travancore battery stood, and the shore road running south toward Kanyakumari. Far out on the open sea, a few Dutch ships ride small and distant. Light: clear mid-morning sun, the smoke of the burning gone. No stone fort, bastions, harbour or town on this beach, and no European tents left standing. No red coats, no blood or bodies, and no coats, uniforms, hats or turbans on the Nair soldiers.
