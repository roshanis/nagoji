V15 graphic novel panel page-12-panel-01 for chapter 14. Drawn in the book's inked graphic-novel style: clean ink line and painted colour, never photorealistic. Place: Colachel beach below the burned stockade.

Wide, twilight. Nagoji rides up to where Ramayyan stands. Nagoji in the commander look: rust-red turban, cream tunic, broad red sash, cream trousers, boots, talwar; thick curled moustache, clean-shaven chin, small gold ear stud; no stubble. Ramayyan (older, thin, receding hair, bare-headed, mud-and-soot-splattered white dhoti) slides the curved Nair sword home into its sheath, the piece of silk he wiped it with still in his other hand, the motion as careful as calligraphy.

PEOPLE IN THIS PANEL: Nagoji, Ramayyan Dalawa. Draw each named person once, with no doubles.

LOOKS (face, build, hair, facial hair, scars, marks and ear ornament always follow these lines; footwear, sleeves, headwear, props in hand and pose follow the panel description above wherever it is more specific):
Kayal: Nagoji's bay mare, an ochre red-brown coat with a black mane, tail and lower legs, no white markings, sized between a Madurai charger and a Maravar pony.
Nagoji: Constant identity: strong cheekbones, straight nose, a THICK, bushy curled handlebar moustache, CLEAN-SHAVEN CHIN, thick curly black hair, one tiny flat gold stud flush on the earlobe with nothing hanging from it, lean cavalryman build. No forehead marks, no sacred thread, no royal jewellery, no pearls. No facial scar.; Commander sheet: rust-red turban, cream tunic, broad red sash, cream trousers, boots, talwar. Nails healed but ridged. The Portuguese brand on the inner LEFT forearm, a hand's width above the wrist, seen when sleeves are pushed up: a pale puckered patch of raised ridges blurred past reading, not a cross. A long, thin white scar along the same forearm, running from the wrist toward the elbow, beside the brand.
Ramayyan Dalawa: thin, sharp fine features, receding hair, plain white cloth, palm-leaf bundles and stylus. Battle: mud-splattered white dhoti, curved Nair sword.

- No blood or gore; stage aftermath.
- Clay or brass oil lamps; open wicks.
- Non-Maratha raiders' and rivals' banners are plain cloth.
- Dutch East India Company soldiers wear blue coats. No soldier, Indian or European, wears a British red coat.

MUST MATCH:

- Ramayyan Dalawa: Older, thin, sharp fine features and receding hair, bare-headed, no jewellery, no forehead marks or sacred thread. Plain white cloth in camp; in battle and after it a mud-splattered white dhoti and a curved Nair sword with no shield.

SETTING (this panel): The wide Colachel beach below the burned-down Dutch stockade, its timber palisade reduced to glowing embers and smoke drifting low over the sand. Dutch survivors in ash-stained blue coats huddle on the sand under guard of Nair spearmen in white mundus, and when the sea is in view the Dutch ships are only small, distant shapes. Light: twilight, ember glow from the ruined stockade. No blood or bodies, no ropes at any prisoner's neck, no turban on any Nair, no British red coats, no lettering on flags.
