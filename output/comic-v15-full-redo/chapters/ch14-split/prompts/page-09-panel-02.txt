V15 graphic novel panel page-09-panel-02 for chapter 14. Drawn in the book's inked graphic-novel style: clean ink line and painted colour, never photorealistic. Place: Open sand between the trenches and the burning stockade.

The first Dutch volley. A long bank of musket smoke along their line; our forward trench, men ducking and falling back from the parapet. Staging and distance only.

PEOPLE IN THIS PANEL: no principal characters.

ART RULES:
- 1740s dress, arms, objects.
- No blood or gore; stage aftermath.
- Clay or brass oil lamps; open wicks.
- Non-Maratha raiders' and rivals' banners are plain cloth.
- Dutch East India Company soldiers wear blue coats. No soldier, Indian or European, wears a British red coat.

LETTERING: the letterer adds it afterwards, so draw none of it. This panel will carry exactly 1 lettering area(s): a caption (medium). Leave clear, low-detail space (sky, wall, floor or shadow) for them, preferably in the upper part of the frame. Keep faces, hands and important action outside that space. Draw NO balloons, caption boxes, panel borders or a border line around the picture, and no written characters, numerals, pseudo-writing, logos or watermark anywhere, including on banners, flags, cloth, walls and documents. Any balloon, caption or letterer wording in the description means space only, never painted lettering.

SETTING (this panel): The open sand and trampled scrub between the Travancore forward trench and the burning Dutch stockade, smoke rolling across it, where Dutch East India Company infantry in blue and grey coats form a square with fixed bayonets against the Nair line of round shields, swords and spears. The allied horse are Madurai lancers in knee-length white jamas and high white turbans with long bamboo lances, Deccan riders in off-white turbans and light Maravar horse; only Nagoji wears the rust-red turban and red sash. Light: afternoon glare through smoke and flame. No blood or close-up bodies, no green turbans, no second rust-red turban or red sash, no turban on any Nair, no British red coats, no lettering on flags.
