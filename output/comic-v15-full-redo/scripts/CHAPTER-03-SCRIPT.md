## Adaptation notes

- **Page count.** 10 pages and 47 panels against a target of 9, inside the 7 to 11 range. The extra page goes to the deck and to Keshavrao's loss (pages 8 to 10), where the chapter's drama is. The hold before the ring breaks, which is mostly exposition, fits into four pages.
- **Cut, to avoid gore and repetition.** The prisoner whose arm bends the wrong way under another man's weight. It repeats the violence of the wave and would break the no-gore rule.
- **Cut, for space.** The second round of Portuguese orders, reduced to two overhead panels. "The spell broke." Keshavrao's swallow and his Adam's apple. The long explanation of slipping the chain from the soft wood, simplified to a ring left hanging on its last bolt. "Again" and the four separate pulls, reduced to one pull and its result. "The storm offered no time for noble speeches now." "Sometimes the gods look kindly on men who refuse to freeze." "And stories" after "liquor and song". The thought of Keshavrao's "with you, sahib" at the rail, since the reader has just seen the rope.
- **Cut, because the art shows it.** "We were still in a dark box, chained at wrists and ankles" (6.1). "It was not loaded" (6.3): the empty pan shows it and the next caption implies it. "The wave fell" (8.4). "Then I hurled myself over the side": 10.4 draws the leap, so its captions carry the rest of the "Forgive me" sentence instead. The guard's "wet, final sound" is carried by 7.2 and 7.3. "Keshavrao was gone" is kept verbatim, but it is lettered over the palms in 9.5 and not over the empty hatch in 10.1, where it would only repeat the image.
- **Softened for the standing art rules.** The novel's blood on Keshavrao's forehead becomes a rain-washed graze with no blood drawn. The guard's broken wrist is staged side-on at a distance and lettered only as "Bone cracked." The rope that "peeled my skin" is drawn as scorched, reddened palms and stripped bandages, with no torn flesh or blood. No chain rises vertically above a head, and no one is pulled by the neck (the no-nooses rule): the haul in 5.4 is on fists and shoulders, and the collars are stripped by hand in 5.5.
- **Language.** As in the novel, the guards' Konkani and the sailors' Portuguese are lettered in English. Nagoji's orders to the hold are tagged as Marathi, because that is the moment he speaks for all the prisoners.
- **Callbacks to keep in the neighbouring scripts.** "The horse who died better than I would" (10.4) pays off Chapter 1, panel 7.1 ("Then he died better than I will"). "Muskets are like horses" keeps the horse motif going. Keshavrao's "Do not leave me" (9.1), "follow wherever I jumped" (9.2) and "the boy I promised not to leave" (10.4) depend on Chapter 2's "I will follow wherever you jump, sahib" (6.2) and "If there is a chance, I will not leave you" (6.4). Chapter 2's "One boy's loyalty wrapped itself like a rope around my chest" (6.3) pays off in the rope Nagoji knots round his own chest in 10.3. Keshavrao's hand curled for an absent sword is planted in Chapter 2 at 1.5 and 6.2 and used for the last time in 9.1. The guard's "If any of you break your chains" (1.4) is the chapter's ironic plant. The two turns of rope round his chest carry into Chapter 4, panel 1.2.
- **Continuity.** Nagoji wears the captivity sheet throughout: clean-shaven chin, thick curled moustache, gold ear stud, torn ochre sleeveless tunic, cream dhoti, bandaged hands and wrists, barefoot, hair loose, no turban. He also wears the ship irons fixed in 1.1, which match the revised Chapter 2: a hinged collar with a side ring, wrist manacles on a short slack chain about a forearm long, and ankle cuffs on a short hobble chain. After 5.5 the collar is empty, but every iron stays on into the sea. Keshavrao uses the Chapter 2 lock wherever he appears, and in two-shots Nagoji is on the left and Keshavrao on the right. The Portuguese ship guards are lean, dark-bearded men in morions and buff leather jerkins with cross-belts, like the Chapter 2 guards. They carry no key ring and wear no wide belt, so no guard reads as João. Never attach João's description or any gaol reference to a guard frame. Buff leather is for guards only; the sailors wear shirts and slops. The rope logic: the thrown length parts at a splice in 9.5 while the rest of the coil stays under his knee, which leaves him rope for 10.3. He ends the chapter with scorched palms and two turns of rope knotted round his chest.
