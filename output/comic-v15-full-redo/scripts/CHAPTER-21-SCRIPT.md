## Chapter 21: Ramayyan's Ledger

Source: `book1_horse_servant/book3_chapter21_ramayyans_ledger.md`, 2,140 words, adapted as 13 pages, 38 panels and 2 insets.

### Art locks for this chapter

- **Reference sheets.** Attach `nagoji-v2.png` to every frame that shows Nagoji's face or figure and `varma-v1.png` to every frame that shows Varma. Ramayyan has no V13 sheet; he is held apart from Nagoji by the tags below. Pass the cast overrides listed in the adaptation notes to `prepare`.
- **Nagoji** (Ananthan Pillai, CONTINUITY ch17-27, chapter 21 state): Kerala topknot, no turban, thick curled moustache, clean-shaven chin, small gold ear stud, cream mundu and shoulder cloth, silver chain of office across his chest, first grey at the temples, the Goa brand on his left wrist. No knife (he gave it to Dhanaji in chapter 20), no bandages (chapter 22), no coat (chapter 24). Every panel that shows him repeats the short tag.
- **Ramayyan**: thin face, sharp fine features, receding hairline, plain white cloth, ink-stained fingers, no chain, no jewellery. Every panel that shows him repeats the short tag.
- **Screen direction in the archive room.** In every two-shot Nagoji stays on frame left and Ramayyan on frame right.
- **LEAF and LEDGER panels.** Each leaf is drawn flat and square to camera, filling the width of its frame, with exactly as many empty horizontal bands as the panel has LEAF lines. The bands are the text reserves: no outlined boxes, no marks. Fingers rest in the left margin beside a band, never over it. LEAF lines are lettered later directly onto the bands in a small incised-script face, never in caption boxes.
- **Flashbacks** (8.3, 11.5, 12.1, 12.2) bleed in with stain-edged borders instead of clean frames. The festival panels share one warm lamp palette.

## Adaptation notes

- **Page count** is 8 against a target of 7, inside the 6 to 8 range. The extra page goes to the chapter's two memories (Pune on page 8, the festival night across pages 11 and 12) and the Goa ledger echo in 13.2. It is paid for by cutting Ramayyan's explanatory speeches, since the chapter is otherwise one long conversation in one room. Pages 1 and 6 run four panels each so that the room reveal (1.3) and the loyalty inset (7.2) get height. Pages 8 and 10 are the densest pages; if the letterer finds a reserve too small, cut "in his mind" from 8.3 before touching anything else.
- **The storehouse meeting** is reduced to the establishing panel 1.1, with the plan board as its only cue. "History sat in rows," "He did not look surprised to see me" and "There was no dust" are dropped as captions that would only describe the art; the swept floor and the seated man carry them.
- **Nagoji's leaf** keeps name, origin, first appearance, family, loyalties and risks. "Skills" and "(mitigated by Velinadu bond)" are cut for space; "emotional ties northward" stays because Ramayyan's "half your weight in the north" on page 11 answers it.
- **Padmini's heir and death date** are kept as a flash-forward caption in 4.1 to plant Nandini and chapter 27.
- **The Kottarakkara leaf** is kept across two inserts (5.1, 5.2) as the chapter 27 plant. As in the novel, Ramayyan's finger pauses on the Velinadu connection line, which keeps "Leverage: significant," and only "before next monsoon" is underlined twice. "Chief: confined. Health: declining. Succession: disputed" and "Claims: legitimate under matrilineal law" are cut. Savitri's face is withheld until chapter 27. Friends get ghosted faces (Padmini, Revathi, De Lannoy) and the threat gets only ink, so the difference reads without a caption.
- **Revathi's answer.** Page 4 lets the reader suspect Revathi through her blood tie to the Dutch-linked house. 9.2 answers it in the novel's words: she has refused three offers from Dutch factors in the last month. This also shows Ramayyan's claim that writing leaves fear less room to invent actually working. The "three" mirrors "Letters intercepted: three" on the Kottarakkara leaf.
- **"Roots remain"** on the Eight Houses leaf (4.2) is kept deliberately so that Ramayyan's "Roots are harder to kill than branches" (11.2) lands as an echo. That line now closes the novel's single run "Marriages end. Gold runs out. Roots are harder to kill than branches."
