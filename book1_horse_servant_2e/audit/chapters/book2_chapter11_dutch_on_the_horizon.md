The chapter is split. Its scenes and dialogue are good, often very good: the boy on the palm, the guard's "piss in step", the king's "piss in our waters" and "my horse standing in your streets in Batavia", Revathi's "your clerk's handwriting", the hundi anecdote, Karl August's broken-Portuguese briefing, and Ibrahim's "Then imagine him drowning" and "Let his wig itch". The narration around them runs on a machine template: a summary opener with a kicker, a correction construction ("It was not X. It was Y."), a reaction shot (stylus, jaw, heartbeat, ripple), then an aphorism to close the paragraph. Almost every section ends on a moral or maxim (L57, L163, L363, L409, L477). The Dutch divide-and-rule policy is lectured three times (L5, L211, L269). The ending stacks seven codas and gives away De Lannoy's surrender.

The score is 4 and not 5 because the clusters are separable. Cut the narrator's glosses and the scenes stand up unaided. It is 4 and not 3 because the pattern is on every page and the rations are exceeded several times over:

## Opening

**Current (L3):** "It was late in 1739 when the horizon finally changed, ending the years of quiet consolidation. This was not yet the war fleet that would come two years later, only a probing squadron, sent to measure distances and count our guns. But we did not know that then."

**Verdict: rewrite.** Three summary paragraphs, each closing on a kicker ("But we did not know that then", "sharpen knives in our shadows", "the eye of the shark"), sit in front of the real opening at L11. L3 also plants "horizon" for the bookend at L477.

**Proposed.** Delete L3 to 9 and open on the scene. L11 ("The first time I saw Dutch sails close enough to count...") is a good line, but Chs 1, 3, 7 and 18 already open on "The first / second..." (OPENINGS_ENDINGS_MOTIFS). Open on the boy's call instead:

> "Three ships," the boy called down in Malayalam. "No, four. The new one is smaller. They fly the same colours."
>
> He was perhaps fifteen, all shin and elbow, standing on a wooden platform lashed to the top of a coconut palm on the headland, a long glass tube held to his eye. I had ridden up the sandy path under the midday sun and reined in at the foot of his tree. The sea wind tugged at his hair and his threadbare cloth.

L17 ("The guard at the base of the tree looked up.") follows unchanged. Then move the facts the reader needs into one plain paragraph after L65, before the war hall:

> That was late in 1739. The Dutch had held Kochi since they took it from the Portuguese in 1663, and in all those years they had never stopped playing raja against raja. That year the army Ramayyan built had marched north, taken Kollam and broken the lines of Desinganadu, and the Raja of Desinganadu had fled and left his pepper fields in Marthanda Varma's hands.

This keeps the 1663 date that the Foreword audit relies on, keeps the year that Ch 14 L13 counts from, and resolves backlog N-49 and N-51.

## Ending

**Current last lines (L467 to 477):**

> The sea gave no answer. It rarely does.
>
> But as I sat there, the breeze shifted slightly, bringing with it the faintest trace of unfamiliar smoke. Not coconut husk, not jackfruit wood, not the resin of local trees.
>
> Ship smoke.
>
> Dutch smoke.
>
> Threads had been woven in that hall. Some would hold. Some would snap. Either way, the fabric of this coast had been pulled a little tighter.
>
> The horizon had begun to move nearer, whether it wished to or not.

**Verdict: rewrite.** L451 to 477 stack seven codas: the sea erasing distance, the officer's imagined inner life, a spoiler, the triple "not yet", "the shape of the air", the Goa theme paragraph, an aphorism, a fragment ladder, a fabric summary and a title echo. The last paragraph begins "But", and the chapter ends on summary and mood, which the style sheet (section 3) bans. Two things in the section are real: the older watchman (L449) and the soldier's nose for enemy cook fires.

**Proposed (replacing L451 to 477):**

> In the dark the ships were only clusters of lights, their masts gone. They could have been fishing boats.
>
> Somewhere out there, on the largest hull, the pale-haired officer was asleep or pacing. He would have his own maps, and his own Ramayyan at his elbow, and masters in Batavia waiting for his letters. I did not yet know his name. I would learn it at Colachel.
>
> I put my palm flat on the rock beside me.
>
> "Come closer," I said to the lights. "Come stand where I can reach you. Let us see how your neat lines fare when the ground shifts under you."
>
> Then the breeze turned and brought me a smoke that was not coconut husk or jackfruit wood. I had last smelled it through the hatch of a Portuguese hold: salt pork over a ship's fire. Out on the water, the Dutch were cooking their supper.

This ends on an image and makes the enemy small and exact, which is how Nagoji jokes (VOICE_BIBLE section 1). The hatch is established at Ch 2 L81 and L101. It follows the OPENINGS_ENDINGS_MOTIFS proposal for this chapter and shares no closing idea with another chapter. Keep the word "turned": on this coast the night wind usually blows off the land, so it is the turn that brings ship smoke ashore. About 190 words replace about 390.
