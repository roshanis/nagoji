## Verdict

| Measure | Call |
|:-|:-|
| Severity | **4 of 5** |
| Recommended intensity | **Deep** (line-level re-voicing of the two confrontation scenes, a rebuilt ending, and four continuity repairs) |
| Estimated word cut | **About 18%** (2,605 to about 2,130 words, after the few short additions proposed below) |

The chapter has a strong spine and a machine-made skin. The spine is authored. Padmini's stick goes silent. "Pepper is not rice." "We will lose men in inches." Nagoji insists on lighting the first torch himself. The fire jumps to a branch the men missed. The water tastes of smoke. Padmini throws "Not all. Enough." back at him. "Your word does not make plants grow faster." Ramayyan keeps Ibrahim's accounts for him. Around these lines the dialogue runs on a template. Padmini, Revathi, the king and Ramayyan all speak in mirrored maxims and "They will... They will..." triads, and every exchange ends on something quotable. The swap test in VOICE_BIBLE 3.9 fails for most of the long speeches.

Three structural faults matter more than any single line:

1. **The theme is stated, not shown.** King over house appears as a statement at L121, L207, L235, L239, L249, L253 and L265. Wound, carve, scar, mark and vein appear fourteen times. Padmini's scene and Revathi's scene make the same argument in the same shape (L191 against L229) and end on the same exit beat (L209 against L255).
2. **Commentary replaces action at the two moments that most need Nagoji.** When the torch is in his hand (L115 to L121), he gets "the weight of it", a face flashing in his mind, and a quotation Revathi never said. After the burn (L147 to L153) he gets three verdict paragraphs. Nowhere does the chapter use who he is. He is a Maratha raider who burned Portuguese warehouses on the Konkan (Ch 6 L99). Only a month earlier, in Ch 25 L108, he pictured Padmini's estate "burned not by Kollamkara's men but by my own countrymen". He is also Padmini's adopted son (Ch 17, Ch 19 L257). And he rides a horse that "does not like cannon smoke" (Ch 20 L123), yet it gets no mention on a day of smoke.
3. **The chapter ends four times.** It ends at L255, at L257 to L261, at L263 to L267, and again at L283.

The 18% cut is above the 5 to 10% the PLAN expects book-wide. That is deliberate. About 40% of it is the stacked endings and the second half of the Revathi scene. No plot beat or historical fact is lost. Every scene stays, and so do both confrontations and the Ibrahim coda.

**Where I overruled the auditors or the book-level docs:**

- **L39 ("The path is too narrow. The supports too close. The canal banks too soft.").** The pattern auditor calls it a rehearsed triad. It is a horseman's footing check: three concrete things, in the order he would test them. VOICE_BIBLE (T2, 3.4) allows verbless lists when they are an inventory. I keep it and fix the triad at L43 instead.
- **L81, "consent".** The pattern auditor calls the word modern. It is period English, and it echoes the adoption rite, which needs "the consent of its senior woman" (Ch 17 L111). The real faults are the tangled syntax and the "do not pretend" formula, which returns at L151 and L253.
- **L243, "a corner of my house and a sliver of my heart".** The pattern auditor calls it stock. Revathi is quoting Nagoji's own words from Ch 20 L117. It is a planted callback, so it stays. Only her second sentence goes.
- **L107, "We do not hit them here. We hit their shield."** The pattern auditor lists it as a "not X, Y" retort. STYLE_SHEET allows one such line per chapter, in dialogue, and this is the one to keep: it is a commander's order and it turns the L61 image into a plan. Every other "not X, Y" in the chapter goes (L93, L137, L197, L239).
- **The ending.** OPENINGS_ENDINGS_MOTIFS proposes ending on a revised L261 with "stitches in a cut that has been closed badly". I reject it, because that is one more wound image in a chapter already overloaded with them. I follow VOICE_BIBLE Appendix A and both auditors, and end on L281.
- **L279, "That is usually enough."** OPENINGS_ENDINGS_MOTIFS allows the "enough" formula only once in the book (Ch 18 L179). I defend this one because it is a threat dressed as bookkeeping, not a closer. An alternative is given below in case the author enforces the rule.

## Opening

Current first lines (L3 to L17): "The fire started as a thin, uncertain line on the horizon. / At first it looked like nothing more than the usual dry season burn, a farmer clearing undergrowth, a careless coal from a cook fire. ... / Then the line thickened. **Verdict: revise.** The idea is right. A man who has learned what dry-season smoke looks like on this coast sees it turn into something else. ## Chapter-specific notes (continuity and history)

1. **Padmini is Nagoji's adoptive mother, and the chapter never lets it register.** She took him into her house in Ch 17 (L111). The name Pillai marks him "as Padmini's son" (Ch 19 L257). He carries the fire to her pyre in Ch 27 (L37) and afterwards keeps "the vines alive" on her estate (Ch 27 L39). In this chapter the king and Ramayyan call her "Amma", but Nagoji never does, and his narration treats her as a neighbouring landholder. The smallest fix is one word at L85: "Amma, I will ride there myself." Her cold "Pillai" at L207, the house name she gave him, then carries its full weight, and so does the Konkan line proposed for L115 ("never fed me at their table"). Keep "Ananthan" at L191 and "Pillai" at L207. The shift from the one to the other is her withdrawing, and it should stay.
2. **Padmini's last words in the book (backlog BL-03).** L207 is the last thing she says before Ch 27 L31, "By then Padmini Amma was gone." The backlog asks for a small repair beat. The least intrusive way is in the coda's new time marker (L271): "when the cuttings Padmini Amma had set along the burned strip put out their first leaves". She replants, and says nothing. That is a reconciliation in her own register, and it needs no new scene.
3. **The same ground as Ch 23.** Ch 23 (L191 to L251) already has smoke near Padmini's northern fields, a narrow pepper strip along a canal, Kollamkara's men, "By late afternoon", "We reined in on a ridge" and Ponnan's "bleed for every shrub". This chapter repeats all of them. The confirmed fixes remove the repeated phrasing. To turn the déjà vu into meaning, let Nagoji say it once, at L37: "I had ridden that strip after Kollamkara's first raid." Padmini's "Of course they did" then means "again". Settle the fields as well: L11 says "upper", L187 says "lower", and Ch 23 L191 says "northern". Ch 20's opening also calls them "upper fields", so "upper" and "northern" can be the same place (the raiders come down from the northern hills, L19). "Lower" is the error.
4. **L187 against Ch 23 (backlog N-52, C10-48).** In Ch 23 Padmini defended him ("Ananthan did what was necessary", L371), and the king rebuked him for "a massacre" (L351). She never called anything cowardice, and the raid was weeks earlier, not "years ago". The proposed narration at L187 uses the real history. That also gives the chapter a reversal the first edition misses: the woman who defended him then is the one he has wounded now, and the king who called him a butcher then has now approved fire.
5. **Unplanted and misplaced lines.** Revathi's "You will always choose the king first" (L121) is never said earlier (backlog N-40), so cut it. Her "Today" at L229 and L243 clashes with "a few evenings later" at L215. At L243 "my land" makes Padmini's estate Revathi's. The fix quotes her own Ch 20 L121 ("This land was never yours to own") so that "land" means the coast, as it does there.
6. **The cavalry payoff (L57 against L141).** The plan is to burn so the raiders must "retreat into the open where our horse can run". The execution sends patrols to block that retreat, and no horseman rides in the whole battle. In a book called *Horse of the Servant*, the confirmed L141 fix and the optional horse line at L87 put the horse back into the chapter in two sentences.
7. **How pepper is grown, and how the burn works.** Pepper gardens on this coast were traditionally trained on live standards (coral tree, jackfruit, mango, areca), not on dead posts. The chapter says "supports" (L23, L39), "support post" (L125), "posts" (L159, L261) and "tree" (L43). "Standards" or the name of a tree would be more exact, and Nagoji names things. Verify, and choose one term. Living green vines also burn badly on their own, so the burn needs dry fuel packed around them (the L133 addition) and dry-season leaf litter (L111, already there). Burning pepper gives off an irritant smoke, which makes "eyes streaming" at L149 literally true. An optional kitchen image at L93, in his register (VOICE_BIBLE 3.2): "It caught the throat like chillies scorched in a pan."
8. **History.** Dutch guns in the hands of local chiefs after Colachel is right. Through the 1740s the VOC backed Kayamkulam and the northern houses against Travancore (for example, the 1742 attack on Kilimanoor; verify the date against the author's sources). "Mysore" at L207 is the book's chosen coming threat (Ch 27 L7). Historically Mysore did not press on Travancore until Hyder Ali in the 1760s, so it stands as a book-wide choice. In Padmini's mouth it reads as foresight. "Maratha distraction" at L19 is ambiguous. It could mean the court distracted by Pune's letters (Ch 25) or rumours of a Maratha move south. Make it one or the other: "whispers of Dutch backing, and of Maratha letters keeping the king's eyes north".
9. **The Konkan line proposed for L115 is grounded in the text.** Ch 6 L99 has him recalling "how many days it had taken for replacement ships to arrive after we had burned a warehouse". Ch 19 L263 has Padmini's "Dinner is ready. Do not make us wait." A second option pays off Ch 25 L108 instead: "In Ramayyan's room I had pictured Maratha horse coming down on these vines with fire. I had not pictured the Maratha on foot." Use one of them, not both.
10. **The ending, where the book-level docs disagree.** OPENINGS_ENDINGS_MOTIFS (row 26) says to move or cut the coda and end on a revised L261. VOICE_BIBLE Appendix A says to end on L281. Both auditors favour L281, and so does this audit (see Ending). The backlog item C10-49 says the Ibrahim question is never answered, since Ibrahim does not appear in Ch 27 or Ch 28. With the fixes, the coda rests on Ramayyan's control rather than on a mystery, which makes the open question acceptable. If the author wants it closed, a single line in Ch 28 would do it.
11. **Cross-chapter repeats removed by the confirmed fixes:** Ch 18 L113 (hung in the air like smoke), Ch 14 L97 (for a heartbeat, nothing happened), Ch 24 L511 (turned to leave, then paused), Ch 23 L245, L249 and L251 (late afternoon, the ridge, bleeding in every shrub), Ch 25 L332 (Revathi's reaction came later), Ch 28 L141 (we will stand in the same halls), Ch 8 L15 (did not look up from his palm leaves), Ch 24 L701 (traded on this coast longer), Ch 21 L19 (in the centre of the room).

Other items: "oil soaked" (L113) should be "oil-soaked", and "zig zag" (L137) should be "zigzag". "The Dutch's desired vines" (L193) goes with its paragraph. "Sari" (L173) for a Nair noblewoman of the 1740s, whose dress would be mundu and neriyathu, is a book-wide decision already made in Ch 8 L65. Note it for the glossary pass, not for this chapter alone.
