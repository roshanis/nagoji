# Second-Edition Audit: Chapter 4, The Fishermen of the Pepper Coast

- Source (frozen first edition): `book1_horse_servant/book1_chapter04_fishermen_of_the_pepper_coast.md`, 129 lines, 1,713 words. Line numbers refer to that file.
- Method: I read the whole chapter and ruled on all 58 flags from the pattern auditor and all 22 suspect passages from the holistic reader, merging duplicates. I cross-checked the end of Ch 3, the opening of Ch 5, Ch 1 (nails, Konkan forts), Ch 2 (bandages, Nashik roads), Ch 8 (Padmini's hair), STYLE_SHEET.md, VOICE_BIBLE.md, OPENINGS_ENDINGS_MOTIFS.md, REGISTER_AND_ANACHRONISM.md and REVIEW_BACKLOG.md.
- Tally after adjudication: 39 confirmed, 15 borderline, 23 defended. Where the two auditors disagreed (L27, L57, L83, L103, L113, L127, L129), the ruling and reason are under Defended.

## Verdict

| Measure | Call |
|---|---|
| Severity | **3 of 5** |
| Recommended intensity | **Medium** (tics plus tightening, with four small continuity repairs) |
| Estimated word cut | **About 17%** (1,713 to about 1,420 words, net of about 90 words added for the repairs) |

The slop is not spread evenly. It sits at the two edges and in one bridge passage:

1. **The first page (L3 to L13).** "Spat me out ... without ceremony", "Breathing was a battle", "limbs felt like they belonged to someone else", two "Perhaps" sentences, "minutes or hours", "The sea was no longer alone. / Voices." Taken alone, this page reads at 4 or 5.
2. **The carry to the village (L77 to L89).** The relief of being on land is stated four times ("something else", a fragment triad, "felt like gifts", "first solid ground"), and two of those are physically impossible for a man being carried.
3. **The last page (L121 to L129).** A storm aphorism, a recap-and-resolve, a fourth wound inventory and "In that silence, I was being weighed."

Between those, the chapter is alive. The bargaining on the beach (L49 to L57), "Before the crabs claim you", "If he dies, burn him quietly" and the "Asked questions" exchange (L105 to L111) are some of the best minor-character writing in Part 1. Most of the fixes are cuts. Line-level re-voicing is not needed.

Three problems are not style problems, and the edit should fix them at the same time:

- **Language logic.** Nagoji says he cannot follow Malayalam (L47), yet he quotes every Malayalam speech in full. A single retrospective line placed before the speech fixes this and keeps the dialogue (see L41 and L47 below).
- **Chains and brand.** He went over the side in wrist and ankle irons (Ch 3 L117), and in this chapter the irons vanish (backlog N-20). The brand also moves from the arm (L19) to the shoulder (L45) (N-21).
- **Keshavrao.** Ch 3 ends on "Forgive me" to the boy, and this chapter never mentions him, not even at L39, where Nagoji looks at the wreck and sees "No bodies". One searching line fixes it.

Ration check after the recommended edit: fragments for effect, 1 ("Sand."; "Not a clerk. Not a merchant." is dialogue). "as if" 0 or 1 (L61 is borderline). "like a" similes 0, with one "the way a horse trader" comparison. Soft adverbs 1 ("quietly", L97). Rhetorical questions 0. Correction constructions: 1 in dialogue (L43) and 1 descriptive (L103, defended).

## Opening

**Current (L3 to L7):**

> When the sea finally spat me out, it did so without ceremony.
>
> Sand. My chest slammed into it so hard I thought my ribs had cracked. Salt water poured from my mouth, my nose, places I did not know could hold water. The retreating surf clawed at my legs, trying to pull me back. I dug my fingers into the coarse grit and held on.
>
> Breathing was a battle. ...

**Verdict: revise.** L3 is a writer clearing his throat. It answers Ch 3's last line ("The sea closed over my head like the hand of an angry god") with a neat swallow-and-spit, and it uses the book's most repeated opener word ("ceremony"). OPENINGS_ENDINGS_MOTIFS already recommends cutting it. The second paragraph is the real opening, physical and fast. It misses one true pain beat: his nails were taken in Goa (ch1:13), and he is digging those fingers into grit.

**Proposed first three paragraphs:**

> Sand. My chest slammed into it so hard I thought my ribs had cracked. Salt water poured from my mouth and nose. The retreating surf dragged at my legs. I dug my ruined fingers into the coarse grit and held on, and the salt found every place my nails had been.
>
> Each time I drew in air it brought more salt, more pain. My arms and legs would not answer me. Somewhere in the confusion the rope around my chest had come free of the spar. I never learned how.
>
> I lay there I do not know how long, cheek pressed to wet sand, waves washing over my back and receding. At some point the noise in my ears changed, and there were voices in it, rising and falling with the surf. Then words began to separate.

(The nails clause is optional. Everything else is cuts or plain substitutions.)

## Ending

**Current last lines (L121 to L129):**

> I closed my eyes for a moment, not to sleep but to gather what little strength I had left. Storms on land were different from storms at sea. They did not roar and crash; they crept in on quiet feet, through conversations and calculations.
>
> I had escaped the Portuguese dungeon and the drowning ship by chance and stubbornness. If I was to survive whatever came next, I would need more than that.
>
> ...
>
> His eyes moved over me the way a horse trader's eyes move over new stock. He noted the brand, the bandaged hands, the way I held myself despite the wounds. I had seen that look before, in market squares where men were sold. For a long moment he said nothing. In that silence, I was being weighed.

**Verdict: revise.** Three habits the style sheet bans are stacked here: a storm aphorism balanced on a semicolon (the motif is reserved for Ch 13), a recap-and-resolve ("whatever came next"), and a closing mood line that explains the simile. The right last image is already on the page, two sentences early.

**Proposed ending (L121 to L129):**

> I closed my eyes.
>
> Footsteps sounded outside, firm and quick. A new voice spoke in Malayalam, low but carrying, and the fishermen stood up to answer it.
>
> When the *kapitan* stepped into the house, the smell of tar and pepper came in with him. He was shorter than the fishermen but broader, with a scar that ran from his left ear to the corner of his jaw, the kind of scar a blade leaves when it just misses the throat.
>
> His eyes moved over me the way a horse trader's eyes move over new stock. I had seen that look in market squares where men were sold.

This ends on an image from his own life. It is bitter without comment and ties the horse trade to the servant of the title. It also gives Ch 5 a clean seam if Ch 5 opens on Ibrahim returning (see notes).

## Chapter-specific notes (continuity and history)

1. **Keshavrao (missing beat).** Ch 3 L205 to L209 has the rope carrying him over the side, and L225 has "Forgive me". A body on this shore is possible, and Nagoji never looks for one. Add at the end of L39: "I searched the waterline for a pair of narrow shoulders until one of the fishermen stepped between me and the sea." ("Narrow shoulders" is his own phrase for the boy at ch2:17 and ch3:225.) This is an action, not a feeling, and it hands on to the fisherman lifting his hand at L43.
2. **The irons (backlog N-20).** Ch 3 L117 has him shackled at wrists and ankles when he goes over. The L43 fix shows the iron. Also add at L101: "They laid me on a woven mat. One of the men struck the irons off my wrists and ankles with a stone, and they set about stripping off the salt-stiffened rags that clung to me."
3. **The brand (backlog N-21).** Cutting L45 leaves it on the arm (L19, L93, L95). If the author adopts the backlog's "left forearm everywhere", L19 becomes "See the brand on his forearm." Note that Ch 1 never shows the branding (Duarte stops the brazier with "Not yet", ch1:85), so the reader first learns of it here.
4. **Language logic.** Everything quoted on the beach and in the village before L105 would be Malayalam. The L41 frame ("What they said I had that night from the one who spoke Konkani") covers the beach, L93 turns to gesture, and the optional relay at L97 covers the woman. Also at L57: he already spoke Konkani at L29, so "switched to a broken Konkani" should read "switched back to his broken Konkani".
5. **Seam with Ch 5 (for the Ch 5 audit):**
   - ch5:3 opens "The man they called *kapitan*", which repeats ch4:127. The L127 fix removes the repeat on this side.
   - ch5:9 "after a day of rest" does not fit Ch 4 ending with the kapitan in the room. Proposed Ch 5 opener: "The *kapitan* came back the next morning and gave his name as Ibrahim."
   - ch5:27 "The fishermen had spoken the name Travancore" is wrong, because Nagoji says it himself at L67. Change it to "I had said the name Travancore on the beach, but I wanted to hear it from this man's mouth."
   - ch5:13 to ch5:23 repeats this chapter's exchange ("You fought them." "Yes." "Our king fights them too ... the Dutch"), and ch5:11 is a fourth wound inventory. Ch 4 should own the exchange, since it carries the "teeth" plant. Trim it in Ch 5.
6. **History, L63 "Our king to the south fights them too".** In 1738 Travancore was not at war with the Portuguese. Its fights were with neighbouring rajas, some of them backed by the Dutch, and open war with the Dutch began in 1739. A fisherman can boast, but "has no love for them" would be safer. Ch 5 repeats the claim, so the author should rule on it once for both chapters.
7. **Period dress.** Cloths in place of lungis and vests (L25), and the cloth at her waist in place of a sari (L95). REGISTER_AND_ANACHRONISM flags L25 as P2. The Voice Bible lists "lungi" among the coast words he learns, so if the author wants the word at all, it belongs in a later chapter, once he has lived there. Save *mundu* for the moment he learns it.
8. **Voyage length (backlog C10-03).** Cutting L85 removes "in weeks", the only duration cue for the voyage. Either add a day count in Ch 2 as the backlog proposes, or carry it at L101: "It was the first roof I had lain under in weeks that did not creak."
9. **Spelling and italics of *kapitan*.** Italic at L97, L99, L119 and L127, roman at L111 and L113. Unify here. REGISTER_AND_ANACHRONISM and the backlog (FM-08) propose Kappittan book-wide, which is the author's call.
10. **Rope burns.** Ch 3 L205 has the rope peeling the skin from his palm. At L43, the fisherman who finds "soldier's calluses" is looking at a raw palm. Optional: "soldier's calluses under the rope burns".
11. **"sea gods" (L7).** Cut with the rest of L7. If a god is ever named in these pages, it should be one of his own (Khandoba, Bhavani), not a generic sea god.
12. **"marata" (L53).** This is the Portuguese form (João uses it at ch1:89 and ch2:57), and it is plausible in a trading fisherman's mouth. Keep it, but settle roman or italic book-wide.
