| Estimated word cut | **About 26%** (8,326 to about 6,150 words) |

Two voices are fighting in this chapter. One is Nagoji at his best. You can hear it in "The gun does not fear your heartbeat. Make it go slower than the drum."; in "The same drills. Different cloth. Mount up."; in the officer who "was very particular about his cuffs"; in Lannoy's "The quartermaster is very particular about inventory"; in the Rani taking her coin back; and in "If we are to be blamed together, we may as well be armed together." The other voice is a machine glossing each scene. It shows up in four places:

- **The thesis is stated over and over.** "The man inside" or "the coat is only cloth" comes five times (L175, L215, L247, L259, L367). The Prince's change is announced three times (L611, L647, L649). Why his uncle sent him is explained three times (L403, L591, L627 to 631).
- **Nearly every section ends on a buttoned close.** Thirteen of the fourteen section endings close on an aphorism or a one-line kicker. The one exception is L269 (the northern patrol), and it is also the liveliest close in the chapter.
- **Every speaker talks in the same quotable antithesis.** That includes a fourteen-year-old ("The others speak around the truth. You speak through it."), a Jewish merchant, a Syrian Christian gunner and a Portuguese priest.
- **The register slips are heavy:** mission and vision, "do the work", "deer in torchlight", "Downrange", "company policy", "overcommitted", "Every transaction is a story".

The 26% cut is well above the PLAN's 5 to 10%. I checked it line by line against the risk of over-cutting:

- **Most of it is repetition.** About two thirds of the cut words restate something shown in this chapter or already told in Ch 15 and 16: the second to fifth statements of the thesis, the recap of Lannoy's capture, the candles, the uniform rationale told four ways, the temple recap, the three "why my uncle sent me" lines, and the two outlasting speeches.
- **Nothing is lost.** No scene, plot beat, historical fact or line that Ch 25 quotes is removed. Every section survives.
- **I declined the biggest structural cuts proposed.** The holistic reader suggested dropping the Thoma and Avraham sections, or moving the armoury scene up. I rejected both (Chapter notes 23), so that material is compressed, not removed.

Where I overruled an auditor:

- **Some auditor continuity flags are wrong or misplaced.**
  - Two of the pattern auditor's are mistaken: L311 against L355 (Ch 1 has both the nod and the wrist-grab) and L399 (Ch 23 does put the king's blood on white silk).
  - One is on the wrong line: L669 (the error is "converted" at L667).
  - The holistic reader's "identity muddle" for Avraham (L745) disappears once L715 is concrete, because he is plainly Paradesi.
- **Both auditors missed four real problems.**
  - The opening time stamp contradicts "four years" and "more than a decade".
  - Ch 25 L260 says Duarte already knew Nagoji was alive, which undercuts the surprise at L315.
  - L365 credits Duarte with Nagoji's own words.
  - L137's logic fails, because Dutch soldiers wear blue and grey.

**Verdict: revise.** The nickname is a strong hook: concrete, period-plausible, slightly rude, and it plants the tooth without comment. Three things around it are wrong.

- **It comes last in a time-stamp sentence.** This is one of the book's seven time-stamp openings (OPENINGS_ENDINGS_MOTIFS).
- **The time stamp is wrong.** "The first dry season after" puts the chapter within a year of Ch 23. L387 (four years), L309 (more than a decade) and Ch 25 L300 (eleven years since Goa) all put it about four years later.
- **L5 explains the joke** that L23 to 27 tells better. Then L19 to 21 turn the pennant into a thesis.

The deeper problem is mode. L1 to 105 is iterative summary covering a year, and it swings between live exchanges (L13 to 17, L23 to 27, L33 to 35, L67 to 85, L97 to 101) and summary (L5, L19, L29 to 39, L59 to 65, L87 to 89). The confirmed rows keep every exchange and cut most of the summary.

**Proposed** (replaces L3 to 27; about 215 words against 260):

> By the fourth dry season after the Kollamkara valley, the men in the fort had a name for the newest bastion: “the foreigner's tooth.”

If the author prefers a year to a count of seasons, "By 1748 the men in the fort had a name for the newest bastion" also settles backlog CL-06. Ch 14 opens on a year, so this is not a new habit.

## Ending

Current last lines (L821 to 831):

> We left the armoury and stepped out onto the wall.
>
> Below, under the foreigner's tooth, men still drilled in the waning light.
>
> Travancore men, moving in patterns that had never existed on this coast before European hulls, Deccan riders and Dutch captains all smashed into it.
>
> Whatever names future scribes gave this army, for this moment, it was ours.
>
> We stood under its standard.
>
> Together.

**Verdict: rewrite.** Both auditors agree, and so does every audit document that mentions it: this is the book's most recognisably machine-written close. L825 is syntactically broken ("it" has no referent, and "smashed into" is modern and casual). L827 turns to future scribes and "for this moment". L829 to 831 drop the title and end on a one-word kicker. The scene above it is half excellent and half retread:

- **Excellent (L781 to 791):** the talwar, "If the ground is cluttered, curve your steel", the hilt offered, and "blamed together ... armed together". It is the chapter's best exchange between the two men.
- **Retread (L793 to 805):** it repeats the coat conversation and Ch 16, then labels Nagoji's thoughts and glosses the theme.

**Proposed** (L807 to L819 with the fixes above, after L779 to 801 as fixed; ends on dialogue):

> “Whom do we serve, Ananthan Pillai?” he asked. “The king? This coast? The men we buried at Colachel?”
>
> “All of them,” I said. “On different days.”
>
> He nodded. “On the days when it is the king,” he said, “we must be careful.”
>
> “You have been talking to Revathi,” I said.
>
> “She scolds me too,” he said. “Apparently my foreignness does not exempt me.”

Why this ending:

- **It is the author's own text with the aphorism and the coda removed.** Nothing is invented.
- **It closes the frame.** Revathi's scapegoat warning (L97 to 101) opens the chapter's argument, L789 pays it off, and the last line brings her back with a joke, without comment.
- **It follows the chapter's own best close.** The liveliest section close in the chapter (L269) is also Lannoy deflating a heavy moment.
- **It avoids a clash with Ch 16.** The Ch 16 audit proposes closing that chapter with the two men "on the wall until the light went", with a gun crew below. Ending Ch 24 on the wall at dusk above the drill would give two chapters the same closing picture (STYLE_SHEET section 3).

**Alternative (image)**, only if Ch 16 takes a different ending. This builds on OPENINGS_ENDINGS_MOTIFS's optional extension of L823:

> We left the armoury and went up onto the wall. Below, under the foreigner's tooth, the last drill of the day was forming: Nair musketeers in white coats, Maravar horse wheeling between the posts, and a boy of fourteen in the front rank, calling the count.

A third option is to end on L809 ("All of them," I said. "On different days."). It is honest and unresolved, but it loses Revathi.
