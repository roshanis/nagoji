## Verdict

| Measure | Call |
|:-|:-|
| Severity | **5 of 5** |
| Recommended intensity | **Deep**. Line-level re-voicing from L41 to the end. The Padmini passage (L33 to 39) needs a light touch only. |
| Estimated word cut | **About 27%** (2,321 to about 1,700 words: roughly 790 cut and 170 added back in concrete detail) |

Outside the Padmini passage this is one of the most machine-sounding chapters in the book. The scanner finds 32 one-sentence narration paragraphs, 16 verbless fragments, 7 short paragraph-final stings, five "not X, Y" shapes, the only "etched" in the whole book, and 21 edits needed to meet the style sheet. The texture is worse than the counts suggest. From L83 on nearly every paragraph carries a tell, and the dialogue is epigram volleys. The king, Ramayyan, Lannoy, Savitri and Nagoji all trade paired maxims ("This is siege without walls." / "Walls can be rebuilt."; "Exile." / "Survival."; "Empires fall. Women remain."; "I build for the living, not the remembered"). Savitri speaks twenty-first-century political theory ("power flows downward from one throne", "which man thinks he owns me"). The book's stock beats are all here: "It was not a question", "He did not need to", "Our eyes met", "She smiled, but there was no warmth in it", "For a long moment, neither spoke", "Something flickered". Nagoji himself is missing. He has no horse, gives no order and counts nothing. He goes to "measure" Savitri and reports no measurement.

The chapter has a sound spine to build on. That spine is the map and its colours (called back at Ch 28 L255), the king's throats and fist, Padmini's pyre under the jackfruit tree, Nandini's water pot, "They will eat him", Savitri reading Nagoji's errand, the white cloth gone grey on horseback, "I will not watch it from a ship", the laugh across courtyards, and the king's terms of exile.

The 27% cut is far above the 5 to 10% PLAN.md expects. I checked it against the risk of over-cutting, and almost all of it is restatement:

- Savitri's claim is stated four times (L73, L99 to 103, L109, L119).
- That it was always coming is said three times (L53, L115, L161).
- Her "we outlast you" thesis appears four times (L149, L157, L215, L219).
- The Raja flees twice (L63, L93).
- Kottarakkara is announced three times (L41, L45, L49) and then reintroduced as "a new name" (L57).
- The chapter has five endings (L221 to 231).

No plot beat or historical fact is lost. The additions give Nagoji his trade back: hill paths held by his riders, a count at Savitri's gate, the paddy bunds that break a horse's stride, a count of muskets and guns, and a horse from his lines at the end.

Decide four things before the line edit, because several fixes depend on them:

1. What happens to the Raja (backlog N-18). I recommend that he dies in confinement; see Chapter notes 1.
2. One sequence for Savitri's movements (Chapter notes 2).
3. Whether the chapter opens on Padmini's death (Opening).
4. Which single Savitri "outlasting" line survives. I recommend the garden line at L157, against OPENINGS_ENDINGS_MOTIFS, which keeps L219 (see Defended).

## Opening

Current first lines (L3 to 5): "The map in the war hall had grown crowded. / Not with our own marks, with others'. Ramayyan had drawn them in different inks, each colour a different threat, each line a different direction from which death might come."

**Verdict: revise, and move Padmini's death to the head of the chapter.** The first sentence is good, and both auditors agree the trouble starts at L5. What follows is 167 words of briefing in one template, a stock "not a question" beat, and the king's thesis. Then comes the structural fault. Padmini Amma, a major figure since Ch 8, dies in a flashback wedged into the war-hall scene ("By then Padmini Amma was gone."). The chapter stitches back with a seam line that contradicts the scene (L41), and then announces Kottarakkara three times (L41, L45, L49).

Opening on the fever and the pyre does four things. It gives Padmini's death its due (backlog BL-03, C12-03), it removes the seam, and it lets the map scene run straight into the king naming Kottarakkara. It also sets one old house passing to a child against another that will not pass at all. Nothing is cut except the flourishes.

**Proposed** (replaces L3 to 49; about 460 words against 700; every fact kept):

> The fever that followed the last rains took Padmini Amma in three days, quick and unsentimental, as she would have chosen. The same jackfruit tree that had shaded her stick when she scolded kings shaded the pyre where her body burned, the smoke carrying the smell of ghee and pepper leaves.
>
> Her heir was her sister's girl, Nandini, still small enough that the brass water pot she carried looked too big for her hands. The house elders came to Velinadu and to the fort both, cloths knotted tight, and asked Revathi and me to stand with them until the child was old enough to carry the name herself.
>
> I was Padmini's adopted son, and some duties fell to me that no woman, however senior, could perform in the eyes of temple and custom. I helped carry her body out and round the pyre, and I put the fire to it. When the priests chanted the rites for the dead, they looked to me to answer where a son might have stood.
>
> I did not inherit that estate. No man does, here. But as her adopted son and Revathi's husband I became its guardian. When tax men came or Marakkars haggled or temple stewards asked about lamp oil, I signed beside Nandini's mark. My work was to keep the roof sound and the vines alive until she grew into her own temper.
>
> When the rites were done, the king sent for me.
>
> The map in the war hall had grown crowded. Ramayyan had inked the other powers on it in colours: Mysore in red to the north, still half rumour, a hill kingdom learning to move men and guns together; the Nizam's lands in black to the northeast, far off, though his armies had a way of arriving; Arcot in brown to the east, where the Nawab sold his horse to the English or the French, whichever paid that season. All round the coast ran the blue of the Europeans, Dutch, Portuguese, English and French, with a ship off every port that had pepper to sell.
>
> "We are small," Marthanda Varma said, his finger on the borders of his kingdom. "Smaller than any of them. If they came together, if even two of them agreed to split us, we would last a season. Perhaps two."
>
> He looked up at me. "Do you know why I have spent twenty years taking every small kingdom on this coast?"
>
> "To deny them footholds," I said. "Every petty raja who might sell his port to Mysore or his pepper to the Dutch without your leave. Every chief who might let them in behind you while you face the front."
>
> "Partly," he said. He tapped the map. "A hundred small kingdoms are a hundred small throats to cut. One larger kingdom is a single throat, yes, but it is also a single fist. Divided, we are meat. United, we are at least a problem."
>
> His finger moved inland from Kollam and stopped.
>
> "Kottarakkara," he said. "The last of the old houses that still believes it can stand alone."

If the author wants the Ch 26 rupture answered (backlog BL-03), one plain sentence after the first paragraph does it without explaining anything: "She had not sent for me since the pepper fields burned, and she did not send for me at the end."

**Fallback, if the map must stay first:** keep L3, use the map paragraph above for L5 to 13, cut L21 and "But there is more.", change L31 to "Padmini Amma had been three months dead by then.", apply the L33 to 39 fixes, cut L41 to 47, and end the section with the king's finger and L49 as above.

## Chapter-specific notes

1. **The Raja's fate (backlog N-18).** In this chapter he flees to Kochi twice (L63, L93), yet Savitri says her kinsman "died in a room he could not leave" (L137). The king answers "More than your kinsman received" (L211), and Ch 28 L173 has "men like Savitri's kinsman die in rooms they cannot leave". Ch 21 L68 records "Chief: confined. Health: declining." The death in confinement has four supports against the flight's two, both of them in this chapter, so I recommend keeping the death. The L93 fix has him run, get caught, and die under guard, which keeps the Kochi intent and gives Savitri's charge its ground. Knock-ons: L101 becomes "the raja was dead", and Ch 21 L68 ("confined", written years before the campaign) would read better as "Chief: old. Health: declining." If the author prefers the flight, then L137, L211 and Ch 28 L173 must change instead, and Savitri would share her Kochi exile with him, which is awkward.
2. **Savitri's movements.** The first edition has her "did not run" (L93), barricaded (L123), "fled to the Dutch" (L163), leading troops at Kottarakkara (L169), "captured trying to reach the coast" (L181), then refusing flight ("I will not watch it from a ship", L191). The fixes give one sequence. She holds the palace (L93, L123), goes out by night to the Dutch when it cannot hold (L163), comes back at the head of Dutch-armed men (L169), is taken on the field without running (L181), and is exiled north (Ending).
3. **Chronology.** "Twenty years" (L19) dates the campaign to about 1749, which matches Ch 28 L5 and the order at L57 (Kayamkulam 1746; Thekkumkur and Vadakkumkur 1749 to 1750). The real in-book conflict is Ch 21 L72, "before next monsoon", which sits about five years earlier (N-19). History puts the Elayadathu annexation in 1742. The Author's Historical Note already discloses the compression ("The novel compresses and braids these threads"), so no change is needed here beyond N-19. If Ch 28 L5 moves to Makaram 1750 (REGISTER), the battle falls in January, and "before the rains" in the L93 fix still works.
4. **Savitri is not new.** Nagoji has heard her name from Revathi (Ch 9 L146), in court (Ch 11 L253), from Padmini (Ch 13 L17), and on Ramayyan's leaf (Ch 21 L69). L57, L61 and L73 contradict all four and are cut.
5. **Geography.** Kottarakkara lies inland, a long ride east and north of Kollam. There is no salt air on its fields (L227), no ships to flee to from the field (L179), and from Kochi Savitri could see nothing of Travancore (L225). The Dutch post at Kollam (Thangasseri) is the natural place for the factors to run to; it is worth the author's check before naming it.
6. **Padmini (BL-03, C12-03).** Ch 26 L207 ends her last scene on "I will remember that", and her death here never answers it. The Opening proposal moves her death to the front, and the optional sentence ("She had not sent for me since the pepper fields burned, and she did not send for me at the end.") answers the rupture in one line. Ch 28 L9 ("Fever had taken her months before") agrees.
7. **The funeral rite.** "Around the inner stones" (L37) is unclear. If the author wants a named rite, the one to check with a Kerala source is the chief mourner walking round the pyre with a water pot that is pierced and then broken. In a house of the sister's line the rites would usually fall to a nephew. An adopted son standing in is fair fiction, and L37 already says so.
8. **"Matrilineal"** (L101, L145) is a 19th-century anthropologist's word. Use one period phrase book-wide, and REGISTER proposes "the law of the mothers". It also affects Ch 8 L55, Ch 21 L69 and glossary L72. The Author's Historical Note, which speaks in the author's own voice, can keep the modern term.
9. **"British"** (L11, L13) should be "English" (N-42), as should Ch 28 L165.
10. **Mysore.** The map rumour stays (L7; Ch 28 L255). Savitri's "when Mysore finally moves south" (L153) and "hold against Mysore" (L215) are hindsight, since the push into Malabar is 1766, and both are cut. Revathi's Mysore line at Ch 28 L143 is a separate call.
11. **Nagoji's part.** The first-edition chapter gives him no horse, no order and no count. The only additions this audit makes are one line each at L87, L93, L123, L167, L177 and the Ending, about 170 words in all. Each is plain work: paths held, a man taken, a gate counted, bunds fought along, guns counted, a horse sent.
