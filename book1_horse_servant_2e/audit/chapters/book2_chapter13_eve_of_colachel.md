## Verdict

| Measure | Call |
|:-|:-|
| Severity | **4 of 5** |
| Recommended intensity | **Deep.** The Sarpa Kavu and farewell (L249 to 309) and the history block (L105 to 167) need line-level re-voicing. The rest of the chapter needs medium work: cut the tics and tighten. |
| Estimated word cut | **About 30%**, from 4,407 words to about 3,100 after the short additions proposed below. About 12 points of that come from compressing the unbuilt history block. The other 18 come from line-level cuts. |

The chapter has a strong, authored core, and every section has at least one line only this book could contain. Examples: the recruits' ships "like houses turned on their sides ... With too many sticks", horses feeling "the ground under different tides", lamp heat pressed to the eyelids, Ibrahim's "Best to have several listening", the mourning clothes laid where they could be found, "The gods have poor handwriting", and "do not make me a widow for a king I did not choose". None of this should be lost.

Between those lines the surface runs on template, in three places:

- **The war-council insert (L105 to 167).** SOURCE_OF_TRUTH identifies it as a 29-paragraph addition made after the last build. It uses straight quotes, a lecture register, a flashback that slips back into present-scene tags, and "returning to the present" to rejoin the old scene.
- **The Sarpa Kavu and Revathi farewell (L249 to 309).** This is the densest stretch in the book. It moves from fantasy portent into romance cliché into therapy vocabulary.
- **Kickers and maxims through the rest of the chapter.** There are about ten quotable maxims across seven speakers. There are eight "not X, but Y" corrections against a ration of one, three held-breath images (L5, L217, L275), and a double one-line close.

Two voice misses matter more than any single tic, and both are chances to gain character, not just to cut:

- At L189 Nagoji finds the encirclement plan "colder" and lacking "glory". That is a European heavy-cavalry reflex. A Maratha huzurat rider was raised on refusing pitched battle and starving the enemy, so he should recognise the plan with a professional's pleasure.
- In the snake grove (L261) he feels horror-film dread. A Deccan man would know snake worship at the anthill from Nag Panchami at home, so the grove should strike him as strange and familiar at once.

One timeline fault has to be settled before the prose pass, because several fixes depend on it. It is covered in Chapter notes, item 1. The insert places this eve on 9 August 1741, six months after the landing, which matches the historical battle date, the sibling Ch 12 audit and REVIEW_BACKLOG N-13. The older war-council lines (L167 to 187) still plan the siege as if the Dutch had not landed. I rule for the insert's timeline and recast the council as the last tightening of the ring. The fixes below assume that.

Tally after merging: **96 confirmed**, **27 borderline**, **34 defended**. Thirteen table rows and three chapter notes are adjudicator additions, marked (A).

## Opening

Current first lines (L3 to 9):

> The day before Colachel smelled of coconut smoke and rain that had not yet fallen.
> Clouds had gathered over the sea since morning, thick and low, their underbellies a dark grey that matched the mood along the coast. The air was heavy, each breath like drinking warm water. Even the waves seemed to roll in with less enthusiasm, as if waiting for something.
> We waited with them.
> Reports had come in all day.

**Verdict: revise.** The first line is the right first line. After it the paragraph follows the machine sequence: an image, then an explanation of the image ("matched the mood", waves "as if waiting"), then a one-line kicker that turns the image into theme ("We waited with them."), then a second one-liner. Three report paragraphs of identical shape follow, and the first scene with a person in it does not arrive until L23. The opening also never tells the reader that the Dutch have been ashore for six months, which the insert assumes.

**Proposed** (about 110 words where there were 155; it signals the stockade and brings Ramayyan in by the second paragraph):

> The day before Colachel smelled of coconut smoke and rain that had not yet fallen. Cloud had lain low over the sea since morning, and the air was so heavy that each breath was like drinking warm water.
>
> Reports came in all day. From the boy on the palm tower came word of the Dutch ships shifting their anchorage off the stockade, their boats running between them and the beach. Ibrahim and his Marakkar cousins sent word of supply boats set adrift in the night with their cables cut, and small fires on distant decks. Messengers from the inland chiefs spoke of men mustering, some eager, some sullen, and of certain houses grumbling that this was not their quarrel.
>
> Kottarakkara sent nothing. Ramayyan's stylus paused over the name, then moved on.

Continue with "When I asked about it, he said only, 'They are waiting for the price to change.'"

## Ending

Current last lines (L313 to 321):

> ... and through the smoke, Revathi's sorrowful smile watching me.
> In the last fragment before dawn, I stood alone on a beach that was both Colachel and not, the sea on one side, the fort on the other, the sound of drums carrying on the wind.
> When the real drums began, calling men to arms in the grey before sunrise, they sounded exactly like the ones in my dream.
> The eve was over.
> The storm was here.

**Verdict: revise.**

- The drums at L317 are a real image ending, and the dream before them mostly works.
- Cut the two weak dream phrases: a smile cannot watch, and "both Colachel and not" is the stock dream formula.
- "The eve was over." is a summary line that restates the chapter title. Cut it.
- "The storm was here." is the house's chosen storm payoff (planted at Ch 5 L105), so keep it. It should not follow a summary, though, and it should land on something physical. The chapter opens on rain "that had not yet fallen", and a thin drizzle comes at dusk (L197). Let the rain arrive with the drums so the frame closes without being announced.
- Ch 14 L79 has the sun breaking through on 10 August, which fits rain at dawn that clears by midmorning.

**Proposed:**

> In a third, I was on that wet strip of sand, horses pounding beside me, Dutch muskets spitting fire, and somewhere behind the smoke, Revathi's lamp.
>
> In the last fragment before dawn, I stood alone on a beach that was Colachel one moment and a Godavari sandbank the next, the sea on one side, the fort on the other, the sound of drums carrying on the wind.
>
> When the real drums began, calling men to arms in the grey before sunrise, they sounded exactly like the ones in my dream. I took up my sword and went out to the horses. The night's drizzle had turned to rain, and it was coming in off the sea hard enough to flatten the smoke over the cooking fires.
>
> The storm was here.

A fallback, if the author later drops the storm motif altogether, is to end on "... hard enough to flatten the smoke over the cooking fires." That is an image close with no one-liner.

## Chapter-specific notes (continuity and history)

1. **Timeline: which eve is this? (backlog N-11, N-13; SOURCE_OF_TRUTH Decision 2.)** The unbuilt insert (L105 to 167) sets this night about six months after the February landing, after the Tengapattanam raid, after Hackert sailed in May and left Rijtel in charge. That is historically sound for the eve of the 10 August 1741 battle, and it is the author's latest text. Ch 12's audit has already ruled the same way, cutting Ch 12 L153's "Tomorrow ... They would land at Colachel". The older lines of this chapter still read as the eve of a landing: L99 ("They expect to march inland"), L167 ("Two thousand men"), L175 ("We pin them"), L177 ("A siege?"), L179 ("We dig in") and L187 ("once they step onto that beach"). The confirmed rows recast the council as the last tightening of the ring, and the king's "No charge. Not yet." then sets up the charge through the smoke in Ch 14. **Knock-on for Ch 14:** its opening (L1 to 21, "by the time we closed our ring", "The first days were confusion", "For weeks, we pinned them") narrates the siege as if it began after this chapter. It must become a retrospective or be trimmed. Its L65 (mud ramparts, Hackert, Rijtel) duplicates this chapter's L145 to 151, so keep those facts in one chapter only.
2. **Garrison size (backlog N-12).** L151 gives 250 Europeans and 50 lascars. L167 says "Two thousand men". The confirmed L167 fix uses "Three hundred". Check that Ch 14 and Ch 15 agree. Ch 15 also names Donnadi as senior surviving officer while this chapter names Rijtel (SOURCE_OF_TRUTH, section 9, item 4).
3. **Revathi's whereabouts.** At L19 she writes from Velinadu that she "would remain within her halls". At L263 she is in Padmini's grove the same night, with no explanation. The confirmed L263 fix ("So much for her halls in Velinadu.") turns the gap into a dry beat. The alternative is to soften L19.
4. **Dhanaji (backlog N-01).** "who had survived Goa's dungeons alongside me" (L221) contradicts Chs 1 to 4. The fix is "one of my old riders from the Deccan". His knowledge of the ship jump at L241 is fine: Nagoji could have told him.
