REPETITION_AND_DENSITY ranks this chapter second in the book by tic index, and the audit bears that out. The ceremony is built as a trailer: a thesis before the scene (L3), a negation hook (L7), a "Not X. Not Y. Z." reveal (L75), one-line suspense paragraphs (L29, L45, L67, L73, L85, L89) and a narrator who explains every gesture the moment it is made (L53 to 55, L67 to 69, L83, L91, L97). Every section after the first ends on an aphorism (L153, L189, L249), and the book itself ends five times (L279, L291 to 293, L299, L301 to 303, L305). Four characters who should sound nothing alike, Revathi, the king, De Lannoy and Duarte, all speak in the same balanced antitheses ("Closed is not the same as open", "the walls become a prison and the accounts become chains", "The banner does not ask where we came from"). The chapter leans on weight (4), price and cost (9), garden (6), remember (9) and silence (5), and it lists the forts, walls, roads and army three times.

It is a 4, not a 5, because the human material is real and it is where it should be. The chief's whisper and his neighbour's twitching lips (L57 to 63), Ramayyan checking the mortar during the rite (L65) and the joke that pays it off in the chapel (L223 to 227), the dusty dhoti and ash-grey hands (L93), the clipped Revathi exchange with "Liar" and "Perhaps" (L111 to 125), the verandah and the loaded musket (L263, touchstone T15), the horses that feared the sand (L273), "I miss what it cost" (L279), and the Nair house that kept his children as its own (L287). None of these needs more than a word changed.

The cut is high, well above the PLAN's 5 to 10% book-wide. I checked it against over-cutting line by line, and about four-fifths of it is restatement: the theme stated a second, third or fourth time within the same scene, speeches that duplicate Ch 26 L253 and Ch 27 L15 to 29 and L197 to 219, and the stacked codas. No plot beat, historical fact or character moment is lost. The fixes add back a few things the chapter lacks: a date, what a Kerala temple asks of a man (bare to the waist, a god seen through three doors), the historical return of the sword to the king, and a last page that ends on Nagoji's hands instead of a maxim.

One decision sits under the line edit and should be taken first: **Revathi is Nagoji's wife** (Ch 20 L262, Ch 26 L249, Ch 27 L39), and this chapter's own coda gives them children and grandchildren (L261, L287, L295). L153 ("this woman I might have loved in another life") and L127 ("as if seeing me for the first time") play them as strangers. The fixes below assume the marriage stands and play L107 to 151 as a husband and wife, which costs very few words.

## Opening

Current first lines (L3 to 9): "The fall of Kottarakkara broke the resistance of the old houses, but Marthanda Varma knew that broken things could be mended if the glue was strong enough. He needed an act that would seal the kingdom forever, beyond the reach of Dutch intrigue or noble ambition. / Three days later, in the monsoon month of 1750, he called every noble in the kingdom to Padmanabhaswamy. / Not for war council. Not for treaty. For something else entirely. / The temple at night was a different creature than by day. ..."

**Verdict: rewrite L3 to 13.** Both auditors agree. L3 is a thesis in the king's head that Nagoji cannot know, and Ch 27 has just ended on the same idea ("Now came the work of making sure they could never rise again"). L5 has the wrong season. L7 is the chapter's first "Not X. Not Y. Z." hook. L9 is a stock establishing shot with gothic shadows. The chapter comes alive at L11 with Padmini's empty place and at L15 with the conch. OPENINGS_ENDINGS_MOTIFS proposes the same shape.

What the opening lacks is Nagoji. At the most Hindu ceremony in the book, a Maratha stands in a Kerala temple and notices nothing a Maratha would notice. Two details do that without any interior monologue: the rule that men go bare to the waist, and a god too long to be seen through one door. Both are given in the table (L13, L31).

**Proposed** (replaces L3 to 13; about 125 words against 244):

> In the month of Makaram, in the first days of 1750, the king called every noble in the kingdom to Padmanabhaswamy after dark.
>
> Oil lamps burned in brass holders along every wall, their flames doubled in the polished stone floor. Camphor smoke gathered under the roof beams.
>
> I stood at the back, among the lesser officers. Near the front there was a place where Padmini would once have stood, stick planted, eyes sharp. Fever had taken her months before, yet in that packed hall the gap she left felt as visible as any banner. Revathi had not come.
>
> The nobles stood in the mandapa by rank, bare to the waist as the temple required, their jewels catching the lamplight.

Then L15 as written. Optional, at L31, if the author wants one line of his Deccan eye: "In the Deccan temples of my boyhood a man could see his god whole from the door."

## Ending

Current last lines (L295 to 305):

> The monsoon came that year, as it always did. The rains fell on the forts and the fields and the roads. They fell on Kochi, where Savitri's garden drank them gratefully. They fell on Velinadu, where Revathi sat and remembered and my grandchildren ran laughing through wet corridors.
>
> They fell on me, on these walls, on this kingdom that had eaten so many smaller ones to become something that might survive.
>
> I lifted my face to the rain and let it wash away what it could.
>
> Some things do not wash.
>
> Some things we carry.
>
> For as long as we live, and in the stories that outlast us.

**Verdict: rewrite (L281 to 305).** This is the last page of the novel and the most machine-sounding passage in it (VOICE_BIBLE C8). After "I miss what it cost" (L279) the chapter ends five more times: the officer explained (L281), the lesson of the dungeon stated (L283), "somewhere ... somewhere ... here" (L285 to 289), "That was the price. / That was always the price." (L291 to 293), the rain falling on every place in turn (L295 to 297), the washing cliché (L299, already used at Ch 25 L256), and three maxims ending on stories that outlast us (L301 to 305, already said by Savitri at Ch 27 L219). The last line is a mood-summary fragment, the shape the STYLE_SHEET bans.

The materials for a true ending are already on the page: the rain, the wall he guards, the grandchildren in a house that is not his line (L287), and his hands. Ch 1 opens on his fingernails ("They had already taken my fingernails", Ch 1 L13) and on water "steady as monsoon rain on a Deccan roof" (Ch 1 L9). Ending on those hands closes the book without saying what it means.

**Proposed** (replaces L281 to 305; the officer leaves, then one paragraph of image and action):

> "I miss what it cost," I said.
>
> He waited for more. When none came, he touched his brow and went down to see to the watch.
>
> The monsoon came that year as it always did, on Savitri's garden in Kochi and on the courtyards at Velinadu, where my grandchildren ran shouting through the wet in a house that had taken me in as a guest and kept my children as its own. I was on the wall when it reached us. I held out my hands to it, the nails still ridged where João's wedges had gone in, and let them fill.

This keeps the garden and the grandchildren once each, the matrilineal clause, and the monsoon, and it ends on an action. It shares its closing idea with no other chapter (Ch 25's rain washes; this rain fills).

**Alternative** (VOICE_BIBLE C8, if the author would rather end on counting than on the hands): after "He waited for more ...", end with "I stayed on the wall in the rain until the watch changed, counting the lamps as they were lit along the ramparts, as I always had." The last four words answer Ch 1 L95 ("I counted, as I always had.").

The section endings inside the chapter need the same treatment, and the table does it: the ceremony already ends well at L103; the Revathi scene now ends on her riding away (L151); the rampart on "I had no comfort to offer." (L187); the chapel on De Lannoy's line (L241).

## Chapter-specific notes

1. **Revathi is his wife (N-05).** Ch 20 L262 ("my wife, my *wife*"), Ch 26 L249 ("I married you with my eyes open"), Ch 27 L39 ("Revathi's husband"). L127 and L153 contradict it; L261, L287 and L295 confirm it with children and grandchildren. The fixes at L127, L133, L137, L149, L151 and L153 play the scene as a marriage under strain. Separately, no birth is shown anywhere in the book; Ch 20 L109 and L318 raise children as a hope. If the author wants the grandchildren on the last page to land, a single earlier mention of a child (Ch 26 or 27) would plant them.
2. **The date (CL-05, REGISTER 28:5).** The Thrippadidanam was on 3 January 1750 (5 Makaram 925 ME). "Three days later" after Kottarakkara also pins the Ch 27 battle to the end of December 1749. Historically the Elayadathu house at Kottarakkara was taken in the early 1740s and Thekkumkur and Vadakkumkur came last; the book has already reordered this at Ch 27 L57, so the L5 fix only avoids making the date problem worse.
3. **Who may enter the temple.** Non-Hindus were not admitted to Padmanabhaswamy. L11's "Marakkar captains" should wait outside. This is also the place to answer backlog C10-49 (Ibrahim is absent from Chs 27 and 28): for example, in L11, "The Marakkar captains, Ibrahim among them, waited outside the east gate; the temple was not theirs to enter." Author's choice whether to add it.
4. **The sword.** L71 leaves it at the god's feet; L103 says "god's servant still held the sword". Tradition has the king lay the state sword before the god and receive it back from the priests as Padmanabhadasa. The L95 fix adds that handback, which resolves the contradiction and is the historical shape of the rite.
5. **The broom (N-09).** Ch 21 L193 already shows the king sweeping at a festival, broom in hand, so this chapter's broom is not a first. The backlog recommends fixing Ch 21 (make it a lamp procession). Separately, the best-known royal sweeping rite is the Gajapati king's at Puri (Chhera Pahanra). If the author has a source for sweeping at the Thrippadidanam, cite it in the Historical Note; if not, note it there as invention.
6. **The god in the sanctum.** The reclining image is seen through three doors, and the temple's Anantha is usually described as five-hooded with the hoods turned inward. Both details are used in the L31 and L35 fixes. Check that the three-door view is right for 1750; the image was remade during the king's rebuilding of the temple in the 1730s.
7. **The herald (REGISTER 28:17).** "His Highness" is the style fixed on Indian princes under British rule. "Listen and witness. Sree Anizham Thirunal Marthanda Varma, Maharaja of Travancore, ..." Also decide "Padmanabha Dasa" (L99) against the usual one-word "Padmanabhadasa" and put the choice in the glossary.
8. **The treasury (N-30).** If any of L33's coin list survives, "aurae" must be "aurei". The inventory itself reflects the vault opening of 2011; "men said" keeps it as hearsay.
9. **Mavelikkara.** L159 "returned to Mavelikkara": Ch 18 L67 and L157 put the earlier truce at Mannar, so "came to". The 1753 date and the terms are right.
10. **The English (N-42, REGISTER 28:165).** "British" at L165 against "English factors" at L273. The English had held Madras since 1640, so "have begun building" is wrong for 1753; the Carnatic war over Arcot (1749 to 1754) is what an envoy would bring news of. REGISTER's own suggested fix keeps "begun building", which does not solve the history.
11. **Savitri's kinsman (N-18).** L173 repeats Ch 27 L137 (he "died in a room he could not leave"), but Ch 27 L63 and L93 have the Raja flee to Kochi. Settle it in Ch 27; L173 follows.
12. **Revathi and the laugh.** L149 has Revathi describe the laugh "she will never laugh again", a moment only the king saw (Ch 27 L197 to 203). The L149 fix removes it.
13. **De Lannoy (N-04).** Born 1715, so about 37 or 38 in 1753: "greying head", not "grey head". He was a Fleming in the Company's service, so "Dutch soldier" (L211) and "Dutch artillerist" (L213) are loose; "Company officer" and "Kappittan" are what the fixes use. His wish to be buried at Udayagiri (L231) is historically grounded: he died in 1777 and lies in the church inside the fort under a Latin inscription recording his service, so "a stone that says I served Travancore" is a fair nod. Keep it.
14. **The chapel.** A small mission chapel of the 1750s would have had mats, not pews. Mass was not said in the evening then; "prayers" (L197) is safe, so do not turn L195 into a Mass.
15. **L203 "a kingdom that just surrendered itself to ours".** The Offering was three years earlier. "in a kingdom that belongs to ours" keeps the joke and drops "just".
16. **L211 "a Hindu kingdom that had broken the power of both their empires".** Travancore never fought Portugal. Cut with the sentence.
17. **L213 "Twenty years ago ... across three continents".** 1738 to 1753 is fifteen years, and the Marathas were fighting the Portuguese at Vasai, not the Dutch.
18. **L245 "a sword blessed by a Brahmin".** Not planted anywhere in Chs 1 to 27 (checked). Cut with the paragraph, or plant it earlier.
19. **Counting the kingdoms.** L109 "a dozen", L257 "a hundred". Ch 27 uses both (L29 and L215 "a hundred" in the king's and Savitri's mouths; L105 "the dozen petty rajas"). In narration, Nagoji should give the smaller, counted figure.
20. **Madurai (N-26).** No Nayak reigned at Madurai after 1736. L273: "borrowed lancers from Madurai".
21. **The army at L275.** Fifty thousand is a fair figure for the king's last years. "A navy" overclaims. Star forts "from Kottar to the northern marches" would include the Travancore Lines, built from 1764, after the king's death; that is acceptable only because the coda is "years later". The fix names only De Lannoy's bastions.
22. **Ch 1 misremembered.** L283 has Nagoji "telling a priest that belief did not matter". At Ch 1 L101 he decided it alone, in the night, and told no one.
23. **Echoes in other chapters.** L299 repeats Ch 25 L256 (rain washing); L305 repeats Ch 27 L219 (stories outlast); L227 "ghost of a smile" repeats Ch 20 and Ch 24; "turned away, then back" repeats Ch 26 L251 and Ch 27 L205; L97's board image is kept at Ch 21 L55 (VOICE_BIBLE Appendix B); L129's river is kept at Ch 19 L153; L229's storm is kept at Ch 13.
24. **Small corrections if the lines survive.** L47 "servant of He" should be "Him". L65 "the King" should be lower case. L157 "cannons" should be "cannon" (REGISTER).
25. **Typography.** Straight quotes at L99, L201, L219, L269 and L279; curly elsewhere.
