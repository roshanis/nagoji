# Second Edition Style Sheet, v2 (calibrated)

*Horse of the Servant*, second edition. The rules every line edit follows.

**Status.** Draft replacement for `STYLE_SHEET.md`, built from the 30 unit audits in `audit/chapters/`, the book-level reports in `audit/`, and the blind pilot in `pilot/` (summarised in `audit/SUMMARY.md`). It takes effect when the author approves it after the calibration batch (Chapters 1 to 3 and 27). Until then `STYLE_SHEET.md` stands unchanged.

**What changed from v1.** v1 listed patterns to kill and five rations. v2 keeps all of that, and adds: a line standard set by the pilot (section 0); rations recalibrated to what the adjudicated audits kept, plus new rations for rhythm, stock gestures and props (section 2); fidelity guardrails from the judges' grafts (section 4); an explicit Do-not-cut list from the 647 defended flags (section 5); an ending register for all 30 units with no repeated closing idea (section 6); the motif plan (section 7); the register lexicon (section 8); and the house spelling list (section 9).

**How examples are cited.** `ch7:145` means Chapter 7, line 145 of the frozen first-edition file in `book1_horse_servant/`. Every "Before" quotation is exact. "After" lines come from the chapter audits, the Voice Bible (`VOICE_BIBLE.md`), or the pilot versions (`pilot/A_Q.md` and the others); they are models of register, and the author decides the final words. An After marked *(needs approval)* adds a fact and waits for the author's yes.

**When documents disagree**, this order holds: (1) the author's Gate 0 decisions (`audit/SUMMARY.md` section 6); (2) this sheet; (3) the chapter audit, for line-level fixes; (4) `VOICE_BIBLE.md`, for voice; (5) `OPENINGS_ENDINGS_MOTIFS.md` (OEM), `REGISTER_AND_ANACHRONISM.md` and `REPETITION_AND_DENSITY.md`, for book-wide lists. When the story and a rule disagree, the story wins; log the exception in `notes/changelog.md`.

## 0. The standard

### 0.1 One line standard for every chapter

**Deep in narration, medium in dialogue, never in facts.**

- **Narration (deep).** Rebuild paragraphs rather than trimming inside them. Replace an abstraction with the object it stands for. Fold fragments and triple anaphora into ordinary sentences. End each paragraph on a fact, a gesture or a line of speech.
- **Dialogue (medium).** Keep every line's meaning and every pause the scene needs. Cut tags, stock beats and the narrator's gloss on what was said. Collapse a triple to its concrete item. Keep each speaker's trade and tongue.
- **Facts (never).** No new events, numbers, names, places or objects with story weight. Staging the scene already implies is allowed (section 4, G1).

In the blind pilot this standard is what the judges asked for: the deep versions won on residue (8.0 of 10) and readability (8.17); the medium version won on fidelity where dialogue carried the scene; every judge's grafts moved the chosen version toward deep narration with medium dialogue. The light pass left about 12 tells per passage and is retired.

**What varies by chapter is scope, not strength.** The Medium or Deep label in the `audit/SUMMARY.md` triage table says how many blocks get rebuilt. The line standard is the same in Chapter 1 and Chapter 27.

### 0.2 Method

1. **Cut first. Rewrite only what the cut breaks.** Most fixes are deletions; about two thirds of a typical passage survives (Voice Bible section 5).
2. **When you cut, re-paragraph.** Deleting sentences from short paragraphs without joining them makes the rhythm worse. In the pilot the light pass on Ch 27 raised the share of one-sentence narration paragraphs from 60.6% to 65.4%; the medium and deep passes brought it to about 32%.
3. **Replace, do not just delete, when the scene needs a breath.** "Something flickered in her face. Memory. Pain." (ch27:199) became "She looked down at her bound hands." Two judges called this the best single fix in the pilot.
4. **A rewrite obeys the same rules as the original.** New kickers, epigrams, modern idiom and purple lines introduced by the editor count as failures (G5).
5. **Look up before writing an ending.** The true last line is usually one to five paragraphs above the current one (section 6).

### 0.3 The three questions

Put every doubtful sentence through them (Voice Bible section 0).

1. Could Nagoji touch, count, ride, eat, price or smell the thing this sentence is about?
2. Would he say it aloud to Dhanaji at a cook fire, or shout it at a recruit on the sand?
3. Has the sentence before it already shown this?

Yes, yes, no: keep it. Any other answer: rework it or cut it.

### 0.4 The voice in brief

Nagoji writes like a cavalry officer keeping a campaign book he does not expect anyone to read. He sees footing before scenery, prices before beauty, hands before faces. When he is afraid he counts. He is funny by making things smaller and more exact. His tenderness goes to horses and comes out as chores. He judges foreigners by their tools and habits. He hears Malayalam in fragments and Portuguese as the language of the torture room. He almost never names what he feels: he says what his throat did, and then what he did next. Reread the fifteen touchstones (section 5.1) before each session.

## 4. Dialogue and fidelity guardrails

These ten rules come from the pilot: they are the grafts the blind judges asked for, written as rules. They keep the deep standard from dropping beats or inventing.

**G1. No new facts.** Staging the scene already implies is allowed; new events, numbers, names, places and objects with story weight are not, without the author's approval.

- Allowed: "João was watching my face over the tongs." (A_Q); "the sweat of frightened men" for "old fear" (A_Q, A_X); "She looked down at her bound hands." (B_Q).
- Needs approval: the Ch 27 audit's "three muskets to each of hers, and Thoma's light guns"; its horse "from my own lines" for Savitri; its letter "under the Elayadathu seal". The list of audit additions is `audit/SUMMARY.md` section 3.7.

**G2. Keep each line's meaning; cut tags and beats, not content.** Two "Tell him" clauses make an envoy's message; four make a chant.

## 6. Openings and endings

### 6.1 Rules

1. The last paragraph is an act, an image or a line of dialogue. If one sits within the last five paragraphs, cut down to it before writing anything new.
2. No closing cascade: at most one short standalone paragraph in the last ten lines.
3. The last paragraph does not open with But, For now, For the moment, And, Perhaps, Now, So or Yet.
4. Do not name the lesson, restate the chapter title, or announce the next chapter.
5. No two units close on the same idea. Only Ch 13 ends on the storm. No chapter ends on weighing, blades, "enough", survival, "the next" anything, or a maxim.
6. Openings start on one concrete fact in scene (the model: Chs 2, 13, 16, 21, 26, and Ch 1's first line). No opening correction ("Not with..."), no date-and-backstory paragraph before the scene, and at most one smell opening in the book (Ch 13).

## 10. Process for each chapter

1. **Read first**: the chapter's row in `audit/SUMMARY.md` (scope and decisions), the chapter audit (confirmed, borderline, defended, ending, flab), and this sheet's sections 5 and 6 for the chapter.
2. **Blocks before lines.** Rebuild or compress the named blocks, then do the line edit, so you do not polish sentences that will go.
3. **Line edit** to the standard in section 0: kill list (section 1), rations (section 2), voice rules (section 3), guardrails (section 4). Re-paragraph as you cut.
4. **Opening and ending** to section 6; motifs and signatures to section 7; register and spelling to sections 8 and 9.
5. **Scan** the working copy against the first edition and record the counts:
   `python3 book1_horse_servant_2e/audit/tools/scan_slop.py book1_horse_servant_2e/manuscript -b book1_horse_servant -o book1_horse_servant_2e/audit/<batch>.md`
   Add `-k <marker>` to list the lines for one marker. Then search for the two-sentence correction the scanner misses:
   `grep -nE "(was|is|were|are) not [^.?!]{1,80}\. (It|He|She|They) (was|is|were|are) " <chapter file>`
6. **Accept the chapter only if**: style-sheet excess is 2 or less; no scanner marker rose; one-sentence narration paragraphs are under 35%; the ending matches the register or the change is logged; every Do-not-cut item is present or its change is logged; no new fact entered without approval; dialogue share held or rose; the cut is within five points of the audit estimate; the scanner's dash and quote report is clean.
7. **Read aloud** the first and last pages, and run the swap test on each scene's dialogue.
8. **Log** in `notes/changelog.md` under two headings: prose edits (anti-slop) and content changes (continuity, history, the Gate 0 decisions). Record before and after word counts.
