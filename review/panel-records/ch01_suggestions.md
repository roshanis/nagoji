## A. The ending (main suggestion)

The chapter currently closes on a cascade of seven short paragraphs (lines 97 to 113): a simile, a rhetorical question, a decision, the rumour, hope, a maxim and a storm epigram. The style sheet (6.3) gives a cut-only fallback for Ch 1 that uses your own words.

**A1. Cut the storm close (lines 109 to 113).**

> Original: "Ships meant movement. Movement meant chances."
> "The Portuguese had taken my name and tried to break my body. They had forgotten the simplest lesson of the Deccan monsoon."
> "Storms do not ask permission."

Suggested: cut all three paragraphs. They restate the chapter (name, body) and close on a maxim. The storm motif is reserved for its plant in Ch 5 ("That one carries storms in his bones") and its payoff in Ch 13 ("The storm was here."), and Ch 7 repeats this same line. "The first thing they took was my name" stays as the opening and keeps its later callbacks; only the bookend goes.

**A2. End on the listening (line 107).**

> Original: "Back pressed to cold stone, I listened, and for the first time since they dragged me into this fortress a thin thread of hope tugged at me."

Suggested: "Back pressed to cold stone, I listened."

This makes the chapter end on an act. The rumour paragraph before it has already done the work of hope (ships, the south, bodies that do not ask questions), so the reader supplies the hope. It also drops "for the first time" and the thread image, which the style sheet keeps out of the book until the *tali* scene in Ch 20.

*Lighter alternative if A2 feels too bare:* keep line 107 whole and make only cut A1. The chapter then ends on hope stated once, without the maxim.

**A3. Cut the two one-line paragraphs before "That night" (lines 97 and 99).**

> Original: "The priest's question lingered like smoke in my mind."
> "Did I believe in anything beyond survival and the honour of my people?"

Suggested: cut both, so that "I counted, as I always had. Horses, men, paces, opportunities." runs straight into "That night, as rats skittered along the edges of the straw..."

Duarte asked this question on the page twenty lines earlier, and "I decided that belief did not matter in this place" still answers it. Cutting these also removes a rhetorical question in narration and a stock "lingered like smoke".

With A1 to A3 the last page reads:

> I counted, as I always had. Horses, men, paces, opportunities.
>
> That night, as rats skittered along the edges of the straw and someone sobbed quietly in the darkness, I decided that belief did not matter in this place. Numbers did. Chains did. Ships did.
>
> Rumour moved through the dungeon the next day, low and cautious, like water seeping through a crack.
>
> They were gathering prisoners, men said in whispers. Not for the stake, not for the scaffold, but for transport. Somewhere far to the south the Portuguese needed labour. Somewhere beyond the horizon they were building something that required bodies that did not ask questions.
>
> Back pressed to cold stone, I listened.

"Numbers did. Chains did. Ships did." stays. It counts real things and sets up the ships.

## D. Mechanical pass

- **Em dashes:** none in Ch 1.
- **Double quotes:** all curly in Ch 1.
- **Apostrophes:** straight throughout Ch 1 (Goa's, gaoler's, scholar's, trooper's, sardar's, Duarte's, Viceroy's, Kanka's, someone else's, priest's). The manuscript is mixed book-wide (about 540 straight, 49 curly), so this belongs to the book-wide curly normalisation and is not a Ch 1 edit.

"toward" (line 61) matches the house form. The ellipsis in Duarte's line 59 ("If you do not...") matches the book's usual three full stops.

## E. Continuity

- **Kanka (needs D4).** Ch 1 has Kanka as a black horse referred to as "his", shot by the Portuguese when Nagoji was captured, last ridden "near Chaul" (line 55). Ch 7 (line 1115 of the manuscript) says Kanka was "a black mare" who "died at Vasai", after which he "walked the last miles to Goa on foot", and line 1147 says "the first time since Vasai that I had named a horse". Ch 1 already matches the plan's D4 recommendation (a black stallion shot at capture), so no Ch 1 change; the fix goes in Ch 7 once D4 is settled. Note too that the big Vasai siege falls in 1739, after Nagoji's 1738 capture.
- **Duarte at the gate.** Ch 24 recalls Duarte's question "whether I believed in God or only in powder" and the slow sign of the cross at the gate. Both are set up correctly (Ch 1 line 69 to 73; Ch 2's gate scene).
- **Nails.** "taken my fingernails" (line 13) and "what remained of my left thumbnail" (line 81) agree with Ch 19's ridged nails and the Ch 28 ending.
- **Outline drift (09_outline.md, Ch 1 bullets):** the outline opens "in the Portuguese interrogation chamber" and has him captured "during a failed raid on a coastal fort". The chapter opens in the cellar and never says how he was taken. Suggest updating the outline to match the chapter, not the other way round.
- **Support documents beyond Ch 1, noticed in passing:** the synopsis and character guide still say "diwan" (D19 makes it Dalawa), the outline still calls De Lannoy "captured at Colachel" (settled D2 is desertion), and the character guide still calls him "A Dutch officer".

## F. History and period register

- **Chimaji "coming for Goa" (line 63).** "They knew what we knew: the war was not over. Chimaji was coming for Goa." Goa city never fell. Maratha forces did push into Goa's outer provinces in early 1739, so a Maratha rider in 1738 believing Goa was next is plausible. As his belief, it can stand. The style sheet's G7 offers "I told myself" if you want the hindsight signalled. That would be a sentence change outside the agreed scope, so it is your call, and I recommend leaving it.
- **Not covered by the Historical Note:** the 1737 to 1739 Konkan campaign (the "string of Portuguese forts" in line 11, the "northern strongholds" in line 59, Chaul in line 55) is not dated in `04_historical_note.md`. Nothing in Ch 1 contradicts the record as far as I know, but it should go in the fact table. The plan already lists sources for the Vasai and Goa campaigns as a need.
- **Viceroy:** the title is right for Goa in 1738. The chapter wisely does not name him.
- **Register:** no modern units, clock times or office words. "plantation", "lever" and "conversationally" are all period-safe.
